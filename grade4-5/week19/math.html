🍑 Why a Packing House Runs on Volume

Nonfiction Connection
📖

Real Talk: Peaches in Georgia

Georgia is nicknamed the “Peach State,” and Fort Valley is the county seat of Peach County, right in the middle of peach country. Peaches are picked by hand in late spring and summer. At a packing house, the fruit is washed, sorted by size, and packed into boxes. The boxes are stacked on pallets, cooled in cold storage rooms, and loaded into refrigerated trucks. Every one of those spaces has to be planned: a box that is too small bruises the fruit, and a truck that is not packed well wastes fuel and space. That planning starts with volume, the amount of space inside a solid figure.

🖼️ Picture It: Unit Cubes and Layers

👁

Filling a Box with Cubes

A unit cube is a cube that is 1 unit long, 1 unit wide, and 1 unit tall. It has a volume of 1 cubic unit. The volume of a box is the number of unit cubes that fill it with no gaps and no overlaps. If the cubes are 1 inch on each side, the volume is in cubic inches; if they are 1 foot on each side, it is in cubic feet.

Cubes in one layer × number of layers = volume
Step 1 — Build the bottom layer: the box is 4 cubes long and 3 cubes wide, so the bottom layer has 4 × 3 = 12 cubes.
Step 2 — Stack the layers: the box is 2 layers tall, so 12 × 2 = 24 cubes.
Step 3 — Name the unit: the volume is 24 cubic units. You never have to count every cube one by one.

Using a Layer Table

Every new layer adds the same number of cubes. For a box with 12 cubes in each layer, the rule is “layers × 12 = cubes.”

📦 Two Volume Formulas

🍑

Uncle Thaddeus’s Peach Boxes

Uncle Thaddeus orders boxes that are 12 inches long, 10 inches wide, and 6 inches tall. Counting cubes works for small boxes, but for real boxes and rooms it is faster to multiply. The layers idea turns into two formulas.

V = l × w × hVolume = length × width × height
V = B × hVolume = area of the Base × height
12 × 10 × 6 = 720 cubic inches
Formula 1: V = l × w × h = 12 × 10 × 6. First 12 × 10 = 120, then 120 × 6 = 720 cubic inches.
Formula 2: The base is the bottom face. Its area is B = 12 × 10 = 120 square inches. That is how many 1-inch cubes fit in the bottom layer! Then V = B × h = 120 × 6 = 720 cubic inches.
Any order works: 12 × 10 × 6 = 6 × 12 × 10 = 10 × 6 × 12. Pick the order that makes the math easiest.
💡

Units Tell the Story

Length is measured in units (inches), area in square units (square inches), and volume in cubic units (cubic inches). You may also see cubic inches written as in³, which is read “inches cubed.”

🧱 Composite Figures

🏗️

The Loading Dock Steps

Workers climb onto the loading dock using concrete steps. The steps are not one box. They are a composite figure: two rectangular prisms joined together with no overlap. To find the volume, split the figure into prisms, find each volume, and add.

80 + 30 = 110 cubic feet
Step 1 — Prism A (the bottom): 8 × 5 × 2 = 80 cubic feet.
Step 2 — Prism B (the top step): 3 × 5 × 2 = 30 cubic feet.
Step 3 — Add: 80 + 30 = 110 cubic feet of concrete.
Careful: add the two volumes. Do not multiply them, and do not count any part twice.
🧩

Working Backward

If you know the total volume and the volume of one part, subtract to find the other part. Total 200 cubic feet, Prism A 120 cubic feet: Prism B is 200 − 120 = 80 cubic feet.

🚚 Packing Problems and Missing Measurements

🚚

Mr. Kowalski’s Truck

Before Mr. Kowalski drives to Atlanta, he needs to know how many boxes fit in his trailer. Volume helps with three kinds of questions: finding a missing measurement, comparing boxes, and fitting smaller boxes inside a bigger space.

Missing height = V ÷ (l × w)
Example 1 — Missing measurement: a cooler holds 240 cubic feet. It is 10 feet long and 6 feet wide. The base area is 10 × 6 = 60 square feet, so the height is 240 ÷ 60 = 4 feet. Check: 10 × 6 × 4 = 240.
Example 2 — Doubling: if you double just one measurement of a box, the volume doubles too. A 4 by 3 by 2 box holds 24 cubic feet; a 4 by 3 by 4 box holds 48 cubic feet.
Example 3 — A cube: every edge of a cube is the same length. A cube with 3-foot edges has a volume of 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 cubic feet.

How Many Boxes Fit?

A space is 8 feet long, 6 feet wide, and 4 feet tall. Crates are 2-foot cubes. Fit them one direction at a time: 8 ÷ 2 = 4 crates long, 6 ÷ 2 = 3 crates wide, 4 ÷ 2 = 2 crates tall. That is 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 crates. Check with volume: the space is 192 cubic feet, each crate is 8 cubic feet, and 192 ÷ 8 = 24.

🧩

Multi-Step Plan

1. Read the whole problem and decide what it asks: a volume, a missing length, or a number of boxes. 2. Write the measurements you know. 3. Do one step at a time. 4. Label your answer with the right unit and check that it makes sense.
