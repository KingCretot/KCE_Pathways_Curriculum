🏗️ How Nonfiction Is Built

Nonfiction Skill

Writers don't just dump facts on a page. They organize them in a pattern — and once you can spot the pattern, the whole piece gets easier to understand and remember.

StructureWhat it doesSignal words
Cause & EffectExplains why something happens and what resultsbecause, so, as a result
Compare & ContrastShows how two things are alike and differenthowever, both, unlike
Problem & SolutionNames a problem, then offers a fixproblem, solve, answer
SequencePuts events in time orderfirst, next, finally

📄 Source A

Source A • Science Magazine for Students Why Teens Are So Tired

As children reach their early teens, their internal body clock naturally shifts later. Because of this shift, most teens do not feel sleepy until around 11pm — even when they get in bed earlier. As a result, a student who must wake at 6am to catch the bus often gets seven hours of sleep instead of the nine that doctors recommend. Over weeks, that missing sleep adds up, and students report more trouble focusing in their morning classes.

+9

What TEXT STRUCTURE does Source A use?

📄 Source B

Source B • School Board Newsletter One District's Answer

Our district faced a real problem: too many students were arriving to first period exhausted and unfocused. To solve it, the board moved the middle school start time from 7:30am to 8:20am. To make the change work, bus routes were rescheduled and after-school practices were shortened by fifteen minutes. Since the switch, first-period attendance has improved, though some families say the later dismissal makes evening schedules tighter.

+9

What TEXT STRUCTURE does Source B use?

💡 Putting Two Sources Together

👁

Think About This

Neither source alone answers the whole question. Source A explains WHY the problem exists. Source B shows what happened when one district actually DID something about it. Together, they're far stronger than either one on its own.

🧩

Picture It: Synthesizing

A synthesized answer sounds like this: "Teens' body clocks shift later (Source A), which is why one district's later start time improved first-period attendance (Source B) — though it created new scheduling tradeoffs."

⚠️

Careful Readers Notice This

Source B also admits a downside — tighter evening schedules. A strong report includes that, instead of pretending every source agrees perfectly.
