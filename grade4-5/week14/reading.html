🖼️ Picture It: Quote or Paraphrase?

When you research, you borrow information from a source. There are two honest ways to use it. You can quote the author’s exact words, or you can paraphrase the idea in your own words. Either way, you name the source. Here is one sentence from the magazine article used both ways.

📄 The original sentence (Source B)

In the late afternoon, the cruise ships sail out through a channel called Government Cut, past the tip of Miami Beach.

🗨️ QUOTE: exact words

Marisol Ortega writes, “In the late afternoon, the cruise ships sail out through a channel called Government Cut, past the tip of Miami Beach.”

🔄 PARAPHRASE: your own words

Marisol Ortega explains that cruise ships leave Miami near the end of the day by passing Miami Beach on a waterway named Government Cut.

🚫 PLAGIARISM: copied, no credit

In the late afternoon, the cruise ships sail out through a channel called Government Cut. (No quotation marks and no source, so it pretends the words are the writer’s own.)
“ ” quotation markssource named, comma after the tagcapital first word. end mark inside
RuleExample
1. A quote copies the exact words. Put quotation marks around them and change nothing.“Then the biggest workers of all start to move,” writes Marisol Ortega.
2. Name the source in a tag before or after the quote. Use a comma between the tag and the quote.Marisol Ortega writes, “On another side of the island, bright white cruise ships fill up with passengers.”
3. Capitalize the first word of a quoted sentence, and keep the end mark inside the closing quotation mark.The fact sheet says, “In 2024, more than 8 million cruise passengers passed through.”
4. A paraphrase keeps the meaning but uses your own words and sentence pattern. No quotation marks, but you still name the source.The fact sheet explains that the port is on an island close to downtown Miami.
5. Plagiarism is using someone’s words or ideas without credit. Copied words need quotation marks even when you name the source.Copied + no marks = plagiarism
6. Cite simply: give the title plus the author or organization.“Giants of the Bay” by Marisol Ortega
💡

Quote or Paraphrase: Which Should I Use?

Quote when the exact words are special: a vivid comparison, a strong opinion, or a person’s own words, like a worker describing his job. Paraphrase most facts and explanations, so your report sounds like YOU.

🗨️ Quote or Paraphrase?

🔎

How to Tell

Look for quotation marks around words that match the source exactly: that is a quote. If the idea is restated in new words with no quotation marks, it is a paraphrase. A name or the word “says” does NOT make something a quote. Copied words with no quotation marks are plagiarism.

🔄 Paraphrase Like a Pro

🪜

Four Steps to a Strong Paraphrase

  1. Read the sentence until you understand the idea.
  2. Cover the sentence so you can’t peek.
  3. Write the idea from memory, in your own words and your own sentence pattern. Name the source.
  4. Check: Is the meaning the same? Did you copy 4 or more words in a row? Names, numbers, and special words (like Dodge Island or container) can stay.
🧪

Worked Example

Original (Source A): “The port sits on Dodge Island in Biscayne Bay, right next to downtown Miami, Florida.”

❌ Too close: The port sits on Dodge Island in Biscayne Bay, close to downtown Miami.
Nine words in a row are copied (crossed out), and no source is named. That is plagiarism, even though a few words changed.

✅ Strong: The Biscayne Bay Learning Network explains that Miami’s port is on an island called Dodge Island, just a short trip from the city’s downtown.
Same meaning, new words and sentence pattern, and the organization is named. No 4 words in a row are copied. Only names (Dodge Island, Miami) and single words like port and downtown stayed the same, which is fine.

🎯

Choose Relevant Information

Before you take notes, write your research question. Then keep only facts that help answer it. If your question is “How do containers get on and off ships?”, the cranes are relevant, but the number of cruise passengers is not, even though it is interesting.

📝 Writer’s Turn: Say It Your Way

Your Paraphrase Is Checked by Four Fair Rules

Your sentence must (1) have enough words to be a full idea, (2) not copy 4 or more words in a row from the original (names can stay), (3) keep the key ideas, and (4) name the source. Use the four steps: read, cover, write, check. If a rule is missed, the feedback tells you which one.

Original (Source B, paragraph 4, by Marisol Ortega): Fruits and vegetables from Central and South America must stay cold, so they ride inside refrigerated containers that plug into power on the ship.
  • Use at least 8 words.
  • Do not copy 4 or more words in a row from the original. (The name Central and South America is fine to keep.)
