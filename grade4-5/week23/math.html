🌊 Why a Tide Pool Survey Runs on Data

Nonfiction Connection
📖

Real Talk: Haystack Rock

Haystack Rock is a sea stack of basalt, a rock formed from lava, that rises 235 feet above the beach at Cannon Beach, Oregon. At low tide, people can walk right up to the tide pools around its base. The rock and its tide pools are protected: the State of Oregon made the area a Marine Garden, where nothing may be taken, and the rock itself is part of the Oregon Islands National Wildlife Refuge. Tufted puffins raise their chicks on the rock in spring and summer. The tide pools hold sea stars, sea anemones, crabs, chitons, and limpets. The intertidal zone is the strip of shore that is underwater at high tide and uncovered at low tide. Scientists and volunteers count and measure these animals year after year, and the numbers they collect show whether the tide pools are healthy.

🖼️ Picture It: Reading a Line Plot

👁

Every X Is One Measurement

Ms. Uchida measured the shell widths of 12 purple shore crabs to the nearest 1/8 inch. Mr. Varga put the data on a line plot. A line plot shows data as X marks above a number line. Each tick mark on the number line stands for 1/8 inch. Data are the numbers you collect, like the widths of 12 crabs.

Count the X’s, read the numbers below them
How many crabs? Count every X: 1 + 2 + 4 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 12 crabs.
Most common width: the tallest stack is at 1 3/8 inches, with 4 X’s.
Wider than 1 1/2 inches: 2 X’s at 1 5/8 and 1 X at 1 7/8, so 3 crabs. (The crabs at exactly 1 1/2 do not count.)
Widest minus narrowest: 1 7/8 − 1 1/8 = 6/8 = 3/4 inch.
🧩

Reading the Tick Marks

Between 1 and 2 there are 8 equal spaces, so each space is 1/8. The tick at 2/8 past 1 is named 1 1/4, and the tick at 4/8 past 1 is named 1 1/2. An empty tick, like 1 3/4 here, just means no crab had that width.

📏 Making a Line Plot

🐚

Vikram’s Hermit Crab List

Vikram measured the shells of 10 hermit crabs, holding his ruler beside each one. His list, in inches: 3/4, 1, 7/8, 3/4, 1 1/4, 6/8, 1, 1 1/8, 7/8, 1 2/8. Mr. Varga says, “Choose a scale that gives every measurement its own tick mark.”

List → number line → X’s
Step 1 — Find the least and greatest: the shortest shell is 3/4 inch and the longest is 1 1/4 inches.
Step 2 — Pick the scale: 7/8 and 1 1/8 are eighths, so the number line needs a tick mark every 1/8 inch. Eighths also include every half and fourth.
Step 3 — Rename equivalent fractions: 6/8 = 3/4 and 1 2/8 = 1 1/4. 1/2 and 4/8 are equivalent fractions, so they share one stack of X’s. The same is true for 6/8 and 3/4.
Step 4 — Plot and check: one X for each measurement. Vikram counts 10 X’s, the same as his list. Give the plot a title and label the units.
💡

Which Scale?

If the data use only halves, mark halves. If any value is in fourths, mark fourths. If any value is in eighths, mark eighths. Use the smallest unit that appears, so no X has to squeeze between two tick marks.

➕ Solving Problems with Line Plot Data

📓

Mr. Varga’s Survey Questions

Mr. Varga looks back at the crab line plot and asks questions that need adding and subtracting. Before you add or subtract, rewrite the fractions with a common denominator.

Same denominator first, then add or subtract
Example 1 — A total: what do the four 1 3/8-inch crabs measure together? 1 3/8 + 1 3/8 + 1 3/8 + 1 3/8 = 4 12/8 = 4 + 1 4/8 = 5 1/2 inches.
Example 2 — Unlike denominators: how much wider is the widest crab than a 1 1/4-inch crab? Rename 1 1/4 as 1 2/8. Then 1 7/8 − 1 2/8 = 5/8 inch.
Example 3 — Regrouping: how much narrower than 2 inches is a 1 3/8-inch crab? Rename 2 as 1 8/8. Then 1 8/8 − 1 3/8 = 5/8 inch.
🧩

Read Carefully

“All the crabs at 1 1/2 inches” means every X in that stack, so multiply or add once for each X. “A 1 1/2-inch crab” means just one of them.

🫙 Finding the Fair Share

🫙

Ms. Uchida’s Sample Jars

Ms. Uchida scoops seawater from 8 tide pools into 8 jars to test it later. The jars are not filled evenly. The fair share is the amount each jar would hold if all the water were poured together and shared equally.

Fair share = total ÷ number of X’s
Step 1 — Find the total: rename everything in eighths and add one value for each X: 2/8 + 2/8 + 3/8 + 4/8 + 4/8 + 5/8 + 6/8 + 6/8 = 32/8 = 4 cups.
Step 2 — Count the X’s: there are 8 jars.
Step 3 — Share equally: 4 cups ÷ 8 jars = 4/8 = 1/2 cup in each jar. A fraction is a division: 4 ÷ 8 = 4/8.
Check: the jars above 1/2 cup have 1/8 + 2/8 + 2/8 = 5/8 cup extra, and the jars below 1/2 cup are missing 2/8 + 2/8 + 1/8 = 5/8 cup. The extra fills the gaps exactly.
⚠️

Divide by the X’s, Not the Columns

This plot has 5 stacks but 8 X’s. Each X is one jar, so divide the total by 8, not by 5.
