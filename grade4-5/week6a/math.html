standard algorithm
The stacked, step-by-step way of multiplying larger numbers on paper.
partial product
One piece of a multiplication problem, found before adding all the pieces together.
regroup
Carrying extra value into the next place value column.
factor
A number being multiplied.
estimate
A smart, quick guess made by rounding first.
product
The answer to a multiplication problem.
dividend 🆕
The total amount being split up — the number you're dividing.
divisor 🆕
The number you're splitting the dividend into equal groups by.

🔀 NEW! Division Is Multiplication's Undo Button

👁

The Big Idea

Every multiplication fact has a division fact hiding right inside it. If 6 × 7 = 42, then 42 ÷ 7 = 6 AND 42 ÷ 6 = 7. Three numbers, four related facts — that's a fact family, and division is really just multiplication working backward.

42
6
7
🧩

Reading the Triangle

Cover the top number: 6 × 7 = 42. Cover either bottom number: 42 ÷ 7 = 6, or 42 ÷ 6 = 7. Same three numbers, every time.

🤝

Let's Build One Together

Start with 8 × 9 = 72. The fact family is 8, 9, and 72. Flip it around: 9 × 8 = 72. Now undo it: 72 ÷ 9 = 8, and 72 ÷ 8 = 9. Four facts, three numbers — every fact family works exactly the same way.

🎯 NEW! Quotient Estimation Game

👁

Why Estimate BEFORE You Divide?

Long division (coming up next week!) is much easier when you already have a smart guess for the answer. Round the dividend and divisor first, then estimate — it keeps you from making a wildly wrong answer without noticing.

Example: 182 ÷ 6 — round 182 to 180. 180 ÷ 6 = 30. So the real answer should land close to 30. (Math often uses the symbol ≈ to mean "is about" — you'll see it below, and now you know what it means!)

🤝

Let's Try Together

216 ÷ 4 — round 216 to 220 (nearest ten). 220 ÷ 4 = 55. So 216 ÷ 4 is about 55. When you actually divide it out, the real answer (54) lands right next door to your estimate — that's how you know your estimate did its job.
