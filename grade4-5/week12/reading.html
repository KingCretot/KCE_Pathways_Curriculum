🖼️ Picture It: Build a Paragraph Like a Building

Informative writing teaches the reader facts about a real topic. Each paragraph is built around ONE main idea, just like each floor of a building rests on the one below it. Look at how the parts stack up.

📄

A Model Paragraph

Every worker on a building site wears safety gear. First, each person puts on a hard hat to protect against falling objects. Next, workers lace up boots with steel toes. They also wear bright vests so the crane operator can see them. Safety gear helps every member of the crew go home safe at the end of the day.

PartIts job
Topic sentenceStates the main idea. It usually comes first.
Supporting detailsFacts, examples, and explanations that back up the main idea.
Transition wordsConnect one idea to the next so the reader can follow along.
Concluding sentenceWraps up the paragraph by restating the main idea in a new way.
⚠️

Stay on One Idea

Every sentence in a paragraph must support the topic sentence. If the safety-gear paragraph said, “My cousin loves pizza,” that sentence would be off-topic. It wanders away from the main idea, so a good writer takes it out.

🔗 Transition Words: Hallways Between Ideas

Transitions are like the hallways and stairs in a building. They lead the reader from one idea to the next. Each transition has a JOB, so pick the one that matches what you mean.

JobTransitionsExample
Show order or stepsfirst, next, then, finallyFirst, the surveyor marks the corners. Next, the digging begins.
Give an examplefor exampleHouston has many tall buildings. For example, some downtown towers are more than 70 stories tall.
Show a contrast (a difference)howeverWood is light and easy to cut. However, it can burn.
Show an effect (what happened because of something)as a resultThe rain lasted all week. As a result, the crew could not pour concrete.
Add another detailalsoElectricians run wires. They also install lights.
✏️

Comma Rule

When a transition like First, However, For example, As a result, or Finally starts a sentence, put a comma right after it: Finally, the inspector signs the papers.

🧩

Worked Example: Pick the Transition

The crane broke down on Monday. ___, the steel delivery had to wait until Tuesday.

Ask: how are these two ideas connected? The delivery waited because the crane broke. That is a cause and its effect, so the best transition is As a result. “However” would signal a difference, and “For example” would need an example of the first idea, so neither fits.

🌟 Vocabulary Round-Up

Flip each card to review today's words.

informative writing
Writing that teaches the reader facts about a real topic.
topic sentence
The sentence that tells the main idea of a paragraph. It usually comes first.
supporting detail
A fact, example, or explanation that backs up the main idea.
transition
A word or phrase, like first or however, that connects one idea to the next.
concluding sentence
The last sentence of a paragraph. It wraps up the main idea.
off-topic
A sentence that wanders away from the main idea and does not belong.

🏗️ How an Apartment Building Goes Up in Houston, Part 1

Informative Article

1Across Houston, Texas, tall cranes swing above neighborhoods where new apartment buildings are rising. Houston is one of the largest cities in the United States, and thousands of new people move there every year. All of those people need homes. Building an apartment building takes careful planning, strong materials, and many skilled workers. In Houston, it also means dealing with tricky soil and powerful storms.

2First, the crew must build a strong foundation, and in Houston that is a special challenge. Much of the ground under the city is clay soil. When it rains, the clay soaks up water and swells. When the weather is dry, the clay shrinks and cracks. This movement can push and pull on a building. For example, a foundation that rests only on the top layer of clay could crack as the ground rises and sinks. To solve this problem, workers drill deep holes and fill them with steel bars and concrete to make strong columns called piers. The piers reach down past the top layer of clay to firmer ground. As a result, the building rests on solid support that the shifting clay cannot easily move.

3Next, the crew builds the frame, which is the skeleton of the building. The frame is made of steel and concrete. Ironworkers tie steel bars called rebar into grids, and then concrete is poured around the steel. Concrete is very strong when it is squeezed, but it can crack when it is stretched or bent. However, steel is strong when it is stretched. Together, the two materials make a frame that is much stronger than either one alone. Houston summers are very hot, so crews often pour concrete early in the morning, before the hottest part of the day.

🏗️ How an Apartment Building Goes Up in Houston, Part 2

Informative Article

4Houston sits near the Gulf of Mexico, so the building must also be ready for hurricanes. Hurricanes bring powerful winds and heavy rain. Engineers design the frame and roof to hold tight against strong winds, and builders install thick windows made to resist flying objects. Flooding is another danger. After Hurricane Harvey flooded large parts of Houston in 2017, the city passed stricter rules for building in areas that flood. As a result, many new buildings in those areas must have their lowest floor built higher off the ground, so floodwater is less likely to get inside.

5Finally, none of this work could happen without a skilled crew. Surveyors measure the land and mark exactly where the building will stand. A crane operator lifts heavy loads of steel high into the air. Electricians run wires through the walls, and plumbers connect pipes for kitchens and bathrooms. City inspectors visit at each stage to check that the work is safe. Each worker has a different job, but they all work together as one team.

6Building an apartment building in Houston is a big job with many steps. From the deep piers that hold steady in the clay, to the strong steel-and-concrete frame, to the storm-ready windows and raised floors, every part is planned with care. The next time you see a crane over a city, you will know how much teamwork goes into each new home.

📓 Writer's Notebook: Build Your Own Paragraph

Your Turn to Write

Write an informative paragraph of 4 to 6 sentences about one building or structure in your own community, like a barn, a bridge, a library, a school, or a store. Or write about one job on the Houston crew.

  • Start with a topic sentence that tells the main idea.
  • Add at least two supporting details that stay on that one idea.
  • Use at least two transition words (first, next, however, for example, as a result, finally, also).
  • End with a concluding sentence that wraps it up.
