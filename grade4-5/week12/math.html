🖼️ Picture It: Same Floor, Different Tiles

👁

Equivalent Fractions Name the Same Amount

Diego is tiling three hallways that are exactly the same size. Hallway A uses 2 big tiles, Hallway B uses 4 medium tiles, and Hallway C uses 8 small tiles. In every hallway, the blue tiles reach the same dashed line.

1/2 = 2/4 = 4/8
Same amount of blue: each hallway is half blue, so 1/2, 2/4, and 4/8 are equivalent fractions.
Smaller tiles, more of them: from Hallway A to Hallway B, each tile is cut in 2. There are 2 times as many tiles in all (2 → 4) and 2 times as many blue tiles (1 → 2).
The pattern: the numerator and the denominator change by the same factor. That is the secret to every equivalent fraction.

✖️ Multiply or Divide by n/n

💡

Why It Works

Any number (except 0) over itself equals 1: 2/2 = 1, 3/3 = 1, 4/4 = 1. Multiplying or dividing by 1 never changes a number’s value. So when you multiply or divide the numerator and denominator by the same number, you get an equivalent fraction.

2/7 × 4/4 = 8/28
Problem: Write 2/7 using 28ths.
Step 1 — Compare the denominators: 7 × 4 = 28, so the number to use is 4.
Step 2 — Multiply by 4/4: 2 × 4 = 8 and 7 × 4 = 28.
Answer: 2/7 = 8/28.
14/16 ÷ 2/2 = 7/8
Problem: Diego’s tile chart shows 14/16 of a floor finished. Write it using eighths.
Step 1 — Compare the denominators: 16 ÷ 2 = 8, so the number to use is 2.
Step 2 — Divide by 2/2: 14 ÷ 2 = 7 and 16 ÷ 2 = 8.
Answer: 14/16 = 7/8.
⚠️

Watch Out: Never Add!

Adding the same number to the top and bottom does not make an equivalent fraction. Add 1 to the top and bottom of 1/2 and you get 2/3, but 2/3 is more than 1/2. Always multiply or divide by the same number.

📐 Simplest Form with the GCF

📐

Ms. Patel’s Floor Plan

Ms. Patel draws one floor of the building on a grid of 36 squares. The 24 gold squares are apartments, and the gray squares are hallways and stairs. So 24/36 of the floor is apartments. Can we write that fraction with smaller numbers?

24/36 = 2/3
The slow way — small steps: divide by 2/2 to get 12/18, by 2/2 again to get 6/9, then by 3/3 to get 2/3. It works, but it takes three steps.
The fast way — use the GCF:
Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24  •  Factors of 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36
The greatest common factor is 12. Divide by 12/12: 24 ÷ 12 = 2 and 36 ÷ 12 = 3. Done in one step!
Check: the only common factor of 2 and 3 is 1, so 2/3 is in simplest form. Look at the grid: 4 of the 6 rows are gold, and 4/6 = 2/3.
🧩

Simplest Form Means…

A fraction is in simplest form when the numerator and denominator have no common factor except 1. Dividing by the GCF gets you there in one step.

🌟 Vocabulary Round-Up

Flip each card to review today’s words.

fraction
A number that names part of a whole or part of a group, like 3/4.
numerator
The top number. It tells how many equal parts you have.
denominator
The bottom number. It tells how many equal parts make one whole.
equivalent fractions
Fractions that name the same amount, like 1/2 and 4/8.
simplest form
A fraction whose numerator and denominator have no common factor except 1.
greatest common factor (GCF)
The biggest factor two numbers share. Divide by it to simplify in one step.
improper fraction
A fraction with a numerator greater than or equal to its denominator, like 11/4.
mixed number
A whole number and a fraction together, like 2 3/4.

🔧 Mixed Numbers and Improper Fractions

🔧

Mr. Nguyen’s Pipe

Mr. Nguyen measures a pipe in fourths of a foot. It is 11 fourths long: 11/4 feet. That is an improper fraction because the numerator is bigger than the denominator. On the number line you can see it is also 2 whole feet and 3/4 of a foot.

Improper → Mixed: 11/4 = 2 3/4
Divide the numerator by the denominator: 11 ÷ 4 = 2 R3.
The quotient is the whole number: 2 whole feet.
The remainder is the new numerator: 3 fourths are left over, so 11/4 = 2 3/4.
Mixed → Improper: 2 4/5 = 14/5
Problem: Darnell needs a bar that is 2 4/5 feet long. How many fifths is that?
Step 1 — Change the wholes to fifths: 1 whole = 5 fifths, so 2 wholes = 2 × 5 = 10 fifths.
Step 2 — Add the extra fifths: 10 + 4 = 14.
Answer: 2 4/5 = 14/5. The denominator stays the same.
