🥧 Sharing a Fraction: Unit Fraction ÷ Whole Number

🥧

The Leftover Apple Crisp

At lunch, 1/2 of a pan of Ms. Dunbar’s apple crisp is left. Silas shares it equally among 3 pickers. How much of the WHOLE pan does each picker get? The problem is 1/2 ÷ 3. A unit fraction is a fraction with a numerator of 1, like 1/2, 1/3, or 1/8. 1/4 is a unit fraction because its numerator is 1.

1/2 ÷ 3 = 1/6
Read the model: The tape diagram shows the half pan cut into 3 equal shares. Each share is 1 of 6 equal pieces in the whole pan, so each picker gets 1/6.
The pattern: to divide a unit fraction by a whole number, keep the 1 on top and multiply the denominator by the whole number: 1/2 ÷ 3 = 1/(2 × 3) = 1/6.
Why it works: sharing among 3 is the same as taking 1/3 of the amount, and 1/3 × 1/2 = 1/6. That is the warm-up’s bridge!
Check with multiplication: 3 shares of 1/6 should rebuild the half: 1/6 × 3 = 3/6 = 1/2. ✔
Example 2: Grandma Lucinda has 1/4 pound of cinnamon to split equally into 2 jars. 1/4 ÷ 2 = 1/(4 × 2) = 1/8 pound in each jar. Check: 1/8 × 2 = 2/8 = 1/4. ✔
💡

Does It Make Sense?

The quotient, 1/6, tells how much of the pan each worker gets. When you share a unit fraction among a whole number of people, each share must be SMALLER than what you started with. 1/6 is less than 1/2, so the answer makes sense.

🍏 How Many Fit? Whole Number ÷ Unit Fraction

🛍️

Mr. Barrera’s Apple Chip Bags

Mr. Barrera has 3 pounds of dried apple chips. He packs them into bags that each hold 1/4 pound. How many bags can he fill? The problem is 3 ÷ 1/4. This time you are not sharing a fraction. You are asking how many 1/4-pound pieces fit into 3 whole pounds. In 3 ÷ 1/4, the divisor is 1/4, the size of each bag.

3 ÷ 1/4 = 12
Read the model: On the number line, Mr. Barrera counts 12 jumps of 1/4 from 0 to 3. Each whole pound holds 4 quarter-pound bags, and there are 3 pounds: 3 × 4 = 12 bags.
The pattern: to divide a whole number by a unit fraction, multiply the whole number by the denominator: 3 ÷ 1/4 = 3 × 4 = 12.
Check with multiplication: 12 bags of 1/4 pound each should make 3 pounds: 12 × 1/4 = 12/4 = 3. ✔
Example 2: The orchard’s walking trail is 2 miles long, with a marker every 1/5 mile. How many 1/5-mile sections are there? 2 ÷ 1/5 = 2 × 5 = 10 sections. Check: 10 × 1/5 = 10/5 = 2. ✔
💡

Does It Make Sense?

When you divide a whole number by a unit fraction, you are counting small pieces, so the quotient is BIGGER than the whole number you started with. 12 is greater than 3, so the answer makes sense.

🔀 Which Kind of Division Is It?

🧺

Grandma Lucinda’s Two Questions

At the farm stand, Grandma Lucinda asks two kinds of questions all day long. “How much does each person get?” and “How many can we make?” Both are division, but they work in opposite directions. In 1/2 ÷ 3, the dividend is 1/2, the half pan of apple crisp Silas started with.

“How much in each share?” A fraction is shared by a whole number of people or parts.unit fraction ÷ whole number → a SMALLER fraction
“How many fit?” A whole amount is cut into equal groups (pieces) that are each a unit fraction. You count how many groups.whole number ÷ unit fraction → a BIGGER whole number
Start with what you HAVE, then divide
Story A: “Silas has 1/3 of a crate of apples. He splits it equally into 2 baskets. How much of the crate is in each basket?” He HAS 1/3 of a crate, and he shares it 2 ways: 1/3 ÷ 2 = 1/6 of the crate.
Story B: “Silas has 2 crates of apples. Each basket holds 1/3 of a crate. How many baskets can he fill?” He HAS 2 crates, and each piece is 1/3: 2 ÷ 1/3 = 6 baskets.
Order matters: 1/3 ÷ 2 = 1/6, but 2 ÷ 1/3 = 6. Same numbers, very different answers!
🧩

Check Every Quotient

Multiplication and division are inverse operations, so 1/6 × 3 = 1/2 checks 1/2 ÷ 3 = 1/6. To check any division, multiply the quotient by the divisor. If you get the dividend back, your answer is right.

⏰ Two-Step Problems: Divide, Then Keep Going

🍯

Grandma Lucinda’s Spice Shakers

Real problems rarely stop after one step. Sometimes a quotient like 1/8 pound is hard to picture, so you convert it to a smaller unit. Sometimes you divide first and then add or subtract. You learned in Unit 3 that 1 pound = 16 ounces and 1 hour = 60 minutes. Those facts come back today.

1/2 pound ÷ 4 = 1/8 pound = 2 ounces
Step 1 — Divide: Grandma Lucinda splits 1/2 pound of cinnamon sugar equally into 4 shakers. 1/2 ÷ 4 = 1/8 pound in each shaker.
Step 2 — Convert: 1/8 of a pound is 1/8 of 16 ounces: 16 ÷ 8 = 2 ounces in each shaker.
1/3 hour ÷ 2 = 1/6 hour = 10 minutes
Step 1 — Divide: Silas has 1/3 hour to wash 2 apple bins, with the same time for each. 1/3 ÷ 2 = 1/6 hour per bin.
Step 2 — Convert: 1/6 of 60 minutes: 60 ÷ 6 = 10 minutes per bin.
4 ÷ 1/3 = 12 bags, and 12 − 5 = 7 bags left
Step 1 — Divide: Mr. Barrera packs 4 pounds of apple chips into 1/3-pound bags: 4 ÷ 1/3 = 4 × 3 = 12 bags.
Step 2 — Subtract: He sells 5 bags in the morning: 12 − 5 = 7 bags left.
🧩

Two-Step Plan

1. Ask: is this “how much in each share?” or “how many fit?” 2. Write and solve the division. 3. Read the question again: does it want a different unit, or a total, or what is left? Do that second step. 4. Check: does the size of your answer make sense?
