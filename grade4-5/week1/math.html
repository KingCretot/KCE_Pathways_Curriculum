🔢 Breaking Down Mia's Big Number

👁

Think About This

In Grade 3, the biggest place you used was hundreds. Today we go further: ones, tens, hundreds, THOUSANDS, ten-thousands, hundred-thousands, and MILLIONS. Each place is still worth 10 times the one before it — that rule never changes, no matter how big the number gets.

🧩

Picture It: 1,209,647 Visitors

1,000,000 (1 million) + 200,000 (2 hundred-thousands) + 0 (0 ten-thousands) + 9,000 (9 thousands) + 600 (6 hundreds) + 40 (4 tens) + 7 (7 ones)

📖

digit vs. value

A digit is just the symbol (0-9) you see written down. Its value depends on WHERE it sits. The digit 2 in 1,209,647 is worth 200,000 — not just "2" — because of its place.

🌟 Vocabulary Round-Up

Flip each card — these are words Mia will actually use for the rest of the day.

factor
One of the numbers being multiplied together.
product
The answer you get when you multiply.
estimate
A smart, quick guess made by rounding numbers first.
revenue
The total money a business earns from sales, before costs are subtracted.
expense
Money a business spends to operate — like ingredients and gas for the truck.
profit
What's left after you subtract expenses from revenue — what the business actually keeps.

✖️ Mia's Prep Math: The Area Model

🌮

Mia's Problem

Mia thinks she'll serve customers for 26 hours across the whole weekend, at about 34 tacos per hour. She needs to know exactly how many tacos to prep. That's 34 × 26 — a multiplication too big to just "know," so she uses the AREA MODEL.

👁

How It Works

Break both numbers apart by place value (34 = 30+4, and 26 = 20+6), multiply each pair of pieces separately, then add all four results together. It's called the area model because it works like splitting a rectangle into four smaller rectangles.

600 + 80 + 180 + 24 = 884 tacos
