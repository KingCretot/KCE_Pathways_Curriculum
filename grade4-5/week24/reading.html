🖼️ Picture It: Reading Between the Lines

Authors do not always tell you how a character feels or what kind of person the character is. Often they show you through what the character says, does, and thinks, and how others react. You put those clues together with what you already know to make an inference. Read this short model and look for clues about Amos.

Model TextThe Spelling Bee

An original story

Amos crumpled his practice list and shoved it under his bed. “Spelling is pointless,” he muttered. But that night, his little sister found him at the kitchen table, whispering words to himself, long after everyone else had gone to sleep.

Text clue + what I knowMy inference
He crumples the list and says spelling is pointless. People who are worried sometimes act like they don’t care.Amos is nervous about the spelling bee.
He secretly practices late at night. People practice in secret when something matters to them.Amos really does want to do well.

Tracking How a Character Changes

Part of the storyAsk yourself
BeginningWhat is the character like? What is one trait, and what evidence shows it?
Turning pointWhat event or person starts to change the character?
EndWhat is the character like now? What evidence shows the new trait?
ThemeWhat lesson does this change teach anyone, not only this character?
💡

Trait or Feeling?

A feeling can change in a minute: bored, angry, thrilled. A character trait describes how a character usually is: stubborn, curious, generous. When a character truly changes, a trait changes, and the author shows it with actions.

🌟 Vocabulary Round-Up

Flip each card to review today’s words. The last two come straight from today’s story.

inference
A smart conclusion you make by combining text clues with what you already know.
character trait
A word that tells how a character usually is, such as curious, stubborn, or kind.
motivation
The reason a character does something; what the character wants.
turning point
The moment in a story when things begin to change for the main character.
character change
How a character’s traits, feelings, or beliefs are different at the end of a story.
theme
The message about life a story shows, often through how the main character changes.
slumped
Sat or leaned in a tired, droopy way. “She slumped in the corner of the boat with her hood pulled tight.”
scattered
Spread out over a wide area. “the green islands scattered across the sound.”

📖 Text A: The Story

This story is told by a narrator who is not in the story (third person). Watch what Anya says and does at the beginning, what changes her, and how she acts at the end. Notice the paragraph numbers; you will use them.

Text A • Realistic StoryThe Wrong Screen

An original story

1Anya had decided that Sitka, Alaska, was the most boring town on Earth. Her best friend, Amina, had moved to Seattle in June, and now Anya spent her afternoons scrolling through photos of city lights while rain tapped on the window. “There’s nothing to do here,” she told anyone who would listen. “Nothing but fish and fog.”

2So when Grandpa Arne asked her to help on his small fishing boat for a week, she sighed loudly enough for the whole kitchen to hear. On the first morning, she slumped in the corner of the boat with her hood pulled tight, and when Grandpa pointed out a bald eagle on a spruce branch, she didn’t even look up.

3On the third morning, the fog lifted. Across Sitka Sound, the snowy cone of Mount Edgecumbe glowed pink in the sunrise. A humpback whale rose beside the boat, blew a misty spout, and slid back under the water. Anya’s phone was in her pocket, but she forgot to take it out. She just stared.

4That afternoon, a fish hit Anya’s line so hard that the rod bent nearly in half. She pulled and reeled until her arms burned. “Grandpa, help!” she called. But Grandpa only kept a steady hand on the net. “You’ve got it,” he said. “Slow and steady.” When the silver coho finally slid into the net, Anya let out a whoop that echoed off the water.

5That night, Anya’s little brother, Arvid, asked what she had done all day. Anya opened her mouth to say “nothing,” then stopped. Instead, she told him about the whale’s breath hanging in the cold air, the eagle, and the coho that almost pulled her overboard. Arvid’s eyes grew wide. “Can I come tomorrow?” he begged.

6On the last morning, Anya was the first one on the dock, zipping her rain jacket before Grandpa had even finished his coffee. As the boat pulled away, she saw her town the way a visitor might: the bright houses along the harbor, the mountains rising behind them, the green islands scattered across the sound. “Grandpa,” she said quietly, “I think I was looking at the wrong screen.”

✉️ Text B: Anya’s Letter

A few weeks later, Anya writes to her friend Amina. This time Anya tells her own story in the first person, using I. Read closely: what she says about herself is evidence of how much she has changed.

Text B • LetterA Letter to Amina

An original letter

Dear Amina,

1Remember how I used to say Sitka was nothing but fish and fog? Well, I have to take that back. Mostly.

2Last month I spent a week on Grandpa’s boat. I was grumpy for two whole days, and I’m not proud of it. Then a humpback whale came up so close that I could smell its breath. (Trust me, it smells like old fish.) I didn’t even think about my phone.

3Now I go down to the harbor almost every afternoon. Grandpa is teaching me to tie knots and read the tide table, and I’m teaching Arvid. He asks me a thousand questions, and I actually like answering them.

4Seattle sounds amazing, and I still miss you every day. But next summer, when you visit, I’m taking you out on the water. There’s something I want to show you.

Your friend,
Anya

📝 From Change to Theme

🪜

Four Steps to Find a Theme Through Character

  1. Start: name a trait the character has at the beginning, with evidence.
  2. Cause: find what event or person starts the change.
  3. End: name the new trait, with evidence.
  4. Lesson: ask what this change could teach anyone, and write it as a full sentence.
🧪

Worked Example: Inference or Just a Guess?

❌ Weak: Anya likes Seattle better because Seattle has more stores.
The story never mentions stores. With no evidence, this is only a guess.

✅ Strong: Anya misses her friend and feels lonely at first, because the story says her best friend moved away and she spends her afternoons looking at photos of city lights.
A clear inference, backed by details from paragraph 1.

🎯

Theme Through Character

Anya’s change suggests a theme like: “When we really pay attention, we can find wonder in places we thought were boring.” Notice that it is a full sentence about life, not just about Anya or fishing.
