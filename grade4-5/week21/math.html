🦬 Why Rangers Love a Good Grid

Nonfiction Connection
📖

Real Talk: Yellowstone National Park

Yellowstone became the world’s first national park on March 1, 1872. Most of the park is in Wyoming, with smaller parts in Montana and Idaho. It has more than 10,000 hydrothermal features, such as geysers and hot springs, and about 1,000 miles of backcountry hiking trails. The crust around hot springs can be thin and the water can be scalding, so visitors must stay on boardwalks and marked trails. Rangers also ask visitors to stay at least 25 yards from bison and elk and at least 100 yards from bears and wolves. With that much land to cover, knowing exactly where you are matters. A grid turns any spot on a map into a pair of numbers that everyone reads the same way.

📋

🖼️ Picture It: The Coordinate Plane

👁

Two Number Lines That Meet

A coordinate plane is made of two number lines that cross at a right angle. The flat one is the x-axis, and the one that goes straight up is the y-axis. They meet at the origin, the point (0, 0). Every trip on the grid starts at the origin. An ordered pair such as (2, 5) tells you how to get to a point: the first number, the x-coordinate, tells how far to move right; the second number, the y-coordinate, tells how far to move up.

(2, 5) means 2 right, then 5 up
Step 1 — Start at the origin: put your finger on (0, 0).
Step 2 — Move along the x-axis: go 2 units right.
Step 3 — Move up: go 5 units up. You are at the Ranger Station, (2, 5).
Order matters: the Footbridge is at (5, 2): 5 right, then 2 up. Same two numbers, different place!
🧭

Memory Trick: Walk Before You Climb

Walk along the ground (x, right) before you climb the ladder (y, up). A point with an x-coordinate of 0, like (0, 4), sits right on the y-axis. A point with a y-coordinate of 0, like (6, 0), sits right on the x-axis.

📈 Real-World Graphs and Grid Distances

🥾

Ramona’s Hike Chart

Ramona keeps track of her group’s hike. Every hour she writes down how many miles they have walked in all. Ranger Roderick shows her how to turn each line of her chart into an ordered pair: (hours hiked, miles walked).

Hours hiked (x)Miles walked (y)

The point (3, 6) means: after 3 hours, 6 miles
Read a point: the x-coordinate is the hours, and the y-coordinate is the miles. So (3, 6) says the group had walked 6 miles after 3 hours.
Compare two points: from (1, 2) to (4, 8), the miles went from 2 to 8, so the group walked 8 − 2 = 6 miles in those 3 hours.
Distance on a grid line: if two points share the same y-coordinate, like (2, 5) and (7, 5), they are on the same flat line. Subtract the x-coordinates: 7 − 2 = 5 units. If they share the same x-coordinate, subtract the y-coordinates.
Use the scale: if each grid unit stands for 1/2 mile, then 5 units is 5 × 1/2 = 2 1/2 miles.

🔢 Two Rules, Two Patterns

🎖️

Junior Ranger Stamps

Ranger Roderick gives out Junior Ranger stamps. Each day of the program, Quinlan earns 3 stamps and Ramona, who does the extra ranger talks, earns 6 stamps. They both start with 0. Each cousin’s total follows a rule.

Quinlan’s rule: start at 0, add 3. Ramona’s rule: start at 0, add 6.

Pairs: (0, 0), (3, 6), (6, 12), (9, 18), (12, 24)
Step 1 — Generate each pattern: follow each rule. Quinlan: 0, 3, 6, 9, 12. Ramona: 0, 6, 12, 18, 24.
Step 2 — Line up corresponding terms: numbers in the same position go together. The 3rd number of each pattern is 6 and 12.
Step 3 — Make ordered pairs: (Quinlan’s number, Ramona’s number): (0, 0), (3, 6), (6, 12), (9, 18), (12, 24).
Step 4 — Find the relationship: every number in Ramona’s pattern is 2 times the corresponding number in Quinlan’s pattern, because 6 is 2 times 3.
💡

Why 2 Times?

Ramona adds 6 each day and Quinlan adds 3. Since 6 = 2 × 3, Ramona’s total grows twice as fast, so it is always double. Check the corresponding terms: 9 × 2 = 18 and 12 × 2 = 24.

🗺️ Graphing Pattern Pairs

🎖️

Ramona’s Stamp Graph

Ramona plots the stamp pairs from the table on a coordinate plane. Look at the axes: they count by 3s and 6s, so the big numbers fit. Always read the numbers on each axis before you plot.

If Rule B = 2 × Rule A, then 30 pairs with 60
See the line: the points line up in a straight line that starts at the origin. Each step moves 3 right and 6 up.
Predict without listing: Ramona’s number is always 2 times Quinlan’s, so when Quinlan has 30 stamps, Ramona has 30 × 2 = 60 stamps. The point is (30, 60).
Work backward: when Ramona has 42 stamps, Quinlan has 42 ÷ 2 = 21 stamps.
Not every relationship multiplies: Rule A: start at 1, add 2 (1, 3, 5, 7). Rule B: start at 4, add 2 (4, 6, 8, 10). Here each Rule B number is always 3 more, not 3 times.
🧩

Multi-Step Plan

1. Write both patterns as a table. 2. Pair the corresponding terms. 3. Test a relationship on at least two pairs. 4. Use it to predict, then check your answer against the rules.
