🍁 How Sap Becomes Syrup

Nonfiction Connection
📖

Real Talk: Maple Sugaring

Vermont makes more maple syrup than any other state, about half of all the maple syrup made in the United States. In late winter, when nights are below freezing and days are above freezing, sugar maple trees push sweet sap up from their roots. Farmers tap the trees with small metal spouts and collect the sap in buckets or long plastic tubes. Sap is mostly water, so it is boiled in a sugarhouse until most of the water steams away. It takes about 40 liters of sap to make just 1 liter of syrup. The Abenaki and other Native nations of the Northeast were making maple sugar long before European settlers arrived.

🖼️ Picture It: A Decimal Divided by a Whole Number

👁

Equal Shares, Same as Always

Mr. Haddad has 4.8 liters of fresh syrup and 4 empty pots. He wants the same amount in each pot. Dividing a decimal means the same thing as dividing a whole number: split the amount into equal groups.

4.8 ÷ 4 = 1.2
Think place value: 4.8 is 4 ones and 8 tenths. 4 ones ÷ 4 = 1 one, and 8 tenths ÷ 4 = 2 tenths. That makes 1 and 2 tenths, or 1.2.
Long division: Divide as if there were no decimal point, then put the decimal point in the quotient straight above the decimal point in the dividend. 7.56 ÷ 3: 7 ÷ 3 = 2 R1, 15 ÷ 3 = 5, 6 ÷ 3 = 2, so 2.52.
Check by multiplying: 2.52 × 3 = 7.56. It matches, so the answer is right.
⚠️

Two Tricky Cases

Placeholder zero: 0.24 ÷ 6. There are 24 hundredths, and 24 hundredths ÷ 6 = 4 hundredths = 0.04. The 0 in the tenths place holds the spot so the 4 stays in the hundredths place.
Write a zero to keep going: 5.4 ÷ 4 gives 1.3 with 2 tenths left over. Write 5.4 as 5.40 (the same amount) and keep dividing: 20 hundredths ÷ 4 = 5 hundredths. So 5.4 ÷ 4 = 1.35.

📚

Words in the Story

“Mr. Haddad divided 4.8 liters of syrup into 4 pots, so each pot got 1.2 liters.” The dividend is the amount being divided (4.8). The divisor is the number you divide by (4). The quotient is the answer (1.2).

🔟 Dividing by 10, 100, and 1,000

🧪

Caleb’s Tasting Cups

Caleb wants to split 456 milliliters of syrup into 10 tasting cups, then try 100 cups, then 1,000 tiny drops. Each time, the amount in one share gets 10 times smaller. Place value shows exactly what happens.

456 ÷ 10 = 45.6  •  456 ÷ 100 = 4.56  •  456 ÷ 1,000 = 0.456
The rule: Dividing by 10 moves every digit one place to the right, to a place worth 10 times less. Dividing by 100 moves every digit two places, and dividing by 1,000 moves every digit three places. The number of zeros in 10, 100, or 1,000 tells you how many places.
It works for decimals too: 37.2 ÷ 10 = 3.72, and 37.2 ÷ 100 = 0.372.
Fill empty places with zeros: 4.5 ÷ 100 = 0.045. The 4 moves from the ones place to the hundredths place, so a 0 must hold the tenths place. Also, 7 ÷ 1,000 = 0.007.
💡

Find the Pattern

60 ÷ 10 = 6.   6 ÷ 10 = 0.6.   0.6 ÷ 10 = 0.06. Each answer is one tenth of the one before it. Math writers sometimes write 100 as 10² (“ten to the second power,” meaning 10 × 10) and 1,000 as 10³ (“ten to the third power,” meaning 10 × 10 × 10). The small raised number tells you how many places the digits move.

💵 Money and Measurement

🏷️

Grandma Lucille’s Farm Stand

On Saturday, Grandma Lucille sells syrup, maple candy, and maple cream at a stand by the road. Nora helps find the price of one item, and Caleb changes metric measurements so the labels are easy to read. Both jobs use decimal division.

Money: Find the Unit Price

$31.00 ÷ 4 = $7.75
Unit price means the cost of ONE item. A box of 4 syrup bottles costs $31.00, so one bottle costs $31.00 ÷ 4.
Estimate first: $32 ÷ 4 = $8, so the answer should be a little less than $8.
Divide: 31 ÷ 4 = 7 with 3 left over. Write 31 as 31.00 and keep going: 30 tenths ÷ 4 = 7 tenths with 2 tenths left, and 20 hundredths ÷ 4 = 5 hundredths. One bottle costs $7.75. Check: $7.75 × 4 = $31.00.

Measurement: Small Unit to Big Unit

Going from a small unit to a big unit means dividing. Metric units change by 10, 100, or 1,000, so you can use the place value rule.

centimeters (cm) → meters (m)÷ 100: 250 cm = 2.5 m
grams (g) → kilograms (kg)÷ 1,000: 750 g = 0.75 kg
milliliters (mL) → liters (L)÷ 1,000: 1,890 mL = 1.89 L
millimeters (mm) → centimeters (cm)÷ 10: 45 mm = 4.5 cm
🧩

Does It Make Sense?

A kilogram is much bigger than a gram, so the number of kilograms must be much smaller than the number of grams. 750 grams is less than 1 kilogram, so 0.75 kg makes sense, and 7.5 kg or 75 kg do not.

decimal point

🧠 Multi-Step Problems and Checking Your Work

🍯

Bottling Day

Mr. Haddad fills big jugs first, then pours each jug into small bottles. That is two divisions in a row, and the second one is often by 10, 100, or 1,000.

12.6 L ÷ 6 = 2.1 L  →  2.1 L ÷ 10 = 0.21 L
The problem: 12.6 liters of syrup are poured equally into 6 jugs. Each jug is then poured equally into 10 bottles. How much syrup is in each bottle?
Step 1: 12.6 ÷ 6 = 2.1 liters in each jug.
Step 2: 2.1 ÷ 10 = 0.21 liter in each bottle. (Every digit moves one place to the right.)
Step 3 — Check: 0.21 × 10 = 2.1, and 2.1 × 6 = 12.6. It works backward, so it is right.
🚩

Watch Out for These Mistakes

  • Decimal point in the wrong place: 7.56 ÷ 3 is NOT 25.2. Estimate: 7.56 is less than 9, and 9 ÷ 3 = 3, so the answer must be less than 3. It is 2.52.
  • Missing placeholder zero: 0.24 ÷ 6 is NOT 0.4. Check: 0.4 × 6 = 2.4, not 0.24. It is 0.04.
  • Stopping with a remainder: 5.4 ÷ 4 is NOT “1.3 remainder 2.” Write a zero and keep going: 1.35.
  • Wrong number of places: dividing by 100 moves the digits TWO places, not one.
🧩

Multi-Step Plan

1. Read the whole problem and find what the question asks for. 2. Estimate so you know about how big the answer should be. 3. Divide one step at a time, lining up the decimal point. 4. Check by multiplying backward, and make sure the unit (liters, meters, dollars) is the one the question asks for.
