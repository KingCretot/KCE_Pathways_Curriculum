🖼️ Picture It: The Rules of Dialogue

Dialogue is what characters say out loud. Readers need to know exactly which words are spoken and who is speaking, so writers follow a few rules. Look at where every mark goes.

RuleExample
1. Put quotation marks around the speaker's exact words only."Grab your boots," Grandpa said.
2. Capitalize the first word inside the quotation marks.Nadia said, "We're right here."
3. Tag first: put a comma after the tag, before the opening quotation mark.Nadia said, "Let's go."
4. Tag last: end the quote with a comma (not a period) INSIDE the closing quotation mark. The tag finishes the sentence, so she or he stays lowercase."Let's go," she said.
5. Question marks and exclamation points stay inside the quotation marks and take the place of the comma."Tonight?" Nadia asked.
6. Start a new paragraph every time a new character speaks."Is it breathing?" Nadia asked.
"She's breathing!" Grandpa called.
7. Names get capitals, including Grandpa when it is used as a name."It's a girl," Grandpa said.
⚠️

Strong Tags: Use Them Sparingly

Tags like whispered, called, and muttered tell HOW words were said. One or two in a scene add feeling. Too many get in the way, so let plain said and asked do most of the work.

🌟 Vocabulary Round-Up

Flip each card to review today's words.

dialogue
The words characters say to each other in a story.
quotation marks
Marks (“ ”) that go around a speaker's exact words.
dialogue tag
The words that tell who is speaking, like “Nadia said” or “Grandpa asked.”
speaker
The character who is talking. A new speaker means a new paragraph.
narrative
A story that tells about events, usually with characters, a setting and a problem.
strong tag
A vivid tag verb like whispered, called or muttered. Use it sparingly.

💡 Dialogue Does Two Jobs

🎭

Job 1: Dialogue Shows Character

What characters say, and HOW they say it, shows what they're like. Grandpa jokes, “Calves don't read calendars,” so we know he's calm. Nadia whispers, “What if I do something wrong?” so we know she's scared.

🚜

Job 2: Dialogue Moves the Plot

Some lines push the story forward. When Grandpa says, “I think her calf is coming tonight,” the whole adventure begins.

🔗
