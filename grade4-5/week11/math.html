🖼️ Picture It: Factor Pairs of 36

👁

Factors Are the Numbers You Multiply

Nadia needs to put 36 calves into equal rows of pens. Every way she can do it is a multiplication fact that equals 36. The two numbers in each fact are a factor pair.

2 rows of 18
3 rows of 12
4 rows of 9
6 rows of 6
Factor pairs of 36: 1 × 36   2 × 18   3 × 12   4 × 9   6 × 6
Step 1 — Start at 1: 1 × 36. Every number has 1 and itself as factors.
Step 2 — Test 2, 3, 4, 5, 6… If 36 divides evenly, write the pair. (5 does not work: 36 ÷ 5 has a remainder.)
Step 3 — Stop when the pairs meet: at 6 × 6 the numbers start repeating, so you have them all.
All the factors of 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36 — that’s nine factors.

🔢 Prime, Composite, and Divisibility Rules

🏷️

Nadia’s Cow ID Tags

Every cow on the farm wears an ear tag with an ID number. Nadia notices that some tag numbers, like 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, and 13, can only be made one way: 1 times the number. Those are prime numbers.

Prime numberexactly 2 factors: 1 and itself (7, 13, 29)
Composite numbermore than 2 factors (12, 36, 51)
The number 1only 1 factor — neither prime nor composite
The number 2the only even prime

Divisibility Rules: Shortcuts for Finding Factors

Divisible by 2ends in 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8
Divisible by 3digits add to a multiple of 3
Divisible by 5ends in 0 or 5
Divisible by 9digits add to a multiple of 9
Divisible by 10ends in 0
🧩

Watch Out!

Is cow #51 prime? It is odd and does not end in 5, so it looks prime. But 5 + 1 = 6, and 6 is divisible by 3, so 51 is divisible by 3: 51 = 3 × 17. That makes 51 composite. Always test before you decide!

🌟 Vocabulary Round-Up

Flip each card to review today's words.

factor
A number you multiply to get a product. 4 and 9 are factors of 36.
factor pair
Two factors that multiply to a number, like 4 × 9 = 36.
multiple
The product of a number and a counting number. 4, 8, 12, 16 are multiples of 4.
prime number
A whole number with exactly two factors: 1 and itself. Example: 13.
composite number
A whole number with more than two factors. Example: 12.
divisible
Divides evenly with no remainder. 36 is divisible by 4.
greatest common factor (GCF)
The biggest factor two numbers share. The GCF of 18 and 24 is 6.
least common multiple (LCM)
The smallest multiple two numbers share. The LCM of 4 and 6 is 12.

LCM of 4 and 6 = 12
Common multiples are days on both lists: 12, 24, 36…
The least common multiple (LCM) is the smallest one: 12. So the truck and the vet first come on the same day on Day 12, then every 12 days after that.
GCF of 18 and 24 = 6
Packing baskets: Nadia has 18 jars of yogurt and 24 blocks of cheese for the farm stand. Every basket must match, with nothing left over, so the number of baskets has to divide BOTH numbers. It must be a common factor.
Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18  •  Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24
The greatest common factor (GCF) is 6, so she can fill 6 baskets, each with 3 jars of yogurt and 4 blocks of cheese.
🧩

Which One Do I Need?

“When will they happen together?” → multiples → LCM.   “Split into equal groups with nothing left over?” → factors → GCF.
