🏠 Why Tiny-House Builders Use Fractions

Nonfiction Connection
📖

Real Talk: Tiny Houses

Austin is the capital of Texas, and the Colorado River of Texas flows right through the city. Many builders across the United States follow a set of building rules called the International Residential Code. Its tiny-house section describes a tiny house as a home of 400 square feet or less, not counting lofts. A square foot is the area of a square that is 1 foot on each side. With so little space, builders plan every shelf, window, and step carefully, and their measurements are often fractions of a foot, such as 1/2 foot or 2 3/4 feet.

🖼️ Picture It: Multiplying as Scaling

👁

Bigger, Smaller, or the Same?

Ms. Linnea is resizing a 12-foot wall in her plans. Scaling means resizing a number by multiplying it by a factor. A factor greater than 1 makes the product greater than the number you started with. A factor less than 1 makes it smaller, and a factor equal to 1 keeps it the same.

3/4 × 12 < 12 < 5/4 × 12
Less than 1: 3/4 × 12 takes only 3/4 of the wall: 9 feet.
Equal to 1: 1 × 12, or 4/4 × 12, is still 12 feet.
Greater than 1: 5/4 × 12 is the whole wall plus 1/4 more: 12 + 3 = 15 feet.
Predict first: compare the factor with 1 before you multiply. You can tell which way the size changes without computing at all.

🔍 How Much Bigger or Smaller?

🪚

Mr. Lowen’s Shortcut

Mr. Lowen does not just ask whether a board gets longer or shorter. He asks by how much. A mixed number like 1 1/4 is a whole number and a fraction together. So 1 1/4 × 12 means one whole 12 plus 1/4 of 12 more.

1 1/4 × 12 = 12 + 315
3/4 × 12 = 12 − 39
1 1/2 × 10 = 10 + 515
2/3 × 4/48/12, the same as 2/3
1 1/4 × 12 = 12 + (1/4 × 12) = 15
More than 1: the extra 1/4 adds 1/4 of 12, which is 3. So the product is 3 more than 12.
Less than 1: 3/4 is 1/4 less than 1, so 3/4 × 12 is 1/4 of 12 less than 12: 12 − 3 = 9.
Equal to 1: Multiplying by a fraction equal to 1, such as 4/4, gives an equivalent fraction. 2/3 × 4/4 = 8/12, and 8/12 is the same amount as 2/3.
Fractions times fractions: 2/5 × 7/8 is less than 7/8, because you take only 2/5 of 7/8.

🟨 Area by Tiling: Fractional Side Lengths

📐

Ms. Linnea’s Shelf

A shelf top is 3/4 foot long and 2/3 foot wide. To find its area, start with a unit square. A unit square is a square with sides 1 unit long, and its area is 1 square unit. Cut 1 square foot into 4 columns and 3 rows. Each small tile is 1/3 foot by 1/4 foot, so 12 tiles cover the unit square and each tile is 1/12 square foot.

2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12 = 1/2 square foot
Count the tiles: the shelf covers 3 columns and 2 rows, so 3 × 2 = 6 tiles.
Name the tiles: each tile is 1/12 square foot, so the shelf is 6/12 = 1/2 square foot.
Same as multiplying: The area of a rectangle is its length times its width, even when the side lengths are fractions. 2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12.
Why the denominators multiply: 3 rows × 4 columns = 12 tiles in the whole square. Why the numerators multiply: 2 rows × 3 columns = 6 tiles in the shelf.

🧱 Mixed-Number Side Lengths

🪵

Uncle Lisandro’s Bench Top

The bench top is 2 1/2 feet long and 1 1/3 feet wide. Liora splits each side into its whole-number part and its fraction part, then finds the area of each of the four small rectangles.

2 1/2 × 1 1/3 = 3 1/3 square feet
Way 1, partial products: 2 × 1 = 2, 1/2 × 1 = 1/2, 2 × 1/3 = 2/3, and 1/2 × 1/3 = 1/6. Add with sixths: 2 + 3/6 + 4/6 + 1/6 = 2 + 8/6 = 3 2/6 = 3 1/3.
Way 2, improper fractions: 2 1/2 = 5/2 and 1 1/3 = 4/3. Then 5/2 × 4/3 = 20/6 = 3 1/3.
Watch out: 2 × 1 and 1/2 × 1/3 are only two of the four parts. 2 + 1/6 leaves out the middle rectangles.
Scaling check: 1 1/3 is greater than 1, so the area must be greater than 2 1/2. It is.
