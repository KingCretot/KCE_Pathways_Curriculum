🖼️ Picture It: Putting Sources Together

Researchers almost never find a whole answer in one place. They start with a research question, read several sources, and take short notes in their own words. Each note says which source it came from. Then they synthesize: they combine facts from different sources into new sentences, and they cite (name) each source so readers can check it.

Model Research QuestionWhy is the library garden full of bees this summer?

A made-up example

Note in my own wordsSource
Garden club planted 40 lavender plants in May.Garden club newsletter
Bees visit lavender flowers all summer.Interview with the librarian
Library opens at 9 o’clock.Library website (not relevant: cross it out!)

Synthesis sentence: The garden is busy with bees because the garden club planted 40 lavender plants in May (garden club newsletter), and, according to the librarian, bees visit lavender all summer long.

💡

Copy, Paraphrase, or Synthesize?

Copy: using a source’s exact words, which must go inside quotation marks. Paraphrase: retelling ONE source’s idea in your own words. Synthesize: combining ideas from TWO OR MORE sources into one new statement, naming each source.

cite

📖 Source A: The Article

Naveen reads first. His job: find facts that explain WHERE the monarchs go and WHY. Notice the paragraph numbers; you will use them.

Source A • Informational ArticleWinter in the Fir Forests

Written for this lesson, using facts from UNESCO, the United States Fish and Wildlife Service, Journey North, and encyclopedia articles

1Every fall, millions of monarch butterflies that hatched east of the Rocky Mountains, in the United States and Canada, begin a journey of up to about 3,000 miles. They are heading for a few high mountain forests in central Mexico, in the Mexican states of Michoacán and the State of Mexico.

2Many of the butterflies arrive in early November, around Día de Muertos (the Day of the Dead), which families in Mexico celebrate on November 1 and 2. To many people, the returning monarchs are a symbol of the souls of loved ones coming back to visit.

3The butterflies overwinter, from about October to March, in forests of oyamel fir trees. These forests give them a cool, steady climate for the winter. The monarchs pack together in close clusters on the trunks and branches, so many that some branches bend under their weight. Scientists think clustering helps the butterflies hold in heat.

4In 2008, the Monarch Butterfly Biosphere Reserve, about 60 miles northwest of Mexico City, was named a World Heritage Site by UNESCO, the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization. That honor marks it as a place that matters to the whole world.

5People who lived in these mountains knew about the butterflies long before scientists from other countries did. On January 2, 1975, Catalina Aguado and Kenneth Brugger, who were helping the Canadian scientists Fred and Norah Urquhart, hiked up a mountain called Cerro Pelón and found a colony. Their discovery told the wider world of science where the eastern monarchs spend the winter.

📋 Source B: The Fact Sheet

Nayeli reads next. Source B does not talk about Mexico’s forests much. Instead, it explains what makes the migrating monarchs special. As you read, ask: which part of the research question does this source help answer?

Source B • Nature Center Fact SheetThe Super Generation

Written for this lesson, using facts from World Wildlife Fund and the Forest Preserve District of Will County, Illinois

1Most adult monarch butterflies live only about a month. They begin laying eggs when they are just a few days old.

2The monarchs that hatch in late summer, in August or September, are different. Shorter days and cooler temperatures put them into diapause, a kind of pause in which they do not mate or lay eggs. Instead, they save their energy for the long flight south.

3These butterflies are often called the super generation. They live about eight times as long as summer monarchs, around eight months. They can fly 50 to 100 miles a day, often gliding on rising currents of warm air. Scientists think they find their way by using the position of the sun, and possibly Earth’s magnetic field.

4In March, the super generation leaves Mexico and flies north. Females lay their eggs only on milkweed, the one plant monarch caterpillars depend on. It takes three to five generations of monarchs to finish the trip back to the northern United States and Canada.

📝 Writing a Synthesis Sentence

🪜

Four Steps to Synthesize

  1. Sort: group your note cards by the part of the question they answer.
  2. Connect: find facts from different sources that fit together (a cause and its effect, or a problem and its numbers).
  3. Combine: join them with linking words such as because, and, so, while, also, or however.
  4. Cite: name every source: “According to Source A, …” or “(Source B)”.
Part of the questionBest notesFrom
Why do they go?Late-summer monarchs pause breeding and save energy for a long flightSource B
Where do they go?Cool, steady oyamel fir forests in Michoacán and the State of MexicoSource A
How are they doing?7.24 acres in 2025 to 2026, up from 2.22, but far below about 50 acres; threats remainSource C
⚖️

When Sources Disagree

Source B says the super generation lives “around eight months.” A United States Fish and Wildlife Service article says these monarchs “can survive upwards of nine months.” Both are reliable. A careful researcher does not pick one and hide the other. She reports a range and cites both: The super generation lives about eight to nine months (Source B; United States Fish and Wildlife Service).
