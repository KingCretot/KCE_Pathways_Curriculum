Nonfiction Connection

Real Talk: Farming on a Mountainside

The Andes are the longest mountain range on land in the world, and they run through Peru. Long ago, the Inca and the peoples before them built stone terraces, called andenes, that turn steep slopes into flat steps of farmland. Many are still farmed today. Peru grows thousands of kinds of native potatoes, in colors from yellow to deep purple. Quinoa, a seed that is cooked and eaten like a grain, grows well in the cold, high fields. Families raise alpacas for their soft wool, and weavers dye the yarn with plants and with tiny cochineal insects that make a bright red. On market day, people bring potatoes, quinoa, and weavings to sell.

🖼️ Picture It: A Whole Number × a Fraction

👁

Groups of a Fraction

Killa’s family is cooking quinoa soup for market day. Each pot of soup needs 2/3 cup of quinoa, and Killa is making 4 pots. That is 4 groups of 2/3, so we multiply: 4 × 2/3.

4 × 2/3 = 8/3 = 2 2/3 cups
Step 1 — Multiply the whole number by the numerator: 4 × 2 = 8.
Step 2 — Keep the denominator: the pieces are still thirds, so the product is 8/3 (eight thirds).
Step 3 — Change to a mixed number: 8 ÷ 3 = 2 with a remainder of 2, so 8/3 = 2 2/3 cups of quinoa.
🧩

Check It With Addition

4 × 2/3 is the same as 2/3 + 2/3 + 2/3 + 2/3 = 8/3. When you multiply a whole number by a fraction, the denominator stays the same, because the size of each piece does not change.

🌱 A Fraction × a Fraction: The Area Model

🥔

Purple Potatoes on the Terrace

On one terrace, Killa’s family plants potatoes on 2/5 of the field. Of the potato part, 3/4 is planted with purple potatoes. What fraction of the WHOLE terrace grows purple potatoes? We need 3/4 of 2/5, and “of” means times: 3/4 × 2/5.

Blue columns: 2/5 of the field is potatoesRed rows: 3/4 (the purple share)Gold: shaded both ways
3/4 × 2/5 = 6/20 = 3/10
Step 1 — Columns: split the rectangle into 5 equal columns and shade 2 of them. That is 2/5.
Step 2 — Rows: split it into 4 equal rows and shade 3 of them. That is 3/4.
Step 3 — Count the overlap: 6 of the 20 small parts are gold, so the product is 6/20.
The shortcut: multiply the numerators (3 × 2 = 6) and multiply the denominators (4 × 5 = 20).
Simplify: 6/20 = 3/10. So 3/10 of the whole terrace grows purple potatoes.
🧩

Does It Make Sense?

When you multiply two fractions that are both less than 1, the product is smaller than both of them. Taking part of a part always gives a smaller piece.

✂️ Simplifying Products

👁

Finish the Job

A product is not finished until it is in simplest form. Use the greatest common factor (GCF), just like you did last week, so you only have to divide once.

3/8 × 4/9 = 12/72 = 1/6
Step 1 — Multiply: 3 × 4 = 12 and 8 × 9 = 72, so the product is 12/72.
Step 2 — Find the GCF: the greatest common factor of 12 and 72 is 12.
Step 3 — Divide both by the GCF: 12 ÷ 12 = 1 and 72 ÷ 12 = 6. The product is 1/6.
4 × 5/6 = 20/6 = 10/3 = 3 1/3
Step 1 — Multiply: 4 × 5 = 20, so the product is 20/6.
Step 2 — Simplify: the GCF of 20 and 6 is 2, so 20/6 = 10/3.
Step 3 — Write it as a mixed number: 10 ÷ 3 = 3 with a remainder of 1, so 10/3 = 3 1/3.
🧩

Shortcut: Simplify Before You Multiply

In 3/8 × 4/9, the 3 and the 9 share the factor 3, and the 4 and the 8 share the factor 4. Divide them first: 1/2 × 1/3 = 1/6. Same answer, smaller numbers!

⚠️

Watch Out!

If you divide 12/72 by 2, you get 6/36, which is NOT finished. 6 and 36 still share the factor 6. Dividing by the GREATEST common factor gets you to simplest form in one step.

🌟 Vocabulary Round-Up

Flip each card to review today's words.

numerator
The top number of a fraction. It tells how many parts we have. In 2/3, it is 2.
denominator
The bottom number of a fraction. It tells how many equal parts make a whole. In 2/3, it is 3.
product
The answer to a multiplication problem. In 4 × 2/3 = 8/3, the product is 8/3.
area model
A rectangle split into rows and columns. The overlap shows the product of two fractions.
simplest form
A fraction whose numerator and denominator share no factor except 1, like 3/10.
improper fraction
A fraction whose numerator is greater than or equal to its denominator, like 8/3.
mixed number
A whole number and a fraction together, like 2 2/3.
greatest common factor (GCF)
The biggest factor two numbers share. Divide by it to simplify in one step.

🧺 Mixed Numbers at the Market

🥔

Weighing the Potato Bags

On Sunday, Killa’s family takes potatoes to the market in Pisac. Each bag weighs 2 1/2 kilograms, and Killa carries 3 bags. How many kilograms is that? We need 3 × 2 1/2.

Way 1: 3 × 2 1/2 = 3 × 5/2 = 15/2 = 7 1/2
Change the mixed number to an improper fraction: 2 1/2 = 5/2 (2 × 2 + 1 = 5).
Multiply: 3 × 5/2 = 15/2.
Change back: 15 ÷ 2 = 7 with a remainder of 1, so 15/2 = 7 1/2 kilograms.
Way 2: (3 × 2) + (3 × 1/2) = 6 + 1 1/2 = 7 1/2
Break the mixed number apart: multiply the whole number part AND the fraction part, then add. Both ways give 7 1/2 kilograms.
A fraction of a mixed number: 2/3 × 2 1/4 = 2/3 × 9/4 = 18/12 = 1 1/2
The situation: a cook buys 2 1/4 kilograms of quinoa and uses 2/3 of it for soup.
Change, multiply, simplify: 2 1/4 = 9/4. Then 2/3 × 9/4 = 18/12. The GCF of 18 and 12 is 6, so 18/12 = 3/2 = 1 1/2 kilograms.
⚠️

Watch Out!

Don’t multiply only the whole number part. 3 × 2 1/2 is NOT 6 1/2. The 1/2 has to be multiplied by 3 too.
