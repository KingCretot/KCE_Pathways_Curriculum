💡 A factor divides a number evenly. A prime number has exactly two factors; a composite number has more. Use the LCM for “when will they happen together?” and the GCF for “the greatest number of equal groups.”

💡 Quotation marks go around the exact spoken words. Commas and end marks stay inside the closing quotation mark, and a new speaker means a new paragraph.

Week 13 • Reading

🌄 Figurative Language & Poetry

💡 Simile: like or as. Metaphor: one thing IS another. Personification: human actions for a thing. Hyperbole: a huge exaggeration. A stanza is a group of lines.

Alpaca Morning

The sun climbs over the mountain wall,
a golden coin above us all.
The alpacas wake and stretch and yawn,
their wool as soft as clouds at dawn.

The wind runs laughing down the slope
and tugs at Killa’s braided rope.
She’d climb a thousand hills today
to lead her herd along the way.

Week 14 • Reading

🔎 Quoting vs. Paraphrasing

💡 A quote copies exact words inside quotation marks. A paraphrase uses your own words. Both name the source. Copied words with no quotation marks are plagiarism.

From “The Busy Bay,” a magazine article by Lena Torres

Every morning, giant cranes lift steel containers off the ships as if they were toy blocks. The crane operators sit in small cabs high above the dock.
