⚓ Why a Harbor Runs on Measurement

Nonfiction Connection
📖

Real Talk: The Port of Duluth-Superior

Duluth, Minnesota, and its neighbor Superior, Wisconsin, share a harbor at the western tip of Lake Superior, the largest of the Great Lakes. The Port of Duluth-Superior moves more cargo, by weight, than any other port on the Great Lakes, and it is the farthest-inland freshwater seaport in North America, about 2,342 miles from the Atlantic Ocean. Ships carry iron ore, grain, coal, limestone, salt, cement, and even giant wind turbine parts. Many ships enter through the Duluth Ship Canal and pass under the Aerial Lift Bridge. The bridge was built in 1905 and was changed into a lift bridge in 1929 and 1930. When its span is raised, ships can pass under it with 135 feet of clearance, and in the busy shipping season it can lift about 25 times a day. Crews measure everything: rope in feet and yards, cargo in pounds and tons or kilograms, and fuel and water in gallons or liters.

🖼️ Picture It: Yards, Feet, and Inches

👁

Bigger Units, Smaller Units

In the customary system, 1 foot = 12 inches and 1 yard = 3 feet = 36 inches. To change a bigger unit into a smaller unit, multiply: there will be more of the smaller units. To change a smaller unit into a bigger unit, divide: there will be fewer of the bigger units.

4 2/3 yards = 14 feet  •  100 inches = 8 feet 4 inches
Fractions of a unit: 4 2/3 yards = 4 × 3 + 2/3 × 3 = 12 + 2 = 14 feet. And 3/4 yard = 3/4 of 36 inches = 27 inches.
Mixed numbers to a smaller unit: 2 1/2 feet = 2 × 12 + 1/2 × 12 = 24 + 6 = 30 inches.
Smaller to bigger, with a leftover: 100 inches ÷ 12 = 8 R4, so 100 inches = 8 feet 4 inches. A measurement that uses two units at once, like 8 feet 4 inches, is a mixed measurement.
Check: 8 × 12 + 4 = 96 + 4 = 100 inches.

⚖️ Weight and Capacity in the Galley

🍲

Captain Teodoro’s Galley

The tugboat’s galley is tiny, so the cook plans every pot and jar. Ounces and pounds measure weight, while grams and kilograms measure mass. Cups, pints, quarts, and gallons measure capacity, the amount a container can hold.

Weight1 pound = 16 ounces
Capacity1 gallon = 4 quarts • 1 quart = 2 pints • 1 pint = 2 cups
Shortcut1 gallon = 4 quarts = 8 pints = 16 cups

3 gallons → 12 quarts → 24 pints → 48 cups
Chain the steps: 3 gallons × 4 = 12 quarts, 12 quarts × 2 = 24 pints, 24 pints × 2 = 48 cups. Or use the shortcut: 3 × 16 = 48 cups.
Fractions: 1/4 pound = 1/4 of 16 = 4 ounces. 2 1/2 gallons = 2 × 4 + 1/2 × 4 = 10 quarts.
Smaller to bigger: 56 ounces ÷ 16 = 3 R8, so 56 ounces = 3 pounds 8 ounces. Because 8 ounces is half of 16, that is also 3 1/2 pounds.

🌍 Metric Units and the Decimal Point

📦

Crates from Overseas

Some of the cargo that crosses the harbor is labeled in metric units. In the metric system, every unit is 10, 100, or 1,000 times another, so converting is just multiplying or dividing by a power of 10. The prefix kilo- means 1,000, the prefix centi- means one hundredth, and the prefix milli- means one thousandth.

Length1 km = 1,000 m • 1 m = 100 cm = 1,000 mm • 1 cm = 10 mm
Mass1 kg = 1,000 g
Capacity1 L = 1,000 mL

Abbreviations: km = kilometer, m = meter, cm = centimeter, mm = millimeter, kg = kilogram, g = gram, L = liter, mL = milliliter.

2.4 km = 2,400 m  •  375 cm = 3.75 m
Bigger to smaller, multiply: 2.4 kilometers × 1,000 = 2,400 meters. The decimal point moves 3 places to the right. 0.6 kilogram × 1,000 = 600 grams.
Smaller to bigger, divide: 375 centimeters ÷ 100 = 3.75 meters. The decimal point moves 2 places to the left. 2,750 milliliters ÷ 1,000 = 2.75 liters.
Millimeters: 82 millimeters ÷ 10 = 8.2 centimeters.
Does it make sense? Smaller units mean a bigger number. 2,400 meters is a bigger number than 2.4 kilometers, and that is correct.

🚢 Comparing Units and Multistep Problems

🚢

Uncle Torvald’s Tow Lines

A big lake freighter is coming through the canal, and Uncle Torvald gets the tugboat’s lines ready. Some measurements are in yards and some in feet, so he always changes them to the same unit before he adds, subtracts, or compares.

Same unit first, then solve
Example 1 — Compare: which is heavier, 2 1/2 pounds or 42 ounces? Change pounds to ounces: 2 1/2 × 16 = 40 ounces. 42 ounces > 40 ounces, so 42 ounces is heavier, by 2 ounces.
Example 2 — Cut and subtract: Uncle Torvald has a 50-yard line. He cuts 8 pieces that are each 15 feet long. The line is 50 × 3 = 150 feet. The pieces use 8 × 15 = 120 feet. He has 150 − 120 = 30 feet left, which is 10 yards.
Example 3 — Metric: a crate has a mass of 1.2 kilograms and a lid of 450 grams. Together: 1,200 + 450 = 1,650 grams, or 1.65 kilograms.
🧩

Multistep Plan

1. Read the whole problem and circle the unit the answer needs. 2. Change every measurement to the same unit. 3. Do one step at a time. 4. Label the answer and ask: does a smaller unit give a bigger number?
