🖼️ Picture It: Were You There?

An account is a telling of an event. A firsthand account comes from someone who was there and saw or did it. A secondhand account comes from someone who was NOT there; that writer learned about the event from other sources, such as interviews, recordings, photographs, and records. Both kinds are useful. They just show the event from different distances.

Firsthand accountSecondhand account
Who tells itSomeone who was thereSomeone who was not there, using other sources
CluesI, we, my, ourhe, she, they, the crew
Best atFeelings, senses, and close-up detailsThe big picture: dates, numbers, background, and what happened next
LimitsShows only what one person saw in one placeLeaves out the personal feelings; depends on its sources
ExamplesDiary, letter, interview, autobiography, a transcript of what someone saidEncyclopedia, textbook, biography, a news article written later
💡

Firsthand About WHAT?

A person can be a firsthand witness to one part of an event and not another. Michael Collins flew on Apollo 11, but he stayed in orbit around the Moon while his two crewmates walked on it. He could give a firsthand account of orbiting the Moon, but not of walking on it. Always ask: was this person there for the part they are describing?

🌟 Vocabulary Round-Up

firsthand account
A telling of an event by someone who was there.
secondhand account
A telling of an event by someone who was not there, based on other sources.
perspective
A person’s point of view: where they stood and how they saw the event.
transcript
A written copy of words that people spoke.
adhere
To stick to something. “It does adhere in fine layers, like powdered charcoal, to the sole and sides of my boots.”
desolation
A bare, empty place with no life in it. “Magnificent desolation.”

📖 Text A: In Their Own Words

On July 20, 1969, the astronauts talked by radio with Mission Control in Houston, Texas. NASA recorded every word and wrote it down. These are short excerpts, exactly as NASA’s transcript gives them. Notice the excerpt numbers; you will use them.

Text A • Firsthand AccountOn the Moon, July 20, 1969

Source: NASA, Apollo 11 Lunar Surface Journal (corrected transcript of the radio messages)

1Neil Armstrong, just after landing the lunar module Eagle: “Houston, Tranquility Base here. The Eagle has landed.”

2Armstrong, stepping onto the Moon: “That’s one small step for (a) man; one giant leap for mankind.” (NASA puts the “a” in parentheses. Armstrong later said he meant to say “a man,” but the “a” is hard to hear in the recording.)

3Armstrong, moments later: “Yes, the surface is fine and powdery. I can kick it up loosely with my toe. It does adhere in fine layers, like powdered charcoal, to the sole and sides of my boots. I only go in a small fraction of an inch, maybe an eighth of an inch, but I can see the footprints of my boots and the treads in the fine, sandy particles.”

4Armstrong, after walking a few steps: “It’s absolutely no trouble to walk around.”

5Armstrong, looking around: “It has a stark beauty all its own. It’s like much of the high desert of the United States. It’s different, but it’s very pretty out here.”

6As Buzz Aldrin climbed down to join him:
Aldrin: “Beautiful view!”
Armstrong: “Isn’t that something! Magnificent sight out here.”
Aldrin: “Magnificent desolation.”

Text B • Secondhand AccountApollo 11

An original encyclopedia-style article written for this lesson, using facts from NASA

📝 How to Compare Two Accounts

🪜

Four Questions to Ask

  1. Who told it, and were they there? For which part of the event?
  2. What is the focus? One close-up moment, or the whole event?
  3. What does only ONE account give you? Feelings and senses? Dates and numbers?
  4. What can each account NOT tell you? Every account leaves something out.
🧪

Worked Example: Writing the Comparison

Frame: Both accounts tell about ___. The firsthand account focuses on ___, while the secondhand account focuses on ___.

❌ Weak: Text A is better because astronauts are cool. Text B is boring.
These are opinions. They do not say what each account focuses on or what details it gives.

✅ Strong: Both accounts tell about the first Moon landing. The firsthand account focuses on how the dust and the view seemed to the astronauts, while the secondhand account focuses on the dates, the crew, and what happened from launch to return.
It names the shared topic, the focus of each account, and the kind of details each gives.

🎯

Quote a Firsthand Source Exactly

When you quote someone, copy every word and mark exactly as the source gives it, and name who said it. Changing even one word, such as “That’s” to “That was,” turns a quotation into a misquotation.
