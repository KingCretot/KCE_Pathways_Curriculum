🥕 Why Every Vendor Uses Decimals

Nonfiction Connection

Real Talk: Selling by Weight

Farmers markets, delis, and grocery stores nearly all price food by the pound. Since real produce almost never weighs exactly 1 or 2 pounds, the scale shows numbers like 2.4 or 3.75 — and the price has to be figured out on the spot.

🔢 Multiplying Decimals

👁

Remember Week 6?

You already know the standard algorithm. Multiplying decimals uses the EXACT same steps — you just ignore the decimal points while you multiply, then put the decimal point back at the end.

2.4 × 3 = 7.2
Step 1 — Ignore the decimal: Multiply 24 × 3 = 72.
Step 2 — Count decimal places: 2.4 has 1 digit after the decimal point. 3 has 0. Total = 1.
Step 3 — Put it back: Move 1 place from the right in 72 → 7.2.
🧩

Does It Make Sense?

Always check with estimation: 2.4 is close to 2, and 2×3=6. Our answer 7.2 is near 6 — that's reasonable. If you'd gotten 72 or 0.72, estimation would catch the mistake instantly.

🌟 Vocabulary Round-Up

Flip each card to review today's words.

decimal
A number that uses a decimal point to show parts of a whole, like 2.4.
decimal place
A digit's position after the decimal point — tenths, hundredths, and so on.
tenths
The FIRST place after the decimal point. In 2.4, the 4 is in the tenths place.
hundredths
The SECOND place after the decimal point. In 1.25, the 5 is in the hundredths place.
equivalent
Naming the same amount two ways — 1/4 and 0.25 are equivalent. (A Week 8 callback!)
estimate
A smart, quick guess made by rounding first — in Weeks 1, 4, 6, 7, 8, and 9!

🔄 Fractions and Decimals Are the Same Thing

👁

Remember Week 8?

You worked with fractions like 1/4 and 1/2. Those exact same amounts can be written as decimals — and at a market stand, the scale gives you decimals even when a customer asks for "half a pound."

1/4 = 0.25
Why: A fraction is really a division problem. 1/4 means 1 ÷ 4 = 0.25.
The ones worth memorizing: 1/2 = 0.5  •  1/4 = 0.25  •  3/4 = 0.75  •  1/10 = 0.1  •  1/5 = 0.2
🧩

At the Stand

A customer asks for "three quarters of a pound" of green beans. Elena reads that as 0.75 lb, then multiplies: 0.75 × $3.20. Same amount, easier math.
