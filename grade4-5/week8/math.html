🌱 Why Gardens Need Fraction Math

Nonfiction Connection
📖

Real Talk: Community Gardens

Real community gardens are planned in advance so every plant gets enough room and light. Planners divide space using fractions all the time — and different plants rarely need the same-size fraction.

🌱

Sophia's Plan

Tomatoes need 1/3 of the plot. Peppers need 1/4. Those denominators don't match — so Sophia can't just add the tops together yet. By the end of today, you'll know exactly how to handle that.

🔢 Finding a Common Denominator

👁

Remember Week 3?

You already know how to add fractions when the denominators MATCH. When they don't match, you first rename each fraction so they share the same denominator — without changing their actual value.

1/3 + 1/4
🧩

Picture It: Step by Step

Find a number both 3 and 4 divide into evenly — 12 works. 1/3 becomes 4/12 (multiply top and bottom by 4). 1/4 becomes 3/12 (multiply top and bottom by 3). Now add: 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12.

🌱 Comparing Unlike Fractions

👁

Think About This

The common-denominator trick isn't just for adding — it also lets you COMPARE fractions that look totally different at first glance, like 2/5 and 3/8.

2/5 vs 3/8
🧩

Picture It

Common denominator of 5 and 8 is 40. 2/5=16/40. 3/8=15/40. Since 16/40 > 15/40, we know 2/5 > 3/8 — even though 3 and 8 look like "bigger" numbers than 2 and 5!
