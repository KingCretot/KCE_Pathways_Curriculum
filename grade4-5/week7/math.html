🥫 Why Charities Divide Carefully

Nonfiction Connection

Real Talk: Food Banks

Real food banks and pantries track exact numbers so every family and every partner site gets a fair share — no guessing, no favorites. That takes division, often with big, uneven numbers.

🥫

Deja's Total

Deja's class collected 552 cans this month. There are 24 pantries in the area that need a share. 552 ÷ 24 is bigger than anything Week 2's single-digit division could handle alone — today you'll learn the tool that can.

🔢 Long Division with 2-Digit Divisors

👁

Remember Week 2?

You already know division means splitting into equal groups. Long division just gives you a step-by-step way to do that with bigger numbers: Divide, Multiply, Subtract, Bring down — then repeat.

96 ÷ 24 = 4
Step 1 — Divide: How many times does 24 fit into 96? Try 4: 24×4=96.
Step 2 — Multiply: 24×4=96.
Step 3 — Subtract: 96−96=0.
Step 4 — Remainder: 0 left over, so the answer is exactly 4.
🧩

When It Doesn't Divide Evenly

Sometimes there's a remainder — cans, people, or objects left over that can't be split further. That's real life too: 100 ÷ 24 = 4, remainder 4 (4 pantries would need one extra can each, or those 4 go in a "next month" bin).

🥫 Division with a Remainder

👁

Think About This

In real life, a remainder isn't a mistake — it's information. If Deja has cans left over after dividing evenly, she has to decide what to do with them: hold them for next month, or give one extra to a few sites.

100 ÷ 24 = 4 R4
24×4=96. 100−96=4 left over. The quotient is 4, remainder 4.
