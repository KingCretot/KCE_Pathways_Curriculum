Word roots

Roots are the building blocks of words. When you meet a word you don’t know on the ACT, break it into parts: the root usually gives you enough of the meaning to answer. Your list grows every week; each new sheet adds roots and reviews the ones before.

sub-under
  • submerge: to put under water
  • subordinate: ranked under; less important
  • subway: a train that runs underground
A subordinate clause, like “which was built in 1888,” sits under the main sentence and can’t stand alone.
jectthrow
  • subject: what a sentence is about
  • project: to throw forward
  • interject: to throw a remark in between
Find the subject before you choose the verb.
temp / temportime
  • tense: the time a verb shows
  • temporary: lasting a short time
  • contemporary: from the same time
Verb tense tells you when; keep it steady unless the time really changes.
modmeasure, manner
  • modify: to change the manner of
  • modifier: a word that describes another
  • moderate: within a sensible measure
Put a modifier right next to the word it describes.
pro-for, forward
  • pronoun: a word that stands for a noun
  • propel: to push forward
  • promote: to move forward
A pronoun stands in for a noun: “Rosa” becomes “she.”
junct / joinjoin
  • conjunction: a word that joins ideas
  • juncture: a point where things join
  • adjoin: to be next to and joined with
The FANBOYS words are conjunctions: they join sentences together.
clud / clusclose, shut
  • conclude: to bring to a close
  • include: to shut in; contain
  • exclude: to shut out
The ACT often asks which sentence best concludes a paragraph.
sequ / secufollow
  • sequence: an order that follows
  • consequence: what follows from an action
  • consecutive: following one after another
Tense keeps events in sequence, so the reader knows what happened first.
tractpull, draw
  • contraction: words pulled together
  • attract: to pull toward
  • distract: to pull attention away
“It’s” is a contraction: “it is” pulled together.
ced / ceed / cessgo, yield
  • antecedent: what goes before
  • proceed: to go forward
  • exceed: to go beyond
A pronoun’s antecedent is the noun that goes before it.
Five minutes a day: cover the words and say what each root means, then name one word that uses it.

Rules and formulas from this week

This week’s grammar rules, plus the Week 1 formulas you used in the warm-up and review. Say each rule out loud, then check yourself with the example.

Rule or formulaRuleExample
Find the real subjectCross out “of …” phrases. The verb matches what’s left.The box of photos sits in the attic.
Either / neitherWith or / nor, the verb matches the nearer subject.Neither the coach nor the players were ready.
There is / there are“There” is never the subject. Look after the verb.There are three reasons.
Its vs. it’sit’s = it is / it has. its = belongs to it.The company changed its logo.
Their / there / they’rethey’re = they are. their = belongs to them. there = a place.They’re parking their truck over there.
Me or IDrop the other person and listen.The coach thanked Jada and me.
Who or whomhe → who. him → whom.The volunteer who organized it (he organized it)
Keep tense steadyMatch the time of the sentence and the passage.Last summer he worked and repaired boats.
Had + past formUse “had” for the earlier of two past actions.The crew had finished before the rain started.
Would haveNever “would of.” After have / has / had, use the past participle.would have bought · has frozen · had come
Commas and dashes in pairsExtra information gets matching marks on both sides.The lighthouse, built in 1888, still stands.
ColonsOnly after a complete sentence. Never after “such as.”We need three things: flour, sugar, and eggs.
No lone commasNo single comma between a subject and its verb.The students who finished early went home.
PossessivesPlural first, then the apostrophe.one player’s shoe · twelve players’ shoes
Slope(y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁)(1, 4) to (3, 10): 6 ÷ 2 = 3
Circle areaA = πr²r = 5 → 25π
Percent offprice × (1 − discount)$60, 25% off: 60 × 0.75 = $45
Average (mean)sum ÷ number of items72, 85, 92 → 249 ÷ 3 = 83
