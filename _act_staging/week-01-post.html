Part 1 · English

Joining sentences, saying it once, and two rules from next week.

English · Joining sentencesQ1
My brother plays trumpet in the band, he practices every night.
Not yet — one more try
Cover the comma. Is each side a complete sentence? If so, a comma alone can't join them.

“My brother plays trumpet in the band” and “he practices every night” are both complete sentences. A semicolon joins them: band; he. The original is a comma splice, “band he” is a run-on, and the last choice jams in an extra word that breaks the sentence.

English · Joining sentencesQ2
The old bridge was closed for repairs, drivers had to take a longer route.
Not yet — one more try
A comma can join two complete sentences only with a joining word (for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so). Where does the comma go?

Comma plus “so” joins the two sentences, and “so” also shows cause and effect: the bridge closed, so drivers detoured. The comma goes before “so” and nowhere else: repairs, so drivers. The original is a comma splice, the second choice is a run-on, and the last choice adds an extra comma after “so.”

English · Say it onceQ3
For the science fair, the students collaborated together on a group project.
Not yet — one more try
What does “collaborate” already mean?

“Collaborate” already means “work together,” so adding “together” repeats the idea. Say it once: collaborated.

English · Joining sentencesQ4
After the game ended, the players shook hands with their opponents.
Not yet — one more try
Cover the comma. Is “After the game ended” a complete sentence on its own?

“After the game ended” can't stand alone; “after” makes it depend on the rest. When a part like that comes first, a comma follows it, so NO CHANGE is correct.

A semicolon or period would leave “After the game ended” as a fragment. Without a comma, the words run together and the sentence is harder to read.

English · Joining sentencesQ5
The concert sold out in an hour, some fans had waited in line overnight.
Not yet — one more try
Cover the punctuation. Is each side a complete sentence on its own?

“The concert sold out in an hour” and “some fans had waited in line overnight” are both complete sentences. A comma alone can’t join them, so use a semicolon.

English · Say it onceQ6
The first-ever premiere of the film debuted on Friday.
Not yet — one more try
A premiere is already the first showing. Which choice says that only once?

“Premiere” and “debut” both mean the first showing, and “first-ever” says it a third time. “The film premiered on Friday” says it once.

English · Agreement (preview)Q7
Each of the players have a new jersey.
Not yet — one more try
Find the real subject. Cross out “of the players.” What’s left?

The subject is each, which is singular. “Of the players” is extra information, so cross it out: “Each has a new jersey.”

The trap: “players” sits right next to the verb and sounds plural. That’s next week’s rule.

English · Its / it’s (preview)Q8
The dog wagged it’s tail.
Not yet — one more try
Read “it’s” as “it is”: “The dog wagged it is tail.” Does that work?

It’s always means “it is” or “it has.” To show something belongs to it, use its, with no apostrophe. “Its’” is never correct.

Part 2 · Reading

Prove every answer from the text.

Reading · ToneQ9

Ramón planted tomatoes in April, certain he would have a harvest by June. By May the plants were tall but bare of fruit, and his neighbor, a retired farmer, suggested he stop watering so often. Ramón doubted her, but he cut back anyway. Within two weeks, small green tomatoes appeared.

Ramón's first reaction to his neighbor's advice is best described as:
Not yet — one more try
Find the exact words that show how Ramón first felt about her suggestion.

The passage says “Ramón doubted her.” To doubt is to be skeptical. Nothing shows anger. He wasn't indifferent, because he still followed the advice. He may have become grateful later, but the question asks about his first reaction.

Reading · InferenceQ10

Keisha read the email twice, set her phone face-down, and stared out the window. She had expected good news.

Keisha’s reaction to the email is best described as:
Not yet — one more try
She “had expected good news.” What do the phone face-down and the staring suggest she got instead?

She expected good news, then put her phone face-down and stared away. Those details point to disappointment. Nothing shows she was confused, and “bored” doesn’t fit someone who read the email twice.

Part 3 · Math

Backsolve, pick numbers, and do percents one step at a time.

Math · PercentQ11
A store raises the price of a $40 item by 15%. What is the new price?
Not yet — one more try
Up 15% means multiply by 1.15.

$40 × 1.15 = $46.00.

The trap: $55 adds 15 dollars instead of 15 percent.

Solve it another way
Another way: 10% of 40 is 4, and 5% is 2, so 15% is 6. Then 40 + 6 = 46.
Math · BacksolvingQ12
If 4(x + 2) = 3x + 15, what is x?
Not yet — one more try
Backsolve: try a choice and see if both sides come out equal. Or distribute the 4 first.

Try 7: the left side is 4(7 + 2) = 36, and the right side is 3(7) + 15 = 36. They match. ✓

Solve it another way
Another way: distribute to get 4x + 8 = 3x + 15, then subtract 3x and 8 from both sides: x = 7.
Math · Pick numbersQ13
If k is a positive even integer, which of the following must be odd?
Not yet — one more try
Pick an even number, like k = 4, and work out each choice.

With k = 4: 2(4) = 8 (even), 4 + 2 = 6 (even), 4 + 1 = 5 (odd), 4² = 16 (even).

The rule behind it: even + 1 is always odd.

Math · RatesQ14
Maria reads 24 pages in 30 minutes. At this rate, how many pages will she read in 75 minutes?
Not yet — one more try
Find her pages per minute first, or notice how 75 minutes compares to 30 minutes.

24 ÷ 30 = 0.8 pages per minute, and 0.8 × 75 = 60 pages.

Solve it another way
Another way: 75 minutes is 2.5 times 30 minutes, so she reads 2.5 × 24 = 60 pages.
Math · Linear equationsQ15
If 6x − 4 = 2x + 20, what is x?
Not yet — one more try
Get the x terms on one side first: subtract 2x from both sides. Then add 4.

Subtract 2x: 4x − 4 = 20. Add 4: 4x = 24. Divide by 4: x = 6.

Solve it another way
Another way: backsolve with 6. Left side: 36 − 4 = 32. Right side: 12 + 20 = 32. ✓
Math · PercentQ16
What is 30% of 90?
Not yet — one more try
10% of 90 is 9. How many 10%s make 30%?

30% is 0.30, and “of” means multiply: 0.30 × 90 = 27.

Solve it another way
Another way: 10% of 90 is 9, so 30% is 3 × 9 = 27.
Math · PercentQ17
A $50 item is marked 20% off. Then 8% sales tax is added to the sale price. What is the total cost?
Not yet — one more try
Two steps, in order. First the sale price: 50 × 0.80. Then add the tax by multiplying that by 1.08.

Sale price: 50 × 0.80 = $40. With tax: 40 × 1.08 = $43.20.

The trap: $44.00 comes from combining the percents (20% off minus 8% tax = 12% off). The tax is on the sale price, so do one step at a time.

Math · Pick numbersQ18
If m is an odd integer, which statement about m + 3 is true?
Not yet — one more try
Pick numbers. Try m = 1 and m = 5. What do you get?

Odd + odd = even. Test it: 1 + 3 = 4, 5 + 3 = 8, 7 + 3 = 10. It is always even.

Solve it another way
Another way: picking numbers turns a letter problem into arithmetic. Try two or three to be sure.
Math · RatesQ19
If 9 notebooks cost $13.50, how much do 4 notebooks cost at the same price?
Not yet — one more try
Find the price of one notebook first: 13.50 ÷ 9.

One notebook: 13.50 ÷ 9 = $1.50. Four notebooks: 4 × 1.50 = $6.00.

Part 4 · Scoring & strategy

How the composite works and how to finish a section.

ScoringQ20
A student scores English 21, Math 19 and Reading 23. What is the composite?
Not yet — one more try
Add the three scores, divide by 3, then round.

21 + 19 + 23 = 63, and 63 ÷ 3 = 21. The composite is the average of English, Math and Reading.

Test strategyQ21
You have 2 minutes left in English and 5 questions you haven't started. What's the best plan?
Not yet — one more try
What does a wrong answer cost, and what does a blank earn?

A wrong answer costs nothing, but a blank earns nothing. Grab the quick points first, then guess on the rest. Every guess has a 1-in-4 chance, and a blank has none.

ScoringQ22
A student scores English 20, Math 22 and Reading 23. What is the ACT composite?
Not yet — one more try
Add the three scores and divide by 3. Composites are whole numbers, so round.

20 + 22 + 23 = 65, and 65 ÷ 3 = 21.67. Round to the nearest whole number: 22.

The trap: 21.67 isn’t a possible composite. ACT scores are always whole numbers.
