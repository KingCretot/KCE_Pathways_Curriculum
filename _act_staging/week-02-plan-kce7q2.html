Instructor Lesson Plan — ACT Week 2

English I — Conventions · lesson guide, run of show and complete answer keys
Instructor only · do not share with students

At a glance

Goal. The student can find the real subject, choose the correct pronoun and verb form, and punctuate by rule. ACT reporting category: Conventions of Standard English.
Student pages. week-02-lesson.html (together, 31 questions), week-02-post.html (alone, 22 questions) and week-02-roots-formulas.html (printable sheet).
Materials. Screen, paper and pencil. No calculator needed. No timer in lessons.
Before the session. Review the Week 1 post-lesson email’s “needs review” list and the diagnostic’s Conventions of Standard English misses. Start the warm-up there.

Run of show · 60 minutes

TimeSegmentWhat to do
0:00Warm-up (Q1–Q6)Week 1 review: two English rules, two roots, two formulas. Misses on Q3–Q6 mean the Week 1 sheet wasn’t studied; assign it again.
0:05Agreement (Q7–Q11)Make the student physically cross out every “of” phrase on paper. Say the subject and the verb together out loud before choosing.
0:16Pronouns (Q12–Q16)On every its/it’s, their/they’re, who’s/whose question, the student reads the contraction as two words out loud. Who/whom: answer with he/him.
0:26Verbs (Q17–Q20)Before each answer, the student names the time of the sentence (past, present, earlier past). Drill three irregular triples aloud: freeze/froze/frozen, run/ran/run, come/came/come.
0:35Punctuation (Q21–Q26)For every comma, the student names its rule. No rule, no comma. Q22 and Q26 are the “extra comma” traps; watch for hesitation there.
0:46Passage Practice (Q27–Q31)Student names the rule being tested before picking. This is the real ACT format; note which rules they can’t spot inside a paragraph.
0:55Rule CardsStudent says each rule before flipping. Point to the printable Week 2 Roots & Formulas sheet; check root and rule recall at the start of the next lesson.
0:58Wrap-upStudent enters name, sends results and opens the Post-Lesson Practice. Remind them Full-Length Practice Exam 1 comes after this week.

How the two-try questions work

First miss: a hint appears and that choice locks; the student gets one more try.
Second miss: the answer shows with its logic. Have the student read it aloud.
Results email (Formspree xoevydrv) marks each question first try, second try or missed, with a “needs review” list.

Answer key · Lesson (31 questions)

#AnsQuestionWhy
Warm-up
Q1A
Week 1 review · Joining sentences
The bus was late again, so we walked to school.
“The bus was late again” and “we walked to school” are both complete sentences. A comma plus so (a FANBOYS word) joins them correctly, so NO CHANGE.
Q2J
Week 1 review · Say it once
Maria repeated the steps again before she left.
“Repeated” already means “did again,” so “again” says it twice. “Repeated the steps” says it once.
Q3B
Week 1 roots · dict
The root dict means “say.” Based on its parts, what does contradict mean?
contra (against) + dict (say) = to say against, or to say the opposite. On ACT Reading, a wrong answer often contradicts the passage.
Q4J
Week 1 roots · cis
The root cis means “cut.” A precise answer is one that is:
pre (before) + cis (cut): cut to size ahead of time. A precise answer is exact, with nothing extra.
Q5B
Week 1 formulas · Slope
What is the slope of the line through (1, 4) and (3, 10)?
Rise: 10 − 4 = 6. Run: 3 − 1 = 2. Slope: 6 ÷ 2 = 3.
Q6H
Week 1 formulas · Circle area
A circle has a radius of 5. What is its area?
A = πr² = π × 5² = 25π.
Agreement
Q7C
Agreement · Find the real subject
The box of old photographs sit in the attic.
Cross out the “of” phrase: “The box … sit.” The subject is box, which is singular, so the verb is sits.
Q8G
Agreement · Neither / nor
Neither the coach nor the players was ready for the storm delay.
With either/or and neither/nor, the verb agrees with the nearer subject. “Players” is closer, and it’s plural, so were.
Q9D
Agreement · Subject after the verb
There is three reasons the plan failed.
“There” is never the subject. The real subject is reasons, which is plural, so are. Flip it to check: “Three reasons are …”
Q10H
Agreement · Find the real subject
The list of supplies for the camping trip are taped to the fridge.
Strip the phrases: “The list … is taped to the fridge.” List is singular, so is.
Q11A
Agreement · Each, every, everyone
Each of the students needs a library card.
The subject is each, which is always singular. “Each needs” is correct, so NO CHANGE.
Pronouns
Q12G
Pronouns · Its / it’s
The company changed it’s logo last year.
It’s means “it is” or “it has.” To show something belongs to it, use its, with no apostrophe. A company is one thing, so “their” doesn’t fit.
Q13D
Pronouns · Their / there / they’re
The twins said their planning to visit in June.
The sentence needs “they are,” and the contraction is they’re. “Their” shows belonging; “there” is a place.
Q14H
Pronouns · Agreement
The two bakeries on Oak Street lowered its prices.
The pronoun refers to bakeries, which is plural, so it needs a plural pronoun: their prices.
Q15B
Pronouns · Me or I
Our coach gave the awards to Jada and I.
Take out the other person: “gave the awards to me.” After a word like “to,” use me, not “I.”
Q16J
Pronouns · Who / whom
The volunteer whom organized the food drive was thanked at the assembly.
He organized the drive, not “him.” If “he” fits, use who; if “him” fits, use whom. “Which” is for things, not people.
Verbs
Q17C
Verbs · Keep tense steady
Last summer, Andre worked at a marina, where he repairs boats and sold bait.
“Last summer,” “worked” and “sold” all point to the past, so the verb stays in the past: repaired.
Q18G
Verbs · Had + past participle
By the time the rain started, the crew finish the roof.
The roof was finished before the rain started. For the earlier of two past actions, use had + the past form: had finished.
Q19D
Verbs · Would have
If I had known about the sale, I would of bought more paint.
“Would of” is a spelling of how “would’ve” sounds. The correct form is would have, and the past form of “buy” is bought.
Q20H
Verbs · Irregular forms
The pond has froze over twice this winter.
After has, have or had, use the past participle. For “freeze,” that’s frozen: freeze, froze, has frozen.
Punctuation
Q21B
Punctuation · Commas in pairs
The lighthouse, which was built in 1888 still guides ships into the harbor.
“which was built in 1888” is extra information. It opens with a comma, so it must close with a comma: 1888,.
Q22J
Punctuation · Subject and verb
The students who finished early, went to the library.
The subject is “the students who finished early,” and the verb is “went.” A single comma never separates a subject from its verb, so early went.
Q23A
Punctuation · Colon
The recipe needs three things: flour, sugar, and eggs.
A colon comes after a complete sentence and introduces what follows. “The recipe needs three things” is complete, so the colon is right: NO CHANGE.
Q24H
Punctuation · Plural possessive
All twelve players uniforms arrived a week late.
Twelve players, so start with the plural, players. It already ends in s, so add only an apostrophe: players’.
Q25B
Punctuation · Dashes in pairs
The trail—the steepest in the park, climbs 1,200 feet in two miles.
Pairs match. If the extra information opens with a dash, it closes with a dash: park—climbs.
Q26J
Punctuation · Unneeded commas
The shop sells tools such as, hammers, drills, and saws.
Nothing goes between “such as” and its list. as hammers, is correct.
Passage Practice
Q27C
Passage · Agreement
[1] … the parking lot behind the old train depot turn into a night market.
The subject is lot, which is singular: “the parking lot … turns.” The phrase in between is a trap.
Q28G
Passage · Pronouns
[2] Farmers, bakers, and a man who sharpens knives set up they’re tables before sunset.
The tables belong to the vendors, so use the possessive their. “They’re” means “they are,” and “there” is a place.
Q29A
Passage · Who / whom
[3] The market was started by Rosa Medina, who sold honey there for eleven years.
She sold honey, so who is correct: NO CHANGE. “Which” is for things, and “whom” would match “her.”
Q30J
Passage · Tense
[4] At first, only six vendors come; last year, more than forty did.
The sentence is about the past (“At first,” “last year … did”), so the verb is came.
Q31C
Passage · Joining sentences
[5] Rosa says the best part isn’t the food it’s the neighbors who finally learn each other’s names.
“Rosa says the best part isn’t the food” and “it’s the neighbors …” are both complete. Nothing joins them now (a run-on), and a comma alone makes a splice. A semicolon joins them.

Answer key · Post-Lesson Practice (22 questions)

#AnsQuestionWhy
Agreement & verbs
Q1D
Agreement · Find the real subject
The flock of geese fly south every October.
The subject is flock, one group, so the verb is singular: flies.
Q2H
Agreement · Either / or
Either the parents or the principal have the final say.
With “either … or,” match the nearer subject. Principal is closer and singular, so has.
Q3B
Agreement · Subject after the verb
Along the fence grows rows of sunflowers.
Flipped: “Rows of sunflowers grow along the fence.” The subject is rows, which is plural.
Q4F
Agreement · Everyone
Everyone in the choir knows the words by heart.
Everyone is singular, like each and every. “Everyone knows” is correct, so NO CHANGE.
Q5D
Verbs · Keep tense steady
Yesterday, the storm knocked out power, and the whole street stays dark until midnight.
The sentence is in the past, so the verb stays in the past: stayed.
Q6H
Verbs · Irregular forms
The runners had ran five miles before sunrise.
After had, use the past participle. For “run,” it’s run: run, ran, had run.
Q7B
Verbs · Would have
If Carlos had studied the map, he wouldn’t of gotten lost.
“Would of” should be would have. The sentence imagines a past that didn’t happen, so wouldn’t have gotten.
Pronouns
Q8J
Pronouns · Its / it’s
The museum reopened it’s doors after the repairs.
The doors belong to the museum, so use the possessive its, with no apostrophe.
Q9C
Pronouns · Their / there / they’re
The Garcias parked there truck by the barn.
The truck belongs to the Garcias, so use the possessive their. “There” is a place.
Q10G
Pronouns · Me or I
Between you and I, the answer is B.
After words like between, to, for and with, use me: “between you and me.”
Q11D
Pronouns · Whose / who’s
The student who’s essay won the contest goes to our school.
Who’s means “who is.” The essay belongs to the student, so use the possessive whose.
Q12F
Pronouns · Agreement
The volunteers brought their own tools.
Volunteers is plural, and the tools belong to them, so their is correct: NO CHANGE.
Punctuation
Q13C
Punctuation · Commas in pairs
Our neighbor, a retired pilot still flies on weekends.
“a retired pilot” is extra information. It opens with a comma, so it closes with one: pilot,.
Q14G
Punctuation · Subject and verb
The dog that lives next door, barks at every mail truck.
The subject is “the dog that lives next door,” and the verb is “barks.” No single comma between them: door barks.
Q15D
Punctuation · Colon
I need two things from the store, milk and bread.
The first part is a complete sentence, and a list follows. A colon introduces it: store:.
Q16H
Punctuation · Plural possessive
Both dog’s tails wagged when the mail carrier arrived.
Two dogs: plural first, dogs, then the apostrophe: dogs’ tails.
Q17B
Punctuation · Dashes in pairs
The recipe—my grandmother’s favorite, calls for three lemons.
It opens with a dash, so it closes with a dash: favorite—calls.
Q18F
Punctuation · Joining sentences
It rained all morning, but the game went on anyway.
Comma before the FANBOYS word, none after it: morning, but. That’s NO CHANGE.
Roots & review
Q19B
Week 2 roots · sequ
The root sequ means “follow.” Consecutive days are days that:
con (together) + sequ (follow): consecutive days follow one after another, like Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday.
Q20J
Week 2 roots · ced
A pronoun’s antecedent is the noun it refers to. Using the roots ante (before) and ced (go), the antecedent usually:
ante (before) + ced (go) = goes before. The antecedent usually comes before the pronoun: “Rosa said she …”
Q21C
Week 1 review · Percent
A $60 jacket is 25% off. What is the sale price?
60 × 0.75 = $45.
Q22G
Week 1 review · Average
What is the average of 72, 85, and 92?
72 + 85 + 92 = 249, and 249 ÷ 3 = 83.
