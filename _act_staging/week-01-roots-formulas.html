Word roots

Roots are the building blocks of words. When you meet a word you don’t know on the ACT, break it into parts: the root usually gives you enough of the meaning to answer. Your list grows every week; next week’s sheet adds new roots and reviews these.

com- / con-together, with
  • composite: made of parts put together
  • consensus: general agreement
  • converge: to come together
Your ACT composite puts your English, Math and Reading scores together.
pos / ponput, place
  • component: one part of a whole
  • postpone: to put off until later
  • position: the place something is put
“The meeting was postponed” already means it was moved later; don’t say it twice.
re-back, again
  • redundant: repeating what was already said; more than needed
  • revise: to look at again and improve
  • reject: to throw back; refuse
On ACT English, cut redundant words: the shortest correct choice usually wins.
dictsay, speak
  • contradict: to say the opposite
  • predict: to say before it happens
  • verdict: a judgment that is spoken
A wrong Reading answer often contradicts a line in the passage.
fercarry, bring
  • infer: to conclude from evidence
  • transfer: to carry across
  • reference: something you carry back to or point to
An inference question asks what the passage suggests, not what it says word for word.
cis / cidcut
  • concise: cut down to only what is needed
  • precise: exact; cut to the right size
  • incision: a cut
The ACT rewards concise writing: say it once, clearly.
vid / vissee
  • evidence: what can be seen that proves something
  • visible: able to be seen
  • revise: to see again
Before you pick a Reading answer, point to the evidence in the passage.
graphwrite
  • paragraph: a written section of text
  • graph: a written picture of data
  • biography: a written account of a life
ACT Reading passages number each paragraph so questions can point to it.
pre-before
  • predict: to say before
  • precede: to come before
  • preview: to see before
Skim the questions to preview what the passage will ask about.
in- / im- / ir-not
  • inaccurate: not correct
  • implausible: not believable
  • irrelevant: not related to the topic
If a sentence is irrelevant to the paragraph, the ACT wants it deleted.
Five minutes a day: cover the words and say what each root means, then name one word that uses it.
