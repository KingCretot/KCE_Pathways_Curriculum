Instructor Key — ACT Diagnostic

40-question screener (English 15 · Math 14 · Reading 11) + optional Science 10 · all content original to KCE
Instructor only · do not share with students

How it works

Student page: diagnostic.html on the ACT program hub. Untimed; time per section is recorded silently and reported to you.
No feedback during the test. Students can move back and forth and change answers. Right/wrong isn’t shown until they finish; then they see estimated scores only.
Your email (Formspree xoevydrv, subject “KCE ACT Diagnostic — [name] — est. composite N”) includes each section’s raw score, estimated scale score with a ±3 range, time per section, total time, results by ACT reporting category (the same categories on official ACT score reports), and every miss with the student’s pick, the right answer and seconds spent. An alerts line at the top flags rushing (under 8 seconds per question), mostly-instant answers, chance-level sections and blank questions; when effort is in doubt the subject line ends in “check effort.”
About the estimates: scores are converted with ACT’s official Enhanced ACT raw-to-scale tables, in proportion to section length. They are still estimates: this is a different, much shorter set of questions than ACT’s forms. With 11–15 questions per section, treat each section score as ±3 and the composite as ±2. Use them to set a starting target and to choose which weeks to emphasize, not as a prediction to quote to families.

Reading the results

  • English: Conventions misses point to Week 2; Production of Writing and Knowledge of Language misses point to Week 4.
  • Math: Integrating Essential Skills misses (percents, rates, averages, area) are the fastest points to bank; Preparing for Higher Math misses show which of Weeks 3, 5 and 7 to lean on.
  • Reading: Key Ideas and Details misses mean slow down and prove answers; Craft and Structure misses mean word meaning and purpose; Integration misses mean evidence and competing claims. Passages here are about a third of real length, so Reading time does not predict test-day pacing.
  • Time: a very fast English or Reading time with many misses suggests rushing; a very slow Math time suggests the student needs strategy (backsolving, picking numbers) more than content.
