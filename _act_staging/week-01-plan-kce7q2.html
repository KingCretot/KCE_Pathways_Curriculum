Instructor Lesson Plan — ACT Week 1

ACT Foundations: The Full Map · lesson guide, run of show and complete answer keys
Instructor only · do not share with students

At a glance

Goal. The student understands the ACT format and scoring, and leaves with three English rules, two math strategies (backsolve, pick numbers) and a proof-first reading habit.
Student pages. week-01-lesson.html (done together, 32 questions) and week-01-post.html (done alone before next session, 22 questions). Both email results to you.
Materials. Screen for the lesson, paper and pencils, the student’s own calculator. No timer in lessons; timers are used only on the full-length practice exams.
Before the session. Have the student’s KCE ACT Diagnostic results email in hand. The diagnostic should be done before this lesson. If it isn’t, have the student take it right after the session; it’s also a catch-up item on the post-lesson checklist.

How the two-try questions work

First miss: a hint appears and the choice is locked; the student gets one more try.
Second miss: the correct answer is shown with its full logic. Have the student read the logic out loud before moving on.
Your results email (Formspree xoevydrv) lists every question as correct on try one, correct on try two, or missed with both picks shown, plus a “needs review” list and the student’s planned test format. Use “needs review” to open the next lesson.

Answer key · Lesson (32 questions)

#AnsQuestionWhy
Warm-up
Q1B
English · Joining sentences
My sister loves chemistry, she wants to become a pharmacist.
Both halves are complete sentences, so a comma alone can’t join them. That’s a comma splice. A semicolon can join them.
Q2H
English · Say it once
The meeting was postponed until later at a future date.
“Postponed” already means moved to a later time, so every other choice repeats the idea. Deleting the phrase leaves “The meeting was postponed.” It’s short, clear and correct.
Q3B
Math · Percent
What is 15% of 60?
“Percent” means “out of 100,” so 15% is 0.15. “Of” means multiply: 0.15 × 60 = 9.
Q4H
Math · Linear equations
If 5x − 8 = 27, what is x?
Add 8 to both sides: 5x = 35. Divide by 5: x = 7.
Q5C
Math · Rates
A car travels 150 miles in 2.5 hours. What is its average speed, in miles per hour?
150 ÷ 2.5 = 60 miles per hour.
Q6G
Math · Expressions
Which expression is equivalent to (x + 3)²?
(x + 3)(x + 3) = x² + 3x + 3x + 9 = x² + 6x + 9.
The Map
Q7B
Pacing
ACT English gives you 50 questions in 35 minutes. About how much time is that per question?
35 minutes × 60 = 2,100 seconds. 2,100 ÷ 50 = 42 seconds per question. That's the fastest pace on the whole test, which is why English gets its own pacing plan.
Q8H
Test format
Which statement about the ACT Science section is true?
Your composite comes from English, Math and Reading only. Science is optional and reported on its own. Whether to take it depends on the colleges you apply to, not on your composite.
Q9D
Test strategy
You have 1 minute left in Math and 4 questions you haven't read yet. What's the best move?
A wrong answer costs nothing, but a blank is a guaranteed zero. With four choices, each guess has a 1-in-4 chance of being right. Four guesses usually earn you about one extra point. Pick one letter, fill in all four, and finish.
Your Score
Q10G
Scoring
A student scores English 24, Math 20, Reading 25. What is the composite?
24 + 20 + 25 = 69. 69 ÷ 3 = 23 exactly. So the composite is 23. No rounding needed this time.
Q11B
Scoring
To earn a composite of 24, what is the smallest total the three section scores can add up to?
72 ÷ 3 = 24 exactly, but we can go lower. 71 ÷ 3 = 23.67, which rounds up to 24. ✓ 70 ÷ 3 = 23.33, which rounds down to 23. ✗ So 71 is the smallest total. A student at 19 has sections adding up to about 57, so reaching 24 takes 14 more section points spread across three sections. For example: +5 English, +4 Math, +5 Reading.
English
Q12G
English · Joining sentences
The library opened a new study room, students can reserve it online.
“The library opened a new study room” is a complete sentence. “students can reserve it online” is a complete sentence. Two complete sentences can be joined by a semicolon, so room; students works. NO CHANGE is a comma splice. “room students” has no punctuation at all, so it's a run-on. “room, which students can reserve it” doesn't work either, because “which” already means the room, so “it” repeats it.
Q13C
English · Say it once
Each spring, the town holds a festival that celebrates the annual yearly harvest.
“Annual” already means “every year,” so adding “yearly” or “every year” repeats the idea. the annual harvest says it once and clearly. Every other choice repeats itself.
Q14F
English · Joining sentences
Marcus finished his shift at the hospital, and then he drove straight to class.
Both halves are complete sentences, so they need a proper joint. The original uses a comma plus “and,” which is one of the FANBOYS. That's correct. “then” is not a FANBOYS word, so “, then he drove” is a comma splice, and “then he drove” with no comma is a run-on. A semicolon already joins the sentences, so “; and” doubles up. NO CHANGE was right all along.
Q15D
English · Joining sentences
Dr. Alvarez studies coral reefs, her research takes her to the Florida Keys every summer.
“Dr. Alvarez studies coral reefs” and “her research takes her to the Florida Keys” are both complete. Comma plus a FANBOYS word works: reefs, and her. The comma goes before “and,” not after it. The original is a comma splice, and “reefs her” is a run-on.
Q16H
English · Say it once
At this point in time, the team is currently reviewing the proposal.
Once. The team is currently says “now” one time and reads cleanly. The other choices say it two times over.
Math
Q17C
Math · Percent
A number increased by 40% is 84. What is the number?
Backsolve: 60 × 1.4 = 84. ✓
Q18H
Math · Backsolving
Adult tickets cost $12 and student tickets cost $7. A group bought 10 tickets for $95. How many were adult tickets?
Try 5 adults: 5 × $12 = $60. That leaves 5 students: 5 × $7 = $35. Total: $60 + $35 = $95. ✓
Q19B
Math · Percent
An $80 jacket is on sale for 25% off. A 6% sales tax is then added to the sale price. What is the total cost?
Discount: $80 × 0.75 = $60.00. Tax: $60.00 × 1.06 = $63.60.
Q20J
Math · Pick numbers
If n is an odd integer, which of the following must be even?
With n = 3: 2(3) + 1 = 7 (odd), 3 + 2 = 5 (odd), 3 × 3 = 9 (odd), 3 + 1 = 4 (even). The rule behind it: odd + 1 is always even.
Q21B
Math · Rates
A printer prints 18 pages per minute. How many minutes will it take to print 405 pages?
Minutes = total pages ÷ pages per minute. So 405 ÷ 18 = 22.5 minutes. The printer finishes 18 pages every minute, and 405 pages take 22 full minutes plus half of one more.
Q22G
Math · Proportions
If x/4 = 9/12, what is x?
9/12 simplifies to 3/4. So x/4 = 3/4, which means x = 3.
Reading
Q23B
Reading · Main idea
The passage mainly focuses on:
¶1: he teaches by listening. ¶2: she learns to notice what the truck is “saying.” ¶3: she carries his habit with her. The thread through all three is learning to pay attention. The grandfather gives no rules (¶1: “said nothing”), so the first choice is wrong. The truck's sounds are normal signals, not problems. Her frustration shows up only “at first” in ¶2, so it's too narrow for the main idea.
Q24J
Reading · Detail
According to the passage, the steering wheel “shivered” when:
¶2 says “the steering wheel shivered when her tires drifted onto the rough edge of the road.” That's a direct match. Waiting too long to shift made the engine climb in pitch, a different signal from the same sentence. This is the “true but off-target” trap.
Q25A
Reading · Meaning in context
The grandfather's words “Truck will tell you” (¶1) mean that Nia should:
¶2 explains his meaning. She began to hear the engine's pitch and feel the steering wheel shiver: “The truck… was talking constantly.” So he means she should learn from its sounds and movements. The passage never mentions warning lights, which makes the second choice a twisted trap. Nothing suggests asking someone else, and he isn't telling her to stop caring; he's telling her where to look for the answer.
Q26H
Reading · Inference
The final paragraph suggests that Nia:
She taps the dashboard “twice, lightly, the way he did.” Those last four words show she has taken on his habit. She “missed the silence,” so she clearly still thinks of him. Nothing says she prefers company. Nervousness is a guess the passage doesn't support; the tap is described as his habit, not as fear.
Pace Practice
Q27D
English · Joining sentences
The bakery on Oak Street sells out of bread by noon, customers line up before it opens.
Both sides are complete sentences, so a semicolon joins them: noon; customers. The original is a comma splice. “noon customers” is a run-on. “, so,” adds an extra comma after “so”; the fix is “, so customers” with no comma after “so.”
Q28H
English · Say it once
In my opinion, I think that the new schedule works better.
Say it once: I think the new schedule works better. Every other choice says the same idea twice.
Q29B
English · Subject–verb agreement
The coach, along with her assistants, are reviewing game film tonight.
Cross out “along with her assistants.” It's extra information set off by commas. What's left: “The coach ___ reviewing game film tonight.” “Coach” is one person, so the verb is is. “are,” “were” and “have been” are all plural forms. Subject–verb agreement gets its own full lesson next week.
Q30F
English · Joining sentences
Because the storm knocked out power, classes were canceled for two days.
“Because the storm knocked out power” is not a complete sentence; “because” makes it depend on the rest. When a part like that comes first, a comma separates it from the main sentence, so NO CHANGE is correct. A semicolon or a period would leave “Because the storm knocked out power” standing alone as a fragment. With no comma, the two parts run together.
Q31D
English · Word choice
The students turned in there projects on Friday.
their shows ownership: their projects. “there” is a place (over there), “they're” means “they are,” and “theirs” stands alone without a noun after it (“the projects are theirs”).
Q32H
English · Joining sentences
She returned the book to the library it was two weeks overdue.
“She returned the book to the library” and “it was two weeks overdue” are both complete. A semicolon joins them: library; it. The original is a run-on, and a comma alone makes a comma splice. The last choice adds a comma after “it” that doesn't belong there.

Answer key · Post-Lesson Practice (22 questions)

#AnsQuestionWhy
English
Q1D
English · Joining sentences
My brother plays trumpet in the band, he practices every night.
“My brother plays trumpet in the band” and “he practices every night” are both complete sentences. A semicolon joins them: band; he. The original is a comma splice, “band he” is a run-on, and the last choice jams in an extra word that breaks the sentence.
Q2H
English · Joining sentences
The old bridge was closed for repairs, drivers had to take a longer route.
Comma plus “so” joins the two sentences, and “so” also shows cause and effect: the bridge closed, so drivers detoured. The comma goes before “so” and nowhere else: repairs, so drivers. The original is a comma splice, the second choice is a run-on, and the last choice adds an extra comma after “so.”
Q3B
English · Say it once
For the science fair, the students collaborated together on a group project.
“Collaborate” already means “work together,” so adding “together” repeats the idea. Say it once: collaborated.
Q4F
English · Joining sentences
After the game ended, the players shook hands with their opponents.
“After the game ended” can't stand alone; “after” makes it depend on the rest. When a part like that comes first, a comma follows it, so NO CHANGE is correct. A semicolon or period would leave “After the game ended” as a fragment. Without a comma, the words run together and the sentence is harder to read.
Q5B
English · Joining sentences
The concert sold out in an hour, some fans had waited in line overnight.
“The concert sold out in an hour” and “some fans had waited in line overnight” are both complete sentences. A comma alone can’t join them, so use a semicolon.
Q6H
English · Say it once
The first-ever premiere of the film debuted on Friday.
“Premiere” and “debut” both mean the first showing, and “first-ever” says it a third time. “The film premiered on Friday” says it once.
Q7B
English · Agreement (preview)
Each of the players have a new jersey.
The subject is each, which is singular. “Of the players” is extra information, so cross it out: “Each has a new jersey.”
Q8J
English · Its / it’s (preview)
The dog wagged it’s tail.
It’s always means “it is” or “it has.” To show something belongs to it, use its, with no apostrophe. “Its’” is never correct.
Reading
Q9C
Reading · Tone
Ramón's first reaction to his neighbor's advice is best described as:
The passage says “Ramón doubted her.” To doubt is to be skeptical. Nothing shows anger. He wasn't indifferent, because he still followed the advice. He may have become grateful later, but the question asks about his first reaction.
Q10G
Reading · Inference
Keisha’s reaction to the email is best described as:
She expected good news, then put her phone face-down and stared away. Those details point to disappointment. Nothing shows she was confused, and “bored” doesn’t fit someone who read the email twice.
Math
Q11B
Math · Percent
A store raises the price of a $40 item by 15%. What is the new price?
$40 × 1.15 = $46.00.
Q12G
Math · Backsolving
If 4(x + 2) = 3x + 15, what is x?
Try 7: the left side is 4(7 + 2) = 36, and the right side is 3(7) + 15 = 36. They match. ✓
Q13C
Math · Pick numbers
If k is a positive even integer, which of the following must be odd?
With k = 4: 2(4) = 8 (even), 4 + 2 = 6 (even), 4 + 1 = 5 (odd), 4² = 16 (even). The rule behind it: even + 1 is always odd.
Q14H
Math · Rates
Maria reads 24 pages in 30 minutes. At this rate, how many pages will she read in 75 minutes?
24 ÷ 30 = 0.8 pages per minute, and 0.8 × 75 = 60 pages.
Q15C
Math · Linear equations
If 6x − 4 = 2x + 20, what is x?
Subtract 2x: 4x − 4 = 20. Add 4: 4x = 24. Divide by 4: x = 6.
Q16G
Math · Percent
What is 30% of 90?
30% is 0.30, and “of” means multiply: 0.30 × 90 = 27.
Q17B
Math · Percent
A $50 item is marked 20% off. Then 8% sales tax is added to the sale price. What is the total cost?
Sale price: 50 × 0.80 = $40. With tax: 40 × 1.08 = $43.20.
Q18F
Math · Pick numbers
If m is an odd integer, which statement about m + 3 is true?
Odd + odd = even. Test it: 1 + 3 = 4, 5 + 3 = 8, 7 + 3 = 10. It is always even.
Q19B
Math · Rates
If 9 notebooks cost $13.50, how much do 4 notebooks cost at the same price?
One notebook: 13.50 ÷ 9 = $1.50. Four notebooks: 4 × 1.50 = $6.00.
Scoring & strategy
Q20G
Scoring
A student scores English 21, Math 19 and Reading 23. What is the composite?
21 + 19 + 23 = 63, and 63 ÷ 3 = 21. The composite is the average of English, Math and Reading.
Q21D
Test strategy
You have 2 minutes left in English and 5 questions you haven't started. What's the best plan?
A wrong answer costs nothing, but a blank earns nothing. Grab the quick points first, then guess on the rest. Every guess has a 1-in-4 chance, and a blank has none.
Q22H
Scoring
A student scores English 20, Math 22 and Reading 23. What is the ACT composite?
20 + 22 + 23 = 65, and 65 ÷ 3 = 21.67. Round to the nearest whole number: 22.
