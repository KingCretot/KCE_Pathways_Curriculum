📰 Nonfiction: What Makes a Partnership Fair

Nonfiction Passage

When two people run a business together — even a small one, like a lemonade stand — disagreements about fairness are extremely common. Business experts say the strongest partnerships are the ones where both people can clearly explain WHAT they contributed, using real evidence, and are willing to COMPROMISE when their views differ.

It's rarely as simple as splitting everything exactly in half. Sometimes one partner puts in more hours. Sometimes another partner brings a different, harder-to-measure kind of value — like marketing, design, or planning. Fair doesn't always mean identical.

🍋 Splitting the Profits, Part 1

Priya counted the cash box: $22.25 total. She grinned and started dividing it in her head — half for her, half for Jordan. Simple.

"Wait," Jordan said, looking over her shoulder. "I made the signs. And I picked the spot on the corner where all the foot traffic is. That took like two hours by itself."

Priya paused. She HAD worked more hours actually standing at the stand — six hours over the weekend, compared to Jordan's four. But Jordan's signs really had brought in extra customers; she'd seen people stop and point at them. Neither of them was wrong, exactly. They just hadn't thought about how to measure two very different kinds of work.

🍋 Splitting the Profits, Part 2

"What if we don't split it exactly in half?" Jordan said slowly. "What if we count the signs and the spot as worth something too — not just the hours?"

Priya thought about it. It made sense, actually. Jordan's signs weren't nothing — she'd seen three different families stop because of them. "Okay," Priya said. "What if I get a little more because of the extra hours, but you get a little more because of the signs and spot? So it evens back out somewhere in the middle?"

Jordan nodded slowly, then smiled. "That actually sounds fair. Not exactly half, but fair."

✂️ How to Summarize

👁

Think About This

A good SUMMARY keeps the main idea and the most important supporting details, but leaves out small extra description. It's much shorter than the original — but someone who reads only the summary should still understand what really happened.

🧩

Example Summary

"Priya and Jordan disagreed about how to split their lemonade stand profits because they'd contributed different kinds of work. They compromised on a split that wasn't exactly half, but felt fair to both of them."
