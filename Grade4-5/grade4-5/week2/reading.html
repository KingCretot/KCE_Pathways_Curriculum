📰 Nonfiction: Why Siblings See Things Differently

Nonfiction Passage

Psychologists who study families have found something interesting: brothers and sisters often remember the exact same event in very different ways. An older sibling put in charge might feel the weight of responsibility — worried about doing a good job. A younger sibling in that same moment might feel something completely different — maybe excited, maybe annoyed at being told what to do, maybe just having fun.

Neither sibling is "wrong" about how they felt. This is called PERSPECTIVE — the particular way one person sees and feels about the same event someone else experienced differently. Understanding another person's perspective, even when it's different from your own, is one of the most valuable skills a reader — and a person — can build.

🧒 Zoe's Perspective

Zoe checked the clock for the fifth time. Only forty minutes in, and Milo had already asked for a snack twice, spilled juice on the counter, and demanded to know exactly why he couldn't have the WHOLE bag of pretzels to himself.

"I have to be fair," Zoe explained, dividing the pretzels into three even piles — one for her, one for Milo, and one for his dinosaur, because apparently that mattered today. Her shoulders felt tight. Being in charge was a lot more work than she'd expected, and there were still three more hours before Mom and Dad got home.

She glanced at Milo, who was now lining up his pretzels by size instead of eating them. Zoe sighed and reminded herself: this was supposed to be practice for being responsible. It didn't feel like practice. It felt like a test she hadn't studied for.

👀 Same Moment, Different Perspective

👁

Think About This

Two characters can experience the EXACT same event and feel completely different things — and both feelings can be real and valid at the same time. Zoe felt stressed about being fair and responsible. What do you think Milo, age 5, might have felt about that same pretzel moment?

🤝

Predict Before You Read

Before turning the page, think: Milo is 5 years old, wanted the whole bag, and ended up lining up his pretzels by size instead of eating them. What might HIS perspective on this same moment be?

🧸 Milo's Perspective

Milo loved pretzel time. Zoe always counted them out SO carefully, like it was a very important job. He watched her split them into three piles, which was silly because his dinosaur, Rex, didn't actually eat pretzels — but Milo liked pretending Rex got a turn too.

He lined his pretzels up from biggest to smallest before eating them, because that's what you were supposed to do, obviously. Milo didn't notice Zoe checking the clock or sighing. He was just having a really good afternoon with his sister, who was letting him be in charge of deciding whether Rex's pretzels should be "eaten" by Milo or saved "for later" (also by Milo).

"Is it fair?" Milo asked again, mostly because he liked hearing Zoe explain the counting out loud. He already knew the answer. He just liked the game.
