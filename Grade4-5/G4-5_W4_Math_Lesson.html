💵 Why Money Uses Decimals

Nonfiction Connection
📖

Real Talk: Why Decimals Matter

Every price tag, every receipt, every bank balance uses decimals — because money is measured down to the cent, which is a hundredth of a dollar. Understanding decimals means understanding exactly how much money you actually have.

🍋

Priya & Jordan's Ledger

Their notebook is full of numbers like $1.25 and $0.75. Before they can figure out if they've hit their savings goal, they need to actually understand what those numbers mean.

🔢 What a Decimal Really Means

👁

Think About This

A decimal is a way to write a part of a whole using place value, just like fractions — but based on TENS. The first digit after the decimal point is the TENTHS place. The second digit is the HUNDREDTHS place.

Picture It: $1.25

1 whole dollar, 2 tenths of a dollar (20 cents), and 5 hundredths of a dollar (5 cents) — 1 dollar, 2 dimes, and a nickel, all together = $1.25.

⚖️ Comparing Decimals

👁

Think About This

To compare decimals, look at each place value LEFT to RIGHT — ones first, then tenths, then hundredths — just like comparing whole numbers. A common mistake is to think MORE digits always means BIGGER, but that's not true for decimals.

Priya's Cup
$0.75
Jordan's Cup
$0.8
🧩

Picture It: 0.75 vs. 0.8

0.8 is the SAME as 0.80 — and 80 is more than 75, so $0.80 is actually MORE than $0.75, even though 0.8 has fewer digits!
