📰 Nonfiction: What Is a Food Truck, Really?

Nonfiction Passage

Food trucks aren't a new idea — mobile kitchens have served workers and travelers for over a century. But in the last twenty years, food trucks have become their own kind of small business, giving owners a way to start a restaurant without the huge cost of renting a building.

Running a food truck comes with real challenges. Owners have to plan their food supply carefully, since trucks have limited space to store ingredients. They also depend heavily on foot traffic — the number of people walking by — which is why festivals, fairs, and busy street corners are so valuable to them. A single unexpectedly large event, like a festival, can be either the best day of the year for a food truck owner, or a stressful scramble if they didn't plan enough supplies.

📖 Vocabulary: foot traffic

The number of people walking past a business. More foot traffic usually means more potential customers.

🌟 Vocabulary Round-Up

Flip each card — these are the reading tools you'll use for the rest of today's lesson.

conflict
The main problem a character faces in a story.
resolution
How the main problem finally gets solved or settled.
infer
To figure something out using clues, even though the text doesn't say it directly.
perspective
The particular way one character sees or feels about what's happening.
theme
The deeper lesson or message a story teaches — not just what happens, but what it means.
evidence
Specific details or quotes from the text that support an answer or an idea.

🌮 The Line Around the Block, Part 1

Mia had checked the prep list four times. Two hundred tacos, sliced limes, extra hot sauce — everything for what she figured would be a good, steady Saturday. Her uncle had let her run the numbers herself this year, and she was proud of that.

By nine in the morning, the festival grounds were already busier than she'd ever seen. By ten, there was a line. Not a short one — a line that curled around the corner of Fifth Street and kept growing. Mia's stomach tightened as she watched more people join the back of it every few minutes.

"Uncle Theo," she said quietly, "I don't think two hundred tacos is going to be enough."

🔍 How Inference Really Works

👁

Think About This

Authors don't always tell you exactly how a character feels. Instead, they show you through actions, physical reactions, and small details — and it's YOUR job as the reader to connect those clues into a conclusion. That's inference: clue + your own knowledge = a smart guess.

🧩

Try It: "Mia's hands moved faster than she meant them to."

Clue: her hands are moving fast, without her even meaning to. What you know: people's bodies often speed up without thinking when they feel rushed or anxious. Inference: Mia is feeling the pressure of the growing line, even though the text never says "Mia felt stressed."

🌮 The Line Around the Block, Part 2

Uncle Theo didn't answer right away. He was watching the line too, doing his own math in his head. Finally, he wiped his hands on his apron and looked at Mia.

"You did the math this morning. You tell me — what do we do?"

Mia's hands moved faster than she meant them to as she reached for her notebook. She could either keep serving at the normal pace and risk running out with half the line still waiting, or she could call the backup supplier her uncle always kept as an emergency contact — the one he'd told her was "for real emergencies only." She chewed her lip. This felt like exactly that.

"Call the backup supplier," she said. "I'll recheck the numbers while we wait."

🌮 The Line Around the Block, Part 3

The backup order arrived forty-five minutes later — more tortillas, more meat, just enough to get them through. Mia's recalculated numbers turned out close to right after all; they'd finish the day with only a few tacos to spare, not the disaster she'd feared at ten a.m.

By the time the line finally shrank to nothing, the sun was low and Mia's arms ached from serving all day. Her uncle handed her a bottle of water and sat down on the truck's back step beside her.

"You almost panicked back there," he said. It wasn't a question.

"A little," Mia admitted.

"But you didn't. You checked the numbers instead of guessing." He nudged her shoulder. "That's the whole job, really."

💡 Finding the Theme

👁

Think About This

The PLOT is what happens (Mia almost ran out of food, called a backup supplier, and made it through). The THEME is the deeper lesson underneath the plot — something true about life, not just about tacos.

🤝

Look at the Last Line Again

"You checked the numbers instead of guessing. That's the whole job, really." Uncle Theo isn't just talking about food trucks — he's naming the lesson of the whole story.
