⚠️ The Trail Sign Is Missing

Theo’s First Hike (continued): Near the top of the climb, the trail suddenly split into two paths, and the wooden sign that should have pointed the way was gone — knocked over by wind, half-buried in pine needles at Theo’s feet. His stomach tightened. To the left, the path curved into thick, shadowy trees. To the right, it opened onto bare rock. He had no idea which way led to Lookout Peak, and for a moment, he thought about turning around and heading back down.

Every good story has a conflict — a problem the character has to face. Conflict is what makes a story worth reading. Without a problem, a story is just a list of things that happened.

👁 Watch Me Think

When I read this, I ask: what's Theo's PROBLEM? The sign is gone, and there are two paths — he doesn't know which one is right. That's the conflict. I also notice HOW the author shows me it's a problem, not just an event: "his stomach tightened." That's a physical clue for worry, the same way shaky hands meant nervous last week.

🧩 Picture It: The Fork in the Trail

No sign. No clue which way is correct. THAT gap is the conflict.

🧠 Theo Tries to Solve It

Theo’s First Hike (continued): Theo took a breath and thought about what to do. He could guess and hope he picked right. He could turn back and give up on seeing the valley. Or he could try something else. He remembered the old brass compass clipped to his belt — the one his father had given him that morning. He unclipped it, held it flat and steady in his palm, and watched the needle settle, pointing firmly north. Even the wind seemed to go still as he waited. Then he pulled out the folded trail map from his backpack and matched it against the compass. Lookout Peak, the map showed, was almost due north from where he stood.

When a character faces a conflict, they usually make an attempt to solve it — a try. Sometimes the first attempt works. Sometimes it doesn’t, and the character has to try again. Watching HOW a character solves a problem tells us a lot about them.

👁 Watch Me Think

Theo has THREE choices here: guess, give up, or think of something else. I notice he doesn't pick the easy one (guessing) or the safe one (giving up) — he remembers a TOOL he already has, the compass, and uses it on purpose. That's not luck. That's Theo using something he was given at the start of the story to solve a NEW problem later.

🧩 Picture It: Theo's Three Choices

CHOICE 1: Guess → risky
CHOICE 2: Turn back → gives up on his goal
CHOICE 3: Use the compass + map → the ATTEMPT he picks

Good readers notice which choice a character makes, and why.

🤝 Let’s Try Together

Back to Mia and the mixed-up lunchbox: her brother hates trading, so what could Mia TRY? She could hide the mistake and hope he doesn't notice. She could trade a snack from her own lunch instead of the whole thing. Or she could just tell him the truth and apologize. Just like Theo, Mia has more than one option — the story isn't over until she picks one and we see what happens.

🏆 The Resolution

Theo’s First Hike (continued): According to the compass, north pointed straight through the open rock path — not the shadowy trees. Theo clipped the compass back onto his belt, took one more breath, and turned right, toward the sunlight. Fifteen minutes later, the trees thinned out completely, and there it was: the whole valley, spread out below him just like his grandfather always said it would be, mist rising gold in the early sun. Theo grinned. He hadn’t just followed a trail today — he’d solved his first real problem, all on his own.

The resolution is how a story's problem gets fixed. Theo's CONFLICT (missing sign) led to an ATTEMPT (the compass), which led to a RESOLUTION (he picked the right path and reached the peak). That chain — problem, try, result — is called cause and effect: one thing causes the next.

👁 Watch Me Think

I trace the whole chain: conflict (no sign, two paths) CAUSES Theo to use his compass. Using the compass CAUSES him to know north is through the rock path. Choosing that path CAUSES him to reach the peak. Every step is a cause for the next effect — that's what makes a plot feel connected instead of random.

🧩 Picture It: The Full Chain

CONFLICT (missing sign) → ATTEMPT (uses compass) → RESOLUTION (reaches Lookout Peak)

Every resolution traces back to the choice the character made during their attempt.

🤝 Let’s Try Together

Mia's resolution: she decides to tell her brother the truth right away, and offers to trade her own snack instead. He's annoyed for a second — but agrees, since she was honest. CONFLICT (wrong lunch) → ATTEMPT (tells the truth, offers to trade) → RESOLUTION (he agrees, problem solved). Same shape as Theo's story, totally different problem.
