🦋 The Butterfly Life Cycle

Every living thing has a life cycle. A butterfly's life cycle has four stages. First comes the egg. Next is the larva, or caterpillar. Then comes the pupa, inside a chrysalis. Last is the adult butterfly. This big change is called metamorphosis.

👁 Watch Me Think

I see a tiny egg on a leaf. A caterpillar hatches. It eats and eats and grows. Then it makes a hard case called a chrysalis. Inside, its body changes. Out comes a butterfly! The butterfly lays eggs. The cycle starts again. That is why we call it a cycle.

🧩 Picture It: Four Stages

Egg (tiny, on a leaf) → Larva (caterpillar, eats a lot) → Pupa (chrysalis, body changes) → Adult (butterfly, lays eggs) → back to Egg

🤝 Let's Try Together: Which Stage Eats the Most?

Let's think. The egg does not eat. The pupa does not eat. The adult sips nectar from flowers. The larva eats leaves all day long! Eating and growing is the caterpillar's main job.

🐸 Frogs and Grasshoppers

Frogs go through metamorphosis too. A frog starts as an egg in water. A tadpole hatches. It has a tail and breathes with gills. It grows legs and its tail shrinks. Then it becomes an adult frog with lungs.

👁 Watch Me Think

Some insects change in a smaller way. A grasshopper hatches from an egg as a nymph. A nymph looks like a tiny grasshopper with no wings. It grows and gets wings. There is no pupa stage. So a grasshopper has three stages: egg, nymph, adult.

🧩 Picture It: Two Life Cycles

FrogGrasshopper
egg (in water)egg (in soil)
tadpole: tail, gillsnymph: no wings yet
froglet: legs, short tailadult: has wings
adult frog: lungs, no tail

🤝 Let's Try Together: Tadpole or Frog?

Let's compare. A tadpole lives only in water. A frog can live on land and in water. A tadpole breathes with gills. A frog breathes with lungs. A tadpole has a tail. An adult frog has no tail. Same animal, very different body!

🌱 Plants and Other Life Cycles

Plants have life cycles too. A seed sprouts into a seedling. The seedling grows into an adult plant. The plant makes flowers. Flowers make new seeds. Some animals, like dogs and people, have no metamorphosis. Their babies look like small adults.

👁 Watch Me Think

I plant a bean seed. I give it water. A few days later, a tiny root and stem pop out. That is a seedling. It grows tall and makes flowers. The flowers turn into bean pods. Inside are new seeds. The cycle can start again!

🧩 Picture It: A Bean Plant's Cycle

Seed → Seedling (sprout) → Adult plant → Flowers → Pods with new seeds → back to Seed

🤝 Let's Try Together: Puppy or Tadpole?

A puppy is born looking like a small dog. It has four legs, fur, and a tail. It just gets bigger. A tadpole looks nothing like a frog. So the frog goes through metamorphosis. The dog does not.

🎨 Scientists Who Watched Things Grow

Long ago, many people believed insects came from mud or rotting things. These scientists used careful watching to learn the truth.

Maria Sibylla Merian

1647–1717 · Germany and the Netherlands

Maria raised caterpillars herself. She watched them eat, make a pupa, and turn into butterflies and moths. She painted every stage, with the plant each one ate. Her pictures showed insects come from eggs, not mud. In 1699, she sailed to Suriname in South America to study insects there.

Jean-Henri Fabre

1823–1915 · France

Jean-Henri was a teacher in France. He watched insects in his garden for many years. He wrote books about how beetles, wasps, and crickets live and grow. He studied them alive, not just in a box.

Tyrone Hayes

born 1967 · United States

Dr. Hayes is a biologist who studies frogs. He tests how chemicals in water can change the way tadpoles grow into frogs. His work helps us protect frogs and clean water.
