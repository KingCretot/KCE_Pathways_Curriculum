🏢 Meet the Mayor

Every community needs a leader — someone who guides the group and helps make decisions for everyone's good. A city or town's leader is called the mayor.

👁 Watch Me Think

A mayor is a lot like a team captain, but for a whole town. A team captain helps decide plays and keeps the team working together. A mayor helps decide things like where a new park should go, or how to keep streets safe — and works with the community's helpers (police, firefighters, teachers) to get it all done.

🧩 Picture It: Who Leads What?

A TEAM → led by a CAPTAIN
A CLASSROOM → led by a TEACHER
A CITY OR TOWN → led by a MAYOR

Every group, big or small, usually has someone guiding it.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Think about your class president or student council, if your school has one. What do they do? They represent the other students and help make decisions for the class. A mayor does the same thing, just for a whole town instead of one classroom.

📜 What Does Government Do?

The government of a town isn't just the mayor — it's a whole group of people who work together to make laws and provide services. A law is different from a classroom rule: it applies to EVERYONE in the community, not just one group.

👁 Watch Me Think

Let's trace a law from start to finish. The government decides: "no littering in the park." Why? So the park stays clean for everyone who visits, not just today, but every day. That law didn't just appear — the government made it ON PURPOSE, to solve a real problem the whole community shares.

🧩 Picture It: Rule vs. Law

CLASSROOM RULE: "Raise your hand before speaking" — only applies in ONE classroom
COMMUNITY LAW: "Stop at red lights" — applies to EVERYONE in the whole town

Same idea (a guideline to follow), but a law covers a much bigger group of people.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Government also PROVIDES SERVICES, not just laws. Who pays for the fire department to have trucks and equipment? The government. Who funds public schools and parks? The government too. Laws AND services — that's the government's whole job, working for the community.

🗳️ Voting for Leaders

How does a community choose its mayor? They vote. Every person's vote gets counted, and whoever gets the MOST votes wins.

👁 Watch Me Think

Imagine 5 hikers can't agree which trail to take: North Loop or Lake Trail. They each vote. 3 pick Lake Trail, 2 pick North Loop. Lake Trail has more votes, so that's the trail they take — even the 2 who wanted North Loop agree to go along, because that's how voting works. A whole town choosing a mayor works exactly the same way, just with way more people voting.

🧩 Picture It: Counting Votes

5 HIKERS VOTE: Lake Trail ★★★ (3) vs. North Loop ★★ (2)
MOST STARS WINS → Lake Trail

A town election works the same way, just with thousands of votes instead of 5.

🤝 Let's Try Together: A Class Election

Your class votes for class president. 12 students vote for Maria. 9 students vote for Sam. Who wins? Maria — 12 is more than 9. Every vote counted, most votes wins. That's the exact same idea a whole town uses to pick its mayor.
