🌊 Most of Earth's Water Is Salty

Water covers most of Earth. Most of that water is in oceans. Earth has five oceans. The Pacific Ocean is the biggest. Ocean water is salt water. People cannot drink it. It would make them sick.

👁 Watch Me Think

🏞️ Fresh Water: Rivers, Lakes, and Ice

Fresh water has very little salt. It is in rivers, lakes, ponds, and ice. Some is hidden underground. Fresh water is only a small part of Earth's water. Most fresh water is frozen in ice and glaciers. People need fresh water to drink, cook, and grow food.

👁 Watch Me Think

Where can I get fresh water to drink? The ocean is salty, so not there. Glaciers are fresh, but they are frozen and far away. Rivers, lakes, and water underground are fresh and liquid. So those are where most people get drinking water.

🧩 Picture It: Where Is Fresh Water?

PlaceWhat it is like
Riverfresh water that flows downhill, often to the ocean
Lakefresh water that sits in a low place with land all around
Glacierfrozen fresh water in a giant sheet of ice
Undergroundfresh water in spaces between rocks and soil

Florida example: Most people in Florida drink water that is pumped up from underground.

🌧️ A First Look at the Water Cycle

Water is always moving. The Sun heats water in oceans, lakes, and puddles. Some water turns into a gas called water vapor. This is evaporation. High up, the vapor cools into tiny drops. The drops make clouds. When drops get big and heavy, they fall as rain or snow.

👁 Watch Me Think

After a rainy day, I see a puddle. The next sunny afternoon, it is gone. Where did it go? The Sun heated it. The water turned into water vapor and went up into the air. That is evaporation. Later, that water can become part of a cloud.

🧩 Picture It: The Water Cycle Loop

1. Sun heats water → 2. Evaporation: water turns into water vapor and rises → 3. Clouds: vapor cools into tiny drops → 4. Rain or snow falls → 5. Water collects in oceans, lakes, rivers, and underground → back to 1

The same water goes around and around. The water in your glass may once have been in a cloud!

🤝 Let's Try Together: Let's Try One Together

Put these in order: rain falls, clouds form, the Sun heats a lake. The Sun heating the water comes first. Then water evaporates and rises. It cools and makes clouds. Then rain falls. So: Sun heats a lake → clouds form → rain falls.

🐋 Explorers of the Ocean

These people studied and explored the ocean in different ways. One dove deep. One mapped the sea floor. One read the waves and stars.

Sylvia Earle

born 1935

An ocean scientist and explorer. She has spent thousands of hours underwater. In 1970, she led a team of women scientists who lived in an underwater lab for two weeks. In 1979, she set a record for the deepest walk on the ocean floor. She wore a special diving suit near Hawaiʻi. People call her "Her Deepness." Today she works to protect special ocean places she calls Hope Spots.

Marie Tharp

1920–2006

A scientist and mapmaker. At first, women were not allowed on the research ships. So she worked with depth numbers the ships collected. With her partner Bruce Heezen, she made the first map of the whole ocean floor. It showed giant underwater mountain ranges. The sea floor was not flat!

Mau Piailug

1932–2010

A master navigator from the island of Satawal in Micronesia. He found his way across the ocean without maps or a compass. He read the stars, the waves, and the birds. In 1976, he guided the Hawaiian canoe Hōkūleʻa from Hawaiʻi to Tahiti. He taught these old skills to new navigators.
