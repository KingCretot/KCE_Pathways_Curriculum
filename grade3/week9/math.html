🛠️ Two-Step Word Problems

Mia had 40 stickers to hand out as rewards. She gave 5 to each of 3 kids who helped carry crates. How many stickers does she have left? This problem needs TWO operations: first multiply to find how many were given away, then subtract from the total.

👁 Watch Me Think

In a two-step problem, I solve ONE small piece at a time. First: 5 × 3 = 15 stickers given away. Second: 40 − 15 = 25 stickers left. I never try to do both steps in my head at once — I write down the answer to step one, then use it for step two.

🧩 Picture It: Step One, Then Step Two

Step 1: 5 × 3 = 15 (given away)
Step 2: 40 − 15 = 25 (left)

🤝 Let's Try Together

A crate had 6 boxes of 4 canteens each. 9 canteens were sold. Step 1: 6 × 4 = 24 total. Step 2: 24 − 9 = 15 left.

🏆 Choosing a Strategy

By opening day, Theo and Mia had a strategy for ANY word problem: read carefully, hunt for clue words, decide which operation (or operations) to use, solve step by step, then ask “does this answer make sense?”

👁 Watch Me Think

My strategy every time: 1) Read the whole problem first. 2) Underline or notice the clue words. 3) Decide the operation(s). 4) Solve carefully, one step at a time. 5) Check — is my answer a reasonable size for this problem? A strategy is just a repeatable plan I trust.

🧩 Picture It: The 5-Step Strategy

1. Read carefully
2. Find clue words
3. Decide the operation(s)
4. Solve step by step
5. Check: does it make sense?

🤝 Let's Try Together

If a problem asks for a total after buying MANY of the same item, and the answer comes out smaller than the price of ONE item, that answer probably doesn't make sense — time to check your work!
