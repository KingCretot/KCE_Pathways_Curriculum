📊 Math Review: Picture Graphs and Bar Graphs

🔁 Remember This? (Week 21)

At Pike Place Market in Seattle, Ms. Akana’s helpers read picture graphs and bar graphs. On a picture graph, read the key first. If each picture stands for 10, then 3 pictures show 30, and half a picture shows 5.

On a bar graph, read the scale. A bar that stops halfway between 40 and 50 shows 45. To find how many more or how many fewer, subtract the smaller number from the bigger number.

🔷 Math Review: Quadrilaterals and Equal Parts

🔁 Remember This? (Week 23)

In Ms. Fresquez’s tile workshop in Santa Fe, we sorted quadrilaterals: flat, closed shapes with 4 straight sides. A rectangle has 4 right angles. A rhombus has 4 equal sides. A square has both, so it is a rectangle AND a rhombus.

When a shape is split into parts with equal area, each part is a unit fraction of the whole. The bottom number tells how many equal parts make the whole. The parts must be the same size, but they do not have to be the same shape.

🧮 Math Review: Two-Step Problems and Rounding

🔁 Remember This? (Week 24)

At the barbecue festival in Kansas City, we solved two-step problems. Find the hidden question first, then use that answer to finish. A letter, like n, stands for the unknown number. When an equation has × or ÷ and also + or −, do the × or ÷ part first.

To check an answer, round. To round to the nearest hundred, look at the tens digit: 462 rounds to 500, because the tens digit is 6. If your answer is close to your estimate, it is reasonable.

🦦 Reading Review: Main Idea and Key Details

🔁 Remember This? (Week 21)

In Monterey, California, Itzel and Joaquin read a booklet about sea otters and kelp. The main idea is the most important point of a paragraph. It can be the first sentence, a middle sentence, the last sentence, or not stated at all.

Test its size. Too big could fit a whole book. Too small is just one detail. Just right covers all the details. Key details are the facts that prove the main idea.

👀 Reading Review: Point of View

🔁 Remember This? (Week 23)

On a Louisiana bayou, we met Mireille and her cousin Nathaniel. The narrator is the voice that tells the story. In first person, the narrator is a character and says I, me, and my. In third person, the narrator is outside the story and uses he, she, and names.

Skip the words inside quotation marks, because those are characters talking. A point of view is how someone thinks or feels. Readers have their own point of view, with a reason after because.

⚾ Reading Review: Biography, Sequence, and Cause and Effect

🔁 Remember This? (Week 24)

In Pittsburgh, Perla read a biography of Roberto Clemente. A biography is the true story of a real person’s life, written by someone else in third person. An autobiography is a person’s own life story, told with I and me.

Dates are the strongest sequence clues. If two events happen in the same year, check the month. A cause is why something happens, and an effect is what happens. Because comes before a cause, and so comes before an effect.

💪 YOUR TURN
Read this biography

Mae Jemison: Reaching for the Stars

Mae Jemison was born on October 17, 1956, in Decatur, Alabama. When she was a young child, her family moved to Chicago, Illinois, so Mae grew up there. She loved science, and she was curious about the stars.

In 1973, when she was 16 years old, Mae started college at Stanford University in California. Later, she became a doctor. In 1987, NASA chose her to be an astronaut, so she began years of training. On September 12, 1992, she flew into space on the space shuttle Endeavour. She was the first African American woman to travel in space.
