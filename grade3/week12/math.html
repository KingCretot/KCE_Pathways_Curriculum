🥐 Picture It: A Morning at a Chicago Bakery

This week we visit the Nowak Family Bakery in Chicago, Illinois. Lena Nowak and her friend Kofi Mensah help Lena's grandmother every Saturday morning. Lena calls her Babcia, the Polish word for grandma. Theo and Mia, from last week's orange grove, are in Chicago visiting their cousin Jada, and they stopped by the bakery too!

Babcia and the bread baker, Mr. Jamal Brooks, bake big batches of rolls, muffins, and paczki. Then everyone shares them into boxes and trays with the same number in each one. Diego Flores rides the delivery bike to bring the boxes to customers.

Sharing a total into equal groups is called division. This bakery runs on division!

🏙️ Chicago Bakery Facts

  • Chicago is a big city in Illinois, on the shore of Lake Michigan.
  • Chicago's trains are called the L, which is short for elevated, because many of the tracks run high above the streets.
  • Downtown, the L tracks make a big loop around the part of the city called the Loop.
  • Many Polish American families live in Chicago. Polish bakeries there sell paczki (say POONCH-kee), round doughnuts filled with jam or custard.
  • Bakers start work very early, often before the sun comes up, so the bread is fresh for breakfast.

Today you will learn to divide by 2 through 9, use fact families to connect multiplication and division, and find the unknown number in a fact.

🌟 Word Wrangler

Flip each card to learn this week's vocabulary! Tap the speaker to hear the word or the meaning.

divide
To split a total into equal groups. 24 rolls in 4 boxes is 6 rolls in each box.
dividend
The total you start with and split up. In 24 ÷ 4 = 6, the dividend is 24.
divisor
The number you divide by. In 24 ÷ 4 = 6, the divisor is 4.
quotient
The answer to a division problem. In 24 ÷ 4 = 6, the quotient is 6.
fact family
Facts that use the same three numbers: 4 × 6 = 24, 6 × 4 = 24, 24 ÷ 4 = 6, 24 ÷ 6 = 4.
unknown
A missing number in a fact. In 5 × ? = 30, the unknown number is 6.

Grown-up math books say multiplication and division are inverse operations. That means one undoes the other!

📦 Sharing Into Equal Boxes

Babcia baked 24 poppy seed rolls. She wants to pack them into 4 boxes with the same number in each box. Lena puts one roll in each box, again and again, until all 24 rolls are packed. Each box gets 6 rolls. We write this as 24 ÷ 4 = 6.

👁 Watch Me Think

Division answers two kinds of questions. Sometimes I know how many groups there are, and I find how many go in each group: 24 rolls in 4 equal boxes is 6 rolls in each box. Other times I know how many go in each group, and I find how many groups: 24 rolls with 6 in each box fills 4 boxes, so 24 ÷ 6 = 4.

🧩 Picture It

24 ÷ 4 = 6
dividend ÷ divisor = quotient
We read 24 ÷ 4 = 6 out loud as “24 divided by 4 equals 6.”

🏠 Fact Families: One Family, Four Facts

Mr. Brooks lined up dinner rolls on a tray in 4 rows of 7. Kofi said, “4 × 7 = 28 rolls!” Lena turned the tray and said, “7 × 4 = 28!” Then Babcia asked two division questions. If 28 rolls go into 4 bags, how many in each bag? 28 ÷ 4 = 7. If each bag holds 7 rolls, how many bags? 28 ÷ 7 = 4.

👁 Watch Me Think

A fact family is a group of four facts that all use the same three numbers. The biggest number is the product when I multiply, and it is the number I start with when I divide. If I know one fact, I know all four!

🧩 Picture It

4 × 7 = 28
7 × 4 = 28
28 ÷ 4 = 7
28 ÷ 7 = 4

🧠 Think Multiplication: Dividing by 6, 7, 8, and 9

Diego has 48 paczki to deliver. Each box holds 6 paczki. How many boxes does he need? Kofi thinks, “6 times what number equals 48?” He knows 6 × 8 = 48 from last week, so 48 ÷ 6 = 8. Diego needs 8 boxes!

👁 Watch Me Think

To divide, I turn the problem into a multiplication question. For 63 ÷ 9, I ask: 9 times what number equals 63? I know 9 × 7 = 63, so 63 ÷ 9 = 7. My multiplication facts are the key to division!

🧩 Picture It

48 ÷ 6 = ?
Think: 6 × ? = 48
6 × 8 = 48
So 48 ÷ 6 = 8
Two quick rules: any number divided by 1 is that same number, so 8 ÷ 1 = 8. Any number except 0 divided by itself is 1, so 8 ÷ 8 = 1.

🔎 Mystery Numbers: Finding the Unknown

Lena found a sticky note on the order board: 32 muffins in 8 boxes. How many in each box? She wrote it as a fact with an unknown number: 8 × ? = 32. To find the unknown, she thought about her 8s: 8 × 4 = 32. So each box holds 4 muffins!

👁 Watch Me Think

An unknown is a mystery number in a fact. I use the fact family to find it. If the unknown is a factor, like 7 × ? = 35, I divide: 35 ÷ 7 = 5. If the unknown is the number I start with, like ? ÷ 6 = 5, I multiply: 6 × 5 = 30. Then I check my answer by putting it back into the fact.

🧩 Picture It

8 × ? = 32. Think: 8 × 4 = 32. The unknown is 4.
? ÷ 6 = 5. Think: 6 × 5 = 30. The unknown is 30.
45 ÷ ? = 5. Think: 5 × 9 = 45. The unknown is 9.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Try 9 × ? = 36. Count by 9s: 9, 18, 27, 36. That is 4 nines, so the unknown is 4. Check: 9 × 4 = 36.
