🛒 Pushes and Pulls Are Forces

A force is a push or a pull. A push moves something away from you. A pull moves something toward you. Forces make still things start moving. Forces also make moving things stop. Gravity is a pull, too. It pulls things down toward Earth.

👁 Watch Me Think

A ball sits still on the grass. Will it ever roll by itself? No. Something must push or pull it. I kick it, and it rolls away. My kick was a push, a force. Then my friend stops it with her foot. Her foot was a force, too.

⚽ Forces Change Speed and Direction

Forces can change how fast something moves. That is its speed. A bigger push makes a ball go faster and farther. Forces can also change the way something moves. That is its direction. Rubbing on the ground, called friction, slowly stops a rolling ball.

👁 Watch Me Think

I roll a ball gently. It goes slow and stops soon. I roll it hard. It goes fast and far. So a bigger push gives more speed. Then my friend kicks it from the side. It turns! So a push from the side changes its direction.

🧲 Magnets Pull Without Touching

A magnet can push or pull without touching! Magnets attract, or pull on, things made of iron and steel. They do not pull on wood, plastic, paper, or most other metals. Every magnet has a north end and a south end. A north end and a south end pull together. Two north ends push apart.

👁 Watch Me Think

Will a magnet pick up a soda can? It is metal, so I guess yes. But I test it, and nothing happens! Soda cans are made of aluminum, not iron or steel. So not every metal sticks to a magnet. Testing beats guessing.

🧩 Picture It: Magnet Test Table

ObjectMade ofDoes the magnet attract it?

Poles: north + south = pull together (attract). north + north or south + south = push apart (repel).

🤝 Let's Try Together: Let's Try One Together

Put a paper clip on top of a sheet of paper. Hold a magnet under the paper. Move the magnet. The clip moves too! The magnet's pull went through the paper. The magnet never touched the clip. Now try two magnets with the north ends facing. They push apart. Turn one around. They snap together.

🚀 People Who Explained and Used Forces

These people explained how things move, used forces in space, found a way to use magnets, and made machines run smoothly.

Isaac Newton

1643–1727

An English scientist who wrote rules about motion. One rule says a still thing stays still, and a moving thing keeps moving, until a force acts on it. He also explained gravity. The same pull that makes an apple fall keeps the Moon going around Earth. He shared his ideas in a famous book in 1687.

Ellen Ochoa

born 1958

An engineer and astronaut from California. In 1993, she became the first Hispanic woman to go to space. She flew in space four times. She used the space shuttle's long robot arm to let go of and then catch a science satellite. In space, things float, so a tiny push sends them drifting! Later she led NASA's Johnson Space Center in Houston.

Shen Kuo

1031–1095

A scientist in China about 1,000 years ago. He wrote about a magnet needle that points north and south. That is a compass! Later, sailors used compasses like this to find their way. He also noticed the needle did not point exactly north.

Elijah McCoy

about 1844–1929

An inventor born in Canada. His parents had escaped slavery in Kentucky. As a teen, he trained as an engineer in Scotland. Later he worked on trains in Michigan. Moving train parts rubbed and got hot. In 1872, he invented an oil cup that oiled the engine while the train kept moving. Less rubbing meant less friction.
