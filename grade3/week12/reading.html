🖼️ Picture It: The Text Feature Toolbox

Nonfiction books have special tools called text features. Text features help you find information fast and understand it better. Look at this page from a nonfiction booklet. Each gold tag names one text feature.

📕TitleNames the whole book and tells what it is about.
📋Table of contentsNear the front. Lists each section and its page.
🔖HeadingNames one section and tells what it is mostly about.
🅱️Bold wordDark, thick letters say: this word is important!
📖GlossaryAt the back. Gives word meanings in ABC order.
🖼️Picture and captionThe caption tells what the picture shows.
🔧Diagram and labelsLabels name the parts in the drawing.

🚦 Signs for Readers

Think of text features like the street signs in a big city such as Chicago. Signs help you find your way without walking down every street. Text features help you find the information you need without reading every single word.

Bread While You Sleep

Chicago, Illinois, is a big city on the shore of Lake Michigan. At midnight, most people in the city are asleep. But at Sunrise Bakery, the lights are on. Bakers work all night so that fresh bread is ready by morning. By 6 o'clock, warm loaves are on their way to shops, cafes, and restaurants around the city.

Page 2

Flour Arrives

Bread starts with flour. Flour is made by grinding wheat, a grain that grows on farms in states such as Kansas and North Dakota. Each week, a delivery truck brings flour to the bakery's back door. The flour comes in heavy paper sacks. Each sack weighs 50 pounds! Workers stack the sacks in a cool, dry storeroom.

Page 3

Mixing and Rising

Late in the evening, a baker measures flour, water, salt, and yeast. Yeast is a tiny living thing. A giant mixer stirs everything together into a soft, stretchy dough. Then the dough rests in big tubs. The yeast makes tiny gas bubbles, so the dough puffs up and gets bigger. This is called rising. Rising can take a few hours.

Page 4

Into the Hot Oven

Around 2 o'clock in the morning, the bakers shape the dough into loaves. Next, the loaves go into a deck oven. A deck oven has stone shelves called decks. A baker uses a long wooden paddle called a peel to slide each loaf onto the hot stone. Steam inside the oven helps the crust get crisp and golden. Look at the diagram to see the parts of a deck oven.

Page 5

The Bakery Team

It takes a team to make bread all night. Mr. Luis Herrera owns the bakery and is the head baker. He decides which breads to bake each night. Ms. Denise Carter mixes the dough and watches it rise. Mr. Sam Kim packs the warm loaves into crates. Then he drives them across the city before the sun comes up. When Chicago wakes up, the bakery team goes home to sleep!

Page 6

Glossary

crust

dough
a soft, thick mix of flour and water that is baked into bread
peel
a long, flat paddle used to move bread in and out of an oven
rising
when dough puffs up and gets bigger because of gas bubbles from yeast
yeast
a tiny living thing that makes gas bubbles in dough

📋 Title, Table of Contents & Headings

The title names the whole book. The table of contents is a list near the front of a book. It shows the name of each section and the page where it starts. A heading is a title for one section. It tells what that section is mostly about.

👁 Watch Me Think

I wonder when the warm bread leaves the bakery. I look at the table of contents. Bread While You Sleep is on page 1, and it sounds like it tells about the bakery at night. I turn to page 1, read the heading, and find my answer: by 6 o'clock, warm loaves are on their way!

🅱️ Bold Words & the Glossary

Authors print important words in bold, dark letters. A bold word tells you to slow down because the word is important. Many bold words are listed in the glossary at the back of the book. A glossary is like a small dictionary. Its words are in ABC order, and each word has its meaning.

👁 Watch Me Think

On page 3, I see the bold word yeast. I am not sure what it means. I flip to the glossary at the back. The words are in ABC order, so I look near the end for Y. It says yeast is a tiny living thing that makes gas bubbles in dough. Now page 3 makes sense!

🖼️ Captions, Diagrams & Labels

Pictures in nonfiction teach, too! A caption is the words under or next to a picture. It tells what the picture shows. A diagram is a drawing that shows the parts of something. Labels are words with lines that point to each part and name it.

👁 Watch Me Think

Imagine a book about pets with a picture of a puppy. The caption says: Max, a beagle, needs a walk every day. The picture shows me a puppy, but the caption tells me his name, what kind of dog he is, and what he needs. The caption adds information!

🧰 Choose the Right Text Feature

Good readers pick the right text feature for the job. To find a section, use the table of contents. To learn what a section is about, read its heading. To find what a bold word means, use the glossary. To learn what a picture shows, read the caption. To learn the parts of something, look at a diagram and its labels.

👁 Watch Me Think

In a book about the moon, I want to read the part about astronauts. I do not read the whole book. I check the table of contents, find Astronauts, and jump right to that page. That is reading like a detective!

⭐ Word Work Bonus: ABC Order

Bonus Practice • builds XP, not Pathway Points

Glossary words are in ABC order, also called alphabetical order. To put words in ABC order, look at the first letter of each word. The word whose first letter comes first in the alphabet goes first. The booklet's glossary goes: crust, dough, peel, rising, yeast.

Bread While You Sleep

Chicago, Illinois, is a big city on the shore of Lake Michigan. At midnight, most people in the city are asleep. But at Sunrise Bakery, the lights are on. Bakers work all night so that fresh bread is ready by morning. By 6 o'clock, warm loaves are on their way to shops, cafes, and restaurants around the city.

Page 2

Flour Arrives

Bread starts with flour. Flour is made by grinding wheat, a grain that grows on farms in states such as Kansas and North Dakota. Each week, a delivery truck brings flour to the bakery's back door. The flour comes in heavy paper sacks. Each sack weighs 50 pounds! Workers stack the sacks in a cool, dry storeroom.

Page 3

Mixing and Rising

Late in the evening, a baker measures flour, water, salt, and yeast. Yeast is a tiny living thing. A giant mixer stirs everything together into a soft, stretchy dough. Then the dough rests in big tubs. The yeast makes tiny gas bubbles, so the dough puffs up and gets bigger. This is called rising. Rising can take a few hours.

Page 4

Into the Hot Oven

Around 2 o'clock in the morning, the bakers shape the dough into loaves. Next, the loaves go into a deck oven. A deck oven has stone shelves called decks. A baker uses a long wooden paddle called a peel to slide each loaf onto the hot stone. Steam inside the oven helps the crust get crisp and golden. Look at the diagram to see the parts of a deck oven.

Page 5

The Bakery Team

It takes a team to make bread all night. Mr. Luis Herrera owns the bakery and is the head baker. He decides which breads to bake each night. Ms. Denise Carter mixes the dough and watches it rise. Mr. Sam Kim packs the warm loaves into crates. Then he drives them across the city before the sun comes up. When Chicago wakes up, the bakery team goes home to sleep!
