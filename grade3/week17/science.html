trait
A feature of a living thing, like fur color or leaf shape. Example: A zebra's stripes are a trait.
inherited trait
A trait passed from parents to their young. Example: Kittens get their fur color from their parents.
offspring
The young of a plant or animal. Example: Puppies are the offspring of dogs.
learned behavior
Something a living thing learns to do by being taught or by practice. Example: A dog learns to sit when someone teaches it.
environment
Everything around a living thing, like light, water, food and weather. Example: A flamingo's food is part of its environment.
instinct
A behavior an animal is born knowing how to do. Example: Sea turtle hatchlings crawl toward the brightest direction by instinct.
variation
Differences among living things of the same kind. Example: Kittens from the same parents can have different colors.
survive
To stay alive. Example: Moths that blend in with the bark are more likely to survive.

🧬 What Is a Trait?

A trait is a feature of a living thing, like its fur color or its leaf shape. Many traits are inherited. That means they are passed from parents to their young, called offspring. Kittens get their fur color from their parents. An acorn grows into a tree with oak leaves, just like its parent. Young look like their parents, but not exactly.

👁 Watch Me Think

A black-and-white dog has puppies. One puppy is black and white. One is mostly black. All of them have four legs, floppy ears and a tail that wags. They got those traits from their parents. That is why a puppy never grows up into a kitten!

🎓 Learned, or Shaped by the Environment?

Some traits are not inherited. A dog learns to sit when someone teaches it. You learn to read and ride a bike. These are learned behaviors. The environment can change living things too. The environment is everything around a living thing: light, water, food and weather. A houseplant by a window bends toward the light. Flamingos turn pink from the food they eat.

👁 Watch Me Think

Flamingo chicks hatch with white-gray, fluffy feathers. They are not pink yet! As they grow, they eat algae and tiny brine shrimp. Colors called pigments in that food slowly turn their feathers pink. It takes several years. So a flamingo's pink color comes from its food, part of its environment.

🐢 Born Knowing How: Instincts

Some behaviors are inherited too. They are called instincts. Nobody teaches them. On Florida beaches, baby sea turtles hatch from eggs in the sand. They are born with a pull to crawl toward the brightest direction. On a natural beach at night, that is the open sky over the ocean. So they reach the water. But bright lights from buildings can trick them. They may crawl the wrong way! People help by keeping beach lights dim or covered in nesting season.

👁 Watch Me Think

A tall sunflower makes seeds. Will the new plants grow tall? They inherited the traits to grow tall. But if they get too little water and light, they may stay short. Traits come from parents, and the environment helps decide how they turn out.

🐱 Variation: No Two Exactly Alike

Living things of the same kind belong to the same species. All house cats are one species. But not all cats look the same! Some are orange, some are gray, and some have stripes. These differences are called variation. Even brothers and sisters from the same parents can look different.

👁 Watch Me Think

I plant 5 bean seeds from the same pack. They get the same soil, water and light. After three weeks, I measure them. They are not all the same height! That is variation inside one kind of plant.

🦋 When a Difference Helps Living Things Survive

Sometimes a difference helps a living thing survive, or stay alive. Long ago in England, most peppered moths were light and speckled. A few were dark. Then smoke from coal-burning factories covered the trees with black soot. Birds could easily see the light moths on the dark bark and eat them. The dark moths were hidden. More dark moths lived to have offspring, so dark moths became common. Later, when the air got cleaner, light moths became common again.

👁 Watch Me Think

Why did dark moths win near the factories? The moths did not choose to turn dark. Some moths were already dark. On sooty trees, those moths were harder for birds to see. So more of them lived and had young like them.

🌽 Scientists and Farmers Who Studied Traits

These people watched traits pass from parents to offspring, and saw how differences can help living things survive.

Gregor Mendel

1822–1884

A friar and scientist at an abbey in Brno, in what is now the Czech Republic. From 1856 to 1863, he grew and studied about 28,000 plants, most of them pea plants. He tracked traits like flower color, seed shape and plant height. He found patterns in how traits pass from parents to offspring. Few people noticed his work until 1900. Today he is called the father of modern genetics.

Barbara McClintock

1902–1992

An American scientist who studied corn. Some corn kernels had spots and streaks of color. She studied corn plants for many years. She found that some genes, the tiny instructions for traits, can move to new places. That can change kernel colors! At first, many scientists did not understand her idea. In 1983, she won the Nobel Prize. She was the first woman to win that prize in medicine without sharing it.

Peter and Rosemary Grant

began their finch study in 1973

A husband-and-wife team of scientists. They studied small birds called finches on a tiny Galápagos island called Daphne Major. In 1977, a drought left mostly big, hard seeds. Finches with bigger, stronger beaks could crack them, so more of them survived. Their offspring had bigger beaks too.

The First Corn Farmers

about 9,000 years ago

Indigenous farmers in the Balsas River Valley of what is now Mexico grew a wild grass called teosinte. It had a tiny cob with a few hard kernels. They saved seeds from the plants with the best traits and planted them. Over thousands of years, those plants became corn, also called maize. Today corn feeds people all over the world.
