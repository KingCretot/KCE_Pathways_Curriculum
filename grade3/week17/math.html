🐄 Picture It: The Long Lane at Clover Creek Dairy

It's a crisp fall morning at Clover Creek Dairy, a family farm near Madison, Wisconsin. Mrs. Halvorsen, the farmer, has a long dirt lane that runs from the red barn to the pasture gate. The lane is exactly 1 mile long.

Wen, Amara, and Tomas are helping for the day. Mrs. Halvorsen draws the lane as a number line. The red barn is at 0, and the pasture gate is at 1. These two ends are called the endpoints.

The team will set fence posts in equal parts along the lane. The water trough will sit at 34 of a mile. The denominator, 4, tells that the lane is cut into 4 equal parts. The numerator, 3, tells that the trough is 3 of those parts from the barn.

Later they will cut blocks of cheese for the farm stand. Along the way, they will find out that two different fractions can name the very same spot. Fractions like that are called equivalent fractions!

🧀 Wisconsin Farm Facts

  • Madison is the capital city of Wisconsin.
  • Wisconsin is nicknamed America's Dairyland because it has so many dairy farms.
  • Wisconsin makes more cheese than any other state in the United States.
  • Dairy cows are usually milked two or three times every day.
  • A mile is 5,280 feet. Walking a mile takes most people about 20 minutes.

Today you will place fractions on a number line, find fractions that are greater than 1, write whole numbers as fractions, and discover equivalent fractions that land on the same point!

🌟 Word Wrangler

Flip each card to learn this week's vocabulary! Each card shows the meaning and the sentence from the story where the word appears. Tap the speaker to hear the word or the meaning.

number line
A straight line with numbers placed in order at equal spaces. From the story: “Mrs. Halvorsen draws the lane as a number line.”
endpoint
A point at the end of a line segment. Here, the endpoints are 0 and 1. From the story: “These two ends are called the endpoints.”
equal parts
Parts that are exactly the same size. From the story: “The team will set fence posts in equal parts along the lane.”
numerator
The top number in a fraction. It tells how many equal parts we count. From the story: “The numerator, 3, tells that the trough is 3 of those parts from the barn.”
denominator
The bottom number in a fraction. It tells how many equal parts make one whole. From the story: “The denominator, 4, tells that the lane is cut into 4 equal parts.”
equivalent fractions
Different fractions that name the same amount, or the same point on a number line. From the story: “Fractions like that are called equivalent fractions!”

Here is a memory trick: the Denominator is Down on the bottom, and it tells how many parts the whole is Divided into!

📏 Fractions on a Number Line

Mrs. Halvorsen wants fence posts at equal spaces along the 1-mile lane. Wen splits the number line from 0 to 1 into 4 equal parts. Each part is 14 of a mile. The first post after the barn is at 14, the next is at 24, then 34, and the gate is at 44, which is 1 whole mile.

👁 Watch Me Think

To name a point, I count the equal spaces (the jumps), not the tick marks. The number of equal spaces from 0 to 1 is my denominator. The number of jumps from 0 to my point is my numerator. A line cut into 4 equal parts has 5 tick marks, but only 4 spaces!

🧩 Picture It

0 → 14 → 24 → 34 → 1
The trough (T) is 3 jumps of 14, so it is at 34 of a mile.

🤝 Let's Try Together

🚶 Past 1: Counting Jumps to the Creek

After the pasture gate, the cows keep walking 1 more mile to the creek. Now Tomas draws a number line from 0 to 2. He cuts each whole mile into 3 equal parts. He counts the jumps: 13, 23, 33. That is 1 whole mile! He keeps going: 43, 53, 63. That is 2 whole miles.

👁 Watch Me Think

Every whole on the line gets the same number of equal parts. I keep counting jumps past 1, so the numerator can be bigger than the denominator. When the numerator and denominator match, like 33, the fraction equals 1 whole. 63 is 6 thirds, which makes 2 wholes.

🧩 Picture It

33 = 1   and   63 = 2
Point C is 4 jumps of 13, so it is at 43, one third past 1.

🤝 Let's Try Together

On a number line from 0 to 2 cut into fourths, where is 54? Count 4 jumps to reach 1, then 1 more jump. 54 is one fourth past 1, between 1 and 2.

🧸 Same Spot, Different Names

Mrs. Halvorsen sets a salt block for the cows at 12 mile. Amara draws two number lines, one on top of the other. The top one is cut into halves, and the bottom one into fourths. The salt block at 12 lines up exactly with 24. Same spot, same distance from the barn!

👁 Watch Me Think

If two fractions land on the very same point, they are the same distance from 0, so they are equivalent fractions. I can draw a straight line down from one point to the other number line to check. When I cut each part in half, I get twice as many parts, and I need twice as many of them.

🧩 Picture It

12 = 24 = 48
They all land halfway between 0 and 1.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Line up thirds and sixths. 13 lines up with 26, and 23 lines up with 46. So 13 = 26 and 23 = 46.

🧀 Cheese Blocks: Equal Amounts

At the farm stand, Wen and Tomas cut two blocks of cheese that are exactly the same size. Wen cuts his block into 4 equal pieces and sells 2 of them. Tomas cuts his block into 8 equal pieces. To sell the same amount of cheese, Tomas must sell 4 pieces, because 24 = 48.

👁 Watch Me Think

More pieces means each piece is smaller. When the pieces are half as big, I need twice as many to cover the same amount. Equivalent fractions cover the same amount of the same-size whole. Also, a whole block can be named many ways: 22 = 33 = 44 = 1.

🧩 Picture It

24 of a block = 48 of a block
Each fourth is split into 2 eighths.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Amara has 23 of a block. A same-size block is cut into sixths. Each third splits into 2 sixths, so 2 thirds become 4 sixths. 23 = 46.
