🖼️ Picture It: The Parts of a Poem

A poem is built from parts, the way a house is built from rooms. Each row of words is a line. A group of lines is a stanza. The colored letters show the rhyme pattern: lines with the same letter end with the same sound.

Lines & Stanzas

A line is one row of words. A stanza is a group of lines.

Rhyme & Rhythm

Rhyme: same ending sound. Rhythm: the beat you can clap.

Similes & Senses

Simile: compares with like or as. Sensory words: see, hear, smell, taste, touch.

🔍 Be a Parts Detective

To read a poem like a poet, be a parts detective. Count the stanzas by looking for the spaces. Find the rhyming words at the ends of lines. Clap the beat. Then hunt for like or as, and for words that wake up your five senses.

Ask: What parts did the poet use? Then find them in the poem to prove it!

🧱 Lines, Stanzas, and Rhyme

A poem is made of lines. A line is one row of words. A group of lines is called a stanza, and you can spot one because there is a space before the next stanza starts. Rhyming words end with the same sound, like red and overhead. Many poems rhyme at the ends of lines, but some poems, like Fog Day, do not rhyme at all. They are still poems!

👁 Watch Me Think

Morning at the Harbor has a space after line 4. So lines 1 to 4 are stanza 1, and lines 5 to 8 are stanza 2. That is 2 stanzas with 4 lines each, 8 lines in all. Now I check the last word of each line. Red and overhead rhyme. One and sun rhyme. The lines rhyme in pairs.

🧩 Picture It: Rhyme in Pairs

Line 1 ends in red ...... A
Line 2 ends in overhead ... A
Line 3 ends in one ...... B
Line 4 ends in sun ...... B
Same letter = same ending sound!

🤝 Let's Try Together

How many stanzas does The Lobster Boat have? Look for the spaces. There are 3 groups of 2 lines. So it has 3 stanzas, and 3 times 2 is 6 lines in all. Does it rhyme? Wide and tide end with the same sound, so yes!

🥁 Rhythm: Feel the Beat

Rhythm is the beat you hear when you read a poem out loud. Some syllables get a strong beat, and some get a soft beat. When the strong beats come in a steady pattern, the poem almost sounds like a song. Try clapping on the strong beats!

👁 Watch Me Think

I read line 1 of Morning at the Harbor and clap on the strong beats: the SKY wakes UP in PINK and RED. I clapped 4 times. Line 2: the GULLS cry LOUD-ly O-ver-HEAD. 4 claps again! Every line has 4 strong beats. That steady beat is the rhythm of the poem.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Let's count the syllables in line 4 together: then, slip, a, way, to, meet, the, sun. That is 8 syllables. Every line in Morning at the Harbor has 8 syllables and 4 strong beats. That is why it has a steady rhythm.

🪞 Similes: Like or As

A simile compares two different things using the word like or the word as. Similes help you picture something in a new way. In The Lobster Boat, the boat rocks like a cradle on the tide. A boat and a cradle are very different, but both rock gently back and forth.

👁 Watch Me Think

The engine growls as loud as a bear. I see the word as, and it compares the engine to a bear. What do they have in common? Both make a deep, loud growl. So this is a simile, and it tells me the engine is very loud.

🧩 Picture It: How a Simile Works

thing 1 + like or as + thing 2 = simile
boat + like + cradle ...... the boat rocks gently
engine + as loud as + bear ...... the engine is very loud
boat + slow as + snail ...... the boat moves slowly

🤝 Let's Try Together

Is this a simile: I like the smell of the sea? It uses the word like, but it does not compare two things. Here, like means enjoy. So it is NOT a simile. Always ask: are two different things being compared?

👂 Sensory Words: Use Your Five Senses

Sensory words help you use your five senses as you read: see, hear, smell, taste, and touch. Poets use sensory words so you feel like you are right there. In Morning at the Harbor, the gulls cry, which you hear, and the sticky buns smell warm and sweet, which you smell.

👁 Watch Me Think

Fog Day says: I hear the foghorn moan, low and long. The words hear and moan tell about a sound, so this line uses hearing. The next line says wet air tickles my cheeks. That tells how something feels on my skin, so it uses touch.

👀 See

pink, red, gray, the lighthouse disappears

👂 Hear

gulls cry, bell, creak, growls, foghorn moan

👃 Smell

salt, sticky buns smell warm and sweet, mittens smell like the sea

👅 Taste

I taste the spray (salty sea water in the air)

✋ Touch

wet air tickles my cheeks, dock boards beneath my feet

🤝 Let's Try Together

Which sense does The dock boards creak beneath my feet use? Creak is a sound, so it uses hearing. You also feel the boards under your feet, which is touch. One line can use more than one sense!

⭐ Word Work Bonus: Rhyming Word Families

Bonus Practice • builds XP, not Pathway Points

Rhyming words are often in the same word family. A word family is a group of words that share the same ending, like the -ay family: spray, bay, day, and play. Poets use word families to find rhymes. In the harbor poems you can find tide and wide, feet and sweet, and snail and pail!
