🌍 Earth Spins: Day and Night

Earth spins like a top. This spinning is called rotating. Earth spins around an imaginary line called its axis. One full spin takes about 24 hours. That is one day. The side facing the Sun has day. The side facing away has night.

👁 Watch Me Think

In the morning, I see the Sun low in the east. At midday, it is high. In the evening, it sets in the west. Is the Sun moving across the sky? It looks that way. But Earth is the one spinning. We turn toward the Sun, then away from it.

🧩 Picture It: A Flashlight and a Ball

Picture a flashlight shining on a ball in a dark room.

  • The flashlight is the Sun. The ball is Earth.
  • The lit half has day. The dark half has night.
  • Turn the ball slowly. Each spot moves from day to night and back to day.
  • From Earth, the Sun seems to rise in the east and set in the west.

🤝 Let's Try Together: Let's Try One Together

It is noon in Florida. Is it day or night on the other side of Earth? Florida faces the Sun right now. The other side faces away. So it is night there. In about 12 hours, Earth will turn halfway around. Then Florida will have night.

🌞 Shadows Through the Day

A shadow forms when something blocks light. In the morning, the Sun is low, so shadows are long. Around midday, the Sun is high, so shadows are short. In the evening, shadows grow long again. A shadow always points away from the Sun.

👁 Watch Me Think

My shadow is very long and points west. Long means the Sun is low. A shadow points away from the Sun, so the Sun must be in the east. A low Sun in the east? It must be morning.

🍂 Around the Sun: Years and Seasons

Earth also travels around the Sun. This path is called an orbit. One trip takes about 365 days. That is one year. Earth is tilted, or leaning. When our half of Earth tilts toward the Sun, we have summer. When it tilts away, we have winter.

👁 Watch Me Think

Some people think summer comes because Earth is closer to the Sun. Let's check the evidence. Earth is actually a little closer to the Sun in early January. That is winter in the United States! So distance is not the reason. The tilt is the reason.

🔭 People Who Read the Sky

These people studied patterns in the sky. They used them to keep time, find new things, and explain what they saw.

Benjamin Banneker

1731–1806

A free Black American born in Maryland. He was mostly self-taught. As a young man, he carved a wooden clock by hand. It kept time for many years. Later he studied the sky and correctly predicted a solar eclipse in 1789. His almanacs listed sunrise, sunset, and Moon times. He also wrote to Thomas Jefferson that slavery was wrong.

Maria Mitchell

1818–1889

An astronomer from Nantucket, Massachusetts. In 1847 she found a new comet with her telescope. She became one of the first women in the United States to work as an astronomer. She taught astronomy to college students for many years.

Wang Zhenyi

1768–1797

A scientist and math writer from China. In her time, few girls could study science. She learned from her grandfather's many books. To show how a lunar eclipse works, she built a model. A lamp was the Sun. A round table was Earth. A mirror was the Moon. In an eclipse, Earth's shadow falls on the Moon.
