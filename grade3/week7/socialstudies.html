📦 Goods and Services

Goods are things you can touch. Apples, shoes and books are goods. A service is work a person does for you. A haircut and a doctor visit are services.

👁 Watch Me Think

I ask one question: Can I hold it in my hands? A backpack? Yes, I can hold it. It is a good. A bus ride? I can't hold a ride. The driver does work for me. So it is a service. Some stores give both. A bike shop sells bikes, which are goods. It also fixes flat tires, which is a service.

🤝 Producers and Consumers

A producer makes goods or gives services. A consumer buys and uses goods and services. Most people are both! A baker is a producer at work and a consumer at the shoe store.

👁 Watch Me Think

Let me look at Grandpa. When he fixes a backpack strap, he is a producer of a service. When he buys honey from a beekeeper, he is a consumer. Same person, two jobs! One more thing: in Science, a producer is a plant that makes its own food. In Social Studies, a producer makes goods or gives services. Same word, different subject.

🚚 From the Farm to the Store

Most products take many steps to reach you. First, people grow or gather raw materials. Next, workers make the product, often in a factory. Then trucks carry it to a store, and a consumer buys it.

👁 Watch Me Think

Let me follow a jar of peanut butter. Step 1: farmers grow peanuts. Peanuts are the raw materials. Step 2: a factory roasts and grinds them. Step 3: a truck takes the jars to the Trading Post. Step 4: Grandpa sets the price and stocks the shelf. Step 5: a hiker buys a jar. Many producers helped before one consumer took a bite!

🤝 Let's Try Together: A Wool Hat's Trip

Let's put a wool hat in order. A farmer shears wool from sheep. A mill spins the wool into yarn. A knitter makes the hat. A store puts it on the shelf. Then you buy it. The raw material is the wool. The first step is always the raw material.

🥜 Producers Who Made a Difference

Producers make goods, give services and even make new ideas. As you read, ask: What did each person produce, and who used it?

George Washington Carver

about 1864–1943

George Washington Carver was born into slavery in Missouri. He became a scientist and teacher at Tuskegee Institute in Alabama. He was a producer of ideas. He promoted hundreds of uses for peanuts and sweet potatoes, like foods, inks and dyes. He shared ideas and recipes in free booklets for farmers. Many people think he invented peanut butter, but he did not.

Joyce Chen

1917–1994

Joyce Chen was born in Beijing, China. In 1949 she moved to Cambridge, Massachusetts. In 1958 she opened a Chinese restaurant there. She taught cooking classes and had a TV cooking show. She also made goods: a flat-bottom wok that could sit on an American stove, and bottled sauces.

Romana Acosta Bañuelos

1925–2018

Romana Acosta Bañuelos was a Mexican American born in Arizona. In 1933, in hard times, her family was sent to Mexico, along with many Mexican American families. She was a U.S. citizen, and she came back as a young woman. In 1949 she used her savings to start a tortilla factory in Los Angeles. Later she started a bank. In 1971 she became Treasurer of the United States, so her signature was printed on U.S. paper money.
