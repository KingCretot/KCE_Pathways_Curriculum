🥧 Picture It: The Pie Bakery in Lancaster County

It's early morning at Ms. Della's pie bakery in Lancaster County. Green fields stretch out behind the shop, and the kitchen smells like apples, cherries, and molasses. Her helpers, Deshawn, Danica, Declan, and Davina, tie on their aprons.

Every pie gets cut into equal slices. Some pies are cut into 4 slices, some into 6, and some into 8. By the end of the day, the helpers will count more slices than one pie holds, so they will need fractions greater than 1.

🌾 Lancaster County Facts

  • Lancaster County is in southeastern Pennsylvania, and much of the county is farmland.
  • In 1730, land in the city of Lancaster was set aside for a public market. Lancaster Central Market still sells food on that spot today.
  • The Central Market building that stands there now was built in 1889.
  • Shoofly pie is a Pennsylvania Dutch pie made with molasses and a crumb topping. Food historians think it grew out of a molasses crumb cake from the 1870s.
  • Nobody knows for sure how shoofly pie got its name. One story says bakers had to shoo flies away from the sticky molasses, but that may just be a legend! The name may come from a brand of molasses called Shoo-Fly.

🌟 Word Wrangler

Flip each card to learn this week's vocabulary! Each card shows a sentence from the lesson. Tap the speaker to hear the word or the meaning.

numerator
The top number of a fraction. It tells how many equal parts you have.
“When the numerator and the denominator are the same, like 4/4 or 6/6, the fraction is 1 whole.”
denominator
The bottom number of a fraction. It tells how many equal parts make 1 whole.
“When the denominators are the same, compare the numerators.”
whole number
A counting number like 0, 1, 2, or 3, with no fraction part.
“A whole number can be written as a fraction too: 3 = 3/1.”
fraction greater than 1
A fraction with a numerator bigger than its denominator. It is more than 1 whole.
“A fraction greater than 1 has a numerator bigger than its denominator, like 7/4.”
equivalent
Naming the same amount, or the same point on a number line.
“So 12/4 and 3 are equivalent: they name the same amount.”
number line
A line with equally spaced marks that shows numbers in order.
“On the number line, 7/4 is 3 jumps past 1.”

Here is a memory trick: Denominator is Down on the bottom, and it tells how many slices make one whole pie. If the numerator is bigger than the denominator, you have more than one pie!

🥧 Whole Pies Made of Slices

Ms. Della cuts every apple pie into 4 equal slices, so each slice is 14 of a pie. Deshawn lines up 3 whole pies on the counter. One pie is 44, two pies are 84, and three pies are 124.

👁 Watch Me Think

When the numerator and the denominator are the same, like 44 or 66, the fraction is 1 whole. To change fourths into whole pies, I count by 4s: 4, 8, 12. That is 3 counts. So 124 and 3 are equivalent: they name the same amount. A whole number can be written as a fraction too: 3 = 31, because 3 pies that are each cut into 1 piece is still 3 pies.

🧩 Picture It

44 = 1 whole
84 = 2 wholes
124 = 3 wholes
3 = 31 = 62 = 124

🤝 Let's Try Together

Danica has 102 of a pie. Halves come 2 to a pie, so she counts by 2s: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10. That is 5 counts, so 102 = 5 whole pies.

📏 Past 1 on the Number Line

Ms. Della’s long dough table has a tape measure marked from 0 to 3 feet. Each foot is split into fourths. Declan rolls a strip of pie dough that reaches the mark for 74 feet.

👁 Watch Me Think

A fraction greater than 1 has a numerator bigger than its denominator, like 74. I count jumps of 14 from 0. Four jumps reach 1, which is 44. Three more jumps reach 74. On the number line, 74 is 3 jumps past 1, so it sits between 1 and 2.

🧩 Picture It

0 = 04, 1 = 44, 2 = 84, 3 = 124
74 is between 44 and 84
so 74 is between 1 and 2

🤝 Let's Try Together

Where is 104? 2 is the same as 84, and 10 is 2 more than 8. So 104 is 2 jumps past 2, between 2 and 3.

⚖️ Comparing Fractions Greater Than 1

Two families stop by for pie. Danica’s family buys 74 pies of cherry, and Davina’s family buys 54 pies of apple. Who bought more pie? Then Deshawn wonders: is 53 of a pie more than 54 of a pie?

👁 Watch Me Think

When the denominators are the same, compare the numerators. 7 fourths is more than 5 fourths, so 74 > 54. When the numerators are the same, compare the size of the pieces. Thirds are bigger than fourths, so 53 > 54. I can also compare with a whole number: 74 < 2, because 2 = 84.

🧩 Picture It

74 > 54 (same denominator)
53 > 54 (same numerator)
74 < 84, and 84 = 2

🤝 Let's Try Together

Compare 94 and 53. 94 is more than 2, because 2 = 84. 53 is less than 2, because 2 = 63. So 94 > 53.

📦 Whole Pies and Leftover Slices

At closing time, Deshawn counts the cherry pie slices that are left. There are 11 slices, and each slice is 14 of a pie. That is 114 of a pie. Ms. Della wants to put whole pies back together in boxes.

👁 Watch Me Think

4 fourths fill one box. I count by 4s: 4, 8. That is 2 whole pies, and it uses 8 of the 11 slices. 11 − 8 = 3 slices are left over. So 114 is 2 whole pies and 34 of a pie more.

🧩 Picture It

114 = 44 + 44 + 34
= 2 wholes and 34 more

🤝 Let's Try Together

Go the other way! Davina has 2 whole pies and 14 of a pie more. Each whole pie is 44, so 4 + 4 + 1 = 9 fourths. That is 94 of a pie.
