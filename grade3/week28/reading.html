📖 Read: The Buxton Beacon Kids

Hatteras Island is a long, thin island in the Outer Banks of North Carolina. In the village of Buxton stands the Cape Hatteras Lighthouse, the tallest brick lighthouse in the United States, with black and white spiral stripes. Its light helps ships stay clear of Diamond Shoals, dangerous sandbars just off the cape. In 1999, the ocean had crept too close, so workers moved the whole lighthouse 2,900 feet to a safer spot. (The real tower is sometimes closed for repairs. In 2026, it was closed for climbing while it was being restored.) Gavin and Genevieve are in a summer reading club on the island. They are reading The Buxton Beacon Kids, a mini series by Georgina Gale: two short books with the same characters. Read both books, then compare them!

Read both books, or tap 🔊 to hear them. As you read, notice what stays the same and what is different: the problem, the setting, and how Gael and Geneva act.

The Buxton Beacon Kids · Book 1
The Long Climb
by Georgina Gale
1

Gael, age 9, and his little sister Geneva, age 6, were spending the summer with Grandpa Gerardo in Buxton. Grandpa volunteers at the Cape Hatteras Lighthouse. On a hot, sunny morning, he said, “Today we climb to the top!” Gael raced to the tower door.

2

Geneva stopped and looked up, up, up at the black and white stripes. Visitors climb 257 steps to the top. “It’s too high,” she whispered. But Gael was already climbing. “Hurry up, slowpoke!” he called.

3

Halfway up, Gael looked back. The stairs below him were empty. He hurried down and found Geneva sitting on the bottom step, hugging her knees. Gael’s face felt hot. He remembered how scared he had once been of the deep end of the pool.

4

“Let’s climb together,” Gael said, holding out his hand. “We’ll rest at every landing and count the steps out loud.” Step by step, they counted. At each landing, they peeked out a small window at the blue ocean.

5

At last, they stepped out onto the balcony at the top. The wind whipped their hair, and the waves looked tiny below. “Two hundred fifty-seven!” Geneva cheered. Gael grinned. “We did it, together.” Grandpa told them that at night the light flashes every 7.5 seconds to warn ships away from Diamond Shoals, dangerous sandbars off the cape.

The Buxton Beacon Kids · Book 2
The Night of the Turtles
by Georgina Gale
1

Late in the summer, Grandpa Gerardo had a new job. This time he was a sea turtle volunteer. About 60 days before, a loggerhead sea turtle had crawled onto the beach near Buxton and laid her eggs in a nest in the sand. Now the hatchlings could come out any night.

2

After dark, Gael, Geneva, and Grandpa sat quietly near the nest. The moon made a silver path on the water. “When will they come out?” Geneva asked, bouncing on her toes. “Maybe tonight, maybe not,” said Grandpa.

3

Gael sat very still. He remembered the lighthouse, when he had rushed ahead and left his sister behind. He had learned to be patient. Some good things take time.

4

An hour passed. Geneva grabbed her flashlight. “I just want to check!” Gael gently touched her arm. “Wait,” he whispered. “Grandpa says bright lights can confuse the babies. They crawl toward the moonlight shining on the water.” Geneva clicked off her flashlight.

5

Suddenly, the sand began to wiggle. Tiny turtles popped out, one after another, and scrambled down the beach toward the silver water. Geneva held her brother’s hand and watched them reach the waves. “They’re climbing toward the sea, like we climbed the lighthouse,” Gael whispered. Grandpa smiled. “And both times, you two helped each other.”

📚 Same Characters, New Adventure, Part 1

A series is a group of books or stories that share the same main characters. Each book is a new adventure with its own problem. When you read two stories in a series, notice what stays the same (the characters, and often the place) and what is different (the problem, the events, and the time). Watch the characters too: a character can keep the same traits, or grow and change from one book to the next.

👁 Watch Me Think

Both books have Gael, Geneva, and Grandpa Gerardo, and both happen on Hatteras Island. That tells me they are a series. But Book 1 is about climbing the lighthouse, and Book 2 is about sea turtle hatchlings. Same characters, new adventure!

🧭 Compare the Plots, Part 1

The plot is what happens in a story: the problem, the main events, and the solution. To compare two plots, put them side by side. Ask: What is the problem in each story? How is it solved? What is alike, and what is different? Words like both, also, and alike show things that are the same. Words like but, however, and different show things that are not the same.

👁 Watch Me Think

In Book 1, the problem is that Geneva is scared to climb the 257 steps. It is solved when Gael climbs with her and they count the steps. In Book 2, the problem is that Geneva wants to turn on her flashlight, which could confuse the hatchlings. It is solved when Gael reminds her, and she turns it off. Both problems are solved when the kids help each other!

🧩 Picture It: Plot Side by Side

Book 1 problem: Geneva is scared to climb
Book 2 problem: a flashlight could confuse the hatchlings
Both: solved by helping each other

Book 1 plot

Problem: Geneva is scared. Events: Gael runs ahead, then goes back. Solution: they climb together.

Book 2 plot

Problem: Geneva grabs her flashlight. Events: Gael reminds her. Solution: lights off, and the turtles reach the sea.

🌅 Compare the Settings, Part 1

The setting is where and when a story happens. Two books in a series can share a place but have different settings: a different spot, a different season, or a different time of day. The setting can shape the plot. Ask: Could this story happen in another setting? If the answer is no, the setting matters!

👁 Watch Me Think

Book 1 happens on a hot, sunny summer morning, inside and on top of the Cape Hatteras Lighthouse. The tall tower is what makes Geneva scared, so the setting causes the problem. Book 2 happens on a dark night late in the summer, on the beach. The darkness matters, because hatchlings come out at night and crawl toward the moonlight on the water.

🧩 Picture It: Where + When

Book 1: the lighthouse, a sunny summer morning
Book 2: the beach, a dark night late in the summer
Ask: How does the setting shape the plot?

The Long Climb
by Georgina Gale
1

Gael, age 9, and his little sister Geneva, age 6, were spending the summer with Grandpa Gerardo in Buxton. Grandpa volunteers at the Cape Hatteras Lighthouse. On a hot, sunny morning, he said, “Today we climb to the top!” Gael raced to the tower door.

2

Geneva stopped and looked up, up, up at the black and white stripes. Visitors climb 257 steps to the top. “It’s too high,” she whispered. But Gael was already climbing. “Hurry up, slowpoke!” he called.

3

Halfway up, Gael looked back. The stairs below him were empty. He hurried down and found Geneva sitting on the bottom step, hugging her knees. Gael’s face felt hot. He remembered how scared he had once been of the deep end of the pool.

4

“Let’s climb together,” Gael said, holding out his hand. “We’ll rest at every landing and count the steps out loud.” Step by step, they counted. At each landing, they peeked out a small window at the blue ocean.

5

At last, they stepped out onto the balcony at the top. The wind whipped their hair, and the waves looked tiny below. “Two hundred fifty-seven!” Geneva cheered. Gael grinned. “We did it, together.” Grandpa told them that at night the light flashes every 7.5 seconds to warn ships away from Diamond Shoals, dangerous sandbars off the cape.

The Buxton Beacon Kids · Book 2
The Night of the Turtles
by Georgina Gale
1

Late in the summer, Grandpa Gerardo had a new job. This time he was a sea turtle volunteer. About 60 days before, a loggerhead sea turtle had crawled onto the beach near Buxton and laid her eggs in a nest in the sand. Now the hatchlings could come out any night.

2

After dark, Gael, Geneva, and Grandpa sat quietly near the nest. The moon made a silver path on the water. “When will they come out?” Geneva asked, bouncing on her toes. “Maybe tonight, maybe not,” said Grandpa.

3

Gael sat very still. He remembered the lighthouse, when he had rushed ahead and left his sister behind. He had learned to be patient. Some good things take time.

4

An hour passed. Geneva grabbed her flashlight. “I just want to check!” Gael gently touched her arm. “Wait,” he whispered. “Grandpa says bright lights can confuse the babies. They crawl toward the moonlight shining on the water.” Geneva clicked off her flashlight.

5

Suddenly, the sand began to wiggle. Tiny turtles popped out, one after another, and scrambled down the beach toward the silver water. Geneva held her brother’s hand and watched them reach the waves. “They’re climbing toward the sea, like we climbed the lighthouse,” Gael whispered. Grandpa smiled. “And both times, you two helped each other.”

🔄 How the Characters Act, Part 1

Characters show their traits, or what they are like, through what they say, do, and think. In a series, compare how a character acts in each book. Is the character the same, or did the character change? Always prove your answer with evidence: words or actions from the story. Then think about the message the two stories share.

👁 Watch Me Think

In Book 1, Gael races ahead and calls Geneva a slowpoke. That shows he is impatient. But in Book 2, Gael sits very still and waits. When Geneva grabs her flashlight, he gently reminds her of the rule. Now he is patient and helpful. Gael changed! He learned something on the lighthouse stairs that he used on the beach.
