A living thing that breaks down dead plants and animals. Mushrooms are decomposers.

☀️ Energy Starts With the Sun

A food chain shows who eats whom. Every food chain starts with the Sun. Plants use sunlight to make their own food. They are producers. Animals cannot make food, so they eat. They are consumers.

👁 Watch Me Think

Look at this chain: Sun → grass → rabbit → fox. Each arrow points to the one that eats. The grass uses sunlight. The rabbit eats the grass. The fox eats the rabbit. Energy moves along the arrows.

🧩 Picture It: A Meadow Food Chain

Sun → grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → hawk

  • The arrow means "is eaten by."
  • Grass is the producer.
  • Every animal in the chain is a consumer.

🤝 Let's Try Together: Let's Try One Together

Put these in order: frog, Sun, grass, grasshopper. The Sun always comes first. Grass uses sunlight, so grass is next. The grasshopper eats grass. The frog eats the grasshopper. So: Sun → grass → grasshopper → frog.

🍄 Plant Eaters, Meat Eaters, and Cleaners

Consumers eat different foods. A herbivore eats only plants. A carnivore eats only animals. An omnivore eats both. When living things die, decomposers break them down. Mushrooms, worms, and tiny bacteria do this job. They put food back into the soil for plants.

👁 Watch Me Think

A raccoon eats berries, fish, and eggs. Berries come from plants. Fish and eggs come from animals. It eats both kinds. So a raccoon is an omnivore.

🔗 When One Link Is Lost

In a food chain, every living thing is a link. If one link is lost, others change too. The animals that ate it go hungry. The things it ate grow in number. Scientist Rachel Carson warned that a bug poison called DDT moved up food chains. It harmed birds.

👁 Watch Me Think

Chain: grass → rabbit → fox. What if all the rabbits got sick and died? First, foxes lose their food, so there are fewer foxes. Next, nothing eats the grass, so the grass grows thick and tall.

🧩 Picture It: How Poison Moved Up a Food Chain

Spray on trees → leaves → worms and insects eat the leaves → robins eat many worms

  • Each worm had a little poison.
  • A robin eats many worms.
  • So the robin got a lot of poison.

🤝 Let's Try Together: Let's Try One Together

Chain: plants → insects → frogs → herons. A sickness kills most frogs. What happens to herons? They lose food, so there are fewer herons. What happens to insects? Fewer frogs eat them, so there are more insects.

🌿 Scientists Who Protected Living Things

These scientists studied how living things depend on each other. They spoke up to protect them.

Rachel Carson

1907–1964

A scientist and writer who studied the ocean. She learned that birds were dying where a bug poison called DDT was sprayed. She explained how DDT built up as it moved up food chains. Her book Silent Spring (1962) helped lead the U.S. to ban most uses of DDT.

Charles Elton

1900–1991

An English scientist who studied animals in the cold Arctic. In his 1927 book Animal Ecology, he explained food chains. He saw many small animals at the bottom and only a few big hunters at the top.

Ruth Patrick

1907–2013

A scientist who studied tiny living things in rivers. By counting them, she could tell if a river was healthy or polluted.

Ayana Elizabeth Johnson

Living today

A Black American ocean scientist who grew up in Brooklyn, New York. She studied coral reefs and the fish people catch there. She has worked with island communities so people can fish and still keep reefs healthy.
