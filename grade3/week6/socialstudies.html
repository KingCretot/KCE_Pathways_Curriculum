🍎 Needs and Wants

A need is something you must have to live. Food, water, clothes and a safe home are needs. A want is something nice to have. You can live without a want.

👁 Watch Me Think

I ask one question: Can I live without it? Water? No. I need water to live. So water is a need. A video game? Yes, I can live without it. So it is a want. Shoes are tricky. Shoes are a need. But light-up shoes with a cartoon are a want, because plain shoes do the job.

⚖️ Scarcity and Choices

Scarcity means we can't have everything we want. Money, time and things run out. So we must make choices. The next best thing you give up is the opportunity cost.

👁 Watch Me Think

I have $3. A book, a kite and a smoothie each cost $3. I can only buy one. That is scarcity! First, I rank them: 1. book, 2. kite, 3. smoothie. I pick the book. Every choice is a trade-off. My opportunity cost is the kite, because the kite was my next best choice. It is not the smoothie. I only count the next best thing I gave up.

🧩 Picture It: Choose in 3 Steps

  • Step 1: List your choices.
  • Step 2: Rank them: 1st, 2nd, 3rd.
  • Step 3: Pick your 1st choice.

Your 2nd choice is your opportunity cost.

🤝 Let's Try Together: Theo's Saturday

On Saturday, Theo can go to the park or help Grandpa at the Trading Post and earn $2. He has time for only one. That is scarcity of time. Theo picks helping Grandpa. What did he give up? Park time. Park time is his opportunity cost. His trade-off: he got $2 but gave up play time.

🐷 Saving and Spending

When you spend, you use money to buy something now. When you save, you keep money to use later. Saving helps you buy bigger things. It also helps when there is an emergency.

👁 Watch Me Think

I earn $2 each week helping at the Trading Post. I want a $10 kite. If I spend my $2 on candy every week, I will never get the kite. If I save $2 each week, I count: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10. In 5 weeks I have $10! Saving means I have to wait. But waiting gets me the bigger thing I want.

💼 Business Builders

These women made smart money choices and helped others do the same. As you read, ask: Who did their work help?

Madam C.J. Walker

1867–1919

She was born Sarah Breedlove in Louisiana. Her parents had been enslaved. She was the first child in her family born free. She washed clothes for very little pay. Then she made hair-care products for Black women. In 1910 she moved her company to Indianapolis, Indiana. She trained thousands of Black women to sell her products and earn their own money. She became one of the first self-made women millionaires in America. Self-made means she earned it herself.

Annie Turnbo Malone

1869–1957

Annie Turnbo Malone was a Black businesswoman from Illinois. She made hair products for Black women called Poro. Sarah Breedlove once worked selling Poro products. Later the two women became business rivals. Malone opened a school in St. Louis to train beauty workers.

Maggie Lena Walker

1864–1934

Maggie Lena Walker lived in Richmond, Virginia. She was not related to Madam C.J. Walker. Her mother had been enslaved. In 1903 she started the St. Luke Penny Savings Bank. She was the first Black woman in the United States to start a bank. Her bank helped Black families save money and buy homes.
