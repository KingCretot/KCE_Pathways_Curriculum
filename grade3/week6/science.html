🌡️ Temperature: How Hot or Cold?

Weather is what the air outside is like right now. It can be hot or cold, rainy or dry, windy or calm. Temperature tells how hot or cold the air is. We measure it with a thermometer. Temperature is counted in degrees.

👁 Watch Me Think

I look at a thermometer. The line stops at 80. That means 80 degrees. Is that hot or cold? Water freezes at 32 degrees. 80 is much higher than 32. So it is a warm day. Shorts weather!

🧩 Picture It: Reading a Thermometer

The liquid inside a thermometer goes up when it gets warmer. It goes down when it gets cooler. In the United States, we often use degrees Fahrenheit (°F).

TemperatureWhat it feels like
32°FFreezing. Water turns to ice.
50°FCool. Wear a jacket.
70°FMild. Just right for most people.
90°FHot. Find some shade!

🤝 Let's Try Together: Let's Try One Together

In the morning, the thermometer reads 60°F. In the afternoon, it reads 75°F. Did it get warmer or cooler? 75 is more than 60. The line went up. So it got warmer. How much? 75 minus 60 is 15 degrees.

🌧️ Rain and Wind

A rain gauge catches rain. Its marks show how much rain fell, in inches. A wind vane shows which way the wind comes from. An anemometer has cups that spin in the wind. The faster the cups spin, the faster the wind.

👁 Watch Me Think

The wind vane's arrow points north. The arrow points into the wind. So the wind is coming from the north. We call it a north wind. Next I look at the anemometer. Its cups are spinning fast. So the wind is strong today.

On Monday, the rain gauge fills to the 1 mark. That is 1 inch of rain. We empty it. On Tuesday, a big storm fills it to the 3 mark. Which day was rainier? 3 inches is more than 1 inch. So Tuesday was rainier.

📊 Weather Charts and Changes

Weather changes from day to day. One day is sunny. The next may be rainy. Scientists write the weather down every day on a weather chart. Many days of data help them see patterns. A meteorologist uses data to make a forecast, a careful guess about the weather to come.

👁 Watch Me Think

My chart says Monday 72°F, Tuesday 75°F, Wednesday 78°F. Each day is a little warmer. That is a pattern. Now dark clouds are moving in. My forecast: Thursday may be warm with rain. I used evidence, not a wild guess.

🌤️ Weather Scientists

Many kinds of people have helped us measure and understand weather.

June Bacon-Bercey

about 1930–2019

A Black American scientist from Wichita, Kansas. In 1954 she became the first Black woman to earn a college degree in meteorology. She worked as a TV weather forecaster in Buffalo, New York. She won money on a TV quiz show. She used it to help young women study weather science.

Eunice Foote

1819–1888

A scientist and women's rights leader from New York. In 1856 she put thermometers inside glass tubes filled with different gases. She set the tubes in the sunlight. The tube with carbon dioxide, a gas in the air, got the hottest. She was one of the first to show that this gas can trap heat.

Daniel Fahrenheit

1686–1736

A maker of science tools who lived in Europe. He built some of the first thermometers that gave the same reading every time. The °F temperature scale is named after him.

J. Marshall Shepherd

Living today

A Black American meteorologist who worked at NASA and now teaches at a university in Georgia. He studies how big cities can change where rain falls. In 2013 he became the first Black president of the American Meteorological Society.
