🌟 Word Wrangler

Flip each card to learn this week’s vocabulary! Tap 🔊 to hear the word, then flip and tap 🔊 to hear what it means and see how the newspaper uses it.

opinion
What a person thinks or believes. In the newspaper: Ms. Fonda asked readers to write opinion pieces about one question.
reason
Something that tells why a person thinks something. In the newspaper: “I have three reasons.”
example
A fact or detail that proves a reason. In the newspaper: Each reason needs an example, a fact or detail that proves it.
conclusion
The ending that says the opinion again. In the newspaper: Last comes a conclusion that says your opinion again.
support
To help prove an idea with reasons and examples. In the newspaper: Some opinion pieces support their opinion much better than others.
editor
A person who reads writing and decides what goes in a newspaper. In the newspaper: This month, the editor, Ms. Fonda, asked readers to write opinion pieces.

🖼️ Picture It: The Opinion Sandwich

A strong opinion piece is built like a sandwich. The opinion is the top bun. Reasons and examples are the fillings in the middle. The conclusion is the bottom bun that holds it all together. Linking words are like the toothpicks that connect the layers.

Reason or example?

A reason tells WHY. An example is a fact or detail that PROVES the reason. “I like it” is only a feeling.

Linking words

because → a reason · for example → an example · also → one more idea · therefore → the conclusion

Ask: What does the writer think? Why? How do I know?

The Mountain Kids Gazette is a newspaper written by kids at a community center in Asheville, North Carolina, a city in the Blue Ridge Mountains. This month, the editor, Ms. Fonda, asked readers to write opinion pieces about one question: Where should our class go for its spring field trip? Two writers sent in pieces. Fidel picked the Western North Carolina Nature Center, which cares for animals of the Southern Appalachian Mountains. 🥪 The Parts of an Opinion Piece, Part 1

An opinion piece tells what the writer thinks and tries to get readers to agree. It has four parts. The opinion comes first: “I think our class should...” Then come reasons that tell why. Each reason has an example, a fact or detail that proves it. Last, the conclusion says the opinion again in a new way. An opinion cannot be proven true or false, but it can be supported well or poorly.

👁 Watch Me Think

Fidel’s first sentence says, “I think our class should take its spring field trip to the Western North Carolina Nature Center.” I think is a clue, so that is his opinion. His last paragraph begins with Therefore and says the Nature Center is the best choice. That is his conclusion. In between are his reasons.

🔍 Strong Reasons and Examples, Part 1

A reason tells WHY the writer thinks something. An example is a fact or detail that PROVES the reason. Strong reasons are about the topic and give the reader a real reason to agree. Weak reasons are only feelings, like “It is really nice,” or they are off topic, like a sentence about a cousin’s bike. To check a reason, ask: Does it tell WHY? Can I prove it with an example?

👁 Watch Me Think

Fidel’s second reason is, “We can learn how people help animals.” Then he proves it: fewer than 25 red wolves were left, and now there are more than 300 because nature centers and zoos helped. That example uses real numbers, so it makes his reason strong.

🔗 Linking Words, Part 1

Linking words connect ideas so readers can follow your thinking. Because comes before a reason: “Bring water, because the trail is long.” For example comes before an example. Also adds one more idea. Therefore comes before a result or a conclusion; it means “so, as a result.” When a linking word like for example or therefore starts a sentence, put a comma after it.

👁 Watch Me Think

Fidel’s fourth paragraph starts with Also. That tells me he is adding one more reason: the short bus ride. His last paragraph starts with Therefore. That tells me all his reasons lead to a result: the Nature Center is the best choice. The linking words work like signs on a trail!

🧩 Picture It: Which Linking Word?

because → a reason
for example → an example; also → one more idea
therefore → the result or conclusion

because

Bring water, because the trail is long.

for example · also

For example, it has red wolves. It also has otters.

therefore

Therefore, we should go.

⚖️ Judge It, Then Write It, Part 1

To judge two opinion pieces, do not ask, “Which place do I like?” Ask, “Which piece supports its opinion better?” Check for the four parts, for reasons with examples, and for linking words. Then you can revise a weak piece: keep the opinion, add reasons and facts, cut off-topic sentences, and add a conclusion. Any opinion can become strong with good support!
