🖼️ Picture It: The Venn Diagram

When you compare, you tell how things are the same. When you contrast, you tell how they are different. A Venn diagram helps you sort your ideas. It has two circles that overlap. Things that are true of only one go in that circle's outer part. Things that are true of both go in the middle, where the circles overlap.

Here is a Venn diagram about two ways people travel in the Mekong Delta. A bike has wheels and rides on roads. A boat floats on water and travels on canals. Both carry people, and both carry things to market, so those go in the middle.

🔑 Clue Words

Clue words help too. Compare words: both, also, too, alike. Contrast words: but, only, unlike, different.

📰 Read Text A: Rice in the Mekong Delta

At the market, Grandpa bought a bag of rice. The label said the rice was grown in the Mekong Delta in Vietnam. Theo and Mia wanted to know more, so they found two texts about rice farming there. Text A is an article full of facts. Text B is a firsthand account written by Linh, a girl who lives in a rice-farming village. Both texts are about the same topic. Let's read them both!

An article is a nonfiction text that gives facts. Text A has headings that tell what each part is about. Read each paragraph, or tap the speaker to hear it.

📰
Text A • Nonfiction article
Rice in the Mekong Delta
1

A Land of Rivers

The Mekong Delta is in the south of Vietnam. There, the long Mekong River splits into smaller rivers before it reaches the sea. People have dug many canals, too. The land is flat, wet, and warm, so it is a great place to grow rice. Farmers in the delta grow about half of all the rice in Vietnam.

2

Planting

Rice grows in flooded fields called paddies. Farmers fill the paddies with water from the canals. Some farmers scatter rice seeds right onto the wet, muddy field. Others first grow young plants called seedlings. Then they push the seedlings into the mud by hand, one by one.

3

Growing

Rice plants grow with their roots in the mud under the water. The water helps keep weeds away. The plants grow tall and green. After a few months, grains of rice form at the top of each stem. As the grains ripen, the whole field slowly turns gold.

4

Harvesting

When the rice is ripe, farmers drain the water so the field can dry. Then it is time to harvest. Many farmers use big machines that cut the rice and separate the grains from the stalks. Some families still cut the rice by hand with a curved knife called a sickle. The grains are spread out to dry in the sun. Because the weather is warm all year, many farmers in the delta grow two or even three rice crops each year.

✍️ Read Text B: Harvest Day

Text B is a firsthand account. Linh was there, so she tells what happened using words like I, my, and we. She also tells how she felt. Read each paragraph, or tap the speaker to hear it.

✍️
Text B • Firsthand account by Linh
Harvest Day
1

My name is Linh, and I am nine years old. I live in a small village in the Mekong Delta, near the city of Can Tho. My family grows rice. Our house sits beside a canal, and our rice paddy is right behind it.

2

Last week was harvest day! I woke up before the sun came up, because it is cooler in the early morning. Grandma tied on my hat. It is shaped like a cone and made of palm leaves, and it keeps the sun off my face. Our rice had turned gold. Dad had drained the water from the paddy, so the ground was dry.

3

Dad and my uncle cut the rice with curved sickles. My job was to carry the bundles of rice stalks to the edge of the field. They were heavy, and the stalks tickled my arms! Our neighbors came to help us. Next week, we will help them with their field.

4

After lunch, the grown-ups beat the bundles against a big wooden box to knock the grains loose. Then we spread the grains on mats in the yard to dry in the sun. I raked them with a wooden rake to turn them over.

5

In the evening, a neighbor led a big flock of ducks into our empty field. The ducks ate the rice grains that had fallen on the ground. That night, my family ate rice from our own paddy for dinner. I was tired, but I felt proud. We did it together!

Before you compare two texts, find the most important point of each one, and the key details that support it. This is the main idea skill from Week 11. Ask: what is this whole text mostly about?

🤝 What Is the Same? (Compare)

To compare two texts, look for important points and details that both texts tell. They go in the middle of the Venn diagram, where the circles overlap. The word both is a great clue.

👁 Watch Me Think

Text A says the field slowly turns gold as the rice ripens. Text B says Linh's rice had turned gold. Both texts tell it, so it goes in the middle! Text A also says farmers drain the water before the harvest, and Text B says Dad drained the paddy. That goes in the middle too.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Remember: it is not enough for a detail to be in one text. Check the other text, too. If you find it in both, it goes in the middle.
