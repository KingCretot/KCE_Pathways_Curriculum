🌾 Picture It: Squares in the Rice Paddies

This week, Linh and her big brother Minh are helping on their family’s rice farm in a village in the Mekong Delta, in southern Vietnam. Their grandmother, Grandma Hoa, has grown rice here her whole life!

Look at the paddies. Low mud walls divide the land into fields, and a map of the fields looks like a grid of squares. Today we will measure how much space a shape covers. That is called area!

🌾 Mekong Delta Facts

  • The Mekong Delta is in the south of Vietnam, where the Mekong River flows into the sea.
  • In Vietnam, the Mekong is called the Nine Dragons River, because it splits into many branches near the sea.
  • The delta is often called Vietnam’s rice bowl. About half of all the rice grown in Vietnam comes from here.
  • Many farmers in the delta grow two or even three crops of rice each year.
  • Rice fields, called paddies, are divided by low mud walls and are kept flooded with water while the rice grows.
  • After the harvest, families spread the rice grains on mats or flat yards to dry in the sun.
  • At floating markets, people buy and sell fruit and vegetables right from their boats.

Today you will find area by counting unit squares, by multiplying rows × columns, by using square units like square meters, and by splitting L-shaped fields into rectangles.

🌟 Word Wrangler

Flip each card to learn this week’s vocabulary! Tap the speaker to hear the word or the meaning.

area
The amount of space inside a flat shape. We measure it in square units.
unit square
A square with sides that are each 1 unit long. It covers 1 square unit.
square unit
The unit we use to measure area, like square centimeters or square meters.
row
A line of squares going across, from left to right.
column
A line of squares going up and down.
decompose
To break a shape into smaller parts, like splitting an L shape into two rectangles.

Remember: when you cover a shape with unit squares, leave no gaps and no overlaps. Every part of the shape is covered exactly once.

🟩 Counting Unit Squares

Grandma Hoa starts her rice seedlings in a tray. The tray is divided into little squares, and each one is a unit square: a square with sides 1 unit long. To find the area of the tray, Linh counts the unit squares that cover it: 1, 2, 3, all the way to 12. The tray has an area of 12 square units.

👁 Watch Me Think

Area is the space inside a flat shape. To measure it, I cover the shape with unit squares. There can be no gaps, so every part is covered, and no overlaps, so no part is counted twice. Then I count the squares.

🧩 Picture It

1 unit square covers 1 square unit.
12 unit squares cover 12 square units.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Linh’s drying mat is not a rectangle. Its top row has 5 squares and its bottom row has 3 squares. Count them all: 5 + 3 = 8. The area is 8 square units.

✖️ Rows × Columns

Linh’s family paddy is drawn on a map as a grid. Counting one square at a time takes a long time! Linh notices that the grid has 4 rows, and every row has 6 squares. That is 4 equal groups of 6, so she multiplies: 4 × 6 = 24 square units.

👁 Watch Me Think

Every row in a rectangle has the same number of squares, so the rows are equal groups. The number of rows times the number of squares in each row gives the area. I can turn it around, too: 6 × 4 is also 24.

🧩 Picture It

Area of a rectangle = rows × squares in each row.
4 × 6 = 24 square units.

🤝 Let's Try Together

A small seedling tray has 5 rows with 7 cells in each row. 5 × 7 = 35, so the tray has an area of 35 square units.

📏 Square Units: Square Meters and Square Centimeters

Real fields are not drawn on grid paper, so farmers measure with real units. A square that is 1 meter long on each side covers 1 square meter. Grandma Hoa’s big drying mat is 5 meters long and 2 meters wide. That means 2 rows of 5 one-meter squares would cover it: 2 × 5 = 10 square meters.

👁 Watch Me Think

The side lengths tell me the rows and the columns, so I multiply the two side lengths to find the area. Then I name the unit. Small things like labels are measured in square centimeters. Big things like paddies are measured in square meters. In the United States, people also use square inches and square feet. Area is always named in square units.

🧩 Picture It

1 meter by 1 meter = 1 square meter.
2 × 5 = 10 square meters.

🤝 Let's Try Together

The rice label at the family’s market stall is 8 centimeters long and 3 centimeters wide. 8 × 3 = 24, so the label’s area is 24 square centimeters.

📐 Splitting L-Shaped Fields

Grandma Hoa has a paddy shaped like the letter L. It is not a rectangle, so Linh cannot just multiply. Instead, she decomposes it: she draws one line to split it into two rectangles. The tall part has 5 rows of 2 squares: 5 × 2 = 10. The short part has 2 rows with 4 squares in each row: 2 × 4 = 8. Then she adds: 10 + 8 = 18 square units.

👁 Watch Me Think

I look for a place to draw one straight line that makes two rectangles. I find the area of each rectangle with rows times columns. Then I add the two areas together. I never multiply the two areas.

Linh’s split
Minh’s split

🧩 Picture It

Linh: 10 + 8 = 18 square units.
Minh: 6 + 12 = 18 square units.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Minh split the same paddy a different way: a top part with 3 rows of 2 squares, 3 × 2 = 6, and a bottom part with 2 rows of 6 squares, 2 × 6 = 12. Then 6 + 12 = 18 square units. Same paddy, same area!
