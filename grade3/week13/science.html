💡 How We See: Light Travels to Our Eyes

Light comes from a light source, like the Sun or a lamp. Light travels in straight lines. It hits things and bounces off. Some of that light goes into our eyes. That is how we see!

👁 Watch Me Think

Is the Moon a light source? It looks bright at night. But the Moon makes no light of its own. Sunlight hits the Moon and bounces off. Then it reaches my eyes. So the Moon is not a light source. It reflects light.

🔦 Shadows and Reflections

Some things block light. They are opaque, like wood or your body. A dark shadow forms behind them. Clear things, like window glass, let light pass through. A smooth, shiny mirror reflects light. Reflect means to bounce back.

👁 Watch Me Think

Why does my shadow change during the day? In the morning, the Sun is low in the sky. My body blocks the light at a slant, so my shadow is long. At noon, the Sun is high. My shadow is short. My shadow is always on the side away from the light.

🥁 Sound: Things That Vibrate

Sound is made when something vibrates. Vibrate means to move back and forth very fast. The vibrations travel through the air to your ears. Inside your ear, your eardrum vibrates too. Then you hear! Fast vibrations make a high pitch. Slow vibrations make a low pitch.

👁 Watch Me Think

I put my fingers on my throat and hum. I feel a buzz. That buzz is my throat vibrating. Now I stop humming. The buzz stops, and the sound stops too. So sound and vibration go together.

🔬 People Who Worked With Light and Sound

These people used light and sound to help others. One made light bulbs better. One used a laser to help people see. One made a tiny microphone. One proved how our eyes see.

Lewis Latimer

1848–1928

An inventor and expert drafter. His parents had escaped slavery. He taught himself to make careful drawings of machines. He drew the patent drawings for Alexander Graham Bell's telephone. Later he found a better way to make the thin carbon filament inside light bulbs. His bulbs lasted longer. He helped bring electric light to cities like New York and London.

Dr. Patricia Bath

1942–2019

An eye doctor and inventor from Harlem in New York City. Many people go blind from cataracts. A cataract is when the lens of the eye turns cloudy. She invented the Laserphaco Probe. It uses a laser, a strong beam of light, to remove cataracts. In 1988, she became the first Black woman doctor to get a patent for a medical invention.

James West

born 1931

An engineer who studied sound. As a boy, he liked to take radios apart. At Bell Labs, he and Gerhard Sessler invented a small, low-cost microphone in 1962. A microphone turns sound vibrations into electric signals. Their kind of microphone has been used in billions of phones, computers, and hearing aids.

Ibn al-Haytham

about 965 – about 1040

A scientist who lived in Basra, in what is now Iraq, and in Cairo, Egypt. Long ago, many people thought eyes sent out beams to see. He tested that idea. He showed that light travels in straight lines into our eyes. He did experiments in a dark room with a tiny hole. He wrote the Book of Optics.
