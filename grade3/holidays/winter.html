🌍 The Longest Night

Earth leans a little. This lean is called its tilt. In December, the north half of Earth leans away from the Sun. So days are short and nights are long. The longest night is the winter solstice, around December 21.

👁 Watch Me Think

I ask: is it the longest night everywhere? No! In December, the south half of Earth leans toward the Sun. In Australia, Argentina and South Africa, December has the longest day. It is summer there! People south of the equator have their longest night in June.

🤝 Let's Try Together: After the Longest Night

After the December solstice, northern nights get a little shorter each day. Daylight comes back, bit by bit. Long ago, people watched the sky closely and noticed this turn. Some celebrations, like Yalda in Iran and Dongzhi in China, happen on the longest night itself.

🕎 Hanukkah and Christmas

Many Jewish families celebrate Hanukkah for eight nights. Each night, they light one more candle on the menorah. Many Christian families celebrate Christmas to remember the birth of Jesus. Both holidays bring light to dark winter nights.

👁 Watch Me Think

Let me compare the dates. Hanukkah follows the Hebrew calendar, so its date moves each year. In 2026, it starts on the evening of December 4. Many Christians celebrate Christmas on December 25. But many Orthodox Christians, like in Ethiopia, Egypt and Russia, celebrate on January 7. Same holiday, different calendars!

🤝 Let's Try Together: Christmas in Mexico: Las Posadas

In Mexico, many families celebrate Las Posadas for nine nights, December 16 to 24. Posada means inn, a place to stay. Each night, people act out Mary and Joseph looking for a place to stay. They sing at doors until someone lets them in. Then come tamales, warm ponche (fruit punch) and a piñata! Many Mexican American communities celebrate Las Posadas too.

🕯️ Kwanzaa, Dongzhi and Yalda

Kwanzaa celebrates African American families and African heritage. It lasts seven days, December 26 to January 1. Families light one candle each day on a kinara. In China, families celebrate Dongzhi on the longest night. In Iran, families celebrate Yalda Night.

👁 Watch Me Think

Kwanzaa is new compared to the others. Maulana Karenga created it in 1966 in California. Its name comes from Swahili words that mean first fruits. That reminds me of harvest festivals! Each of its seven days has an idea to talk about, like umoja, which means unity.

🤝 Let's Try Together: Same and Different

Let's compare Yalda and Dongzhi. Same: both happen on the longest night. In both, families gather and share food. Different: Yalda families eat red fruits and read poems. Dongzhi families eat round rice balls. The round shape stands for a family being together.

👁 Think Like a Geographer

A geographer looks for patterns. I read the cities from north to south. Anchorage is far north. It has the least daylight. Quito is on the equator. It has about 12 hours. Sydney is south of the equator. It has the most! So in December, the farther north you go, the shorter the day.

🌟 People Behind the Stories

Meet four people from long ago whose stories are part of winter celebrations today. As you read, ask: How do we know about them?

Judah Maccabee

died 160 BCE

He was a Jewish leader in Judea, long ago. A king named Antiochus would not let Jews practice their religion. Judah and his brothers led a fight against the king's army. In 164 BCE, they dedicated the Temple in Jerusalem again. The word Hanukkah means dedication.

Francis of Assisi

about 1181–1226

He was a Christian friar in Italy. In December 1223, in a cave near the town of Greccio, he set up a manger with hay, an ox and a donkey. People came carrying candles and torches. It is called the first living nativity scene. A man who wrote about his life described it.

King Lalibela

ruled about 1181–1221

He was a king in Ethiopia, in East Africa. Ethiopian tradition says he ordered 11 churches carved right out of solid rock. Today the town is named Lalibela after him. Every January 7, big crowds of Ethiopian Christians gather there for Genna, Ethiopian Christmas.

Guo Shoujing

1231–about 1316

He was a scientist in China. He helped build a tall stone tower in 1276 to measure the Sun's shadow at noon. On the winter solstice, the noon shadow is the longest of the year. With measurements like these, he made a calendar with a year almost exactly the right length.
