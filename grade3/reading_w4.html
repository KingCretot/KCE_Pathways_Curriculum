🏠 The Last S'more

Sometimes a story doesn't just tell you what happened — it teaches you something. Read what happens back at Theo's campfire:

After dinner, Theo and Mia roasted marshmallows over the campfire. There was only ONE marshmallow left in the bag. Theo reached for it first — his fingers were almost touching it — when he glanced at Mia. She was staring at the empty bag with a small, disappointed frown, trying hard not to show it.

👁 Watch Me Think

I notice something here: the story is SLOWING DOWN to focus on this one small moment — a marshmallow. Why would the author spend so many words on something so small? That's usually a clue that something important is about to happen. I also notice Mia's face is described in detail (trying not to show her disappointment) — the author wants me to NOTICE her feelings, even though Theo hasn't said anything yet.

🧩 Picture It: Reading Between the Lines

What's SAID: only one marshmallow is left
What's NOT said directly: Mia really wants it too
What I can INFER: this moment is about to test Theo's choice

🤝 Let's Try Together

Why do you think the author described Mia's face instead of just saying “Mia was sad”? Showing DETAILS (a frown she's trying to hide) lets the reader figure out the feeling themselves — that's called inferring, and it makes a story feel more real.

🧠 What Would You Do?

Now let's see what Theo decides. Keep reading:

Theo's hand hovered over the marshmallow. He could hear his stomach thinking about how good it would taste. But he kept looking at Mia's face. He remembered how she'd begged for weeks to come on this trip. Slowly, Theo picked up the marshmallow — and broke it in half. “Here,” he said, holding out the bigger piece to Mia. “Race you to see who can make theirs melt the gooiest.”

👁 Watch Me Think

This is the turning point! Theo COULD have kept the whole marshmallow — nobody would have stopped him. But he made a choice: he broke it in half AND gave Mia the bigger piece. Notice he didn't make it a big serious moment either — he turned it into a game (“race you”) so Mia wouldn't feel like it was charity. That's thoughtful, not just generous.

🧩 Picture It: The Turning Point

Theo COULD have: kept the whole marshmallow
Theo CHOSE to: share it, and give Mia the bigger half
HOW he did it: turned it into a fun game, not a big deal

🤝 Let's Try Together

Why does it matter that Theo gave Mia the BIGGER piece, not just an equal half? It shows he wasn't just following a rule (“share equally”) — he was genuinely putting her feelings first.

💡 Finding the Theme

Now let's zoom out. The theme of a story is the LESSON or big idea about life the whole story teaches — not just a summary of what happened, but what it MEANS.

👁 Watch Me Think

To find the theme, I ask myself: what did the CHARACTER learn, or what does the story want ME to think about? Theo could have kept the marshmallow, but chose to share — and it made the moment better for everyone, including him. The theme isn't “Theo ate a marshmallow.” That's just the plot. The theme is something bigger, like: sharing with people you care about feels better than keeping something all for yourself.

🧩 Picture It: Plot vs. Theme

PLOT (what happened): Theo shared the last marshmallow with Mia
THEME (the lesson): Sharing with someone you love feels better than keeping something for yourself

Theme is never just a character's name — it's a full idea about life.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Could this same story teach a DIFFERENT theme, like “always listen to your stomach”? No — that's the opposite of what actually happens and isn't supported by the story. A good theme has to be backed up by what really happens in the story.
