🖼️ Picture It: Two Baskets for Two Kinds of Sentences

A fact is something that is true and can be proved. An opinion tells what someone thinks, feels, or believes. People can disagree about an opinion, but you cannot disagree that a true fact is true.

🔍 The Prove It Test

Use the Prove It Test. Ask: Can I see it, count it, measure it, or look it up in a book I trust? If yes, it is a fact. Then ask: Could someone disagree? Does it use a feeling word like best, worst, should, favorite, I think, or I feel? If yes, it is an opinion.

Ask: Can I prove it? Could someone disagree? Then sort it!

📖 Read: Three Texts From Market Square

It is a sunny Saturday in San Antonio, Texas. Historic Market Square is a real market in downtown San Antonio, a short walk from the River Walk. Lupe’s mom, Ms. Delgado, runs a piñata shop there, and Lupe’s cousin Marcus is visiting from Chicago. In the plaza, they find three things to read: a market guide that Ms. Delgado wrote, a letter that a fruit stand owner named Mr. Pham sent to the city newspaper, and the journal Lupe kept at the shop. Some sentences are facts, and some are opinions. Let’s read them with Lupe and Marcus!

Read each text, or tap 🔊 to hear it. As you read, ask yourself: could I prove this sentence, or could someone disagree?

Market guide
Welcome to Historic Market Square
By Ms. Delgado, Piñata Shop Owner
1

Historic Market Square is in downtown San Antonio, Texas. It is a short walk from the River Walk. The River Walk is a path along the San Antonio River, one level below the city streets.

2

Market Square has about 100 shops, restaurants, and food stands. Strings of cut-paper banners called papel picado hang above the plaza. Many of the shops are owned by local families.

3

Shops sell pottery, piñatas, and handmade crafts. Visitors can walk in for free. On many weekends, people play live music and dance in the plaza. In my opinion, the main plaza is the best spot in San Antonio to hear music.

Letter to the Westside Weekly newspaper
Let’s Plant More Shade Trees!
By Mr. Pham, Fruit Stand Owner
1

Dear Editor, I think our city should plant more shade trees in the plaza at Market Square. Right now, on hot afternoons, visitors crowd into every small patch of shade.

2

Shade trees would keep the plaza cooler, so children could play there safely on hot days. Trees would also help our shops, because visitors would stay at the market longer. Last summer, about 300 people stood in the shade of my fruit stand on the busiest days.

3

Shopping at Market Square is the most fun thing to do in the whole city. Please come to the city council meeting on March 4 and speak up for more shade trees! Sincerely, Mr. Pham

🔎 What Is a Fact?

A fact is something that is true and can be proved. You can prove a fact by seeing it, counting it, measuring it, or looking it up in a book or website you trust. A fact stays true no matter who reads it.

👁 Watch Me Think

The guide says, The River Walk is a path along the San Antonio River, one level below the city streets. Can I prove it? Yes! I could look it up in a book about San Antonio, or walk down the steps to the River Walk myself. So this sentence is a fact.

🧩 Picture It: The Prove It Test

Can I SEE it?
Can I COUNT it or MEASURE it?
Can I LOOK IT UP?
If the answer is yes, it is a FACT.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Lupe writes, A family bought 6 small piñatas for a birthday party. Could someone count them? Yes, Marcus and Mom counted them too. A sentence you can prove by counting is a fact.

💭 What Is an Opinion?

An opinion tells what someone thinks, feels, or believes. People can disagree about an opinion, and you cannot prove it true for everyone. Signal words help you spot opinions: best, worst, most beautiful, favorite, should, I think, I feel, and I believe.

👁 Watch Me Think

Lupe writes, It was the most beautiful decoration I have ever seen! Can I prove most beautiful? No. Marcus might think the piñatas were prettier. Most beautiful is a signal word that shows a feeling. So this sentence is an opinion.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Mr. Pham writes, Our city should plant more shade trees. The word should tells what he believes. Could someone at the city council meeting disagree? Yes! So this sentence is an opinion.

🧩 Tricky Sentences: Split and Test

Some sentences mix a fact and an opinion. Split the sentence into parts and test each part. Watch out for two tricks. A number does not make a sentence a fact if it also has a feeling word. And a text that is mostly facts can still slip in an opinion.

👁 Watch Me Think

The market guide is full of facts, but it says, In my opinion, the main plaza is the best spot in San Antonio to hear music. The words in my opinion and best are signals. Even in a fact-filled guide, this sentence is an opinion.

🧩 Picture It: Split It!

Mom’s shop has 3 rooms, | and it is the coolest shop in the market.
Part 1: FACT. You can count the rooms.
Part 2: OPINION. Coolest is a feeling word.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Mr. Pham writes, Last summer, about 300 people stood in the shade of my fruit stand on the busiest days. Is there a feeling word? No. Could someone count the people? Yes. So this sentence is a fact, even though it is in a letter full of opinions.

✍️ Write Your Own Opinion With a Reason

When you write an opinion, say what you think AND tell why. A strong opinion sentence has two parts: your opinion, then the word because, then a reason. The best reasons are facts or examples that really match the opinion.

👁 Watch Me Think

Mr. Pham’s opinion is, Our city should plant more shade trees. One reason is, Shade trees would keep the plaza cooler, so children could play there safely on hot days. The reason tells WHY more trees are a good idea, and it matches the opinion. That is a strong reason.

✏️ On your paper: Finish this opinion. I think the best job in a market shop is ______ because ______. Read it out loud. Does your reason tell WHY?

🤝 Let's Try Together

Marcus thinks hanging piñatas is fun. A weak reason is, because piñatas come in many shapes. That is true, but it does not tell why hanging them is fun. A strong reason is, because he gets to climb the step stool and watch the piñatas spin in the air.

Market guide
Welcome to Historic Market Square
By Ms. Delgado, Piñata Shop Owner
1

Historic Market Square is in downtown San Antonio, Texas. It is a short walk from the River Walk. The River Walk is a path along the San Antonio River, one level below the city streets.

2

Market Square has about 100 shops, restaurants, and food stands. Strings of cut-paper banners called papel picado hang above the plaza. Many of the shops are owned by local families.

3

Shops sell pottery, piñatas, and handmade crafts. Visitors can walk in for free. On many weekends, people play live music and dance in the plaza. In my opinion, the main plaza is the best spot in San Antonio to hear music.

Letter to the Westside Weekly newspaper
Let’s Plant More Shade Trees!
By Mr. Pham, Fruit Stand Owner
1

Dear Editor, I think our city should plant more shade trees in the plaza at Market Square. Right now, on hot afternoons, visitors crowd into every small patch of shade.

2

Shade trees would keep the plaza cooler, so children could play there safely on hot days. Trees would also help our shops, because visitors would stay at the market longer. Last summer, about 300 people stood in the shade of my fruit stand on the busiest days.

3

Shopping at Market Square is the most fun thing to do in the whole city. Please come to the city council meeting on March 4 and speak up for more shade trees! Sincerely, Mr. Pham
