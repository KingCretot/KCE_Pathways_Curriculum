🧵 Picture It: Quilting Day in Gee's Bend

It's quilting day in Gee's Bend, Alabama, a small community where the Alabama River curves around the land. Aunt Loretta, a quilter, is teaching Tasha, Andre, and Lily to sew a quilt top from strips and squares of fabric.

Every strip is the same length. Tasha has sewn 58 of her strip, and Andre has sewn 38 of his. "Let's compare," says Aunt Loretta. "Both fractions have the same denominator, 8, so the pieces are the same size. Now look at the numerator: 5 pieces is more than 3 pieces."

Then Lily cuts one square into 3 equal parts and a same-size square into 6 equal parts. "Each unit fraction piece of 13 is bigger than a piece of 16," she notices. "So 13 is greater than 16, and 16 is less than 13!"

🏠 Gee's Bend Quilt Facts

  • Gee's Bend is a small community in Alabama. The Alabama River wraps around it on three sides.
  • Quilters in Gee's Bend have made quilts for many generations, and families pass the skill down to their children.
  • Gee's Bend quilts have been shown in famous art museums across the United States.
  • In 2006, the United States Postal Service made stamps that showed Gee's Bend quilts.
  • A ferry boat carries people across the river between Gee's Bend and the town of Camden.

Today you will compare fractions. When the denominators are the same, compare the numerators. When the numerators are the same, compare the denominators. You will show each answer with <, >, or = and give a reason!

🌟 Word Wrangler

Flip each card to learn this week's vocabulary! Each card shows what the word means and a sentence from the story. Tap the speaker to hear the word or the meaning.

compare
To decide which amount is greater, which is less, or if they are equal.“"Let's compare," says Aunt Loretta.”
numerator
The top number in a fraction. It tells how many equal parts we are counting.“Now look at the numerator: 5 pieces is more than 3 pieces.”
denominator
The bottom number in a fraction. It tells how many equal parts make the whole.“Both fractions have the same denominator, 8, so the pieces are the same size.”
unit fraction
A fraction with 1 on top, like 1/3. It names one equal part of a whole.“Each unit fraction piece of 1/3 is bigger than a piece of 1/6.”
greater than (>)
More than. The symbol > opens toward the greater amount.“So 1/3 is greater than 1/6.”
less than (<)
Not as much as. The symbol < points to the smaller amount.“1/6 is less than 1/3!”

Here is a memory trick: the symbols < and > are like a hungry mouth. The open side always faces the greater amount, and the point faces the smaller amount. So 58 > 38 and 16 < 13.

🧵 Same Denominator: Compare the Numerators

Aunt Loretta lays two same-size strips side by side. Each strip is cut into 8 equal parts. Tasha sewed 5 parts, so she sewed 58. Andre sewed 3 parts, so he sewed 38. Since both strips are cut into eighths, every piece is the same size. 5 pieces is more than 3 pieces, so 58 > 38.

👁 Watch Me Think

When the denominators are the same, the pieces are the same size. So I only need to compare the numerators. The fraction with more pieces is greater. I also check that the two wholes are the same size.

So 58 > 38

✂️ Same Numerator: Compare the Piece Sizes

Lily has two same-size squares of fabric. She cuts the first square into 3 equal parts and the second square into 6 equal parts. She sews 2 parts of each. Both fractions have the same numerator, 2. But thirds are bigger pieces than sixths, so 23 > 26.

👁 Watch Me Think

When the numerators are the same, I am counting the same number of pieces. So I look at the size of the pieces. The more equal parts a whole is cut into, the smaller each part is. The fraction with the smaller denominator has bigger pieces, so it is greater.

⚖️ Using <, >, and = With Reasons

Andre writes the comparisons on a card for the quilt table. He writes 58 > 38, 16 < 13, and 44 = 88. Aunt Loretta smiles: "44 and 88 are both 1 whole, so they are equal!" Then she holds up a big bed quilt and a tiny doll quilt. "Half of the bed quilt is much more fabric than half of the doll quilt. You can only compare fractions fairly when the wholes are the same size."

👁 Watch Me Think

First I check that the wholes are the same size. Then I decide which fraction is greater, or if they are equal. I write the symbol so the open side faces the greater fraction. Last, I say my reason: same denominator, so compare numerators, or same numerator, so compare piece sizes.

🧩 Picture It

> means is greater than
< means is less than
= means is equal to
44 = 88 = 1 whole

🎀 Who Has More? Fraction Word Problems

Tasha and Andre each have a same-size ribbon cut into 8 equal parts. Tasha used 3 parts for her quilt, so she has 58 left. Andre used 6 parts, so he has 28 left. Who has more ribbon left? 58 > 28, so Tasha has more left.

👁 Watch Me Think

In a two-step problem, I read carefully. Step 1: I find the fractions the question is really asking about, like the part that is left. Step 2: I compare them with my rules. To put three fractions in order, I compare them two at a time.

🧩 Picture It

Tasha: 8 − 3 = 5 parts left, 58
Andre: 8 − 6 = 2 parts left, 28
58 > 28

🤝 Let's Try Together

Put 12, 18, and 14 in order from least to greatest. All are 1 piece, so the smallest pieces come first: 18, 14, 12.
