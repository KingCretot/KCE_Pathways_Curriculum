🌅 What Is Cause & Effect, Really?

Mia woke up to find puddles on the stockroom floor. Something had caused it — but WHAT? She decided to investigate like a detective, working backward from the effect to find the cause.

👁 Watch Me Think

A cause always comes BEFORE its effect in time, even if the story doesn't tell events in that order. To find a cause, I ask “why did this happen?” To find an effect, I ask “what happened because of this?” Good readers can spot cause and effect even when the author doesn't use obvious clue words like “because.”

🧩 Picture It: Cause → Effect

CAUSE: A storm hit overnight
↓
EFFECT: Puddles appeared on the floor

🤝 Let's Try Together

Mia noticed the stockroom smelled musty. The CAUSE was standing water from the storm; the musty smell was the EFFECT.

🔄 Chains: One Thing Leads to Another

Mia traced the puddles back to a loose shutter on the stockroom window. But that was just the START of a much longer chain! The loose shutter cracked in the wind, which let rain inside, which soaked the boxes, which made them collapse, which spilled the goods, which ruined them — leaving less inventory for opening day.

👁 Watch Me Think

Just like a line of dominoes, a chain reaction happens when one effect becomes the NEXT cause, which creates a NEW effect, and so on. To trace a chain, I find the very first cause, then ask “and then what happened?” over and over until I reach the final effect.

🧩 Picture It: The Storm Chain

Loose shutter → cracked in wind
→ rain got inside
→ boxes got soaked
→ boxes collapsed
→ goods spilled and were ruined
→ less inventory for opening day

🔄 One Event, Two Jobs

Mia made an important discovery: “boxes got soaked” wasn't JUST an effect of the rain getting in — it was ALSO the cause of the boxes collapsing! In a chain, most events play a DOUBLE role: they're the effect of what came before, and the cause of what comes next.

👁 Watch Me Think

When I'm in the middle of a chain, I ask two questions about the SAME event: “what caused this?” (looking backward) and “what did this cause?” (looking forward). Only the very FIRST event in a chain is just a cause, and only the very LAST event is just an effect — everything in between does double duty.

🧩 Picture It: Double Role

← Rain got inside (this is the EFFECT of the crack)
BOXES GOT SOAKED
this is the CAUSE of the collapse →
