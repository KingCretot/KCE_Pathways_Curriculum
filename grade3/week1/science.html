🐿 Living or Nonliving?

On the trail, you'll see all kinds of things. Living things — organisms — share four traits: they grow, need food and water, respond to their surroundings, and reproduce (make more of their kind). A nonliving thing does NONE of these on its own, even if it moves.

👁 Watch Me Think

You spot a squirrel on a branch. Does it GROW? Yes. NEED food/water? Yes. RESPOND to you? Yes — it darts away. REPRODUCE? Yes. Four for four: living. Now a trail marker rock: doesn't grow, doesn't need food, doesn't respond, can't make more rocks. Zero for four: nonliving.

🧩 Picture It: The 4-Trait Test

SQUIRREL: grows✓ needs food/water✓ responds✓ reproduces✓ = LIVING
ROCK: grows✗ needs food/water✗ responds✗ reproduces✗ = NONLIVING

Run any trailside object through this same 4-question test.

🤝 Let's Try Together: A Campfire

Tricky one! A campfire moves, flickers, and seems to "grow" bigger. Alive? Run the test: does it need FOOD the way an organism does? No. Can it REPRODUCE more fires on its own? No. It fails — movement alone doesn't make something alive.

💧 The Four Basic Needs

Every organism needs four things to survive (stay alive): food (which gives it nutrients to grow and stay healthy), water, air, and shelter/space to live safely.

👁 Watch Me Think

Let’s check off a squirrel’s needs one at a time. Food/nutrients? Nuts and seeds. Water? A stream nearby. Air? All around it outdoors. Shelter? A hole in a tree. All four boxes checked — that squirrel can survive. Now here’s the important part: if even ONE box goes unchecked for too long — say, no water for days — the whole system breaks down, no matter how much food it has. All four needs matter together, not just one.

🧩 Picture It: A Needs Checklist

SQUIRREL: ☐ nuts/seeds  ☐ stream  ☐ outdoor air  ☐ tree hole
GOLDFISH: ☐ fish flakes  ☐ tank water  ☐ oxygen in water  ☐ the tank itself

Try making this checklist for a pet or animal you know!

🤝 Let’s Try Together: A Classroom Hamster

Let’s build the checklist together. Food? Hamster pellets. Water? A water bottle clipped to the cage. Shelter? The cage itself, with a little hideaway. What’s left? Air — and that one’s already all around us in the room! Notice something: air is usually the easiest need to forget about, because we never have to think about supplying it ourselves.

🏕️ Needs on the Trail

People are organisms too, so we need the same four things! That’s exactly why hikers plan carefully before a camping trip: they pack food and water bottles, they camp somewhere with fresh air, and they set up a tent for shelter.

👁 Watch Me Think

When I pack a hiking backpack, I actually think through the same four-need checklist scientists use for any organism. Food? Trail mix. Water? A water bottle. Shelter? A tent for overnight. Air? Already free on the trail, so I don’t pack anything for that one. Packing a backpack isn’t just about being prepared — it’s literally meeting the same four basic needs every living thing has.

🧩 Picture It: A Habitat Match-Up

Squirrel’s habitat → forest (has trees, food, water nearby)
Fish’s habitat → body of water (has food, oxygen, shelter in the water)
Human hiker’s "habitat" for one night → a campsite (food packed in, water nearby, tent for shelter)

A habitat is just a place that naturally has everything an organism needs.

🤝 Let’s Try Together: A Desert Lizard

Let’s figure out a lizard’s habitat needs together. Food? Insects. Water? It gets most of its water from the food it eats, since deserts don’t have much standing water. Shelter? A burrow underground, away from the hot sun. See how the SAME four needs look different depending on where an organism lives? A desert habitat solves the same problems in a different way than a forest does.
