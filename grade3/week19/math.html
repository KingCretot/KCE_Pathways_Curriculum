🍁 Picture It: Sugaring Season at the Maple Farm

It's a chilly March morning at the Bouchard Family Maple Farm, in a small village in the hills of Vermont. The days are warm, the nights are freezing, and the sap is running! Mr. Bouchard needs help from Nora, Theo, and Amara on the busiest day of sugaring season.

The team will watch the clock while the sap boils, figure out the elapsed time of each job, measure sap in liters, and weigh maple sugar candy in grams. Every job needs careful measuring!

🍁 Vermont Maple Facts

  • Vermont makes more maple syrup than any other state in the United States.
  • Farmers collect sap in late winter and early spring, when days are above freezing and nights are below freezing.
  • A sugarhouse is the building where sap is boiled down into syrup.
  • It takes about 40 liters of sap to make just 1 liter of maple syrup.
  • Montpelier, the capital of Vermont, is the smallest state capital in the United States by number of people.

Today you will tell time to the exact minute, find how much time passes from start to end, and solve word problems about how much liquid a container holds (liquid volume) and how heavy something is (mass).

⏰ Telling Time to the Minute

Mr. Bouchard checks the sugarhouse clock before the first boil. The short hour hand is just past the 7. The long minute hand points 3 small marks past the 9. He counts by fives to the 9: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45. Then he counts on by ones: 46, 47, 48. It is 7:48.

👁 Watch Me Think

First I read the hour hand. If it is between two numbers, the hour is the number it has passed, not the one it is heading toward. Then I read the minute hand. Each big number is 5 minutes, so I count by fives, and each small mark is 1 minute, so I count on by ones. A full trip around the clock is 60 minutes.

🧩 Picture It

Minute hand on the 9: 9 × 5 = 45
3 more small marks: 45 + 3 = 48
Hour hand past the 7: it is 7:48

🤝 Let's Try Together

The minute hand points 2 small marks past the 4, and the hour hand is between the 10 and the 11. Count by fives to the 4: 20. Count on 2 more: 22. It is 10:22. Because the farm is busy in the morning, we write 10:22 a.m.

⏳ Elapsed Time: How Much Time Passes

Nora starts boiling the sap at 8:15 a.m., right after breakfast. The syrup is ready at 9:50 a.m. How long did it boil? Nora jumps on a number line: from 8:15 to 9:00 is 45 minutes. From 9:00 to 9:50 is 50 minutes. 45 + 50 = 95 minutes, which is 1 hour 35 minutes.

👁 Watch Me Think

To find elapsed time, I jump to the next o'clock time first, then jump the rest of the way, and add my jumps. I never subtract clock times like regular numbers, because an hour has 60 minutes, not 100. To find an end time, I jump forward. To find a start time, I jump backward.

🧩 Picture It

8:15 → 9:00 is 45 minutes
9:00 → 9:50 is 50 minutes
45 + 50 = 95 minutes = 1 hour 35 minutes

🤝 Let's Try Together

A batch of maple taffy starts cooking at 10:40 a.m. and cooks for 35 minutes. Jump 20 minutes to 11:00. That leaves 35 − 20 = 15 more minutes. 11:00 + 15 minutes = 11:15 a.m.

🥛 Liquid Volume: Liters of Sap

Theo collects sap from the maple trees. He empties 6 buckets, and each bucket holds 8 liters. That is 6 equal groups of 8: 6 × 8 = 48 liters. He pours it all into a tank that holds 50 liters. How much more sap will fit? 50 − 48 = 2 liters.

👁 Watch Me Think

A liter measures liquid volume, which is how much liquid a container holds. A large water bottle holds about 1 liter. In a word problem, I ask: Am I joining amounts (add)? Taking some away (subtract)? Are there equal groups (multiply)? Am I sharing into equal parts (divide)? To read a measuring jug, I find the top of the liquid and read the mark next to it. This jug shows 8 liters.

🧩 Picture It

Equal groups: 6 × 8 = 48 liters
Room left: 50 − 48 = 2 liters

🤝 Let's Try Together

The boiling pan holds 36 liters. Nora fills it with 4 pails that hold the same amount. How much does each pail hold? Sharing into 4 equal parts means divide: 36 ÷ 4 = 9 liters.

⚖️ Mass: Grams and Kilograms

Amara helps pack the farm stand. Each bag of maple sugar has a mass of 5 kilograms. She puts 4 bags in a crate: 4 × 5 = 20 kilograms. Then she weighs one maple sugar candy on a small scale. It is only about 20 grams!

👁 Watch Me Think

Mass tells how heavy something is. I use grams for light things, like a candy or a paper clip, and kilograms for heavy things, like a bag of sugar or a pumpkin. I only add or subtract masses when they use the same unit. To read a scale, I check what each small mark is worth before I count.

🧩 Picture It

Each small mark = 50 grams
Pointer on 200 = 200 grams
4 bags × 5 kilograms = 20 kilograms

🤝 Let's Try Together

Ten candies go in a gift box, and each candy is 20 grams. The candies have a mass of 10 × 20 = 200 grams, so the scale pointer lands on 200.
