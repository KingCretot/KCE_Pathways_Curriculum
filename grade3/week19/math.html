🎈 Picture It: Morning at the Balloon Fiesta

It's a chilly October morning at Balloon Fiesta Park in Albuquerque, New Mexico. The sun is rising over the Sandia Mountains, and hundreds of hot air balloons are getting ready to fly! Pilot Ms. Yazzie needs help from her ground crew, Ivy, Nico, and Luz, on the busiest morning of the Fiesta.

The crew will watch the clock for each launch, figure out the elapsed time of each job, measure water and cocoa in liters, and weigh sandbags in kilograms. Every job needs careful measuring!

🎈 Balloon Fiesta Facts

  • The Albuquerque International Balloon Fiesta is the largest balloon festival in the world. It lasts nine days in early October.
  • The first Balloon Fiesta, in 1972, had just 13 balloons in a shopping mall parking lot. Today, more than 500 balloons come.
  • A hot air balloon rises because a burner heats the air inside it. The burner uses propane, a fuel kept as a liquid in tanks in the basket.
  • Pilots love the Albuquerque Box. Near the ground the wind often blows one way, and higher up it blows the other way, so a balloon can float out and come back near where it started.
  • The hot air balloon is the official state aircraft of New Mexico.

Today you will tell time to the exact minute, find how much time passes from start to end, and solve word problems about how much liquid a container holds (liquid volume) and how heavy something is (mass).

⏰ Telling Time to the Minute

Ms. Yazzie checks the crew tent clock before the first launch. The short hour hand is just past the 7. The long minute hand points 3 small marks past the 9. She counts by fives to the 9: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45. Then she counts on by ones: 46, 47, 48. It is 7:48.

👁 Watch Me Think

First I read the hour hand. If it is between two numbers, the hour is the number it has passed, not the one it is heading toward. Then I read the minute hand. Each big number is 5 minutes, so I count by fives, and each small mark is 1 minute, so I count on by ones. A full trip around the clock is 60 minutes.

🧩 Picture It

Minute hand on the 9: 9 × 5 = 45
3 more small marks: 45 + 3 = 48
Hour hand past the 7: it is 7:48

🤝 Let's Try Together

The minute hand points 2 small marks past the 4, and the hour hand is between the 10 and the 11. Count by fives to the 4: 20. Count on 2 more: 22. It is 10:22. Because the crew is still busy in the morning, we write 10:22 a.m.

⏳ Elapsed Time: How Much Time Passes

Ms. Yazzie’s balloon lifts off at 8:15 a.m., right after breakfast. It lands at 9:50 a.m. How long was the flight? Ivy jumps on a number line: from 8:15 to 9:00 is 45 minutes. From 9:00 to 9:50 is 50 minutes. 45 + 50 = 95 minutes, which is 1 hour 35 minutes.

👁 Watch Me Think

To find elapsed time, I jump to the next o'clock time first, then jump the rest of the way, and add my jumps. I never subtract clock times like regular numbers, because an hour has 60 minutes, not 100. To find an end time, I jump forward. To find a start time, I jump backward.

🧩 Picture It

8:15 → 9:00 is 45 minutes
9:00 → 9:50 is 50 minutes
45 + 50 = 95 minutes = 1 hour 35 minutes

🤝 Let's Try Together

The crew starts packing up the balloon at 10:40 a.m. and works for 35 minutes. Jump 20 minutes to 11:00. That leaves 35 − 20 = 15 more minutes. 11:00 + 15 minutes = 11:15 a.m.

🥛 Liquid Volume: Liters of Water and Cocoa

Nico carries water to the crew. He brings 6 jugs, and each jug holds 8 liters. That is 6 equal groups of 8: 6 × 8 = 48 liters. He pours it all into a big water cooler that holds 50 liters. How much more water will fit? 50 − 48 = 2 liters.

👁 Watch Me Think

A liter measures liquid volume, which is how much liquid a container holds. A large water bottle holds about 1 liter. In a word problem, I ask: Am I joining amounts (add)? Taking some away (subtract)? Are there equal groups (multiply)? Am I sharing into equal parts (divide)? To read a measuring jug, I find the top of the liquid and read the mark next to it. This jug shows 8 liters.

🧩 Picture It

Equal groups: 6 × 8 = 48 liters
Room left: 50 − 48 = 2 liters

🤝 Let's Try Together

The big cocoa urn holds 36 liters. Ivy fills it with 4 pitchers that hold the same amount. How much does each pitcher hold? Sharing into 4 equal parts means divide: 36 ÷ 4 = 9 liters.

⚖️ Mass: Grams and Kilograms

Luz helps load the chase truck. Each sandbag for the crew tent has a mass of 5 kilograms. She puts 4 sandbags in the truck: 4 × 5 = 20 kilograms. Then she weighs one Balloon Fiesta pin on a small scale. It is only about 10 grams!

👁 Watch Me Think

Mass tells how heavy something is. I use grams for light things, like a pin or a paper clip, and kilograms for heavy things, like a sandbag or a pumpkin. I only add or subtract masses when they use the same unit. To read a scale, I check what each small mark is worth before I count.

🧩 Picture It

Each small mark = 50 grams
Pointer on 200 = 200 grams
4 sandbags × 5 kilograms = 20 kilograms

🤝 Let's Try Together

Twenty pins go in a display box, and each pin is 10 grams. The pins have a mass of 20 × 10 = 200 grams, so the scale pointer lands on 200.
