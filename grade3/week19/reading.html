🌟 Word Wrangler

Flip each card to learn this week's vocabulary! Tap 🔊 to hear the word, then flip and tap 🔊 to hear what it means and see how the play uses it.

cast
The list of characters in a play. In the play: the Cast of Characters names Nana Pilar, Ezra, Mei, Mr. Tsosie, and Pepita.
scene
One part of a play that happens in one place and time. In the play: “Scene 2: Inside Studio A.”
dialogue
The words the characters say. In the play: “EZRA: Hurry, Mei!”
stage directions
Notes in parentheses that tell actors how to move or speak. In the play: “(The cactus wren flies away.)”
airtime
The time when a show is being sent out over the radio. In the play: “We practice about fifteen minutes for every one minute of airtime.”
patient
Able to wait calmly without rushing. In the play: “And I learned the most important step. Be patient.”

🖼️ Picture It: The Parts of a Play

A play is a story that is written to be acted out on a stage. Actors read a script. A script has four main parts. Look for the numbers on the script page.

1: Scene heading

Tells which scene it is and where and when it happens.

2: Character name

Written in capital letters with a colon. It tells who is talking.

3: Stage direction

Inside (parentheses) and slanted. It tells actors how to move or sound. Do not read it out loud.

4: Dialogue

The words the character says. No quotation marks are needed.

🎭 Read Like an Actor

When you read a play, read only the dialogue out loud, the words after each name. Use the stage directions to know how to move and how to sound. Check the scene heading to know where and when you are.

Ask: Who is talking? Where are we? What are they doing?

📖 Read: On the Air in Tucson

It is a sunny Saturday in March in Tucson, Arizona. Tucson is a city in the Sonoran Desert, where giant saguaro cactuses grow. The play you are about to read happens at Mesquite Corner Radio, a small neighborhood radio station where kids help put on a show called Kid Voices. First, read the cast list to meet the characters. Then read all three scenes.

Read each part, or tap 🔊 to hear it. As you read, notice who is talking and what the stage directions tell you.

A Play in Three Scenes
On the Air in Tucson
Setting: A neighborhood radio station in Tucson, Arizona, on a Saturday in March
🎭

CAST OF CHARACTERSNANA PILAR: host of Kid Voices, the kids’ show at Mesquite Corner Radio; patient and wiseEZRA: her grandson, age 9; always in a hurryMEI: Ezra’s neighbor, age 8; asks lots of questionsMR. TSOSIE: runs the bookshop on Fourth AvenuePEPITA: the station cat (does not speak)

Scene 1
The Desert Garden
(Early morning. A tall saguaro cactus stands in the gravel garden behind the radio station. A cactus wren sings from the top.)
1

(Ezra runs in, carrying a sound recorder. Mei follows, out of breath.)EZRA: Hurry, Mei! If we record the bird fast, we can put it on the show by lunch!MEI: (panting) Ezra, wait! Why are we recording a bird?NANA PILAR: (walking in slowly, smiling) Good question, Mei. Today our show is all about the Sonoran Desert. The cactus wren is Arizona’s state bird. Its song will let our listeners hear the desert right in their kitchens.

2

MEI: (pointing up) It is singing from the top of that giant cactus!NANA PILAR: The cactus wren sings loudly, but loud noises can scare it away. We must be very quiet.EZRA: (running toward the cactus) Then let’s record it right now!(Ezra’s shoes crunch loudly on the gravel. The cactus wren flies away. PEPITA jumps onto a bench and watches it go.)NANA PILAR: (shaking her head kindly) Slow down, Ezra. A song we scare away is a song we cannot record.

Scene 2
Inside Studio A
(Later that morning. A big microphone hangs over a table covered with buttons and sliders.)
1

MEI: (pulling on big headphones) Whoa! These headphones make everything sound so loud!NANA PILAR: The microphone picks up every little sound. We practice so our words come out clear and smooth.EZRA: (tapping his foot) Is it time yet? Is it time yet?NANA PILAR: Not yet. We practice about fifteen minutes for every one minute of airtime.EZRA: (eyes wide) Fifteen? That will take all day!

2

MEI: (sadly) So the bird we scared away is a song our listeners will never get to hear.EZRA: (quietly, looking at the floor) I guess rushing did not make anything faster.NANA PILAR: (putting a hand on his shoulder) A good show cannot be rushed. Here, you can be in charge of the clock. When it says four o’clock, tell me, and we will go on the air.(Ezra sits on a stool and watches the clock closely.)

Scene 3
Studio A, On the Air
(Late afternoon. A red ON AIR sign hangs over the studio door. It is not lit yet.)
1

EZRA: (jumping up) Nana! The clock says four o’clock!NANA PILAR: (switching on the ON AIR sign) Right on time, Ezra. You watched it perfectly.(The red sign lights up. Everyone leans toward the microphones.)MEI: (in a clear, smooth voice) Good afternoon, Tucson! Welcome to Kid Voices!

2

(When the show ends, the sign turns off. MR. TSOSIE knocks and opens the door, holding a small radio.)MR. TSOSIE: (holding up his radio) I heard the whole show at my bookshop! Your voices were so clear. Who made this show?MEI: (proudly) We all did. Ezra was the clock boss.EZRA: (grinning) And I learned the most important step. Be patient.(Everyone laughs. Pepita swishes her tail. The lights fade.)THE END

🎭 Cast, Setting & Scenes, Part 1

Every play starts with a cast of characters. The cast is a list of everyone in the play, often with a few words about each one. Next comes the setting, which tells where and when the play happens. Then the play is broken into parts called scenes. A new scene begins when the place or the time changes.

👁 Watch Me Think

I look at the beginning of the play. The cast list names five characters. The setting is a neighborhood radio station in Tucson, Arizona, on a Saturday in March. Then I see three scene headings: the desert garden in the early morning, Studio A later that morning, and Studio A in the late afternoon. Each new scene jumps to a new place or a new time.

🧩 Picture It: A Play Is Built in Parts

Cast of characters: who is in the play
Setting: where and when the play happens
Scenes: the parts of the play, like chapters in a book

🤝 Let's Try Together

Let’s try one together. Scene 2 and Scene 3 both happen inside Studio A. So why are they two different scenes? Look at the time! Scene 2 is in the morning, and Scene 3 is in the late afternoon. The time changed, so a new scene began.

💬 Dialogue, Part 1: Who Is Talking?

Dialogue is the words the characters say. A play is told mostly through dialogue. In a script, the character’s name comes first, in capital letters, followed by a colon. There are no quotation marks. What characters say can show you how they feel and what they are like.

👁 Watch Me Think

I read: EZRA: Then let’s record it right now! The name before the colon is EZRA, so Ezra is talking. He wants to record the bird right now. That shows me Ezra is in a hurry.

🧩 Picture It: Reading a Line of Dialogue

NAME: the words the character says
Look left of the colon: WHO is talking
Look right of the colon: WHAT they say

🤝 Let's Try Together

Let’s try one together. Read: NANA PILAR: Slow down, Ezra. Who is talking? The name before the colon is Nana Pilar. Who is she talking to? Ezra. What does she want? She wants him to stop rushing.

🎬 Stage Directions, Part 1

Stage directions are notes for the actors. They are written inside parentheses and are often in slanted letters. Stage directions tell actors how to move, how to sound, and what is happening on the stage. They can also describe the setting and sounds. Actors do NOT read stage directions out loud. They act them out.

👁 Watch Me Think

I read: EZRA: (running toward the cactus) Then let’s record it right now! The words in parentheses are a stage direction. The actor playing Ezra runs toward the cactus while he says his line. That shows me he is rushing, even before the bird flies away!

🧩 Picture It: Two Jobs of Stage Directions

Action: (The cactus wren flies away.)
Feeling or voice: (quietly, looking at the floor)

📝 Summarizing a Scene, Part 1

A summary is a short retelling of the most important events. To summarize a scene, answer four questions: Who is in the scene? Where does it happen? What happens? How does it end? Keep the events in order, use your own words, and leave out tiny details and your own opinions.

👁 Watch Me Think

Let me summarize Scene 1. Who? Nana Pilar, Ezra, and Mei. Where? The desert garden behind the station. What happened? Nana Pilar explains why they want to record the cactus wren, but Ezra rushes and scares it away. How did it end? Nana Pilar tells him to slow down. I left out small details, like Pepita jumping onto a bench.
