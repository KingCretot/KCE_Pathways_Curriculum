🏙️🏡🌾 Three Cousins, Three Communities

Meet three cousins in Florida. Florencia lives in an apartment in downtown Tampa, an urban community. In 2020, Tampa's population was 384,959. She can walk to stores, and big ships come into the port.

Fletcher lives in a house with a yard in Brandon, a suburban community east of Tampa, with 114,626 people in 2020. His mom drives to her job in Tampa. Quilla lives on a family ranch outside Arcadia, in DeSoto County, a rural area of farms, orange groves and cattle ranches. The whole county had 33,976 people in 2020.

👁 Watch Me Think

Which community has the most people? Let me read the numbers. Tampa has 384,959. Brandon has 114,626. All of DeSoto County has 33,976. So the city has the most, and the whole rural county has the fewest, even though it covers lots of land. The United States Census Bureau, the group that counts everyone every 10 years, says 80 out of every 100 Americans live in urban areas. It counts cities AND their busy suburbs as urban areas.

🚂 Why Communities Grow: The Story of Tampa

Long ago, Tampa was a small town. In the 1880s, Henry Plant's railroad reached Tampa and connected it to the rest of the country. Ships could also bring tobacco from Cuba into Tampa's port.

In 1885, Vicente Martínez Ybor moved his cigar business from Key West to Tampa. By 1886, his factories were rolling cigars. Immigrants came from Cuba, Spain and Italy to work there. The neighborhood that grew around the factories became Ybor City, and Tampa grew into a city.

👁 Watch Me Think

Communities grow for reasons. Let me follow the chain. First, the railroad and the port made it easy to move goods. Next, that brought factories. Factories meant jobs. Jobs brought people. People needed homes, stores and schools. So one cause led to an effect, and that effect caused another one.

🧩 Picture It: How Tampa Grew

🤝 Let's Try Together: Whose Story Is It?

Is Tampa's story only about Henry Plant and Vicente Martínez Ybor? No. Thousands of workers built the city, too. Cuban, Spanish and Italian families rolled the cigars and opened the shops. When we tell how a community grew, we ask: Who did the work, and whose names are missing?

🏡 Why Suburbs Spread

After World War II ended in 1945, millions of soldiers came home and needed houses. In 1947, a company called Levitt and Sons started building Levittown, New York, one of the first big suburbs. It began with about 2,000 homes and grew to more than 17,000.

More families owned cars, and new highways were built. People could live in a suburb and commute to jobs in the city. That is still true today: many families in Brandon commute to jobs in Tampa. As suburbs grow, farms and woods near cities are often turned into houses and stores.

👁 Watch Me Think

Was Levittown open to everyone? No. The rules said only white families could live in the houses, so Black veterans who had served in the war were kept out. In 1948, the United States Supreme Court said courts could not enforce rules like that, but unfair treatment in housing went on for many years. So when I read about a new suburb, I ask: Who was welcome, and who was left out?

🗺️ Reading a Land-Use Map

A land-use map shows how each part of a community is used. Homes are residential land. Stores and offices are commercial land. Factories are industrial land. Farms and ranches are agricultural land. Parks are for everyone to play and rest.

Town leaders and planners use maps like this to decide where new things should go. Neighbors can come to public meetings and share their ideas, too.

👁 Watch Me Think

Let me read the map. First, I find north with the compass rose: north is at the top, so the river is on the west side. Next, I use the map key to match each color and label to a land use. The factory is next to the river in the north. The farms are all on the east side. The park is on the west side, by the river.

🧩 Picture It: A Growing Town

Map key: Homes (residential)   Stores (commercial)   Factory (industrial)   Farm (agricultural)   Park (recreation)

🤝 Let's Try Together: Plan Like a Planner

The town wants a new grocery store. Where should it go? People shop in the commercial squares, and many homes are close to them. A store near the homes means shorter trips. So a good spot is next to the stores and homes, not out on the farms.

🕵️ Source Detective: Who Shapes a Community?

Read two real sources. One is a writer's big idea about cities. The other is a rule from early Levittown. Compare them.

Primary Sources: Jane Jacobs (1961) and a Levittown Lease (late 1940s)

Source 1: Jane Jacobs, The Death and Life of Great American Cities, 1961 (part of it)

"Cities have the capability of providing something for everybody, only because, and only when, they are created by everybody."

Word help: capability = the power to do something. created = made.

Source 2: A rule in early Levittown leases (part of it)

Homes could not "be used or occupied by any person other than members of the Caucasian race."

Word help: occupied = lived in. Caucasian race = the words the rule used for white people.

Source 1: Jane Jacobs, The Death and Life of Great American Cities (1961), chapter 12. Source 2: Levittown, New York, lease wording, as shown by the Smithsonian National Museum of African American History and Culture.

👁 Think Like a Historian

Let me compare. Source 1 says a city works for everybody only when everybody helps make it. Source 2 is a rule that kept Black families and other families of color out. The two sources point in opposite directions. Source 2 shows us a group that was left out. Asking "Who was missing?" helps us see the whole story of how communities grew.

🌟 People Who Built and Shaped Communities

Meet the people behind four real communities. As you read, ask: Why did this community grow or change, and who made it happen?

The People of Cahokia

about 1050–1150 · Illinois

Long before Europeans came, Mississippian people built a city called Cahokia, near today's St. Louis. At its biggest, about 10,000 to 20,000 people lived there. It was the largest city north of Mexico at that time. They built huge earth mounds. The biggest, Monks Mound, is about 100 feet tall. The name Cahokia comes from an Illinois Native group who lived there later.

Vicente Martínez Ybor

1818–1896 · born in Spain

He was born in Valencia, Spain, and moved to Cuba at age 14. He started making cigars there, then moved to Key West, Florida. In 1885 he moved his business to Tampa. Workers from Cuba, Spain and Italy came, and Ybor City grew up around his factories.

Allen Allensworth

1842–1914 · born in Kentucky

He was born into slavery in Louisville, Kentucky. He became an Army chaplain with the Buffalo Soldiers and retired in 1906 as a lieutenant colonel, the first African American to reach such a high rank. In 1908 he helped found Allensworth, California, a town founded, paid for and run by African Americans. The town's water supply ran low, crops failed, and the town shrank. Today it is a California state historic park.

Jane Jacobs

1916–2006 · born in Pennsylvania

She was born in Scranton, Pennsylvania, and lived in New York City. In the 1950s, a powerful city planner named Robert Moses planned a road through Washington Square Park. She and her neighbors fought the plan, and the park was closed to cars. In 1961 she wrote a famous book saying cities should be planned for the people who live in them.
