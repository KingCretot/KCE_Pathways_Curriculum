🌟 Word Wrangler

Flip each card to learn this week's vocabulary! Tap 🔊 to hear the word, then flip and tap 🔊 to hear what it means and see how the field guide uses it.

index
A list of topics in ABC order at the back of a book, with the pages where you can find them. In the field guide: “Page 9: Index.”
keyword
The most important word in your question, the one you look up. In the field guide: the keyword crickets leads to “crickets, 5” in the index.
sidebar
A box beside the main text with extra facts. In the field guide: “Sidebar: Fast Facts.”
passage
A long, narrow path through a cave. In the field guide: “Explorers have mapped more than 420 miles of passages.”
limestone
A soft rock that water can dissolve. In the field guide: “Mammoth Cave is carved in a rock called limestone.”
endangered
In danger of dying out forever. In the field guide: “It lives only in and near Mammoth Cave National Park, and it is endangered.”

🖼️ Picture It: The Map of a Field Guide

Nonfiction books have tools that help you find answers fast, without reading every page. Think of a field guide like a map with three parts: the front, the middle, and the back.

🚪 Front

Title and table of contents: the big sections, in page order.

📄 Middle

Headings, bold words, pictures with captions, diagrams with labels, and sidebars full of fast facts.

🔍 Back

Glossary: word meanings in ABC order. Index: topics and keywords in ABC order, with every page number.

🔎 Search Tools

Search tools help you jump to the answer. Pick a keyword, the topic word in your question. Look it up in the index, or type it in a search box online. Online, blue underlined words are hyperlinks: click one to jump to more information.

🔍 crickets→Index: crickets, 5→Page 5

Online example: Visitors walk on mats to slow white-nose syndrome.

Ask: What do I want to know? Which tool gets me there fastest?

Welcome to Mammoth CaveMammoth Cave National Park is in south-central Kentucky. Under the park lies Mammoth Cave, the longest known cave system in the world. Explorers have mapped more than 420 miles of passages, and they keep finding more! The name Mammoth means huge. It comes from the cave’s giant passages, not from woolly mammoths.

Caption: The Green River flows through Mammoth Cave National Park.
⭐ SIDEBAR: Fast Facts
Became a national park: 1941
Named a World Heritage Site: 1981
Temperature inside the cave: about 54 degrees Fahrenheit all year
3

How the Cave FormedMammoth Cave is carved in a rock called limestone. Rainwater soaks into the ground and picks up a little natural acid. Over millions of years, that water slowly dissolved the limestone and opened up the passages. A layer of tough sandstone sits on top like a roof and helps protect the cave.

4

Cave FormationsIn wet parts of the cave, dripping water leaves behind tiny bits of rock. Over thousands of years, the bits build up into shapes called formations. A stalactite hangs down from the ceiling. A stalagmite pushes up from the floor. When a stalactite and a stalagmite meet, they make a column. The most famous formation in Mammoth Cave is Frozen Niagara, a giant wall of rock called flowstone.

Caption: Diagram: Dripping water builds formations from the ceiling down and from the floor up.
5

Animals in the DarkSome animals spend their whole lives in the dark. The Kentucky cave shrimp has no eyes, and its body is see-through. It lives only in and near Mammoth Cave National Park, and it is endangered. The cave is also home to two kinds of eyeless cavefish, three kinds of cave crickets, and several kinds of bats.

Caption: The Kentucky cave shrimp grows to about 1.25 inches long.
6

Stephen Bishop, Cave ExplorerStephen Bishop was an enslaved African American man who arrived at Mammoth Cave in 1838. He began guiding visitors through the cave as a teenager. Stephen and a visitor crossed a deep hole called the Bottomless Pit, and many more miles of the cave opened up to explore. In 1842, Stephen drew a map of the cave’s passages from memory.

7

Visiting SafelyBring a light jacket, because the cave stays cool all year. Be ready for lots of stairs. Never touch the cave walls or formations. Even the oil from your hands can stop formations from growing. After your tour, you will walk across special mats. The mats help slow the spread of white-nose syndrome, a disease that harms bats.

📑 Table of Contents vs. Index, Part 1

The table of contents and the index both give page numbers, but they do different jobs. The table of contents is at the FRONT. It lists the big sections in page order. The index is at the BACK. It lists small topics and keywords in ABC order, with EVERY page where each one shows up.

👁 Watch Me Think

I want to read about bats. The table of contents has no section called Bats. So I flip to the index at the back. It says bats, 5, 7. Two pages! Page 5 tells about cave animals, and page 7 tells about a disease that harms bats. The index found both.

🧩 Picture It: Front or Back?

Big section? Use the table of contents (front).
Small topic or keyword? Use the index (back).
The index can list more than one page.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Let’s try one together. Where can we read about Frozen Niagara? It is not a section title, so it is not in the table of contents. In the index, F comes after crickets: Frozen Niagara, 4. Turn to page 4!

🔍 Keywords and Search Tools, Part 1

A keyword is the most important word in your question. Skip small words like how, the, and does. Look up the keyword in the index, or type it in a search box. If one keyword does not work, try another. A sidebar is a box beside the main text with extra facts, and online, a hyperlink jumps you to more information.

👁 Watch Me Think

My question is: How long does the Kentucky cave shrimp grow? The keyword is shrimp. In the index, I find shrimp, Kentucky cave, 5. On page 5, the caption says it grows to about 1.25 inches long. I found the answer without reading the whole guide.

🧩 Picture It: Search Like a Scientist

1. Ask your question.
2. Circle the keyword.
3. Look it up, then read that page.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Let’s try one together. Question: When did Stephen Bishop draw his map? Keywords: map or Bishop. The index says map, 6. Page 6 says 1842. Both keywords lead to the same page!

The most famous formation in Mammoth Cave is Frozen Niagara, a giant wall of rock called flowstone.
