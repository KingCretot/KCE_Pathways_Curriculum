🪵 Picture It: The Ozark Woodshop

High in the Ozark Mountains of Arkansas, near the town of Mountain View, Mr. Calloway runs a small woodworking shop. He makes birdhouses, wooden spoons, and toy cars to sell at the fall craft fair.

His helpers Clementine, Dashiell, and Delia measure every piece with a ruler. Their cousin Cormac writes the lengths down and puts them on a line plot, so Mr. Calloway can see all the sizes at once.

🌲 Ozark Mountain Facts

  • The Ozark Mountains, also called the Ozarks, cover parts of four states: Arkansas, Missouri, Oklahoma, and Kansas.
  • The Ozarks are covered with thick forests, and they are cut by rivers such as the White River and the Buffalo River.
  • The Boston Mountains, in Arkansas, are the highest part of the Ozarks.
  • The Ozark Folk Center State Park in Mountain View, Arkansas, opened in 1973. Visitors can watch old crafts such as woodcarving, broom making, blacksmithing, and pottery.

Here is a memory trick: a half inch cuts an inch into 2 pieces, like half a sandwich, and a quarter inch cuts it into 4 pieces, like the 4 quarters in a dollar.

📏 Measuring to the Half Inch

Clementine lays a wooden peg on the ruler and lines up its left end with the 0. Each inch on this ruler is split into 2 equal parts, so the small mark in the middle is the half inch mark. The peg ends at the half inch mark between 3 and 4, so it is 312 inches long.

👁 Watch Me Think

First I line up one end of the object with the 0. Then I find the last whole inch the object passes. If the object ends at the middle mark, I add a half: the length is that whole number and 12 more.

🧩 Picture It

Left end on 0 ✔
Passes the 3, stops at the middle mark → 3 and 12 more → 312 inches

🤝 Let's Try Together

Delia measures a spoon handle. It ends right on the 5. That is exactly 5 inches. If it had ended at the middle mark between 5 and 6, it would be 512 inches.

📐 Measuring to the Quarter Inch

Dashiell uses a ruler that splits every inch into 4 equal parts. Each part is a quarter inch, which is the same as 14 inch. His toy car axle ends at the third mark past 2, so it is 234 inches long.

👁 Watch Me Think

Between two whole numbers there are 3 marks. The first short mark is 14 past the whole number, the middle mark is 24, which is the same as 12, and the last short mark is 34. After 34 comes the next whole inch.

🧩 Picture It

2 → 214 → 212 → 234 → 3
The axle stops at the third mark past 2 → 234 inches

🤝 Let's Try Together

Clementine's carving knife handle stops at the first short mark after 4. That mark is 14 inch past 4, so the handle is 414 inches long.

❌ Making a Line Plot

Cormac wrote down the length of each peg Delia measured: 2, 212, 212, 3, 212, 312, 3, and 212 inches. A line plot is a number line with an X for each measurement, so Cormac draws one X above the right length for every peg.

👁 Watch Me Think

First I find the shortest and the longest lengths, so I know where my number line starts and ends. I label the line in the same steps as the ruler: halves or quarters. Then I take each measurement, one at a time, and draw one X above it. I cross it off my list so I do not count it twice.

🧩 Picture It

Shortest: 2   Longest: 312 → label 2, 212, 3, 312
212 appears 4 times → 4 X's above 212
8 pegs → 8 X's in all

🤝 Let's Try Together

Check: 1 + 4 + 2 + 1 = 8 X's, and Delia measured 8 pegs. Every peg has exactly one X, so the line plot is done!

🔍 Reading a Line Plot

Mr. Calloway's line plot shows the lengths of 12 birdhouse perches. The tallest stack of X's is above 312, so 312 inches is the most common length. The X farthest to the right is above 4, so the longest perch is 4 inches.

👁 Watch Me Think

To answer a question, I find the right lengths on the number line and count their X's. “Longer than” means the lengths to the right. “Shorter than” means the lengths to the left. “How many more” means I subtract the two counts.

🧩 Picture It

Most common: 312 (5 X's)
Longer than 312: 334 and 4 → 2 + 1 = 3 perches
312 vs. 314: 5 − 3 = 2 more perches

🤝 Let's Try Together

How many perches are shorter than 312 inches? Look to the left of 312: 3 has 1 X and 314 has 3 X's. 1 + 3 = 4 perches are shorter.
