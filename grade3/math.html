🏕️ Basecamp: Comparing Numbers

You spot two trail signs: Trail A is 452 feet, Trail B is 397 feet. Which one is taller? To find out, you compare — and you use place value to do it, starting with the BIGGEST place.

👁 Watch Me Think

452 vs 397. I compare the hundreds digit first: 4 vs 3. Since 4 is bigger than 3, I already know 452 is bigger — I don't even need to look at the other digits!

452 > 397. The symbol always points at the smaller number, like an alligator's mouth opening toward the bigger meal.

🧩 Picture It: Two Backpacks on a Scale

Backpack A: 452 — [4 hundreds][5 tens][2 ones]
Backpack B: 397 — [3 hundreds][9 tens][7 ones]

4 hundreds beats 3 hundreds right away. Backpack A wins — heavier — before you even weigh the rest.

🤝 Let's Try Together: 268 vs 271

Hundreds digit: 2 and 2 — a tie! When hundreds tie, move to the tens place. Your turn: tens digit is 6 in 268, and 7 in 271. Which is bigger? (7 > 6.) So 271 wins: 268 < 271.

💡 If a place value TIES, don't stop — move one place to the right and keep comparing. Only when every digit matches are the numbers truly EQUAL.

🗺️ Lining Up the Trail Markers

Once you can compare two numbers, you can order a whole group of them — smallest to biggest, or biggest to smallest.

👁 Watch Me Think

Order 452, 268, 397 from LEAST to GREATEST. I compare hundreds digits first: 2, 3, 4. Smallest hundreds digit goes first: 268, then 397, then 452.

🧩 Picture It: Trail Markers Lined Up

268 -------- 397 -------- 452
SMALLEST →→→ BIGGEST

Same idea as lining up hikers by height — shortest to tallest.

🤝 Let's Try Together: 583, 549, 618 — GREATEST to LEAST

Hundreds digits: 5, 5, 6. 618 has the biggest hundreds digit (6), so it goes first. Now compare 583 and 549 — hundreds tie (5=5), so check tens: 8 > 4, so 583 is bigger. Your turn: what's the order? 618, 583, 549.

💡 When two numbers tie on hundreds, don't guess — move to tens and keep comparing, just like Basecamp 1.

📍 Trail Race Results

Comparing isn't just about "bigger" — sometimes the SMALLER number wins! In a race, the smallest time means the FASTEST finish.

👁 Watch Me Think

Maya finished a trail race in 245 minutes. Sam finished in 198 minutes. Compare hundreds: 2 vs 1 — 245 > 198. But in a RACE, a bigger time means it took LONGER. So Sam, with the smaller number, actually finished FASTER.

🧩 Picture It: Two Finish-Line Clocks

Sam's clock: 198 minutes
Maya's clock: 245 minutes

198 < 245, so Sam's clock stopped SOONER. Smaller number, faster finish.

🤝 Let's Try Together: Order 312, 298, 305 from FASTEST to SLOWEST

Fastest = smallest number first. Compare hundreds: 2, 3, 3. 298 has the smallest hundreds digit, so it's fastest. Between 312 and 305, hundreds tie (3=3) — compare tens: 0 < 1, so 305 is faster than 312. Your turn: what's the full order? 298, 305, 312.

💡 Always read the problem first: does WINNING mean the biggest number or the smallest? Racing and rounds are opposite from things like "most visitors."
