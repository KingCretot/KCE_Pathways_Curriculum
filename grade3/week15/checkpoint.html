🍊 Math Review: Multiplication Facts 6–9

🔁 Remember This? (Week 11)

At Rosa's orange grove, we learned the 6s, 7s, 8s, and 9s facts. The turn-around property says switching the factors keeps the same product, so 7 × 8 = 8 × 7.

The break-apart property splits a hard fact into easier parts. Rosa has 7 rows of 6 oranges. She breaks 6 into 5 and 1: 7 × 5 = 35 and 7 × 1 = 7. Then 35 + 7 = 42, so 7 × 6 = 42.

🥐 Math Review: Division and Fact Families

🔁 Remember This? (Week 12)

At the Nowak Family Bakery in Chicago, Lena and Kofi shared rolls into equal groups. That is division! To divide, think multiplication. For 56 ÷ 8, ask: 8 times what number equals 56? 8 × 7 = 56, so 56 ÷ 8 = 7.

A fact family uses the same three numbers in four facts. To find an unknown number, use the fact family. In ? ÷ 8 = 7, multiply: 8 × 7 = 56.

🌾 Math Review: Area

🔁 Remember This? (Week 13)

In Linh's rice village in the Mekong Delta, we learned that area is the number of square units that cover a flat shape. You can count the squares, or multiply rows × columns. This rectangle has 4 rows of 5 squares, so its area is 4 × 5 = 20 square units.

🌻 Math Review: Perimeter or Area?

🔁 Remember This? (Week 14)

At the Oak Street Community Garden in Atlanta, we learned that perimeter is the distance around a shape. Add all the side lengths. Area is the space inside, measured in square units.

A fence goes around the edge, so it needs perimeter, measured in feet. Soil covers the inside, so it needs area, measured in square feet.

🥐 Reading Review: Text Features

🔁 Remember This? (Week 12)

Remember the bakery booklet? Text features help you find and understand information. The table of contents shows where sections start. A heading tells what a section is about. Bold words are important, and the glossary tells what they mean. Captions tell about pictures, and labels name the parts of a diagram.

Plants need healthy soil to grow. Many gardeners add compost to their beds. Compost is made from old leaves, fruit peels, and grass clippings that break down over time. Worms help, too. They dig tunnels that let air and water reach the roots.

In Florida, many oranges are picked by hand. Pickers climb tall ladders and fill canvas bags that hang from their shoulders. When a bag is full, the picker pours the oranges into a big bin. 🌻 Reading Review: The Author’s Purpose

🔁 Remember This? (Week 14)

Remember the garden sign, flyer, and pumpkin story? Think PIE. Authors write to persuade, to inform, or to entertain. Facts and steps inform. Opinion words and asking you to do something persuade. Made-up characters and funny events entertain.
