A glossary is a great helper, but most stories do not have one! When you meet a new word in a story, you can still figure it out. The words and sentences around it give you clues. Those clues are called context clues, and they are this week’s new skill.

A word that is spelled and said the same way but has more than one meaning. Example: a tree’s bark and a dog’s bark.

🖼️ Picture It: Be a Word Detective

When you meet a word you don’t know, don’t skip it! Be a word detective. Read the whole sentence, and the sentences around it, and look for clues. The clues around a word are called context clues.

Definition Clue

The text tells you the meaning. Look for: is, means, called, which is.

Synonym Clue

A word nearby means the same. Look for: or, also, like.

Antonym Clue

A word nearby means the opposite. Look for: but, unlike, instead of, not.

Multiple-Meaning Word

One word, more than one meaning. Use the sentence to pick the one that makes sense.

🐶🌳 One Word, Two Meanings

Some words have more than one meaning. Bark can be the rough covering of a tree, or the short, loud sound a dog makes. The rest of the sentence tells you which one the author means.

Ask: What clues are around this word? Then use them to prove the meaning!

📖 Read: The First Sap Run

It is early March in Vermont, a state in the northeastern United States that is famous for maple syrup. Cousins Hazel and Desmond are spending the weekend at Aunt June’s maple farm. Maple farmers call this time of year sugaring season. As you read, watch for words you don’t know, and look for clues nearby that tell what they mean!

Read each part, or tap 🔊 to hear it. As you read, ask yourself: what clues help me understand the new words?

Story from a Vermont maple farm
The First Sap Run
A KCE Pathways story
1

Early one March morning, Hazel and her cousin Desmond rode up a muddy hill to Aunt June’s maple farm in Vermont. The air was brisk, so cold and fresh that it made their cheeks sting. Snow still covered the ground, but the sun felt warm. “You came just in time,” said Aunt June. “Cold nights and warm days make the sap run.” Sap is the thin, clear liquid that flows inside a maple tree.

2

Aunt June drilled a small hole in the trunk of a tall maple. Then she handed Desmond a metal spout called a tap. He gently tapped it into the hole with a hammer. The rough bark scratched his mittens. Hazel hung a pail, or bucket, on a hook under the tap. Drip. Drip. Drip. Soon the sap began to run into the pail.

3

All afternoon, the cousins gathered sap from the trees on the hill. Aunt June’s sled was sturdy. It was strong enough to carry six full pails without wobbling or breaking. Hazel dipped a finger into a pail and tasted the sap. “It isn’t sweet at all!” she said, surprised. Aunt June smiled. “It takes about forty gallons of sap to make just one gallon of syrup.”

4

Inside the sugarhouse, the sap boiled in a long, flat pan called an evaporator. As the water evaporated, or turned into steam and floated away, the sap got thicker and sweeter. The steam was fragrant. It smelled sweet, like warm cookies. The first syrup of the season was light, a pale gold color like honey.

5

Boiling sap is tedious. Unlike quick, exciting jobs, it takes hours of watching, waiting, and adding more sap. Desmond grew drowsy in the warm sugarhouse, but Hazel stayed alert, watching every bubble. At last, Aunt June poured hot syrup onto clean snow. It hardened into chewy maple candy. Now Desmond was wide awake! “Was it worth the wait?” asked Aunt June. “Every single drop!” the cousins cheered.

📘 Definition Clues: The Text Tells You

Sometimes the author tells you exactly what a word means, right in the text. That is a definition clue. Look for signal words like is, means, called, and which is. The meaning might be in the same sentence or the very next one.

👁 Watch Me Think

Part 1 says: Sap is the thin, clear liquid that flows inside a maple tree. I see the word is right after sap. The rest of the sentence tells me what sap is. That is a definition clue!

🧩 Picture It: Word + Signal + Meaning

WORD + is / means / called + MEANING
Sap is the thin, clear liquid inside a maple tree.
a metal spout called a tap

🤝 Let's Try Together

Read: The farmer used a drill, which is a tool that makes holes. The words which is tell me a meaning is coming. So a drill is a tool that makes holes.

🟰 Synonym Clues: Same Meaning Nearby

Sometimes the author puts a word with the same meaning nearby. That is a synonym clue. Signal words like or, also, and like can point to it. Here is a detective tip: if or is followed by ONE word that means the same, it is a synonym clue. If or is followed by a whole explanation, it is a definition clue.

👁 Watch Me Think

Part 2 says: Hazel hung a pail, or bucket, on a hook. I see the signal word or, and then just one word: bucket. Bucket means the same as pail. That is a synonym clue, so a pail is a bucket.

🧩 Picture It: Or + One Word, or Or + an Explanation?

a pail, or bucket → or + one word = SYNONYM clue
evaporated, or turned into steam and floated away → or + an explanation = DEFINITION clue

🤝 Let's Try Together

Part 3 says: Aunt June’s sled was sturdy. It was strong enough to carry six full pails. The next sentence uses the word strong, and strong means almost the same as sturdy. A sturdy sled is strong and hard to break.

↔️ Antonym Clues: Opposite Meaning Nearby

Sometimes the author gives a word with the OPPOSITE meaning nearby. That is an antonym clue. Signal words like but, unlike, instead of, and not tell you a meaning is the opposite. Figure out the opposite word, then flip it!

👁 Watch Me Think

Part 5 says: Desmond grew drowsy in the warm sugarhouse, but Hazel stayed alert. I see but, which signals an opposite. Alert means awake and paying attention. So drowsy must mean the opposite: sleepy. Later, Desmond is wide awake, which proves he was sleepy before!

🧩 Picture It: Flip the Opposite

drowsy ←but→ alert
sleepy ↔ awake
Signal words: but, unlike, instead of, not

🤝 Let's Try Together

Read: The pancakes were scorching, not cool enough to eat. The signal word not points to an opposite: cool. Flip it, and scorching must mean very hot.

🔀 Multiple-Meaning Words

Some words are spelled the same and sound the same, but they have more than one meaning. They are called multiple-meaning words. To choose the right meaning, read the whole sentence and ask: Which meaning makes sense here?

👁 Watch Me Think

Part 2 says: The rough bark scratched his mittens. Bark can mean the sound a dog makes, or the rough covering of a tree. Desmond is working on a tree, and a sound cannot scratch anything! So here, bark means the covering of a tree.

🧩 Picture It: Pick the Meaning That Fits

run: (1) move fast on your feet (2) flow like a liquid
“Soon the sap began to run into the pail.” → meaning 2
trunk: (1) the main stem of a tree (2) an elephant’s long nose (3) a storage space in a car

🤝 Let's Try Together

Part 1 says: Cold nights and warm days make the sap run. Can sap move on feet? No! Here, run means to flow like a liquid.

⭐ Word Work Bonus: The Prefix un-

The First Sap Run
A KCE Pathways story
1

Early one March morning, Hazel and her cousin Desmond rode up a muddy hill to Aunt June’s maple farm in Vermont. The air was brisk, so cold and fresh that it made their cheeks sting. Snow still covered the ground, but the sun felt warm. “You came just in time,” said Aunt June. “Cold nights and warm days make the sap run.” Sap is the thin, clear liquid that flows inside a maple tree.

2

Aunt June drilled a small hole in the trunk of a tall maple. Then she handed Desmond a metal spout called a tap. He gently tapped it into the hole with a hammer. The rough bark scratched his mittens. Hazel hung a pail, or bucket, on a hook under the tap. Drip. Drip. Drip. Soon the sap began to run into the pail.

3

All afternoon, the cousins gathered sap from the trees on the hill. Aunt June’s sled was sturdy. It was strong enough to carry six full pails without wobbling or breaking. Hazel dipped a finger into a pail and tasted the sap. “It isn’t sweet at all!” she said, surprised. Aunt June smiled. “It takes about forty gallons of sap to make just one gallon of syrup.”

4

Inside the sugarhouse, the sap boiled in a long, flat pan called an evaporator. As the water evaporated, or turned into steam and floated away, the sap got thicker and sweeter. The steam was fragrant. It smelled sweet, like warm cookies. The first syrup of the season was light, a pale gold color like honey.

5

Boiling sap is tedious. Unlike quick, exciting jobs, it takes hours of watching, waiting, and adding more sap. Desmond grew drowsy in the warm sugarhouse, but Hazel stayed alert, watching every bubble. At last, Aunt June poured hot syrup onto clean snow. It hardened into chewy maple candy. Now Desmond was wide awake! “Was it worth the wait?” asked Aunt June. “Every single drop!” the cousins cheered.
