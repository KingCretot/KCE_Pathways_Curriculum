✂️ Equal Parts of a Whole

Mrs. Whitfield hands Lucia one whole square of cloth for a quilt block and asks her to cut it into 4 equal parts. Lucia folds the square in half, then in half again, and cuts on the folds. Now she has 4 equal parts, pieces that are exactly the same size.

👁 Watch Me Think

Before I name the parts, I check: are they all the same size? If the parts are not equal, I cannot call them fourths or thirds. 2 equal parts are halves, 3 are thirds, 4 are fourths, 6 are sixths, and 8 are eighths. Equal parts do not have to be the same shape, but they must be the same size.

🧩 Picture It

2 equal parts = halves
3 equal parts = thirds
4 equal parts = fourths
6 equal parts = sixths
8 equal parts = eighths

🤝 Let's Try Together

Owen cuts a strip of cloth into 3 pieces, but one piece is much longer than the other two. Are the pieces thirds? No! The pieces are not the same size, so they are not thirds. To make thirds, all 3 pieces must be equal.

🥧 Unit Fractions

Darius slices an apple pie for the farm stand into 8 equal slices. One slice is one eighth of the pie. We write it as 18. A fraction like 18 that names just one equal part is called a unit fraction.

👁 Watch Me Think

The bottom number tells how many equal parts make the whole: 8. The top number of a unit fraction is always 1, because it names just one part. When I put unit fractions together, I count them: one eighth, two eighths, three eighths. Three slices of 18 make 38 of the pie.

🧩 Picture It

1 slice of 8 equal slices = 18
3 slices of 18 = 38

🤝 Let's Try Together

A quilt block is cut into 6 equal parts, so each part is 16. Lucia sews 4 of those parts in red. She used four sixths, or 46, of the block. 46 is 4 copies of 16.

🔢 Numerator and Denominator

Lucia finishes a quilt block made of 4 equal parts. She sews 3 parts in blue cloth and 1 part in yellow. The blue part is a fraction of the block: 34. The 3 is the numerator, and the 4 is the denominator.

👁 Watch Me Think

The denominator, on the bottom, tells how many equal parts make the whole: 4. The numerator, on top, tells how many parts I am counting: 3 blue parts. If I count the yellow part instead, the fraction is 14. The blue parts and the yellow part together make 44, which is one whole block.

🧩 Picture It

3 ← numerator: the parts we count
4 ← denominator: the equal parts in the whole

🤝 Let's Try Together

A quilt block has 6 equal parts, and 2 are green. Green is 26 of the block. The parts that are not green are 46. Check: 26 and 46 together make 66, one whole block.

🥚 Fractions of a Group

At the farm stand, Owen packs a carton of 6 eggs: 2 brown eggs and 4 white eggs. This time the whole is a group, the set of 6 eggs. The brown eggs are 26 of the carton, and the white eggs are 46.

👁 Watch Me Think

For a group, each egg is one part of the whole. For the egg carton, the denominator is 6, because there are 6 eggs in the whole group. The numerator is how many eggs I am counting. I always compare to the WHOLE group, not just to the other color.

🧩 Picture It

2 brown eggs out of 6 eggs in all = 26
4 white eggs out of 6 eggs in all = 46

🤝 Let's Try Together

Mrs. Whitfield has 8 jars of jam: 3 strawberry and 5 peach. Strawberry is 38 of the jars, and peach is 58. Together, 38 and 58 make 88, the whole group.
