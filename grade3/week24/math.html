🏁 Warm-Up: Shapes and Equal Parts Review

Before we fire up the smoker, let's warm up with last week's skill: quadrilaterals and equal parts. A rectangle has 4 right angles, a rhombus has 4 equal sides, and a square has both. When a shape is split into equal parts, each part is a unit fraction of the whole, like 14 or 16.

🍖 Picture It: Barbecue Festival in Kansas City

It's a warm Saturday at a barbecue festival in Kansas City, Missouri. Smoke curls up from the smokers, and the air smells like hickory wood. Pitmaster Mr. Hargrove has been tending his family's smoker since sunrise. His helpers, Gemma, Hector, and Hattie, will run the stand all day.

Running a barbecue stand takes a lot of math! The helpers will count plates, share food into equal trays, find the numbers they do not know, and estimate to check their work.

🔥 Kansas City Barbecue Facts

  • Kansas City, Missouri, sits where the Missouri River and the Kansas River meet. Right next door, across the state line, is Kansas City, Kansas.
  • Henry Perry is called the father of Kansas City barbecue. In 1908 he began selling smoked meat from a stand in an alley downtown. He smoked it over oak and hickory wood and wrapped it in newspaper.
  • Kansas City barbecue sauce is famous for being thick and made with tomatoes and molasses, and many people like it sweet.
  • Burnt ends are a Kansas City favorite. They are the crusty, flavorful pieces cut from the end of a smoked beef brisket.
  • The Kansas City area has more than 100 barbecue restaurants.

🌟 Word Wrangler

Flip each card to learn this week's vocabulary! Each card shows a sentence from the lesson. Tap the speaker to hear the word or the meaning.

two-step problem
A word problem you solve in two parts. The answer to the first step helps you finish the second step.
“In a two-step problem, I find a hidden question first.”
equation
A number sentence with an equal sign, like 6 × 8 + 15 = b.
“Gemma writes an equation, using the letter r for the number of ribs left.”
unknown
The number you do not know yet. A letter can stand for it.
“A letter, like r, stands for the unknown, the number I do not know yet.”
round
To change a number to the nearest ten or hundred so it is easier to work with.
“Round each number to the nearest hundred: 289 rounds to 300, and 412 rounds to 400.”
estimate
A close answer you find with easy numbers.
“My estimate is 300 + 400 = 700.”
reasonable
Makes sense. A reasonable answer is close to the estimate.
“701 is close to 700, so his answer is reasonable.”

Here is a memory trick: solve first, then estimate to check. If your answer and your estimate are close, your answer is reasonable. If they are far apart, look for a mistake!

➕ Two Steps with Adding and Subtracting

Mr. Hargrove's stand starts the day with 250 ribs. By noon, the helpers sell 85 ribs. In the afternoon, they sell 97 more. How many ribs are left? Gemma writes an equation, using the letter r for the number of ribs left: 250 − 85 − 97 = r. Step 1: 250 − 85 = 165. Step 2: 165 − 97 = 68. So r = 68 ribs.

👁 Watch Me Think

In a two-step problem, I find a hidden question first. Here I could also find how many ribs were sold in all: 85 + 97 = 182. Then 250 − 182 = 68. Same answer! A letter, like r, stands for the unknown, the number I do not know yet.

🧩 Picture It

250 − 85 = 165
165 − 97 = 68
r = 68 ribs

🤝 Let's Try Together

Hector's family brings 136 bottles of water, and then 48 more. They sell 95 bottles. How many are left? Let w be the bottles left. 136 + 48 = 184. 184 − 95 = 89. So w = 89 bottles.

✖️ Two Steps with Multiplying and Dividing

Mr. Hargrove fills 6 trays with 8 burnt ends on each tray. Then a new batch comes off the smoker with 15 more burnt ends. How many burnt ends are there now? Hattie writes 6 × 8 + 15 = b. Step 1: 6 × 8 = 48. Step 2: 48 + 15 = 63. So b = 63 burnt ends.

👁 Watch Me Think

When I see equal groups, I multiply. When I share into equal groups, I divide. Then I use that answer for the second step. When an equation has × or ÷ and also + or −, I do the × or ÷ part first.

🧩 Picture It

6 trays × 8 = 48
48 + 15 = 63
b = 63 burnt ends

📝 Writing the Equation

Before solving, Hattie writes an equation that matches the story. The sauce table had some bottles. Mr. Hargrove brought 4 boxes with 6 bottles in each box, and now there are 40 bottles. Hattie lets b be the bottles that were already there: b + 4 × 6 = 40. Step 1: 4 × 6 = 24. Step 2: b + 24 = 40, so b = 40 − 24 = 16 bottles.

👁 Watch Me Think

I read the story and ask: What do I know? What is missing? I pick a letter for the missing number. The letter can be at the start, in the middle, or at the end of the equation. Then I check that each number is in the right place. I do the × or ÷ part first.

🧩 Picture It

b + 4 × 6 = 40
4 × 6 = 24
b + 24 = 40
b = 16

🤔 Is My Answer Reasonable? Estimate by Rounding

The festival sold 289 sandwiches on Friday and 412 on Saturday. Hector adds them and gets 701. Is that reasonable? Round each number to the nearest hundred: 289 rounds to 300, and 412 rounds to 400. My estimate is 300 + 400 = 700. 701 is close to 700, so his answer is reasonable.

👁 Watch Me Think

Rounding to the nearest ten or hundred makes numbers easy to add or subtract in my head. To round to the nearest hundred, I look at the tens digit. If it is 5 or more, I round up. If it is 4 or less, I round down. If my exact answer is far from my estimate, I check my work again.

🧩 Picture It

289 → 300
412 → 400
Estimate: 300 + 400 = 700
Exact: 701. Close, so it is reasonable!

🤝 Let's Try Together

Gemma says 52 + 37 + 18 = 147. Round to the nearest ten: 50 + 40 + 20 = 110. 147 is far from 110, so she checks again. The real answer is 107, which is close to 110.
