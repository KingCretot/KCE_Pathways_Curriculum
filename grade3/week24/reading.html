🌟 Word Wrangler

Flip each card to learn this week’s vocabulary! Tap 🔊 to hear the word, then flip and tap 🔊 to hear what it means and see how the biography uses it.

biography
The true story of a real person’s life, written by someone else. In the story: “This is a biography,” he says. “It is the true story of Roberto’s life.”
cause
Why something happens. In the biography: “Because of a baseball rule, other teams could pick him away from the Dodgers.”
effect
What happens because of a cause. In the biography: “So he decided to fly to Nicaragua with the next plane.”
clinic
A class where an expert teaches a skill. In the biography: “He held free baseball clinics to teach children how to play.”
supplies
Things people need, like food and medicine. In the biography: “Some of the supplies were being stolen.”
unfairly
In a way that is not right or equal. In the biography: “Some people treated him unfairly because of the color of his skin.”

🖼️ Picture It: A Life on a Timeline

A timeline is a line that shows events in the order they happened. The earliest event is at the top. Move down the line, and time moves forward. Here is a timeline of Roberto Clemente’s life.

Sequence clues

Dates are the strongest clues. Time-order words help too: first, then, next, later, after, finally.

Cause and effect clues

The cause is WHY. The effect is WHAT happened. Signal words: because, so, as a result.

Ask: What happened first? What happened next? Why did it happen?

📖 Read: Roberto Clemente: More Than a Ballplayer

It is a sunny September afternoon in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. Perla, age 8, and her grandfather, Abuelo Octavio, walk across the yellow Roberto Clemente Bridge. Outside PNC Park, the Pirates’ ballpark, they stop at a tall bronze statue of a baseball player. “Who is he?” Perla asks. “That is Roberto Clemente,” says Abuelo Octavio. He hands Perla a small book. “This is a biography,” he says. “It is the true story of Roberto’s life.” Read it with Perla!

Read each chapter, or tap 🔊 to hear it. As you read, watch for dates and for the words because and so.

Chapter 1 · 1934 to 1952
A Boy from Carolina
Roberto Clemente: More Than a Ballplayer
1

Roberto Clemente was born on August 18, 1934, in Carolina, Puerto Rico. He was the youngest of seven children. His father, Melchor, was a foreman who watched over workers cutting sugarcane. His mother, Luisa, washed clothes for other families to help earn money.

2

Roberto loved baseball. In high school, he was also on the track team. He threw the javelin, a long spear, and he did the high jump. In October 1952, Roberto joined the Santurce Crabbers, a professional baseball team in Puerto Rico. Scouts from teams in the United States soon noticed his speed and his strong throwing arm.

Chapter 2 · 1954 to 1960
From Puerto Rico to Pittsburgh
Roberto Clemente: More Than a Ballplayer
1

In 1954, the Brooklyn Dodgers signed Roberto. They sent him to play for their minor league team in Montreal, Canada. Because of a baseball rule, other teams could pick him away from the Dodgers at the end of that season. In November 1954, the Pittsburgh Pirates chose him.

2

On April 17, 1955, Roberto played his first game for the Pirates. He went on to play 18 seasons for Pittsburgh, his whole major league career.

3

Life in the United States was not always easy. Roberto was Black and Puerto Rican, and Spanish was his first language. Some people treated him unfairly because of the color of his skin. Some reporters made fun of the way he spoke English, and some called him “Bob” or “Bobby,” even though his name was Roberto. He kept working hard, and he spoke up for fair treatment of Latino and Black players.

4

In 1960, the Pirates won the World Series, the championship of baseball. Roberto got a hit in all seven games of the Series.

Chapter 3 · 1961 to 1972
A Star in Right Field
Roberto Clemente: More Than a Ballplayer
1

Roberto played right field, and his throwing arm was famous. He won 12 Gold Glove Awards in a row. Gold Gloves go to the best fielders. He also won four National League batting titles for having the best batting average in the league.

2

In 1966, Roberto was named the National League’s Most Valuable Player. In 1971, the Pirates won the World Series again, and Roberto was named the World Series Most Valuable Player. After the last game, he spoke in Spanish on television and asked his parents in Puerto Rico for their blessing.

3

On September 30, 1972, at Three Rivers Stadium in Pittsburgh, Roberto hit a double. It was his 3,000th hit! He was only the 11th player in major league history to reach 3,000 hits.

Chapter 4 · 1972 to Today
A Helper Off the Field
Roberto Clemente: More Than a Ballplayer
1

After each baseball season, Roberto went home to Puerto Rico. He held free baseball clinics to teach children how to play. He also dreamed of building a “Sports City,” a place where young people could play sports and learn skills for life.

2

On December 23, 1972, a strong earthquake hit Managua, the capital city of Nicaragua. Many buildings fell, and many families lost their homes. Roberto quickly gathered food, medicine, and money for the people there.

3

Then Roberto learned that some of the supplies were being stolen and were not reaching the people who needed them. So he decided to fly to Nicaragua with the next plane, to make sure the help got there. The plane was carrying too much weight. Soon after it took off from Puerto Rico on December 31, 1972, it crashed into the ocean. Roberto and the four other men on board died. He was 38 years old.

4

People all over the world were very sad. Usually, a player must wait five years after his career ends before he can be elected to the Baseball Hall of Fame. Because of Roberto’s death, the Hall of Fame skipped the wait. In 1973, he became the first Latin American player elected to the Hall of Fame. That same year, the Pirates retired his number, 21, and Major League Baseball renamed one of its awards the Roberto Clemente Award. Each year, it honors a player who helps his community.

5

His wife, Vera, kept working to make his Sports City dream come true. Today, a bronze statue of Roberto stands outside PNC Park in Pittsburgh, a few steps from the bridge named after him.

📘 What Is a Biography?, Part 1

A biography is the true story of a real person’s life, written by someone else. Biographies are nonfiction. They are told in third person, with he, she, and the person’s name. They include real dates, places, and events, usually in time order. An autobiography is different: the person writes about their own life in first person, using I and me.

👁 Watch Me Think

The book says, “Roberto Clemente was born on August 18, 1934, in Carolina, Puerto Rico.” It tells about a real person, it gives a real date and a real place, and it says he, not I. So I know this book is a biography.

🧩 Picture It: Biography or Autobiography?

Biography: someone ELSE tells a real life, using he or she
Autobiography: a person tells their OWN life, using I and me
Both are true, with real dates and places

🤝 Let's Try Together

Let’s try one together. If Roberto had written a book about his own life, would it be a biography? No! A book a person writes about their own life is an autobiography. It would say, “I was born in Carolina.”

🗓️ Sequence: The Order of a Life, Part 1

Sequence is the order in which events happen. A biography often follows a person’s life in time order. Dates are the strongest clues! A smaller year came earlier. If two events happen in the same year, look at the month. Time-order words like first, then, later, and after help too.

👁 Watch Me Think

I see 1952, 1954, and 1955 in Chapters 1 and 2. 1952 is the smallest number, so it came first: Roberto joined the Santurce Crabbers. Next, in 1954, the Dodgers signed him. Then, in 1955, he played his first game for the Pirates.

🧩 Picture It: Putting a Life in Order

Smaller year = earlier event
Same year? Check the month
Time-order words: first, then, later, after

🤝 Let's Try Together

Let’s try one together. Which happened first: Roberto’s 3,000th hit, or the earthquake in Nicaragua? The hit was on September 30, 1972. The earthquake was on December 23, 1972. It is the same year, so we look at the month. September comes before December, so the hit came first.

🔗 Cause and Effect in a Real Life, Part 1

A cause is WHY something happens. An effect is WHAT happens because of it. Signal words like because, so, and as a result help you find them. Ask: What happened? That is the effect. Why did it happen? That is the cause. In a biography, causes and effects show WHY a person made the choices they made.

👁 Watch Me Think

Chapter 4 says the supplies were being stolen. “So he decided to fly to Nicaragua with the next plane.” What happened? He decided to fly. That is the effect. Why? The supplies were not reaching the people. That is the cause. The signal word so connects them.

🧩 Picture It: Cause → Effect

Cause: WHY it happened
Effect: WHAT happened
because comes before a cause; so comes before an effect
Cause
Supplies were being stolen
so
signal word
Effect
He decided to fly with the next plane

Let’s try one together. Read: “Some people treated him unfairly because of the color of his skin.” The word because comes right before the cause. The effect is that he was treated unfairly. The cause is the color of his skin. That was wrong of those people, and Roberto spoke up for fair treatment.

Chapter 4 · 1972 to Today
A Helper Off the Field
Roberto Clemente: More Than a Ballplayer
1

After each baseball season, Roberto went home to Puerto Rico. He held free baseball clinics to teach children how to play. He also dreamed of building a “Sports City,” a place where young people could play sports and learn skills for life.

2

On December 23, 1972, a strong earthquake hit Managua, the capital city of Nicaragua. Many buildings fell, and many families lost their homes. Roberto quickly gathered food, medicine, and money for the people there.

3

Then Roberto learned that some of the supplies were being stolen and were not reaching the people who needed them. So he decided to fly to Nicaragua with the next plane, to make sure the help got there. The plane was carrying too much weight. Soon after it took off from Puerto Rico on December 31, 1972, it crashed into the ocean. Roberto and the four other men on board died. He was 38 years old.

4

People all over the world were very sad. Usually, a player must wait five years after his career ends before he can be elected to the Baseball Hall of Fame. Because of Roberto’s death, the Hall of Fame skipped the wait. In 1973, he became the first Latin American player elected to the Hall of Fame. That same year, the Pirates retired his number, 21, and Major League Baseball renamed one of its awards the Roberto Clemente Award. Each year, it honors a player who helps his community.

5

His wife, Vera, kept working to make his Sports City dream come true. Today, a bronze statue of Roberto stands outside PNC Park in Pittsburgh, a few steps from the bridge named after him.

⛓️ Cause-and-Effect Chains, Part 1

In a real life, one event can cause another, and that effect can cause something else. This is called a cause-and-effect chain. Follow the chain in order, link by link, like a row of falling dominoes. A chain can also help you explain WHY a person is remembered today.

👁 Watch Me Think

Here is a chain from Chapter 4. The earthquake hits Managua. So Roberto gathers supplies. Then he learns the supplies are being stolen. So he decides to fly with the next plane. Each effect becomes the cause of the next link.

→ Roberto gathers supplies
→ supplies are stolen
→ Roberto decides to fly with the next plane

Chapter 2 · 1954 to 1960
From Puerto Rico to Pittsburgh
Roberto Clemente: More Than a Ballplayer
1

In 1954, the Brooklyn Dodgers signed Roberto. They sent him to play for their minor league team in Montreal, Canada. Because of a baseball rule, other teams could pick him away from the Dodgers at the end of that season. In November 1954, the Pittsburgh Pirates chose him.

2

On April 17, 1955, Roberto played his first game for the Pirates. He went on to play 18 seasons for Pittsburgh, his whole major league career.

3

Life in the United States was not always easy. Roberto was Black and Puerto Rican, and Spanish was his first language. Some people treated him unfairly because of the color of his skin. Some reporters made fun of the way he spoke English, and some called him “Bob” or “Bobby,” even though his name was Roberto. He kept working hard, and he spoke up for fair treatment of Latino and Black players.

4

In 1960, the Pirates won the World Series, the championship of baseball. Roberto got a hit in all seven games of the Series.

Chapter 3 · 1961 to 1972
A Star in Right Field
Roberto Clemente: More Than a Ballplayer
1

Roberto played right field, and his throwing arm was famous. He won 12 Gold Glove Awards in a row. Gold Gloves go to the best fielders. He also won four National League batting titles for having the best batting average in the league.

2

In 1966, Roberto was named the National League’s Most Valuable Player. In 1971, the Pirates won the World Series again, and Roberto was named the World Series Most Valuable Player. After the last game, he spoke in Spanish on television and asked his parents in Puerto Rico for their blessing.

3

On September 30, 1972, at Three Rivers Stadium in Pittsburgh, Roberto hit a double. It was his 3,000th hit! He was only the 11th player in major league history to reach 3,000 hits.

Chapter 4 · 1972 to Today
A Helper Off the Field
Roberto Clemente: More Than a Ballplayer
1

After each baseball season, Roberto went home to Puerto Rico. He held free baseball clinics to teach children how to play. He also dreamed of building a “Sports City,” a place where young people could play sports and learn skills for life.

2

On December 23, 1972, a strong earthquake hit Managua, the capital city of Nicaragua. Many buildings fell, and many families lost their homes. Roberto quickly gathered food, medicine, and money for the people there.

3

Then Roberto learned that some of the supplies were being stolen and were not reaching the people who needed them. So he decided to fly to Nicaragua with the next plane, to make sure the help got there. The plane was carrying too much weight. Soon after it took off from Puerto Rico on December 31, 1972, it crashed into the ocean. Roberto and the four other men on board died. He was 38 years old.

4

People all over the world were very sad. Usually, a player must wait five years after his career ends before he can be elected to the Baseball Hall of Fame. Because of Roberto’s death, the Hall of Fame skipped the wait. In 1973, he became the first Latin American player elected to the Hall of Fame. That same year, the Pirates retired his number, 21, and Major League Baseball renamed one of its awards the Roberto Clemente Award. Each year, it honors a player who helps his community.

5

His wife, Vera, kept working to make his Sports City dream come true. Today, a bronze statue of Roberto stands outside PNC Park in Pittsburgh, a few steps from the bridge named after him.
