habitat
The natural home where an animal or plant lives. A pond is a frog's habitat.
adaptation
A body part or behavior that helps a living thing survive. Thick fur is an adaptation for cold.
camouflage
Colors or patterns that help an animal hide. A green frog hides on green leaves.
behavior
The way an animal acts. Hiding, hunting, and flying south are behaviors.
migrate
To travel far away and come back when the season changes. Many birds migrate south in fall.
hibernate
To sleep deeply through the cold winter. Some bears and bats hibernate.
nocturnal
Awake and active at night. Owls are nocturnal.

🏞️ Five Habitats

A habitat is the home where an animal lives. It gives the animal food, water, and shelter. Habitats can be hot or cold, wet or dry. Today we visit five: forest, desert, ocean, wetland, and Arctic.

👁 Watch Me Think

I see a lizard on hot sand. There are no trees and little rain. I ask: Is it hot or cold? Wet or dry? Hot and dry means desert. So the lizard's habitat is the desert.

🐻‍❄️ Body Parts That Help

An adaptation helps an animal survive in its habitat. Some adaptations are body parts. A polar bear has thick fur and a layer of fat called blubber. A duck has webbed feet for swimming. Some animals have colors that help them hide. That is camouflage.

👁 Watch Me Think

A fennec fox lives in the hot desert. It has very big ears. Why? Big ears let out body heat. That helps the fox stay cool. So big ears are an adaptation for heat.

🦋 Behaviors That Help

Some adaptations are behaviors, or ways an animal acts. Many birds migrate to warm places in fall. Some bears hibernate through winter. Owls are nocturnal and hunt at night. Some animals even learn. Bees remember where flowers are. Ants learn the way home.

👁 Watch Me Think

In winter, there is little food for a bear. What can it do? It can sleep deeply until spring. Its body uses saved fat. So hibernating is a behavior that helps it survive winter.

🧩 Picture It: Behavior Board

  • Migrate: Monarch butterflies fly south to Mexico in fall.
  • Hibernate: Some bats sleep in caves all winter.
  • Nocturnal: Owls hunt mice at night.
  • Live in groups: Many fish swim in schools for safety.
  • Learn: Honeybees remember which flowers have food.

🤝 Let's Try Together: Let's Try One Together

A desert mouse stays in its cool burrow all day. It comes out at night to find seeds. Is that a body part? No. It is a way the mouse acts, so it is a behavior. Being active at night keeps it out of the hot sun.

🔬 Scientists Who Studied Animals

Meet scientists who asked how animals live and survive. Some studied tiny insects. Others study sharks and bears.

Charles Henry Turner

1867–1923

A Black scientist from Ohio. He showed that insects can learn. Bees remember colors and times. Ants learn the way home. He did much of his work while teaching high school in St. Louis.

Eugenie Clark

1922–2015

A Japanese American scientist called the "Shark Lady." She showed that sharks can learn too. She started a sea lab in Florida.

Rae Wynn-Grant

Born 1985

A Black American wildlife scientist. She studies bears, like black bears and grizzly bears. She learns how bears change where they go when people build roads and homes near their habitat.
