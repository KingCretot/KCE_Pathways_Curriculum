🎷 New Orleans Facts

  • New Orleans is a city in Louisiana. The Mississippi River curves around it.
  • Jazz music grew up in New Orleans. In 1994, New Orleans Jazz National Historical Park was created to celebrate where jazz began and how it grew.
  • Louis Armstrong, one of the most famous jazz musicians ever, grew up in New Orleans. He learned to play the cornet there, and the cornet player Joe “King” Oliver became his mentor.
  • Mardi Gras is French for “Fat Tuesday.” During the Mardi Gras season, marching bands play in parades all over the city.
  • Brass bands in New Orleans also lead parades called second lines, where people dance along behind the band.

A pattern is something that repeats or changes by a rule. Today you will look for patterns in the multiplication and addition tables, explain them, and add and subtract within 1,000.

even number
A number that can be split into pairs with none left over. It ends in 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8.
“16 players make 8 pairs with nobody left over, so 16 is even.”
odd number
A number that has 1 left over when you split it into pairs. It ends in 1, 3, 5, 7, or 9.
“When 17 players try, one is left without a partner, so 17 is odd.”
pattern
Something that repeats or changes by a rule.
“A pattern is something that repeats or changes by a rule.”
sum
The answer to an addition problem.
“Each step to the right adds 1 to the sum, and each step down adds 1 too.”
product
The answer to a multiplication problem.
“Any product with an even factor is even; only odd × odd makes an odd product.”
regroup
To trade 1 hundred for 10 tens, or 1 ten for 10 ones (and back).
“I regroup 1 hundred as 10 tens, then 1 ten as 10 ones.”

Here is a memory trick: even numbers can march evenly in pairs. An odd number always leaves one player marching alone!

⚖️ Odd and Even Patterns

Mr. Donnell lines the band up in pairs. 16 players make 8 pairs with nobody left over, so 16 is even. When 17 players try, one is left without a partner, so 17 is odd.

👁 Watch Me Think

Even numbers end in 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8. Odd numbers end in 1, 3, 5, 7, or 9. When I add two odd numbers, each one has a player left over, and the two leftovers make a new pair. So odd + odd is even. Even + even is even, and odd + even is odd.

🧩 Picture It

even + even = even
odd + odd = even
odd + even = odd

✖️ Patterns in the Multiplication Table

Before practice, Dominic draws a multiplication table on the band room whiteboard. He colors the 2s row blue and the 4s row green. He notices that every number in the 4s row is double the number above it in the 2s row.

👁 Watch Me Think

4 = 2 × 2, so 4 × 7 = 2 × (2 × 7). That is double 14, which is 28. This works because of the associative property. The table is the same on both sides of the diagonal because of the commutative property: 3 × 5 = 5 × 3. Any product with an even factor is even; only odd × odd makes an odd product.

🧩 Picture It

4s row: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24
each 4s number = double the 2s number

➕ Patterns in the Addition Table

Dion makes an addition table for the drum line’s counting practice. The rows and columns go from 0 to 6. He colors the doubles yellow and a slanting line of 8s pink.

👁 Watch Me Think

Each step to the right adds 1 to the sum, and each step down adds 1 too. The doubles, like 2 + 2 and 3 + 3, run down the diagonal, and they are all even. Along a slanting line going up to the right, the sums stay the same: 2 + 6, 3 + 5, 4 + 4. One addend goes up 1 and the other goes down 1, so the sum does not change.

🧩 Picture It

doubles: 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 (all even)
2 + 6 = 3 + 5 = 4 + 4 = 8

🤝 Let's Try Together

Dina wants 29 + 47. She moves 1 from 47 to 29: 30 + 46 = 76. One addend went up 1 and the other went down 1, so the sum stays the same!

📍 Adding and Subtracting Within 1,000

The parade route down the avenue is 800 yards long. After the first stop, the band has marched 365 yards. How many yards are left?

👁 Watch Me Think

800 − 365: I cannot take 5 ones from 0 ones, and there are no tens to trade. So I regroup 1 hundred as 10 tens, then 1 ten as 10 ones. Now I have 7 hundreds, 9 tens, and 10 ones. 10 − 5 = 5, 9 − 6 = 3, and 7 − 3 = 4. So 435 yards are left. I check by adding: 435 + 365 = 800.

🧩 Picture It

800 = 7 hundreds, 9 tens, 10 ones
800 − 365 = 435
Check: 435 + 365 = 800

🤝 Let's Try Together
