🌎 Natural Resources

A natural resource is something from nature that people use. Water, air, soil, sunlight, trees, rocks, and fuels like coal and oil are all natural resources. Some resources are renewable: nature keeps making more of them, so they will not run out. Sunlight and wind are renewable resources. Other resources are nonrenewable: there is only so much of them, and they take a very long time to form. Coal, oil, and natural gas are nonrenewable resources. They formed from the remains of plants and animals that lived millions of years ago.

👁 Watch Me Think

Is a tree renewable? People can plant new trees, and the new trees grow. So trees can be renewable, as long as we plant new ones and give them time to grow. But if we cut down trees faster than they grow back, we can run short. That is why it matters how we use resources, not just which ones we use.

♻️ Reduce, Reuse, Recycle

Reduce means to use less, so less trash is made at all. Reuse means to use something again, like refilling a water bottle or giving outgrown clothes to someone else. Recycle means to collect old materials and make them into new products. The United States Environmental Protection Agency says reducing and reusing are the best choices, and recycling and composting come next. Composting turns food scraps and yard trim into rich material for soil. In 2018, each person in the United States made about 4.9 pounds of waste every day, counting trash and recycling together!

👁 Watch Me Think

Why recycle an aluminum can? Making new cans from recycled cans uses 95 percent less energy than making them from bauxite ore, the rock that aluminum comes from. And it is quick: on average, a used can becomes a new can in less than 60 days. But it is even better to reduce first. If I use a refillable bottle, there is no can to recycle at all!

💧 Saving Water

Each American uses an average of 82 gallons of water a day at home. Over half of all the water used inside a home is used in the bathroom. Small changes add up! Turning off the tap while you brush your teeth can save 8 gallons of water a day. A faucet that drips once every second can waste more than 3,000 gallons a year. Short showers use less water than baths. To conserve water means to use it carefully so it is not wasted.

👁 Watch Me Think

How can I save water in the kitchen? I can keep a pitcher of drinking water in the fridge, so I do not have to run the faucet until the water gets cold. And if I see a drip, I tell a grown-up. One tiny drip a second does not look like much, but over a whole year, it adds up to thousands of gallons.

👜 Isatou Ceesay, the Queen of Recycling

She turned a village's plastic trash into something useful.

Isatou Ceesay

born 1972

Isatou Ceesay grew up on a farm in the village of N'jau, in the country of The Gambia in West Africa. Over the years, piles of thrown-away plastic bags built up in her village. The bags attracted insects that carry disease, and farm animals died after eating them. Isatou Ceesay and a group of women found a way to help. They washed the bags, cut them into strips, and crocheted the strips into purses to sell. That meant less plastic trash, and the women earned money for their families. Their group is called the N'jau Recycling and Income Generation Group. People call Isatou Ceesay the Queen of Recycling. A children's book by Miranda Paul, One Plastic Bag, tells her story.

🔎 More Scientists and Engineers Who Protect Resources

These people asked, "How can we waste less and make clean water and energy last?"

Veena Sahajwalla

materials scientist, Australia

A materials scientist and engineer who grew up in Mumbai, India. As a child, she watched workers pick through trash for paper, plastic, and cardboard to sell. Today, she is a professor at the University of New South Wales in Sydney, Australia. She invented a way to make steel that uses old tires as part of the fuel, so the tires do not end up in a landfill. In 2018, she launched the world's first e-waste microfactory, a small factory that recycles old electronics.

Ashok Gadgil

born 1950

An engineer born in Mumbai, India. After a disease called cholera spread in India in 1993, he invented UV Waterworks, which uses ultraviolet light to kill germs in drinking water. He also designed the Berkeley-Darfur Stove, which cooks with less than half the firewood of the old way of cooking in Darfur, Sudan. In 2014, he joined the National Inventors Hall of Fame.

Deepika Kurup

young inventor

In 2012, when she was 14, Deepika Kurup of Nashua, New Hampshire, won the Discovery Education 3M Young Scientist Challenge and the title "America's Top Young Scientist." Her invention uses the power of the Sun to clean dirty water. She won a prize of $25,000.
