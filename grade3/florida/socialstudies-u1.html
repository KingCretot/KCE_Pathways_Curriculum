🏛️ Florida's Capital and State Leaders

Florida's capital is Tallahassee. State leaders work there in the Capitol building. The governor leads the state. The Florida Legislature makes laws for all of Florida.

👁 Watch Me Think

I want to know who makes a state law. The governor leads, but the governor does not write laws alone. The Legislature writes and votes on laws. Then the governor signs them or says no. So making a law takes teamwork.

🗺️ Florida's 67 Counties

Florida is split into 67 counties. Each county has its own leaders. The county government meets in the county seat. DeSoto County is one county. Its county seat is Arcadia.

👁 Watch Me Think

I live in a town, and my town is inside a county. My county is inside Florida. So I belong to three communities at once! County leaders take care of things like county roads, parks and libraries.

🏘️ Cities, Towns and You

Cities and towns have their own leaders too. Many have a mayor. Many also have a city council or commission. Voters choose council members. In many towns, voters choose the mayor too. Florida's Seminole and Miccosukee nations also have their own tribal governments.

👁 Watch Me Think

Who gets to choose leaders? Today, citizens 18 and older can vote. Long ago, many people were left out. Most women could not vote until 1920. Black Floridians faced unfair rules that blocked their votes for many years. Brave people worked to change that.

📜 Source Detective: Florida's Constitution

Florida has a written plan for its government. It is called the Florida Constitution. Let's read the start of three real lines and put them in kid words. The ... means the line keeps going.

Primary Source: The Constitution of the State of Florida

Real wordsKid words
Line 1: "The seat of government shall be the City of Tallahassee, in Leon County..."Tallahassee is where the state government works.
Line 2: "The legislative power of the state shall be vested in a legislature..."The power to make laws is given to the Legislature.
Line 3: "The supreme executive power shall be vested in a governor..."The top power to lead the state is given to the governor.
Constitution of the State of Florida (1968, as amended): Article II, Section 2; Article III, Section 1; Article IV, Section 1(a). Each line is the start of a longer section.

👁 Think Like a Historian

A historian reads slowly. First, I find the hard words. Then I ask what each line means. 'Vested in' means 'given to.' 'Executive power' means the power to lead and carry out laws.

🌟 Floridians Who Led and Served

Many kinds of people have led Florida's communities. Meet four of them.

Mary McLeod Bethune

1875–1955

A teacher who started a school for Black girls in Daytona Beach in 1904. It grew into Bethune-Cookman University. She helped Black women sign up to vote. She also gave advice to a U.S. President.

Josiah T. Walls

1842–1905

He was born enslaved in Virginia. After the Civil War, he made Florida his home. He served in the Florida Legislature. Then he became Florida's first Black member of the U.S. Congress.

Betty Mae Tiger Jumper

1923–2011

A Seminole nurse and leader. In 1967, she became the first woman elected to lead the Seminole Tribe of Florida. She worked for health and schools for her people.

Lawton Chiles

1930–1998

A governor of Florida from 1991 to 1998. Years before, he walked more than 1,000 miles across Florida. He wanted to meet voters face to face.
