🌱 What Is Character Motivation?

Every day that week, Mia found a reason to stay in the back: sorting inventory, counting shelves, double-checking the math. She was good at all of it — and none of it meant talking to a stranger. Mia's motivation was simple: she was afraid of saying the wrong thing to a customer.

👁 Watch Me Think

Motivation is the REASON behind what a character does. To find a character's motivation, I ask: what does this character want, or what are they afraid of, and how does that explain their actions? Mia's actions (staying in the back) make sense once I understand her motivation (fear of saying the wrong thing).

🧩 Picture It: Action → Motivation

Mia stays in the back all week
→ MOTIVATION: she's afraid of saying the wrong thing to customers

🔄 Tracking How Mia Changes

On opening day, a line of hikers formed at the counter and Theo couldn't keep up alone. Mia hesitated — then stepped up beside him. She knew every price on every shelf, because SHE had done the math. Customer after customer, she got a little more comfortable.

👁 Watch Me Think

To track character change, I compare the BEGINNING of the story to the END. At the start, Mia hid in the back, afraid to talk to strangers. By the end, she was running the counter herself. That's character change — and I can point to the exact moment (the trigger) that started it: Theo needing help.

🧩 Picture It: Before & After

BEFORE: Mia hides in the back, avoids customers
TRIGGER: Theo is overwhelmed at the counter
AFTER: Mia steps up and runs the counter herself

🤝 Let's Try Together

A character who is afraid of the dark at the start of a story, but walks a friend home at night by the end, has changed — and the trigger might be the friend needing help.

💪 What Makes a Change Feel Earned?

Mia's change didn't happen by magic. It happened because she had a real reason (Theo needed help), a real skill to lean on (the shelf math she'd mastered), and a real moment of courage (stepping up anyway, even though she was still a little nervous). That's what makes a character change feel EARNED.

👁 Watch Me Think

A character change feels earned when three things are true: there's a believable trigger, the character uses effort or a real strength (not luck), and the change happens gradually, not all at once. If a character just suddenly “becomes brave” with no reason, that change feels fake instead of earned.

🧩 Picture It: What Makes It Earned

Real trigger (Theo needs help)
+ Real strength (Mia's shelf math)
+ Real courage (stepping up anyway)
= An earned change
