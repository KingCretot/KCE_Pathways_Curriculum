🏔️ Land and Water Shapes

Geography is the study of land, water, weather and people. A landform is a natural shape of the land. Mountains, valleys, plains and deserts are landforms. Rivers, lakes and oceans are bodies of water.

👁 Watch Me Think

I want to sort what I see. A mountain is very high, rocky land. A plain is wide, flat land. A desert gets very little rain. A river is fresh water that flows downhill. So first I ask: is it land or water? Then I name it.

🏘️ Land Shapes How People Live

Long ago and today, many people live near water. Rivers give water to drink and to grow food. The land and the climate shape people's jobs, homes and clothes. People use natural resources like soil, trees and fish.

👁 Watch Me Think

I look at a place and ask three questions. What is the land like? What is the weather like? What can people use there? A coast has fish, so many people fish. A plain has flat land and rich soil, so many people farm. Florida gets hurricanes, so many homes are built strong.

🌱 Caring for the Land

People also care for the land. This is called conservation. Haudenosaunee farmers plant corn, beans and squash together as the Three Sisters. The three plants help each other and keep the soil healthy.

👁 Watch Me Think

How can three plants help each other? I look at each one's job. The corn grows tall, so the bean vine can climb it like a pole. The beans help put plant food back into the soil. The squash has big leaves that shade the ground. Shade keeps the soil wet and blocks weeds. Each sister gives something to the others.

🧩 Picture It: The Three Sisters Garden

🤝 Let's Try Together: Think Like a Farmer

A farmer plants only corn, year after year. Corn uses up plant food, so the soil wears out. What can the farmer add? Beans, because beans help put plant food back. That is conservation: using the land so it stays healthy for next year.

🌎 Caretakers of the Land

Meet people who cared for the land. As you read, ask: Who lived on this land first? Who benefited, and who paid a cost?

Haudenosaunee Three Sisters farmers

long ago to today

The Haudenosaunee are six nations: the Mohawk, Oneida, Onondaga, Cayuga, Seneca and Tuscarora. Their homelands are in what is now New York and Canada. Haudenosaunee women led the farming. Families still plant the Three Sisters today.

John Muir

1838–1914

John Muir was born in Scotland and moved to Wisconsin as a boy. He loved the mountains of California. He wrote, "Everybody needs beauty as well as bread." He helped Yosemite become a national park in 1890. In 1892, he helped start the Sierra Club. He also wrote some unkind words about Indigenous people.

The Ahwahneechee people of Yosemite

long ago to today

Yosemite Valley was the home of the Ahwahneechee people long before it was a park. In 1851, armed men called the Mariposa Battalion came into the valley. They forced many Ahwahneechee people to leave. Later, park leaders also made Indigenous families move out. Early parks sometimes pushed Indigenous people off their lands. Their families still live nearby and still care for Yosemite.

Charles Young

1864–1922

Charles Young was born enslaved in Kentucky. He grew up to be an Army officer. In 1903, he led Black soldiers called the Buffalo Soldiers in Sequoia National Park, California. He was the park's acting superintendent, the leader in charge. He was the first Black superintendent of a national park. His soldiers built a road so visitors could see the giant sequoia trees.
