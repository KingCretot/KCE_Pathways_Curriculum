🌟 Vocabulary Round-Up

Flip each card to review words from all of Unit 4! Tap the speaker to hear the word or the meaning.

fraction
A number that names part of a whole or part of a group, like 34.
denominator
The bottom number of a fraction. It tells how many equal parts make the whole.
equivalent fractions
Fractions that name the same amount, like 12 and 24. They land on the same point on a number line.
elapsed time
How much time passes from the start of something to the end.
context clues
Words and sentences around a new word that help you figure out what it means.
simile
A comparison of two different things using the word like or the word as.
opinion
What someone thinks, feels, or believes. People can disagree about an opinion.
stage directions
Notes in parentheses that tell actors how to move or how to sound.

🍕 Math Review: Fractions of a Whole and of a Group

🔁 Remember This? (Week 16)

At Russo's Pizza Kitchen, we learned that a fraction names equal parts. The denominator, on the bottom, tells how many equal parts make the whole. The numerator, on top, tells how many parts we are counting.

Mr. Russo cuts a square pizza into 8 equal parts and puts pepperoni on 3 of them. The pepperoni covers 38 of the pizza. For a group, each thing counts as one part: a tray of 8 mushrooms with 3 brown ones is 38 brown.

📏 Math Review: Number Lines and Equivalent Fractions

🔁 Remember This? (Week 17)

At Clover Creek Dairy, Wen split the lane from 0 to 1 into equal parts. To name a point, count the equal spaces, not the tick marks. Past 1, keep counting jumps: on a line cut into fourths, 44 = 1 and 54 is one fourth past 1.

Fractions that land on the very same point are equivalent fractions. On the number line, 12 = 24 = 48.

🧵 Math Review: Comparing Fractions

🔁 Remember This? (Week 18)

In Gee's Bend, Aunt Loretta showed us two rules. Same denominator: the pieces are the same size, so compare the numerators. 58 > 38.

Same numerator: compare the piece sizes. Thirds are bigger pieces than sixths, so 23 > 26. And only compare fractions of same-size wholes!

🎈 Math Review: Time, Liquid Volume, and Mass

🔁 Remember This? (Week 19)

At the Balloon Fiesta, Ms. Yazzie's crew told time to the minute. Count by fives for each big number, then count on by ones for the small marks. The hour is the number the hour hand has passed.

For elapsed time, jump to the next o'clock time first, then jump the rest of the way, and add your jumps. Liters measure liquid volume. Grams are for light things, and kilograms are for heavy things.

🍁 Reading Review: Context Clues

🔁 Remember This? (Week 16)

At Aunt June's maple farm, Hazel and Desmond were word detectives. A definition clue tells the meaning, with signal words like is, means, and called. A synonym clue gives a word that means the same, often after or.

An antonym clue gives the opposite, with signal words like but, unlike, and not. A multiple-meaning word has more than one meaning, so use the sentence to choose the one that makes sense.

💪 YOUR TURN
Read this text

Sugaring Day

Aunt June lit the fire in the sugarhouse. Soon the sap in the pan began to simmer, or bubble gently. Desmond carried a heavy log for the fire, but Hazel carried a light one.

+3

Find the mistake. Desmond says the word light in “Hazel carried a light one” means a lamp that shines. What went wrong?

Read this text

The Sap Tags

Each maple tree has a tag, which is a small card with a number on it. Hazel checked the tags on the hill. The path was slushy, but the barn floor was dry. When Desmond saw his full pail, he was elated, or very happy.

Use the definition clue. A tag is a small ___ with a number on it.

+3

Read this text

Boiling Day

The fire under the pan was roaring. “Stand back,” said Aunt June. “The pan is scalding, unlike the icy snow outside.” Hazel used a ladle, which is a big spoon with a long handle, to scoop the syrup. By night, Desmond felt weary, or tired.

⚓ Reading Review: The Parts of a Poem

🔁 Remember This? (Week 17)

On the harbor walk in Rockport, Josie and Keiko read poems. A line is one row of words, and a stanza is a group of lines. Rhyming words end with the same sound. Rhythm is the beat you can clap.

A simile compares two different things using like or as. Sensory words tell what you see, hear, smell, taste, or touch.

💪 YOUR TURN
Read this poem

Night Watch

The lighthouse stands so tall and white,
It sweeps its beam across the night.

The waves roar like a hungry bear,
And cold salt wind blows through my hair.

+3

Read this poem

Gulls

Gulls float over the dock
like white kites on strings.

I hear them squawk and squeal.

Read this poem

The Fishing Boat

My boat is slow, my boat is old,

🎉 Reading Review: Fact and Opinion

🔁 Remember This? (Week 18)

At Market Square in San Antonio, Lupe and Benicio sorted sentences. A fact is true and can be proved: you can see it, count it, measure it, or look it up. An opinion tells what someone thinks, feels, or believes, and people can disagree.

Watch for opinion signal words like best, should, favorite, and I think. A strong opinion gives a reason with the word because.

💪 YOUR TURN
+3

Type the opinion signal word in this sentence: Mr. Pham sells the tastiest mangoes at the market.

+3

Split it! The fruit stand has 4 baskets of mangoes, and they are the best mangoes in Texas. Which part is the OPINION?

+3

Is this sentence a fact or an opinion? Type fact or opinion. The plaza should have more benches for visitors.

+3

Which explanation is correct? Benicio says, “The shop sold 30 piñatas, and they were the most beautiful piñatas ever” is a fact, because it has a number. Lupe says the sentence mixes a fact and an opinion.

+3

Opinion writers join the opinion to the reason with one word. Finish it: Every market needs music ___ it makes shoppers want to stay longer.

+3

Find the mistake! Lupe wrote, “I think the yellow shop sign is the best sign in the plaza because it is next to a tree.” What went wrong?

🎤 Reading Review: Reading a Play

🔁 Remember This? (Week 19)

At the radio station in Tucson, Ezra and Mei helped put on a show. A play has a cast of characters, a setting, and scenes. A new scene starts when the place or the time changes.

Dialogue is the words characters say. The name comes first, in capital letters, with a colon. Stage directions are in parentheses, and actors act them out instead of saying them. A summary tells who, where, what happened, and how it ended.

💪 YOUR TURN
Read this script

The Lost Headphones

(Morning in Studio A. Ezra looks under the table.)

EZRA: (worried) I cannot find the headphones!

MEI: Did you check the shelf by the door?

NANA PILAR: (smiling) Let us look together, slowly.

(Mei finds the headphones on the shelf. Ezra cheers.)

+3

Who says, “Did you check the shelf by the door?” Type the name.

+3

Find the mistake! Ezra reads his line out loud like this: “worried, I cannot find the headphones!” What went wrong?
