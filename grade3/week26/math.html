🚲 Picture It: The Bike Repair Shop in Portland

It's a rainy-then-sunny Saturday in Portland, Oregon. Bikes are lined up outside a small repair shop near the river. Ms. Castellanos rolls up the garage door and hangs her tools on the wall. Her helpers, Cassius, Celeste, and Cayden, are ready to fix tires, chains, and brakes.

A repair shop runs on numbers! Spokes and patches come in boxes of 10, 20, or more. The helpers will multiply by multiples of 10 and use properties to make the math faster.

🌉 Portland Facts

  • The Willamette River runs through Portland, Oregon, and splits the city into an east side and a west side.
  • Portland has 12 crossings over the Willamette River, so people call it Bridgetown.
  • The Hawthorne Bridge in Portland opened in 1910, so it is more than 100 years old.
  • Tilikum Crossing opened in 2015. Private cars are not allowed on it. People cross it on foot, on bikes, and on buses, streetcars, and trains.
  • On a clear day, people in Portland can see Mount Hood, a tall, snowy volcano to the east.

commutative property
You can switch the order of two factors, and the product stays the same.
“By the commutative property, 3 × 40 = 40 × 3.”
associative property
You can change how three factors are grouped, and the product stays the same.
“By the associative property, (2 × 4) × 30 = 2 × (4 × 30).”
distributive property
You can break one factor into parts, multiply each part, and add the products.
“Cayden uses the distributive property: 4 × 18 = 4 × 10 + 4 × 8.”

Here is a memory trick: commute means to travel back and forth, so the commutative property lets factors switch places. Associate means to group with friends, so the associative property changes the groups.

🔟 Multiplying by Multiples of 10

Ms. Castellanos orders 4 boxes of spokes. Each box holds 30 spokes. How many spokes is that? 30 is 3 tens, so 4 × 30 is 4 groups of 3 tens. That is 12 tens, and 12 tens = 120. So 4 × 30 = 120 spokes.

👁 Watch Me Think

I use a fact I already know, then think in tens. 4 × 3 = 12, so 4 × 3 tens = 12 tens. 12 tens is 120. The fact tells me how many tens, and the tens tell me the size of the product.

🧩 Picture It

4 × 30 = 4 × 3 tens
= 12 tens
= 120

🤝 Let's Try Together

Cayden needs 6 packs of 80 cable ties. 6 × 8 = 48, so 6 × 8 tens = 48 tens. 48 tens = 480. So 6 × 80 = 480 cable ties.

🔄 Switch and Group: Commutative and Associative Properties

Celeste carries 2 crates of bolts to the workbench. Each crate holds 4 boxes, and each box holds 30 bolts. That is 2 × 4 × 30. Celeste groups the factors to find the product: 2 × 4 = 8, and 8 × 30 = 240. Cassius groups them another way: 4 × 30 = 120, and 2 × 120 = 240. Same product!

👁 Watch Me Think

The commutative property says I can switch two factors: 3 × 40 = 40 × 3. The associative property says I can change the grouping of three factors: (2 × 4) × 30 = 2 × (4 × 30). The parentheses show which part I multiply first. I pick the grouping that is easiest for me.

🧩 Picture It

(2 × 4) × 30 = 8 × 30 = 240
2 × (4 × 30) = 2 × 120 = 240

🤝 Let's Try Together

Why does 6 × 80 = 480 work? 80 = 8 × 10, so 6 × 80 = 6 × (8 × 10). Regroup: (6 × 8) × 10 = 48 × 10 = 480.

✂️ Break It Apart: The Distributive Property

Cayden is fixing 4 bikes, and each bike needs 18 new spokes. That is 4 × 18. Cayden uses the distributive property: 4 × 18 = 4 × 10 + 4 × 8. 4 × 10 = 40 and 4 × 8 = 32. 40 + 32 = 72 spokes.

👁 Watch Me Think

When a factor is too big for a fact I know, I break it into a 10 and the ones that are left. 18 = 10 + 8. I multiply 4 by each part, then add the two products. I must multiply both parts, not just one!

🧩 Picture It

4 × 18 = 4 × (10 + 8)
= 4 × 10 + 4 × 8
= 40 + 32 = 72

🤝 Let's Try Together

Celeste needs 3 × 24. She breaks 24 into 20 + 4: 3 × 20 = 60 and 3 × 4 = 12. 60 + 12 = 72, so 3 × 24 = 72.

🧠 Smart Strategies for Two-Step Problems

Ms. Castellanos orders 5 boxes of 40 long spokes and 5 boxes of 20 short spokes. How many spokes in all? One way: 5 × 40 = 200 and 5 × 20 = 100, and 200 + 100 = 300. A faster way: each pair of boxes holds 40 + 20 = 60 spokes, so 5 × 60 = 300.

👁 Watch Me Think

The distributive property works backward too! 5 × 40 + 5 × 20 = 5 × (40 + 20). When two products share a factor, I can add the other factors first. Then I find the product with my tens trick.

🧩 Picture It

5 × 40 + 5 × 20
= 5 × (40 + 20)
= 5 × 60 = 300

🤝 Let's Try Together

Celeste sells 3 water bottles for $10 each and 3 bike bells for $20 each. 3 × 10 + 3 × 20 = 3 × 30 = $90. Check: $30 + $60 = $90.
