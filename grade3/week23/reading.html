🖼️ Picture It: Who Is Telling the Story?

Every story has a narrator, the voice that tells it. The big question is: is the narrator inside the story or outside it? Look at the two picture frames.

First person: the narrator is IN the story

The narrator is a character. Clue words: I, me, my, we, us, our.

Third person: the narrator is OUTSIDE the story

The narrator is not a character. Clue words: he, she, they, him, her, them, and names.

Detective tip: skip the quotation marks!

Words inside “quotation marks” are a character talking. Look at the words outside the quotation marks to find the narrator’s voice.

Ask: Who is telling? Is the narrator in the story or outside it?

📖 Read: One Day, Three Voices

It is a warm Saturday in April in the Atchafalaya Basin (say it: uh-CHAF-uh-LIE-uh) of Louisiana. The Atchafalaya Basin is America’s largest river swamp. Cypress trees, alligators, and crawfish all live there. Mireille (say it: mee-RAY) Naquin lives there on a houseboat. You will read three short texts about the SAME day. Each one is told by a different voice!

Read each text, or tap 🔊 to hear it. As you read, ask: Who is telling this? What does each cousin think and feel?

Text 1 · A Personal Story
My Floating Home
Told by Mireille Naquin, age 9
1

My name is Mireille, and I live on a houseboat on a bayou in the Atchafalaya Basin in Louisiana. It is America’s largest river swamp. From my bedroom window, I can see tall cypress trees covered in gray Spanish moss. I think it is the most peaceful place in the world.

2

Last Saturday, my cousin Nathaniel came to visit from Chicago. I could not wait to show him everything! Mawmaw Nadine, our grandmother, drove us in her little flat-bottom boat to check our crawfish traps. I pulled up the first trap, and it was full of wiggly crawfish. I cheered so loudly that an egret flew off a branch.

3

Then I spotted an alligator resting on a log. “Look, Nathaniel! Isn’t he beautiful?” I whispered. Nathaniel did not say a word. I guess he was too amazed to speak. That night, we ate boiled crawfish on the deck. Crawfish night is my favorite!

Text 2 · A Story with an Outside Narrator
The Visitor from Chicago
A story about Nathaniel Naquin, age 8
1

Nathaniel Naquin had never been on a boat before. When he stepped onto the houseboat, the floor rocked under his sneakers. He grabbed the railing with both hands. “Is it supposed to move like this?” he asked.

2

Mawmaw Nadine smiled and handed him a life jacket. “The bayou is gentle, cher,” she said. (Cher is a Louisiana French word for “dear.” It sounds like “sha.”) In the little boat, Nathaniel watched Mireille pull up a crawfish trap. The crawfish snapped their tiny claws, and he scooted to the far end of the seat.

3

When Mireille pointed at the alligator, Nathaniel’s heart thumped. His mouth felt dry, and he could not say a word. He was not amazed. He was scared. Mawmaw Nadine noticed his face. She slowly steered the boat away and said, “Alligators like their space, and so do we.” Nathaniel whispered, “I am glad we left.”

Text 3 · A Postcard
Nathaniel’s Postcard
Written by Nathaniel Naquin
1

Dear Mom and Dad, Today I rode in a boat in a real swamp! I was nervous when the houseboat rocked, and I was scared when we saw an alligator. Mawmaw Nadine kept us far away from it.

2

Mireille thinks alligators are beautiful. I think they are better in pictures! My favorite part was dinner. We ate boiled crawfish on the deck, and I learned to peel them all by myself. Love, Nathaniel

🧭 The Narrator and First Person, Part 1

Every story has a narrator, the voice that tells the story. In first person, the narrator is a character in the story. The narrator uses the words I, me, my, and we to tell what happened to them. A first-person narrator knows only their own thoughts and feelings. They can only guess what other characters feel.

👁 Watch Me Think

I read Text 1: “I pulled up the first trap.” The word I is not inside quotation marks, so the narrator is telling about herself. The narrator says, “My name is Mireille.” So the narrator is Mireille, and Text 1 is told in first person.

🧩 Picture It: The First-Person Narrator

The narrator is a character IN the story
Clue words outside quotation marks: I, me, my, we, our
Knows only their OWN thoughts and feelings

🤝 Let's Try Together

Let’s try one together. Mireille says, “I guess he was too amazed to speak.” Can Mireille really know what Nathaniel feels inside? No! The word guess shows she is not sure. A first-person narrator can only see inside her own head.

🔭 Third Person: A Narrator Outside the Story, Part 1

In third person, the narrator is not a character. The narrator stands outside the story, like someone watching from far away. A third-person narrator uses names and the words he, she, and they. Characters can still say I inside quotation marks, but the narrator never does. Some third-person narrators can even tell what a character thinks and feels inside.

👁 Watch Me Think

I read Text 2: “He grabbed the railing with both hands.” The narrator says he, not I. The narrator is not Nathaniel and not Mireille. The voice is outside the story, so Text 2 is third person. The narrator even tells me that Nathaniel’s heart thumped, a feeling he never said out loud.

🧩 Picture It: The Third-Person Narrator

The narrator is OUTSIDE the story
Clue words: he, she, they, him, her, them, and names
I inside quotation marks = a character talking

🤝 Let's Try Together

Let’s try one together. Text 2 ends with: Nathaniel whispered, “I am glad we left.” Is Text 2 first person now? No! The word I is inside quotation marks, so that is Nathaniel talking. The narrator still says “Nathaniel whispered,” so the story is still third person.

👀 A Character’s Point of View, Part 1

A character’s point of view is how that character thinks or feels about something. Two characters can see the very same thing in different ways. To find a character’s point of view, look at what the character says, does, and feels. Words like think, feel, love, and scared are strong clues.

👁 Watch Me Think

Mireille and Nathaniel both see the same alligator. Mireille whispers, “Isn’t he beautiful?” Nathaniel’s heart thumps, and his postcard says, “I think they are better in pictures!” Same alligator, two different points of view.

🧩 Picture It: Same Thing, Two Views

Mireille sees the alligator: beautiful!
Nathaniel sees the alligator: scary!
Clues: what they SAY, DO, and FEEL

🤝 Let's Try Together

Let’s try the houseboat. Mireille thinks it is the most peaceful place in the world. What does Nathaniel DO when the floor rocks? He grabs the railing with both hands. So Nathaniel feels nervous about the houseboat, while Mireille feels calm.

🗣️ My Point of View, Part 1

You have a point of view too! As a reader, you might agree with a character, or you might feel differently. To share your point of view, tell what you think and give a reason. The word because often comes before the reason. Your point of view does not have to match a character’s, and that is okay.

👁 Watch Me Think

Nathaniel thinks alligators are better in pictures. My point of view is different. I think alligators are interesting to watch from far away, because Mawmaw Nadine kept the boat at a safe distance. I told what I think, and I gave a reason.

🧩 Picture It: My Point of View

A character’s view: what THEY think
My view: what I think + because + a reason
I can agree or disagree with a character

A tells Mireille’s point of view, not the reader’s.
