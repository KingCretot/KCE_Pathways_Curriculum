🧱 Picture It: The Tile Workshop in Santa Fe

It's a sunny morning in Santa Fe, New Mexico. In an old adobe building, Ms. Fresquez runs a tile workshop. She shapes wet clay into tiles, lets them dry, then paints them turquoise, gold, red, and blue. Today her helpers, Emilio, Estrella, and Felipa, are getting a big order of tiles ready for a new courtyard.

Every tile has a shape. The helpers will count sides and angles, sort tiles into groups, and cut tiles into equal parts. Tile makers have to know their shapes!

🏜️ Santa Fe Facts

  • Santa Fe is the capital of New Mexico. It became a capital city around 1610, which makes it the oldest state capital city in the United States. Pueblo peoples lived in this area for hundreds of years before that.
  • Santa Fe sits about 7,000 feet above sea level, so it is the highest state capital in the United States.
  • Many buildings in Santa Fe are made of adobe. Adobe bricks are made from earth, water, and straw, then dried in the sun.
  • The round wooden beams that poke out of many adobe walls are called vigas. They hold up the roof.
  • Painted clay tiles decorate many Santa Fe homes, doorways, and fountains. Tile makers use shapes like squares, rectangles, and rhombuses to make patterns.

Today you will find quadrilaterals, sort rhombuses, rectangles, and squares by their sides and angles, and split shapes into parts with equal area, naming each part with a unit fraction.

🔷 Meet the Quadrilaterals

Ms. Fresquez lays out clay tiles on her worktable. “Every one of these tiles is a quadrilateral,” she says. A quadrilateral is a flat shape with 4 straight sides and 4 angles. Emilio counts the sides of a kite-shaped tile: 1, 2, 3, 4. He counts its corners, or angles: 4. It is a quadrilateral!

👁 Watch Me Think

To tell if a shape is a quadrilateral, I check its attributes. An attribute is a feature of a shape, like how many sides it has or what its corners look like. I count the straight sides. I need exactly 4, and the shape must be closed, with no gaps and no curved sides. A triangle has 3 sides and a hexagon has 6, so they are not quadrilaterals.

🧩 Picture It

4 straight sides → quadrilateral
3 sides → triangle, not a quadrilateral
6 sides → hexagon, not a quadrilateral

📏 Rectangles, Rhombuses, and Squares

Ms. Fresquez sorts tiles into groups. A rectangle has 4 right angles. A right angle is a square corner, like the corner of a sheet of paper. A rhombus has 4 sides that are all the same length. Then Estrella picks up a square tile. “Where does this one go?” A square has 4 right angles and 4 equal sides, so it belongs in both groups!

👁 Watch Me Think

I check two attributes. Are all 4 angles right angles? Then it is a rectangle. In a rectangle, the sides across from each other are the same length. Are all 4 sides the same length? Then it is a rhombus. If both are true, it is a square. So every square is also a rectangle and also a rhombus. But not every rectangle is a square.

🧩 Picture It

4 right angles → rectangle
4 equal sides → rhombus
4 right angles AND 4 equal sides → square

🤝 Let's Try Together

Emilio measures a tile. Its sides are 4 inches, 4 inches, 4 inches, and 4 inches, but its corners are not square corners. All 4 sides are equal, so it is a rhombus. It has no right angles, so it is not a rectangle and not a square.

🧩 Shapes in More Than One Group

Felipa draws two circles on the workshop chalkboard. One circle says “4 right angles.” The other says “4 equal sides.” Rectangles go in the first circle, and rhombuses go in the second. Squares go where the circles overlap, because they belong to both groups. Then Felipa finds a tile with 4 sides of different lengths and no right angles. It goes outside both circles.

👁 Watch Me Think

When I sort, I check every attribute. A shape can belong to more than one group. Squares, rectangles, and rhombuses are all quadrilaterals. But some quadrilaterals are none of these three. They still have 4 straight sides, so they are still quadrilaterals.

🧩 Picture It

Left circle: 4 right angles (rectangles)
Right circle: 4 equal sides (rhombuses)
Overlap: both (squares)
Outside: other quadrilaterals

🤝 Let's Try Together

A tile has sides of 5, 2, 5, and 2 inches, and no right angles. It does not have 4 right angles, so it is not a rectangle. It does not have 4 equal sides, so it is not a rhombus. It is a quadrilateral that goes outside both circles.

🍰 Equal Parts and Unit Fractions

Before a big square tile dries, Ms. Fresquez cuts it into 4 equal parts. Each part has the same area, which means each part covers the same amount of space. Each part is a unit fraction of the whole tile: 14. Emilio paints one part turquoise.

👁 Watch Me Think

When a shape is split into equal parts, each part is a unit fraction of the whole. A unit fraction has a 1 on top. The bottom number tells how many equal parts make the whole. 4 equal parts means each part is 14. 6 equal parts means each part is 16. The parts must have equal area, but they do not have to be the same shape. A square can be split into 4 small squares, 4 strips, or 4 triangles, and each part is still 14.

🧩 Picture It

2 equal parts → each is 12
4 equal parts → each is 14
6 equal parts → each is 16
8 equal parts → each is 18

🤝 Let's Try Together

Felipa splits a rectangle tile into 3 equal strips. What fraction of the area is each strip? There are 3 equal parts, so each strip is 13 of the area.
