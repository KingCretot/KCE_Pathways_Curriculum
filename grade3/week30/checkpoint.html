🚲 Math Review: Multiplying with Tens and Properties

🔁 Remember This? (Week 26)

At the bike repair shop in Portland, we multiplied by multiples of 10. 5 × 70 is 5 groups of 7 tens. 5 × 7 = 35, so 5 × 7 tens = 35 tens, and 35 tens = 350.

The commutative property lets two factors switch places. The associative property lets you group three factors in a new way. The distributive property breaks a factor apart: 3 × 14 = 3 × 10 + 3 × 4 = 30 + 12 = 42. Multiply BOTH parts!

🏠 Math Review: Area and Perimeter

🔁 Remember This? (Week 27)

In Chattanooga, we planned a playground. Perimeter is the distance around the edge, in feet. Area is the number of square units that cover the inside, in square feet. Rectangles with the same perimeter can have different areas, and the one closest to a square covers the most.

Rectangles with the same area can have different perimeters too. To find the area of a combined shape, split it into rectangles and add, or find a big rectangle and subtract the missing corner. An L shape has 6 sides, so add all 6 to find its perimeter.

🥧 Math Review: Fractions Greater Than 1

🔁 Remember This? (Week 28)

At the pie bakery in Lancaster County, every pie was cut into equal slices. When the numerator and the denominator match, like 66, the fraction is 1 whole. To change slices into whole pies, count by the denominator: 126 is 2 whole pies, because 6, 12 is 2 counts.

A fraction greater than 1 has a numerator bigger than its denominator, like 74. On a number line, 74 is 3 jumps past 1. With the same denominator, compare the numerators. With the same numerator, the bigger pieces win: thirds are bigger than fourths.

🎺 Math Review: Patterns, Adding, and Subtracting

🔁 Remember This? (Week 29)

At parade practice in New Orleans, we found patterns. Even + even is even, odd + odd is even, and odd + even is odd. Any product with an even factor is even. The 6s row in the multiplication table is double the 3s row, because 6 = 2 × 3.

In the addition table, each step to the right or down adds 1. When you subtract from a number like 700, regroup: 700 is 6 hundreds, 9 tens, and 10 ones. Check a difference by adding.

🌍 Reading Review: Folktales and the Central Message

🔁 Remember This? (Week 26)

At Family Story Night in Tampa, Fafa, Fernando, and Fallon told three folktales. A folktale is a made-up story that people told out loud for many generations. It comes from a culture, and it often teaches a lesson.

A good retelling tells the beginning, middle, and end in order, in your own words, without changing what happens. The central message is the big lesson, written as a full sentence. One word, like peaches, is a topic, not a message.

Bramble Builds a Dam

Bramble the young beaver wanted to build the biggest dam in the pond, all by herself. “I don’t need anyone,” she said.

Turtle offered to roll stones to her. Heron offered to carry long sticks. “No, thank you,” said Bramble. Twice the rushing water knocked her dam down.

At last, Bramble asked for help. Turtle rolled the stones, Heron carried the sticks, and Bramble packed the mud. By sunset, the dam stood strong. “Many friends make strong walls,” Bramble said.

📰 Reading Review: Opinion Pieces

🔁 Remember This? (Week 27)

At the Mountain Kids Gazette in Asheville, we read two opinion pieces. A strong opinion piece has an opinion, reasons that tell why, examples that prove the reasons, and a conclusion that says the opinion again.

Linking words connect ideas. Because comes before a reason, for example comes before an example, also adds one more idea, and therefore comes before the result or conclusion. A feeling like “I like it” is not a reason, and off-topic sentences should be cut.

📚 Reading Review: Comparing Books in a Series

🔁 Remember This? (Week 28)

On Hatteras Island, Gavin and Genevieve read The Buxton Beacon Kids, two books with the same characters. A series is a group of books with the same main characters. Each book is a new adventure with its own problem.

To compare, look at the plot, the setting, and how the characters act. Words like both, also, and alike show things that are the same. Words like but, however, and different show things that are not. Prove your ideas with evidence from the books.

🦅 Reading Review: Research and Two Sources

🔁 Remember This? (Week 29)

In Alton, Illinois, Giulia and Gideon researched the bald eagle comeback. Research means looking for facts to answer a question. Use only the facts that answer your question.

A good note is short, in your own words, and exact: names, dates, and numbers never change. Write A or B to show which source a fact came from. Then sort your notes into groups to plan a report.
