👀 What Is Point of View?

A hiker walked into the trading post on opening day, looked around for a minute, and left without buying anything. Theo felt hurt — he thought the hiker was just being rude. But that's only ONE point of view on what happened.

👁 Watch Me Think

Point of view means the perspective a moment is seen from — almost like a camera angle. The SAME event can look completely different depending on who is watching it, and what that person notices, feels, and already knows. Neither point of view is automatically “right” or “wrong” — they're just different angles on the same truth.

🧩 Picture It: Same Moment, Different Angles

THE HIKER LEAVES WITHOUT BUYING
Theo's angle: feels hurt, thinks it's rude
Mia's angle: notices clues, thinks something else is going on

🔍 Reading the Clues: Mia's Perspective

While Theo felt hurt, Mia was looking closer. She noticed the hiker's boots were caked in mud, the strap on his pack was torn, and his water bottle was completely empty. Mia inferred that the hiker wasn't being rude at all — he was exhausted.

👁 Watch Me Think

To understand someone else's point of view, I look for CLUES about what they might be feeling or thinking — even if the story doesn't say it directly. Muddy boots, a torn strap, and an empty water bottle are all details that let me INFER the hiker had a rough hike, without anyone telling me so.

🧩 Picture It: Clues → Inference

Muddy boots + torn strap + empty bottle
→ INFERENCE: the hiker is exhausted, not rude

💙 Two Points of View, Both Real

When Mia explained what she'd noticed, Theo realized something important: his hurt feelings were real, AND Mia's read on the hiker was also probably true. Comparing points of view isn't about deciding who's “right” — it's about understanding the WHOLE picture.

👁 Watch Me Think

When two points of view seem to disagree, I don't have to pick a winner. Instead, I ask: what does EACH point of view notice that the other might be missing? Putting both perspectives together usually gives a fuller, more empathetic understanding than just one alone.

🧩 Picture It: Two Views, One Fuller Picture

Theo's view: hurt feelings (real)
+
Mia's view: hiker is exhausted (also real)
=
A fuller understanding of the whole moment
