🪑 The Main Idea Table

Think of a main idea like the top of a table. Key details are the legs. The legs hold the tabletop up. If a detail does not hold up the main idea, it is not a key detail, even if it is interesting.

Three strong legs hold up the big idea. You'll use this table all lesson long!

📖 Read: From the Grove to Your Glass

It is harvest week at cousin Rosa's orange grove near Arcadia, Florida. Grandpa drove Theo and Mia down to help. On the porch, Rosa handed them a little booklet called From the Grove to Your Glass. This booklet is nonfiction. That means it gives true facts and information instead of telling a made-up story. Let's read it with Theo and Mia!

Read each paragraph, or tap 🔊 to hear it. Notice the numbers. We'll use them to talk about each paragraph.

🍊
Nonfiction booklet
From the Grove to Your Glass
1

Have you ever wondered how orange juice gets to your table? Florida grows a lot of oranges, and most of them are made into juice. Before the juice reaches your glass, the oranges take a long trip. They go through many steps, from the grove to the store.

2

Oranges grow on trees that are planted in long rows. A farm full of orange trees is called a grove. Orange trees need warm weather, lots of sunshine, and water to grow. In spring, the trees are covered with small white flowers called orange blossoms, and bees buzz from flower to flower. A blossom can turn into a tiny green orange, which grows and ripens for many months. Fun fact: the orange blossom is the state flower of Florida.

3

At harvest time, many Florida oranges are picked by hand. Workers climb tall ladders to reach the fruit. They fill big bags that hang from their shoulders. Then they empty the bags into large bins, and trucks carry the bins away from the grove.

4

Some oranges go to a packing house, where they are washed and sorted. First, the oranges are washed with water and soft brushes to clean off dirt. Then workers and machines sort them, and oranges that are bruised or damaged are taken out. The best-looking oranges are packed into boxes to sell as fresh fruit. Most Florida oranges go straight to the juice plant instead, where they are washed and sorted too.

5

At the juice plant, machines squeeze the oranges to get the juice out. The juice is heated for a short time to kill germs and keep it safe. This step is called pasteurizing. Then the juice is poured into cartons or bottles and kept cold. Finally, trucks take the juice to stores, where your family can buy it. So the next time you pour a glass of orange juice, remember the long trip it took from the grove to your glass.

🎯 Topic vs. Main Idea

The topic is what a text is about, in one or two words. The main idea is the most important point the author makes about that topic. A topic is a word or two. A main idea is a whole sentence.

👁 Watch Me Think

When I read the booklet, I ask two questions. First: what is this mostly about? Oranges. That is the topic. Second: what is the author telling me about oranges? Oranges go through many steps to get from the grove to my glass. That is the main idea.

🧩 Picture It: Topic vs. Main Idea

TOPIC (one or two words): oranges
MAIN IDEA (a whole sentence): Oranges go through many steps to get from the grove to your glass.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Topic: bees. Main idea: Bees are busy helpers in an orange grove. See how the main idea tells something important about the topic?

🪑 Key Details Hold It Up

Key details are facts that tell more about the main idea and help prove it is true. They answer questions like who, what, where, when, why, and how. Some details are interesting but do not support the main idea. Those details are not key details.

👁 Watch Me Think

Paragraph 2 says the orange blossom is the state flower of Florida. That is a cool fact! But does it tell me how oranges grow? No. So it is not a key detail for that paragraph.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Main idea of paragraph 3: At harvest time, oranges are picked by hand. Key details: workers climb ladders, they fill shoulder bags, and they empty the bags into bins. All three legs hold up the top!

🔎 The Big Idea of the Whole Booklet

To find the main idea of a whole passage, first find the main idea of each paragraph. Then ask what they all have in common. The title and the first and last sentences give big clues, because authors often state the main idea there.

👁 Watch Me Think

The title is From the Grove to Your Glass. The first paragraph says the oranges take a long trip. The last sentence says to remember the long trip from the grove to your glass. All three clues point to the same big idea!

🧩 Picture It: Paragraphs Add Up

Paragraph 2: grow → Paragraph 3: pick → Paragraph 4: wash and sort → Paragraph 5: squeeze and sell
= Oranges go through many steps to get from the grove to your glass.

🤝 Let's Try Together

If every paragraph in a booklet tells about a different job on a farm, the main idea of the whole booklet might be: A farm needs many kinds of work.
