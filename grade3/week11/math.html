🍊 Picture It: Harvest Day at the Citrus Grove

This week, Theo and Mia are spending harvest week at their cousin Rosa's family orange grove near Arcadia, in DeSoto County, Florida. Grandpa drove them down from the trading post!

Look at the trees. They are planted in straight rows, with the same number of trees in every row. That makes each block of the grove an array, so we can count the trees with multiplication!

🍊 Citrus Grove Facts

  • Florida is famous for growing oranges, and many groves are in DeSoto County.
  • Orange trees grow in long, straight rows in a grove.
  • In Florida, the orange harvest runs from about fall through spring.
  • Many oranges are picked by hand and dropped into big bags, then poured into bins.
  • Most Florida oranges are squeezed to make orange juice.

Today you will learn your 6s, 7s, 8s and 9s facts, plus three big tricks: the turn-around trick, the break-apart trick, and the rules for multiplying by 1 and 0.

🌟 Word Wrangler

Flip each card to learn this week's vocabulary! Tap the speaker to hear the word or the meaning.

factor
A number you multiply. In 6 × 8 = 48, the factors are 6 and 8.
product
The answer to a multiplication problem. In 6 × 8 = 48, the product is 48.
turn-around property
You can switch the order of the factors and get the same product: 6 × 8 = 8 × 6.
break-apart property
Break a factor into two smaller parts, multiply each part, then add: 7 × 8 = 7 × 5 + 7 × 3.
identity property
Any number times 1 equals that same number: 9 × 1 = 9.
zero property
Any number times 0 equals 0: 8 × 0 = 0.

Grown-up math books call the turn-around property the commutative property, and the break-apart property the distributive property. They are the same ideas!

🔄 Turn-Around Facts: 6s and 7s

Rosa showed Theo a block of orange trees: 6 rows with 8 trees in each row. That is 6 × 8 = 48 trees. Then Theo walked around to the side of the block. From there he saw 8 rows of 6 trees. Same trees, same total: 8 × 6 = 48!

👁 Watch Me Think

When I turn an array on its side, nothing is added and nothing is taken away, so the product stays the same. That means if I know 6 × 8 = 48, I also know 8 × 6 = 48. Every fact I learn gives me a free turn-around fact!

6 × 7 = 42 and 7 × 6 = 42

✂️ The Break-Apart Trick

At the packing shed, Mia lined up oranges in 7 rows of 8. She did not know 7 × 8 yet, but she knew her 5s! She broke the 8 into 5 + 3. Then she found 7 × 5 = 35 and 7 × 3 = 21, and added: 35 + 21 = 56.

👁 Watch Me Think

When a fact is hard, I break one factor into two easier parts. Fives are easy for me, so I like to break a number into 5 and something more. I multiply each part, then add the two products together. The answer is the same as the whole fact.

🧩 Picture It

7 × 8 = 7 × 5 + 7 × 3
= 35 + 21
= 56

🤝 Let's Try Together

Find 6 × 9. Break 9 into 5 + 4. 6 × 5 = 30 and 6 × 4 = 24. Add them: 30 + 24 = 54. So 6 × 9 = 54.

🔍 Patterns in the 8s and 9s, plus 1 and 0

Rosa's uncle packs oranges in crates of 9. He showed Theo a secret about the 9s: in 9 × 6 = 54, the tens digit, 5, is one less than 6. And the two digits, 5 and 4, add up to 9! This pattern works for 9 × 2 all the way to 9 × 10.

For the 8s, Mia doubles a 4s fact. 4 × 7 = 28, and double 28 is 56, so 8 × 7 = 56.

👁 Watch Me Think

To find 9 × 8, I think: the tens digit is one less than 8, so it is 7. Then 7 plus what makes 9? 2! So 9 × 8 = 72. I can check with the turn-around fact or by breaking apart: 9 × 5 + 9 × 3 = 45 + 27 = 72.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Any number times 1 is that same number: 1 crate of 9 oranges is 9 oranges. Any number times 0 is 0: 3 empty crates hold 0 oranges.
