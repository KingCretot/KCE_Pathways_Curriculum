🌟 Word Wrangler

Flip each card to learn this week's vocabulary! Tap 🔊 to hear the word, then flip and tap 🔊 to hear what it means and see how the booklet uses it.

kelp
A large seaweed that grows very fast in cool ocean water. In the booklet: “Giant kelp is a seaweed that grows so tall it makes a forest under the ocean.”
holdfast
The part of a kelp plant that grips a rock. In the booklet: “Each kelp plant grips a rock on the sea floor with a holdfast.”
blubber
A thick layer of fat that keeps some sea animals warm. In the booklet: “Seals and whales have a thick layer of fat, called blubber, to keep them warm.”
sea urchin
A small, round sea animal covered in spines. In the booklet: “Sea urchins are small, round animals covered in spines.”
raft
A group of sea otters resting together on the water. In the booklet: “A group of otters resting together is called a raft.”
partners
Two or more living things that help each other. In the booklet: “You can see both partners up close.”

🖼️ Picture It: The Main Idea Table

Informational text teaches true facts. The main idea is the most important point a paragraph makes. Key details are the facts that hold it up, like legs hold up a table.

🔎 Where Can the Main Idea Hide?

The main idea likes to play hide and seek. Ask: Which sentence do ALL the other sentences help explain? That sentence is the main idea.

First sentence

The author says it right away, then explains it. In the booklet: Paragraphs 1, 5, and 6.

Middle sentence

A few facts come first, then the big point, then more facts. In the booklet: Paragraph 4.

Last sentence

The facts build up, and the last sentence pulls them together. In the booklet: Paragraph 2.

Not stated

No one sentence says it. Add up the details to find it yourself. In the booklet: Paragraph 3.

Ask: What is this paragraph MOSTLY about? Which facts prove it?

📖 Read: Partners in the Kelp Forest

It is a foggy spring morning in Monterey, California. The Monterey Bay Aquarium sits right on the edge of the bay, on Cannery Row, where a sardine cannery used to be. Itzel, age 8, and her brother Joaquin, age 9, are about to see sea otters and a real kelp forest. First, they read this information booklet. Read all six paragraphs with them.

Read each paragraph, or tap 🔊 to hear it. Read the heading first. As you read, ask: What is this paragraph mostly about?

Information Booklet
Partners in the Kelp Forest
All about sea otters and giant kelp in Monterey Bay, California
1

A Forest Under the SeaGiant kelp is a seaweed that grows so tall it makes a forest under the ocean. Each kelp plant grips a rock on the sea floor with a holdfast. A holdfast looks like roots, but it only holds on. Small balls filled with gas float the kelp up toward the sunlight. Giant kelp can grow up to two feet in just one day! Fish, crabs, and snails find hiding places in the kelp.

2

Built for Cold WaterThe ocean water in Monterey Bay is chilly all year. Seals and whales have a thick layer of fat, called blubber, to keep them warm. Sea otters do not have blubber. Instead, they have the thickest fur of any animal on Earth, with up to one million hairs on a patch of skin about the size of a postage stamp. Otters spend a lot of time each day cleaning and fluffing their fur so it traps air next to their skin. Their thick, clean fur is what keeps sea otters warm in the cold ocean.

3

Always HungryA sea otter eats about one-fourth of its body weight in food every day. For a 60-pound kid, that would be 15 pounds of food! Otters dive down to the sea floor to find clams, crabs, snails, and spiny sea urchins. They tuck the food into a loose pocket of skin under each front leg. Back at the surface, an otter floats on its back and uses a rock to crack open hard shells.

4

Otters Protect the ForestSea urchins are small, round animals covered in spines. They love to munch on kelp. Sea otters help keep the kelp forest healthy. When there are too many urchins, they can chew through the kelp until the forest is gone. Sea otters eat lots of urchins, so the kelp has a chance to keep growing.

5

A Forest That Helps OttersThe kelp forest helps sea otters, too. When otters rest, they often wrap themselves in kelp so they do not drift away. A mother otter may wrap her pup in kelp while she dives for food. A group of otters resting together is called a raft. The kelp forest is also full of the crabs, snails, and urchins that otters love to eat.

6

See the Partners Up CloseAt the Monterey Bay Aquarium in Monterey, California, you can see both partners up close. The Kelp Forest exhibit is 28 feet tall, and real sunlight shines down into it. It was the first exhibit to keep a living kelp forest growing inside an aquarium. Nearby, rescued sea otters swim, dive, and groom their fur. Sea otters and kelp forests help each other, and that is why scientists work hard to protect them both.

🔎 Where Is the Main Idea Hiding? Part 1

The main idea is the most important point of a paragraph. Sometimes it is the first sentence. But authors can also put it in the middle or at the end. And sometimes the author never writes it at all, so you put the details together to find it.

👁 Watch Me Think

I read paragraph 2. The first sentence is about chilly water. Then I read about blubber, fur, and fluffing. The last sentence says their thick, clean fur is what keeps sea otters warm. Every other sentence helps explain that, so the main idea is hiding at the END.

🧩 Picture It: The Main Idea Test

Read the whole paragraph first.
Ask: Which sentence do all the others explain?
No sentence fits? Add up the details yourself.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Let’s try one together. Read paragraph 4. Is the first sentence, about spiny urchins, the main idea? No! The rest of the paragraph is about how otters protect the forest. The main idea is sentence 3: Sea otters help keep the kelp forest healthy. It was hiding in the middle.

🎯 Too Big, Too Small, Just Right, Part 1

When you write or choose a main idea, test its size. A main idea that is too big is true, but it could be about a whole book. A main idea that is too small is just one detail. A main idea that is just right covers all the details in the paragraph and nothing more.

🐘 Too Big

True, but it could fit a whole book: The ocean is full of animals.

🐜 Too Small

Only one detail: Otters use rocks.

✅ Just Right

Covers all the details in the paragraph: Sea otters eat a lot of food and have clever ways to get it.

👁 Watch Me Think

I test three main ideas for paragraph 3. Animals need food is too big, because it could be about any animal. Otters crack shells with rocks is too small, because it is only one detail. Sea otters eat a lot of food and have clever ways to get it covers every detail, so it is just right.

🧩 Picture It: The Size Test

Too big: could fit almost any paragraph.
Too small: only one detail.
Just right: covers ALL the details.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Let’s try one together. Paragraph 5 tells about otters resting in kelp, mothers wrapping pups, rafts, and food. Is Otters wrap their pups in kelp just right? No, it is only one detail, so it is too small. The kelp forest helps sea otters covers them all.

🦦 Key Details: Proof for the Main Idea, Part 1

Key details are the facts that prove or explain the main idea. To check a detail, ask: Does this fact help show the main idea is true? Some facts are interesting but are about something else. When you explain a key detail, use the word because to tell HOW it supports the main idea.

👁 Watch Me Think

Paragraph 4 says sea otters help keep the kelp forest healthy. I look for proof. Urchins munch on kelp. Too many urchins can wipe out a forest. Otters eat lots of urchins. Each fact holds up the main idea, because together they show how otters protect the kelp.

🧩 Picture It: Explain It With Because

Main idea: what the paragraph is mostly about.
Key detail: a fact that proves it.
Say: This detail supports the main idea because ...

🤝 Let's Try Together

Let’s try one together. Does the fact that a holdfast grips a rock support paragraph 4? No. It is true, but it is about how kelp stays in place, not how otters keep the forest healthy. It is not a key detail for paragraph 4.
