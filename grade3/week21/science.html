🌦️ Weather or Climate?

Four pen pals write letters about home. Kelechi lives in Miami, Florida. Bilal lives in Phoenix, Arizona. Bettina lives in Chicago, Illinois. Kalani lives in Hilo, Hawaii. Kelechi writes, "It is raining right now!" That is weather: what the air is like at one time. Bilal writes, "Phoenix gets very little rain most years." That is climate: the usual weather over a long time. Scientists find a climate by taking the average of the weather over many years, usually 30 years or more.

👁 Watch Me Think

Bettina says, "It was 80 degrees in Chicago today. So Chicago has a hot climate." Hmm. One day is weather. Chicago's January average is only about 25 degrees! To learn the climate, I need the pattern from many years, not one day.

🌍 Climate Zones and the Equator

Scientists sort places with similar climates into climate zones. Near the equator, the middle of Earth, sunlight hits the ground almost straight on. The light is packed into a small space, so it heats the land a lot. Places there are tropical: hot all year. Near the North and South Poles, sunlight comes in at a slant. The same light spreads over a much bigger space, so it is weaker there. Places there are polar: cold all year. Some places in between have warm summers and cold winters. And some places get very little rain at all. Those are dry.

👁 Watch Me Think

Is it hot near the equator because it is closer to the Sun? No! That is a common mix-up. The real reason is the angle of the sunlight. Straight-on light is strong. Slanted light is spread out and weak. Earth is round, so the light hits the middle straight on and the top and bottom at a slant.

⛰️ Up a Mountain: Cold Near the Equator?

Here is a puzzle. Mount Kilimanjaro in Tanzania, Africa, is close to the equator. But its top has had ice fields called glaciers! Why? The higher you go up a mountain, the colder the air usually gets. Even near the equator, the tops of very tall mountains can be cold. Scientists say most of Kilimanjaro's ice has melted away in the last hundred years, but a little still sits near the top.

👁 Watch Me Think

Kalani lives in Hilo, near the beach, where it is warm all year. If Kalani climbs a tall mountain, will it get warmer or colder? The air gets colder as you climb higher. So Kalani should pack a jacket, even in Hawaii!

🏠 Try It at Home: The Flashlight Angle Test

Why is slanted sunlight weaker? Test it with a flashlight! You will shine the same light two ways and see how big the bright spot is. Ask a grown-up to help you set up.

👁 Watch Me Think

I hold the flashlight one ruler-length above graph paper and point it straight down. The spot is small and bright. I trace it. Then I tilt the flashlight, keeping it the same distance away. The spot gets bigger and dimmer. Same light, bigger space, so each square gets less light. That is like sunlight near the poles!

Safety: A grown-up must help. Never shine the flashlight into anyone's eyes. Use a regular flashlight, never a laser pointer, a hot lamp, or a candle. If you dim the room, turn the lights back on before you walk around.

🗺️ Wladimir Köppen, Climate Mapmaker

Wladimir Köppen found a way to sort every place on Earth into climate groups. Scientists still use updated versions of his groups today.

Wladimir Köppen

1846–1940

Köppen was a German scientist who was born in St. Petersburg, Russia. He studied weather and climate. He saw that the kinds of plants that grow in a place depend on how warm it is and how much rain it gets. Rainforests need heat and lots of rain. Deserts get very little. In 1900, he published a way to sort the world's climates into five main groups, using temperature and rainfall. His groups matched the plants that grow in each place.

🔬 More Climate Scientists

Climate scientists come from all over the world. They ask, "What is the usual pattern, and how is it changing?"

Warren Washington

atmospheric scientist

Warren Washington grew up in Portland, Oregon. He was the second African American to earn a doctorate in atmospheric science, the science of air and weather. Starting in the 1960s, he helped build some of the first computer models of Earth's air and climate. Later he added oceans and sea ice to the models. In 2010, he won the National Medal of Science.

Syukuro Manabe

born 1931

Syukuro Manabe was born in Japan and did his science in the United States. He built computer models that show how Earth's climate works. In 2021, he won the Nobel Prize in Physics for this work.

Joanne Simpson

1923–2010

Joanne Simpson was the first woman to earn a doctorate in meteorology, the science of weather. She finished it in 1949. She studied clouds in the tropics, near the equator. Later, at NASA, she led the science team for a satellite that measured rain over the tropics from space.
