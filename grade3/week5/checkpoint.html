🏗️ Remember This? Numbers & Comparing

Back in Weeks 1 and 2, you learned what digits are worth, and how to compare and order numbers. Let's remember how it works!

👁 Remember This

Every digit in a number is worth something based on WHERE it sits. In 573, the 5 is worth 500 (hundreds place), the 7 is worth 70 (tens place), and the 3 is worth 3 (ones place). To compare two numbers, start with the biggest place value and work your way down — whichever number has the bigger digit first wins.

🧩 Picture It: Comparing Step by Step

Compare 462 and 439
Hundreds: 4 = 4, tied — keep going
Tens: 6 vs 3 — 6 is bigger
462 > 439

🤝 Quick Practice

Round 385 to the nearest hundred. The tens digit is 8, which is 5 or more, so you round UP: 385 rounds to 400.

🏠 Remember This? Character & Conflict

In Weeks 1 and 2, you met Theo, learned his setting, and watched him solve a problem on the trail. Let's remember how stories work!

👁 Remember This

Every story needs a MAIN CHARACTER (who it's about) and a SETTING (where and when it happens). Then, most stories have a CONFLICT (a problem), an ATTEMPT (a try at solving it), and a RESOLUTION (how it gets fixed). That's the basic shape almost every story follows — including all of Theo's adventures this unit!

🧩 Picture It: Story Shape

CHARACTER + SETTING → who and where
CONFLICT → the problem
ATTEMPT → the try
RESOLUTION → how it gets fixed

⛰️ Remember This? Regrouping & Word Problems

In Weeks 3 and 4, you learned to regroup when adding and subtracting, and to solve word problems by finding clue words. Let's remember how it works!

👁 Remember This

When a column adds up to 10 or more, you REGROUP: write the ones digit, carry the rest to the next column. When the top digit is too small to subtract, you BORROW from the next column. In word problems, clue words like “total” or “altogether” mean ADD, while “left” or “remaining” mean SUBTRACT.

🧩 Picture It: Regrouping

256 + 178
Ones: 6+8=14 → write 4, carry 1
Tens: 5+7+1=13 → write 3, carry 1
Hundreds: 2+1+1=4
Answer: 434

🤝 Quick Practice

Estimate 342+257 by rounding first: 342 rounds to 300, 257 rounds to 300. Estimate: 300+300=600.

🏠 Remember This? Sequencing & Theme

In Weeks 3 and 4, Theo went camping, and you learned about story sequencing AND how to find a story's theme. Let's remember how it works!

👁 Remember This

Stories have a BEGINNING (where characters are introduced), a MIDDLE (most of the action), and an END (where everything wraps up). Transition words like first, next, then, and last show the order. The THEME is different from the PLOT — the plot is what HAPPENED, but the theme is the LESSON the story teaches, like Theo learning that sharing can feel better than keeping something for yourself.

🧩 Picture It: Plot vs. Theme

PLOT: Theo shared the last marshmallow with Mia
THEME: Sharing with someone you love can feel better than keeping something for yourself

🤝 Quick Practice

In the camping story, Theo pitched the tent FIRST, then built the fire, then cooked chili. Transition words like “first” and “then” keep the order clear.
