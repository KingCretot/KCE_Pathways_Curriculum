🌟 Word Wrangler

Flip each card to learn this week's vocabulary! Tap the speaker to hear the word or the meaning.

perimeter
The distance all the way around a shape. Add the lengths of all the sides.
area
The number of square units that cover a flat shape.
side length
How long one side of a shape is.
polygon
A closed, flat shape with straight sides, like a triangle or a rectangle.
square unit
A square that is 1 unit long on each side. We use square units to measure area.
rectangle
A shape with 4 straight sides and 4 square corners. Its opposite sides are the same length.

Here is a memory trick: the word perimeter has the word rim in it. A rim goes around the edge, just like a perimeter!

📏 Perimeter: The Distance Around

Ms. Washington asked Jalen to put a short fence around a new raised bed. The bed is 6 feet long and 4 feet wide. Jalen started at one corner and walked all the way around, adding each side: 6 + 4 + 6 + 4 = 20 feet of fence. That distance around is called the perimeter.

👁 Watch Me Think

To find a perimeter, I add the lengths of all the sides. I pick a starting corner and go around, so I do not skip a side or count one twice. In a rectangle, opposite sides are the same length. If I know the length and the width, I know all four sides!

🧩 Picture It

6 + 4 + 6 + 4 = 20 feet
Perimeter is a length, so we measure it in feet.

🤝 Let's Try Together

A square bed has 4 sides, and each side is 5 feet long. Add all 4 sides: 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 20 feet. The perimeter is 20 feet.

🔎 Finding a Missing Side

Priya is building a triangle-shaped herb bed. The whole border, all the way around, is 12 feet. Two sides are 4 feet and 5 feet. How long is the third side? Priya added the sides she knew: 4 + 5 = 9. Then she subtracted: 12 − 9 = 3 feet.

👁 Watch Me Think

When I know the perimeter but one side is missing, I add the sides I know. Then I subtract that sum from the perimeter. What is left is the missing side. I can check by adding all the sides again: 4 + 5 + 3 = 12.

🧩 Picture It

4 + 5 = 9
12 − 9 = 3
The missing side is 3 feet.

🤝 Let's Try Together

A rectangle bed has a perimeter of 14 feet. It is 4 feet long. The two long sides are 4 + 4 = 8 feet. 14 − 8 = 6 feet are left for the two short sides, and 3 + 3 = 6. So the bed is 3 feet wide.

🌱 Area or Perimeter?

Mateo is working on the tomato bed. It is 5 feet long and 3 feet wide. First he puts a wooden border around the edge. That is the perimeter: 5 + 3 + 5 + 3 = 16 feet. Then he fills the inside with soil. That is the area: 5 × 3 = 15 square feet.

👁 Watch Me Think

I ask myself: is the job around the edge, or inside the shape? A fence, a border, or a row of bricks goes around, so I need the perimeter in feet. Soil, straw, or grass covers the inside, so I need the area in square feet. Same bed, two different measures!

🧩 Picture It

Around the edge: perimeter = 5 + 3 + 5 + 3 = 16 feet
Covering the inside: area = 5 × 3 = 15 square feet

🤝 Let's Try Together

Ms. Washington wants a row of bricks along the edge of the path, so she needs the perimeter. She also wants to cover the path with wood chips, so she needs the area.

🔄 Same Perimeter, Different Area

Jalen has exactly 12 feet of fence for a new bed. He tries three rectangles. A 1 by 5 bed, a 2 by 4 bed, and a 3 by 3 bed all use 12 feet of fence. But the space inside is different! The 3 by 3 bed has the most room for plants.

👁 Watch Me Think

The same perimeter does not always mean the same area, and the same area does not always mean the same perimeter. Long, skinny rectangles hold only a little space for their fence. Rectangles that are close to a square hold the most space.

Then Priya sets out 12 square stepping stones, each 1 foot on every side, to make a patio. She can make a 1 by 12, a 2 by 6, or a 3 by 4 rectangle. They all have an area of 12 square feet, but the distance around is different.

🧩 Picture It

1 by 5: area 5 square feet
2 by 4: area 8 square feet
3 by 3: area 9 square feet

🤝 Let's Try Together

Which uses less trim, a 2 by 6 patio or a 3 by 4 patio? 2 + 6 + 2 + 6 = 16 feet and 3 + 4 + 3 + 4 = 14 feet. Both have an area of 12 square feet, but the 3 by 4 patio uses less trim.
