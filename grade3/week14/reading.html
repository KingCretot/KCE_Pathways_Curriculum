🖼️ Picture It: Easy as PIE

Every author has a reason for writing. That reason is called the author's purpose. Remember the word PIE. P is for persuade, I is for inform, and E is for entertain.

P: Persuade

Clues: opinion words like best, reasons, and asking you to come, buy, join, or sign up.

I: Inform

Clues: facts, numbers, steps, and headings that teach.

E: Entertain

Clues: characters, made-up events, and funny parts.

🔍 Be a Clue Detective

To find the purpose, be a clue detective. Facts and steps are clues that the author wants to inform. Opinion words, reasons, and asking you to come, buy, join, or sign up are clues that the author wants to persuade. Characters and funny, made-up events are clues that the author wants to entertain.

Ask: Why did the author write this? Then look for clues to prove it!

🥧 Three Reasons Authors Write

Authors write for three main reasons. To inform means to give facts and teach the reader something true. To persuade means to try to get the reader to do something or believe something. To entertain means to tell a story or poem that is fun to read.

👁 Watch Me Think

Imagine a page that says: Sunflowers can grow taller than a grown-up. Young sunflowers turn to follow the sun across the sky. I ask: why did the author write this? The author gives true facts to teach me. So the purpose is to inform.

🧩 Picture It: P.I.E.

P is for persuade: Do this! Believe this!
I is for inform: Learn these facts.
E is for entertain: Enjoy this story.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Read this: Buy a ticket to the Garden Fair. It will be the most fun you have all year! What does the author want? For you to buy a ticket. That means the purpose is to persuade.

🔍 Clues That Show the Purpose

Authors leave clues that show their purpose. Facts, numbers, steps, and headings are clues for inform. Opinion words like best, reasons, and asking you to come, buy, join, or sign up are clues for persuade. Characters, talking animals or objects, and funny events are clues for entertain.

👁 Watch Me Think

A lemonade stand sign says: Our lemonade is the best on the block, so buy a cup today! I see the word best. That is an opinion. I also see buy a cup today. That asks me to do something. Both clues point to persuade.

P: Persuade

Clues: opinion words like best, reasons, and asking you to come, buy, join, or sign up.

I: Inform

Clues: facts, numbers, steps, and headings that teach.

E: Entertain

Clues: characters, made-up events, and funny parts.

🤝 Let's Try Together

Try this one: Once upon a time, a tiny carrot wanted to be as tall as the sunflowers. Is there a character? Yes, a carrot who wants something! A made-up character is a clue for entertain.

🎯 Finding the MAIN Purpose

Some texts have more than one kind of clue. A flyer can have facts, and a story can have a true detail. To find the MAIN purpose, ask: What does the author want most? Do they want me to learn something, to do something, or to enjoy a story?

👁 Watch Me Think

A cereal box says: Each bowl has iron and fiber. Try Crunchy Oats today! The first part is a fact. But the box wants me to buy the cereal, so its main purpose is to persuade. The fact is there to help convince me.

🧩 Picture It: What Does the Author Want Most?

If the author wants you to learn something, the purpose is to inform.
If the author wants you to do or believe something, the purpose is to persuade.
If the author wants you to enjoy a story, the purpose is to entertain.

🤝 Let's Try Together

A zoo poster says: Lions sleep up to 20 hours a day. Come see them at the zoo this weekend! It has a fact, but what does it want most? For you to visit the zoo. The main purpose is to persuade.

🗣️ My Point of View vs. the Author's

An author's point of view is what the author thinks or feels about a topic. Your point of view is what YOU think. You might agree with the author, or you might disagree. Either way, give a reason.

👁 Watch Me Think

A writer says: Rainy days are the best days to read a book. That is the writer's point of view. I think sunny days are better, because I like to read outside. That is my point of view. I keep them apart with two sentence starters: The author thinks... and I think...

🤝 Let's Try Together

Let's try one together. A poster says: Cats make the best pets. The author thinks cats are the best pets. Now you: I agree or I disagree, because... Your reason makes your point of view strong.

How to Plant a Tomato
By Ms. Odessa Freeman, Garden Leader
1

Tomatoes grow best in warm weather. In Atlanta, gardeners usually plant young tomato plants in spring, after the last cold night. Tomato plants need at least six hours of sunshine every day.

2

Step 1: Dig a hole deep enough to cover most of the stem. Tomato plants grow new roots along their stems, so planting them deep makes them strong. Step 2: Hold the plant carefully so the stem does not break. Set it in the hole and fill the hole with soil.

3

Step 3: Push a tall stake into the ground next to the plant. As the plant grows, tie the stem to the stake. Step 4: Water the soil around the plant, not the leaves. Most tomatoes are ready to pick two to three months after planting.
