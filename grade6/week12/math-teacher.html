Unit 3: Rates and Percents · Week 12 · Math · Teacher Plan

Understanding Percents

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Explain a percent as a rate per 100 and model it on a hundred grid.
  • Convert among percents, fractions and decimals.
  • Find a percent of a number using benchmarks, double number lines and multiplication.
  • Find the whole when a part and a percent are known.
  • Calculate sales tax and total cost, including Florida's state and county rates.

Standards

  • MA.6.AR.3.4: Apply ratio relationships to solve mathematical and real-world problems involving percentages using the relationship between two quantities.
  • MA.6.AR.3.5: Solve mathematical and real-world problems involving ratios, rates and unit rates, including comparisons, mixtures, ratios of lengths and conversions within the same measurement system.
  • MA.K12.MTR.2.1: Demonstrate understanding by representing problems in multiple ways.
  • MA.K12.MTR.7.1: Apply mathematics to real-world contexts.
  • Financial literacy: sales tax, tax-exempt items and sales tax holidays (Jump$tart Spending).

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • A hundred grid (drawn or printed) and dry beans
  • A store flyer or website with school supplies
  • Food labels, clothing tags and ads
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • percent: A rate per 100; the symbol % means "out of 100." 45% = 45/100.
  • benchmark percent: An easy percent to use for estimating, such as 10%, 25%, 50% and 1%.
  • part, whole, percent: In "30 is 60% of 50," 30 is the part, 50 is the whole and 60% is the percent.
  • sales tax: Money a state or county adds to the price of many purchases, a percent of the price.
  • subtotal: The total price of items before tax is added.
  • exempt: Not required to pay; in Florida, most groceries are exempt from sales tax.

Key questions to ask

  • Percent of what? What is the whole?
  • Is your answer reasonable compared with a benchmark?
  • Is this item taxed?
  • Can a percent be more than 100? When?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Adding percents that refer to different wholes.
  • Moving the decimal point the wrong way.
  • Confusing finding the part with finding the whole.
  • Thinking percents can never exceed 100.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Keep a benchmark card: 50% = 1/2, 25% = 1/4, 10% = 1/10, 1% = 1/100.
  • Use hundred grids for every conversion at first.
  • Use a percent ratio table template.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Find the tax rates in two other Florida counties and compare the tax on a $500 purchase.
  • Show that finding 7.5% of $40 and finding 40% of $7.50 give the same answer, and explain why.

Background for the teacher

Florida's state sales tax is 6%; county discretionary sales surtaxes bring combined rates to 6% to 8% (2026). DeSoto County's surtax is 1.5%, for a combined 7.5%. Most groceries and prescription drugs are exempt. Florida's permanent back-to-school sales tax holiday (HB 7031, 2025) runs every August 1 to 31 and exempts clothing and bags $100 or less, school supplies $50 or less, learning aids $30 or less and personal computers $1,500 or less. The rain item: P(rain at least one day) = 1 − 0.7 × 0.7 = 0.51 if the days are independent.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. A car travels 210 miles in 3 1/2 hours. What is its speed in miles per hour?
    Answer: 60 miles per hour
    210 ÷ 3 1/2 = 210 ÷ 7/2 = 210 × 2/7 = 60.
  2. A 24-ounce bag of granola costs $4.80. What is the unit price in cents per ounce? (Type the number of cents.)
    Answer: 20 cents per ounce
    $4.80 ÷ 24 = $0.20, which is 20 cents per ounce.
  3. Explain why a pace of 12 minutes per mile is slower than a pace of 9 minutes per mile.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: More minutes for each mile means it takes longer to cover the same distance
    Model answer: At 12 minutes per mile, each mile takes 3 more minutes than at 9, so the runner covers the same distance more slowly.
  4. Complete the ratio table: 3 out of every 5 students walk to the park.
    Answer: 30; 100
    3 : 5 = 30 : 50 = 60 : 100. 60 out of 100 is today's topic: a percent.

Learn 1: What a Percent Means

  1. Complete the conversions.
    Answer: 0.4; 7; 75
    40 ÷ 100 = 0.4; 0.07 × 100 = 7; 3/4 = 75/100.
  2. Write 35% as a fraction in simplest form.
    Answer: 7/20
    35/100; the GCF is 5, so 7/20.
  3. Rosa scored 27 out of 30 on a quiz. What percent is that?
    Answer: 90 %
    27/30 = 9/10 = 90/100 = 90%.
  4. A sunflower was 40 inches tall in June and 60 inches tall in August. Its August height is 150% of its June height. Explain how a percent can be more than 100.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: 100% is the whole original amount (40 inches); more than 100% means more than the original; 60 is 1 1/2 times 40, which is 150%
    Model answer: 100% means the whole June height, 40 inches. The plant grew taller than that, to 60 inches, which is 1 1/2 times as tall, so it is 150% of its June height.

Learn 2: Finding a Percent of a Number

  1. What is 10% of $65?
    Answer: $6.50
    Move the decimal one place left: $6.50.
  2. What is 15% of $60? (Hint: 10% plus half of 10%.)
    Answer: $9
    10% = $6; 5% = $3; 15% = $9.
  3. A phone battery holds 4,000 units of charge. It shows 35%. How many units are left?
    Answer: 1400 units
    0.35 × 4,000 = 1,400.
  4. A family's monthly income is $3,200. They save 12% of it. How much do they save each month?
    Answer: $384
    0.12 × $3,200 = $384 (or 10% = $320 plus 2% = $64).

Learn 3: Finding the Whole

  1. Amara has saved $45, which is 25% of the cost of a bike. How much does the bike cost?
    Answer: $180
    25% → $45, so 100% → 4 × $45 = $180.
  2. A water tank is 40% full and holds 120 gallons right now. How many gallons does it hold when full?
    Answer: 300 gallons
    40% → 120, 10% → 30, 100% → 300.
  3. Priya says, "If 20 is 50% of a number, the number is 10." Explain her mistake and give the correct answer.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: She took 50% of 20 instead of finding the whole; 20 is half of the number, so the number is 40
    Model answer: She found 50% of 20 instead of finding the whole. If 20 is half of the number, the whole number is 40.

Learn 4: Money: Sales Tax

  1. Find the tax on the paint brush set alone ($12.00) at 7.5%.
    Answer: $0.90
    $12 × 0.075 = $0.90.
  2. A $24 garden hose is bought in a county with 7% total sales tax. What is the total cost?
    Answer: $25.68
    Tax: $24 × 0.07 = $1.68. Total: $24 + $1.68 = $25.68 (or $24 × 1.07).
  3. In August, a family buys $90 of school supplies (every item under $50) and a $120 pair of shoes in DeSoto County. How much sales tax do they pay?
    Answer: $9.00
    Supplies under $50 each are tax-free in August. The $120 shoes are over the $100 clothing limit, so the whole price is taxed: $120 × 0.075 = $9.00.
  4. Sales tax pays for things like roads, schools and emergency services. Why do you think Florida exempts most groceries from sales tax?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Gives a reason linked to food being a basic need or to fairness for families; A reasonable explanation
    Model answer: Food is a basic need that every family must buy. Taxing groceries would take a bigger share of the money of families who earn less, so exempting them makes the tax fairer.

Hands-On

  1. List the five percents you found, what each is a percent of, and what it means in your own words.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Five real percents; Correct whole identified for each; Clear meaning in own words
    Model answer: 1. Cereal: 25% Daily Value of iron: one bowl gives a fourth of the iron I need in a day. 2. T-shirt: 60% cotton: 60 of every 100 parts of the fabric are cotton. 3. Phone: 82% battery: 82 of every 100 parts of a full charge are left. 4. Sign: 30% off shoes: you pay $30 less for every $100 of the price. 5. Juice: 100% juice: all of the drink is fruit juice, not a juice drink with added sugar.
  2. Show your list with prices, mark each item tax-free or taxed in August, and calculate the August tax, the October tax at 7.5% and the savings.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A list with prices; Each item correctly marked using the August limits; August and October tax calculated correctly; Savings found
    Model answer: Backpack $28, notebooks and pens $12, sneakers $110 = $150. August: only the sneakers are taxed (over the $100 clothing limit): $110 × 0.075 = $8.25. October: $150 × 0.075 = $11.25. Savings: $3.00.

Think Deeper

  1. You have exactly $50 to spend at a store in a county with 7.5% sales tax. What is the most expensive item you can buy so that the total with tax is $50 or less? Explain your method and check your answer.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Price × 1.075 ≤ $50, so price ≤ $50 ÷ 1.075 ≈ $46.51; Check: $46.51 × 1.075 ≈ $50.00 (and $46.52 goes over); Clear explanation
    Model answer: The total is the price times 1.075, so the price can be at most $50 ÷ 1.075 ≈ $46.51. Check: $46.51 × 0.075 = $3.49 in tax, and $46.51 + $3.49 = $50.00. A $46.52 item would go just over $50.
  2. A forecast says there is a 30% chance of rain on Saturday and a 30% chance on Sunday. Mateo says that means a 60% chance of rain this weekend. Is he right? Explain using what a percent means.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Not right: you cannot simply add the two percents; Each percent is a chance out of 100 for its own day; the chance of rain on at least one day is more than 30% but less than 60%; Reasoning about what each percent refers to
    Model answer: No. Each 30% is a chance out of 100 for its own day, so you can't just add them. If you could, two days of 60% would make 120%, which is impossible. The chance of rain on at least one day is higher than 30% but less than 60%.

Exit Check

  1. Complete.
    Answer: 60; 0.45
    0.6 × 100 = 60%; 45 ÷ 100 = 0.45.
  2. What is 25% of 64?
    Answer: 16
    25% is one fourth: 64 ÷ 4 = 16.
  3. 12 is 40% of what number?
    Answer: 30
    40% → 12, so 10% → 3, so 100% → 30.
  4. Find the total cost of a $30 item with 6% sales tax.
    Answer: $31.80
    $30 × 0.06 = $1.80; $30 + $1.80 = $31.80.
