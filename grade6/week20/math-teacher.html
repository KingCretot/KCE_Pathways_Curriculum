Unit 4: Integers and the Coordinate Plane · Week 20 · Math · Teacher Plan

Unit 4 Review and Checkpoint

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Choose the right integer tool for a problem: absolute value, a coordinate distance or an operation.
  • Compute with integers using all four operations and the order of operations.
  • Plot points, reflect them and find distances on the coordinate plane.
  • Interpret integers in real contexts: elevation, temperature, balances, debt, profit and loss.
  • Demonstrate mastery of Unit 4 on the checkpoint (85 or higher).

Standards

  • MA.6.NSO.1.1: Extend previous understanding of numbers to define rational numbers. Plot, order and compare rational numbers.
  • MA.6.NSO.1.2: Given a mathematical or real-world context, represent quantities that have opposite direction using rational numbers. Compare them on a number line and explain the meaning of zero within its context.
  • MA.6.NSO.1.3: Given a mathematical or real-world context, interpret the absolute value of a number as the distance from zero on a number line. Find the absolute value of rational numbers.
  • MA.6.NSO.4.1: Apply and extend previous understandings of operations with whole numbers to add and subtract integers with procedural fluency.
  • MA.6.NSO.4.2: Apply and extend previous understandings of operations with whole numbers to multiply and divide integers with procedural fluency.
  • MA.6.GR.1.1: Extend previous understanding of the coordinate plane to plot rational number ordered pairs in all four quadrants and on both axes. Identify the x- or y-axis as the line of reflection when two ordered pairs have an opposite x- or y-coordinate.
  • MA.6.GR.1.2: Find distances between ordered pairs, limited to the same x-coordinate or the same y-coordinate, represented on the coordinate plane.

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • 5 envelopes or folded papers, paper, pencil, tape
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • integer: A whole number or its opposite.
  • absolute value: A number's distance from zero.
  • ordered pair: (x, y): a location on the coordinate plane.
  • additive inverse: A number's opposite; together they make 0.
  • average change: Total change divided by the number of periods.
  • net worth: What you own minus what you owe.

Key questions to ask

  • Which tool fits this problem?
  • What does zero mean here?
  • Same side or opposite sides?
  • Same signs or different signs?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • A bigger debt is a greater number.
  • Two negatives make a positive when adding.
  • Subtract before multiplying.
  • Points on an axis belong to a quadrant.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Keep the Unit 4 toolkit chart visible during review (not during the checkpoint).
  • Allow counters and a number line during review.
  • Read checkpoint word problems aloud if reading is a barrier.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Create a five-clue integer escape room for a family member.
  • Find the net worth of an imaginary business with ten entries.

How the lesson runs

During the live lesson, the student shares their screen and answers the Reconnect questions and the Learn checks live while you teach. The Hands-On activity, Think Deeper and Exit Check are completed after class.

Scoring

The interactive lesson scores itself: full points on the first try, half points on the second try, then the answer and explanation appear. Mastery is 85. Written responses arrive in your results email with the score; score them with the rubric below before the student moves on.

Checkpoint scoring

Checkpoint questions are scored separately from the lesson. The student gets one try per question and the answers are not shown, so the checkpoint measures independent mastery and a retake stays fair. Your results email shows the auto-scored checkpoint points, a list of each item marked correct or MISSED, and the points available for the written items. Score the written items with their rubrics, then: checkpoint score = (auto points + rubric points) / total possible × 100. Mastery is 85. Below 85: teach a targeted review lesson on the parts missed (A to D), then the student retakes the checkpoint. Record both attempts in the Weekly Score Log.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. Week 16: What is |−38|?
    Answer: 38
    Distance from zero.
  2. Week 17: Reflect (−4, 7) across the y-axis. Type the ordered pair.
    Answer: (4, 7)
    Across the y-axis, x changes sign.
  3. Week 18: −13 − (−20) = ?
    Answer: 7
    −13 + 20 = 7.
  4. Week 19: (−48) ÷ 6 × (−2) = ?
    Answer: 16
    −48 ÷ 6 = −8; −8 × (−2) = 16.

Learn 1: Review: Integers, Opposites and Absolute Value

  1. Order from least to greatest: 3, −9, 0, −2, 7, −12
    Answer: −12 → −9 → −2 → 0 → 3 → 7
    Farther left on the number line is less.
  2. Account A has a balance of −$30 and account B has −$75. Which balance is greater, and which account has the bigger debt? Explain how both can be true.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: −30 is greater (to the right of −75); Account B has the bigger debt (|−75| = 75 > 30); Explains the difference between comparing values and comparing absolute values
    Model answer: −$30 is the greater balance because it is to the right of −$75 on a number line. But account B has the bigger debt, because |−75| = 75 is larger than |−30| = 30. Comparing balances compares the numbers; comparing debts compares their absolute values.

Learn 2: Review: The Coordinate Plane

  1. How many units apart are (−3, −6) and (−3, 9)?
    Answer: 15 units
    Opposite sides: |−6| + |9| = 15.
  2. Three corners of a square are (−2, 3), (4, 3) and (4, −3). Find the fourth corner and the area. Explain how you know it is a square.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Fourth corner (−2, −3); Sides of 6 and 6 found with absolute value, so it is a square; Area 36 square units
    Model answer: The fourth corner is (−2, −3). The top side, from (−2, 3) to (4, 3), is |−2| + |4| = 6 units, and the right side, from (4, 3) to (4, −3), is |3| + |−3| = 6 units. All sides are 6, so it's a square, and the area is 6 × 6 = 36 square units.

Learn 3: Review: All Four Operations

  1. Evaluate: −6 + 4 × (−5) − (−18) ÷ 3
    Answer: -20
    4 × (−5) = −20; −18 ÷ 3 = −6; −6 + (−20) − (−6) = −6 − 20 + 6 = −20.
  2. Leo wrote: −8 − 5 × (−2) = −13 × (−2) = 26. Find his mistake, explain it, and give the correct answer.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: He subtracted before multiplying (order of operations); Correct work: 5 × (−2) = −10; −8 − (−10) = −8 + 10 = 2
    Model answer: Leo subtracted first, but multiplication comes before subtraction. 5 × (−2) = −10, then −8 − (−10) = −8 + 10 = 2.

Learn 4: Money: Year-End Money Review

  1. What is Maya's net worth? (Add everything she owns and everything she owes, as integers.)
    Answer: 273 dollars
    185 + 140 = 325 owned; −30 + (−22) = −52 owed; 325 + (−52) = 273.
  2. What was her average weekly profit for the last 4 weeks?
    Answer: 27 dollars
    52 − 18 + 40 + 34 = 108; 108 ÷ 4 = 27.
  3. Write Maya two pieces of advice for next year, each backed by one of her numbers.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Two pieces of advice; Each supported with a specific number and correct reasoning (pay off the loan and mill credit with her cash; average $27 profit suggests steady growth; avoid loss weeks)
    Model answer: First, she should pay off the $30 loan and the $22 she owes the mill now, since she has $185 in cash; then she won't owe anyone. Second, her average profit of $27 a week is good, but the −$18 week shows one bad week can erase a lot, so she should keep $50 saved for emergencies like a flour price jump.

Hands-On

  1. Show your work and answer for all five clues. Then write the clue you invented and its answer.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Clue 1: 5; Clue 2: −17; Clue 3: 12; Clue 4: −12; Clue 5: −3; Work shown; A correct original clue with answer
    Model answer: Clue 1: 9 − 4 = 5. Clue 2: −14 + 6 − 9 = −17 feet. Clue 3: |−5| + |7| = 12. Clue 4: three negatives, so negative: 2 × 3 × 1 × 2 = 12, so −12. Clue 5: −24 ÷ 8 = −3 degrees per hour. My clue: "A balance of −$40 gets three payments of $15. What is the new balance?" Answer: −40 + 45 = $5.

Think Deeper

  1. Write a letter to a younger student explaining what negative numbers are and how to add, subtract, multiply and divide them. Use at least two real-life examples and one number line or counter explanation.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Explains what negative numbers mean (opposites, below zero); Correct explanation for each operation; At least two real-life examples; A number line or counter model; Clear and friendly
    Model answer: Dear Sam, negative numbers are numbers less than zero, like a temperature of −5° on a cold morning or a bank balance of −$10 when you owe money. On a number line they go to the left of zero. To add, think of moving: adding a positive moves right, adding a negative moves left, so 3 + (−7) lands on −4. To subtract, add the opposite: 2 − (−5) is 2 + 5 = 7, like canceling a $5 debt. To multiply or divide, work out the numbers first, then the sign: if the signs match, the answer is positive; if they're different, it's negative. So (−3) × 4 = −12, but (−3) × (−4) = 12. Your friend, Jordan

Checkpoint: Part A: Integers and absolute value

  1. The bottom of a Florida spring is 42 feet below the water's surface. Write the bottom as an integer, with the water's surface as 0.
    Answer: -42 feet
    Below the surface is negative.
  2. Which is greater, −11 or −7? Type the number.
    Answer: −7
    −7 is to the right of −11.
  3. What is the opposite of |−16|?
    Answer: -16
    |−16| = 16; its opposite is −16.
  4. A diver is at −35 feet and a pelican is flying at 28 feet. Which is farther from sea level, and by how many feet? Explain using absolute value.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric (4 points toward the checkpoint).
    Rubric: The diver; |−35| = 35 and |28| = 28; 35 − 28 = 7 feet farther
    Model answer: The diver is farther from sea level: |−35| = 35 feet and |28| = 28 feet, so the diver is 7 feet farther.

Checkpoint: Part B: The coordinate plane

  1. Which quadrant contains (6, −2.5)? Type the Roman numeral.
    Answer: IV
    (+, −).
  2. Reflect (−8, −3) across the x-axis. Type the ordered pair.
    Answer: (−8, 3)
    Across the x-axis, y changes sign.
  3. How many units apart are (−9, 4) and (−2, 4)?
    Answer: 7 units
    Same side: 9 − 2 = 7.
  4. A rectangle has corners at (−5, 2), (3, 2), (3, −4) and (−5, −4). Find its perimeter and area. Show how you found each side length.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric (4 points toward the checkpoint).
    Rubric: Sides 8 and 6 found with absolute values; Perimeter 28 units; Area 48 square units
    Model answer: Top side: |−5| + |3| = 8 units. Right side: |2| + |−4| = 6 units. Perimeter: 8 + 6 + 8 + 6 = 28 units. Area: 8 × 6 = 48 square units.

Checkpoint: Part C: Operations with integers

  1. −17 + 9 = ?
    Answer: -8
    Different signs: 17 − 9 = 8, negative.
  2. −6 − 14 = ?
    Answer: -20
    −6 + (−14) = −20.
  3. (−7) × (−9) = ?
    Answer: 63
    Same signs: positive.
  4. Evaluate: 20 ÷ (−4) − 3 × (−6)
    Answer: 13
    −5 − (−18) = −5 + 18 = 13.
  5. Explain why −5 − (−8) equals 3. Use a number line, counters or a real-life example.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric (4 points toward the checkpoint).
    Rubric: Subtracting −8 means adding 8 (or removing a debt); Correct model or story reaching 3
    Model answer: Subtracting −8 is the same as adding 8. Start at −5 on a number line and move 8 to the right: you land on 3. It's like owing $5 and then having an $8 debt canceled.

Checkpoint: Part D: Integers in the real world

  1. At 5 a.m. the temperature was −11°F. By noon it was 16°F. What was the change in temperature?
    Answer: 27 degrees
    16 − (−11) = 27.
  2. A credit card balance is −$140. The owner pays $65, then buys a $28 book. What is the new balance?
    Answer: -103 dollars
    −140 + 65 − 28 = −103.
  3. A business had weekly results of +$45, −$15, +$30 and −$20. What was its average weekly profit?
    Answer: 10 dollars
    40 ÷ 4 = 10.
  4. A submarine descends at 15 feet per minute for 6 minutes, then rises 40 feet. Write one expression for its final depth, evaluate it, and explain what your answer means.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric (4 points toward the checkpoint).
    Rubric: Expression such as 6 × (−15) + 40; Value −50; Meaning: 50 feet below the surface
    Model answer: 6 × (−15) + 40 = −90 + 40 = −50. The submarine ends 50 feet below the surface.

Answer key: printable page

  1. Order from least to greatest: −4, 6, −10, 0, −1, 3.
    Answer: −10, −4, −1, 0, 3, 6.
  2. Name the quadrant of each point: (−3, 5), (2, −7), (−1, −1).
    Answer: II; IV; III.
  3. Find the distance between (4, −6) and (4, 5).
    Answer: |−6| + |5| = 11 units.
  4. Evaluate: (a) −12 + (−9) (b) 8 − 15 (c) −36 ÷ (−4) (d) 7 × (−6)
    Answer: (a) −21 (b) −7 (c) 9 (d) −42
  5. Evaluate: −10 + 6 × (−3) ÷ 2
    Answer: 6 × (−3) = −18; −18 ÷ 2 = −9; −10 + (−9) = −19.
  6. A balance of −$60 receives payments of $25 and $25, then a $12 purchase. Find the balance.
    Answer: −60 + 25 + 25 − 12 = −22.
