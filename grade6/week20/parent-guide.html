Week 20: Unit 4 Review, Checkpoints and Projects

This is the last week of Unit 4. In every subject your child reviews the unit, finishes a project (an argument essay and speech, a life-size body systems map, and a Mediterranean project) and takes the Unit 4 checkpoint. Math reviews a small business's year and solves an integer escape room.

How scoring works: each lesson scores itself. Your child gets two tries per question: full points on the first try, half points on the second, then the answer and explanation appear. A score of 85 or higher means the lesson is mastered. Written answers go to Jonathan, who scores them with a rubric.

Checkpoint scoring: checkpoint questions give one try and do not show answers, so the checkpoint fairly measures what your child can do alone. Jonathan adds the points for written answers and shares the checkpoint score. Mastery is 85; below 85 means a review lesson, then a retake. Projects below 85 are returned with feedback and revised once.

Time this week: About 60 minutes of live lessons per subject, plus project time (about 3 hours per project across the week) and one checkpoint per subject.

Gather this week: 5 envelopes or folded papers, paper, pencil, tape, a family member to hide clues, and optionally a small treat or prize (Math); your child's essay and 3 to 5 index cards, a timer, a phone or tablet to record, a mirror, a family member as an audience, and paper and pencil (ELA); a roll of butcher paper or large sheets taped together (about 5 feet long), markers and colored pencils, construction paper, scissors and glue or tape, red and blue yarn or string, a family member, and notes from Weeks 16 to 19 (Science); index cards or paper slips, markers or colored pencils, small household items to stand for trade goods, a world map, atlas or globe, 2 or more family members, and notes from Weeks 16 to 19 (Social Studies).

Heads up: Checkpoints are taken after class, independently, one try per question, with no answers shown. ELA's speech needs an audience: please listen, record it and give one strength and one suggestion. Science's body map needs floor space and a family member to trace your child. Social Studies' market uses small items: keep them away from young children and pets, and don't eat any food items used as trade goods.

ELA: Unit 4 Checkpoint and Argument Project

Your child reviews claims, evidence, appeals, fallacies, counterclaims and essay structure, finishes a researched argumentative essay, rehearses and records it as a 2- to 3-minute speech, and takes the Unit 4 checkpoint.

Ask at dinner: "What's your strongest piece of evidence, and where did it come from?"

Try it together: Be the audience for the final speech recording.

Signs it's clicking: They speak from note cards instead of reading word for word. Signs they need help: They rush through the speech. Ask them to pause after the hook and after each reason.

Math: Unit 4 Review and Checkpoint

Your child reviews integers, absolute value, the coordinate plane and all four integer operations, helps a home bakery find its net worth and average weekly profit, solves an integer escape room, and takes the Unit 4 checkpoint.

Ask at dinner: "Which tool fits this problem: absolute value, a distance on the grid, or an operation?"

Try it together: Hide the escape-room clues and let your child solve their way through the house.

Signs it's clicking: They explain why −$30 is a greater balance than −$75 but a smaller debt. Signs they need help: They subtract before multiplying. Review the order of operations.

Life Science: Unit 4 Checkpoint and Body Systems Project

Your child reviews levels of organization and the skeletal, muscular, circulatory, respiratory, digestive and nervous systems, explains how they work together to maintain homeostasis on a hot day, builds a life-size body map, and takes the Unit 4 checkpoint.

Ask at dinner: "What's happening inside your body right now as you walk up the stairs?"

Try it together: Trace your child's outline for the body map and listen to their presentation.

Signs it's clicking: They connect three or more systems in one explanation. Signs they need help: They describe each system separately. Ask, "How does that system depend on another one?"

World and American History: Unit 4 Checkpoint and Mediterranean Project

Your child compares how Persia, Athens, Sparta, Rome and Carthage were governed, reviews the Persian and Punic Wars, traces ideas such as the alphabet around the Mediterranean, runs a family market day, finishes the Mediterranean project, and takes the Unit 4 checkpoint.

Ask at dinner: "Which civilization would you most want to live in, and who would you be there?"

Try it together: Play the Mediterranean Market Day as a family.

Signs it's clicking: They ask who wrote a source and why before trusting it. Signs they need help: They think Greece and Rome invented everything. Ask where the alphabet and the calendar came from.
