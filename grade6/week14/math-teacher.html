Unit 3: Rates and Percents · Week 14 · Math · Teacher Plan

Converting Units with Ratios, and Currency Exchange

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Use conversion ratios to convert customary units of length, capacity and weight.
  • Convert metric units by using powers of 10.
  • Choose whether to multiply or divide, and check that the answer makes sense.
  • Use an exchange rate as a ratio to convert between currencies, including fees.
  • Solve multi-step real-world problems that combine unit conversions, unit rates and money.

Standards

  • MA.6.AR.3.5: Solve mathematical and real-world problems involving ratios, rates and unit rates, including comparisons, mixtures, ratios of lengths and conversions within the same measurement system.
  • MA.K12.MTR.2.1: Demonstrate understanding by representing problems in multiple ways.
  • MA.K12.MTR.7.1: Apply mathematics to real-world contexts.
  • Financial literacy: exchange rates and exchange fees (Jump$tart Spending).

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • A tape measure or yardstick, a 1-cup measure, a gallon jug, water
  • Paper, pencil, a calculator for checking only
  • Optional: a kitchen scale
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • conversion factor: A ratio of two equal amounts in different units, such as 12 inches : 1 foot.
  • customary units: The measurement system used in the United States: inches, feet, cups, pounds and so on.
  • metric system: A measurement system based on powers of 10: meters, liters, grams.
  • exchange rate: How much of one currency equals one unit of another, such as 1 U.S. dollar = 18 Mexican pesos.
  • exchange fee: A charge, often a percent, for changing money from one currency to another.

Key questions to ask

  • Should the number get bigger or smaller?
  • What ratio connects these two units?
  • Which direction does the exchange rate go?
  • How does a fee change what you receive?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Multiplying when converting to a larger unit.
  • Treating fluid ounces and ounces as the same.
  • Using 10 or 100 for customary units.
  • Multiplying by the exchange rate when converting back to dollars.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Use ratio tables for every conversion.
  • Post the conversion ladder.
  • Start currency problems with whole-number rates (18 and 155) before 0.90.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Convert your height to millimeters and to miles.
  • Find today's real exchange rate for the euro and compare it with the lesson's example rate.

Background for the teacher

Mars Climate Orbiter was lost September 23, 1999; the mishap investigation board found that ground software reported thruster impulse in pound-force seconds while navigation software expected newton-seconds. The orbiter cost about $125 million (the full mission about $328 million). Customary equivalences: 1 mi = 1,760 yd = 5,280 ft; 1 gal = 4 qt = 8 pt = 16 c; 1 c = 8 fl oz; 1 lb = 16 oz. Exchange rates in the lesson are examples, not current market rates; MA.6.AR.3.5 limits conversions to within one measurement system, so the lesson does not convert between metric and customary units.

Sources

  • NASA, Mars Climate Orbiter Mishap Investigation Board Phase I Report (1999).
  • NIST Handbook 44, Appendix C: General Tables of Units of Measurement.
  • Federal Reserve, Foreign Exchange Rates (for current rates).
  • Jump$tart Coalition, National Standards for Personal Financial Education (Spending).

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. A $48 jacket is 25% off. What is the sale price?
    Answer: $36
    Pay 75%: 0.75 × $48 = $36.
  2. Find a 20% tip on a $55 bill.
    Answer: $11
    10% = $5.50; double it: $11.
  3. A craft seller's supplies cost $14. She uses a 50% markup. What is her selling price?
    Answer: $21
    50% of $14 = $7; $14 + $7 = $21.
  4. Explain why 30% off followed by an extra 20% off is not the same as 50% off.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: The second discount is a percent of the smaller sale price, not the original; Example: $100 → $70 → $56, a 44% total discount
    Model answer: The extra 20% comes off the sale price, not the original price. On $100 you pay $70, then $56, which is only 44% off.

Learn 1: Customary Length: Ratio Tables and Conversion Factors

  1. A homeschool garden bed is 7 feet long. How many inches is that?
    Answer: 84 inches
    Smaller unit, so multiply: 7 × 12 = 84.
  2. A child is 54 inches tall. How many feet is that? Write a decimal.
    Answer: 4.5 feet
    Larger unit, so divide: 54 ÷ 12 = 4.5 feet (4 feet 6 inches).
  3. A family hikes 2 miles. How many feet is that?
    Answer: 10560 feet
    2 × 5,280 = 10,560 feet.
  4. A soccer field is 120 yards long. How many feet is that?
    Answer: 360 feet
    120 × 3 = 360 feet.
  5. Leo converts 15 feet to yards and gets 45 yards. Explain his mistake and give the correct answer.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: He multiplied instead of dividing; yards are larger, so the number should be smaller; Correct answer: 5 yards
    Model answer: He multiplied by 3, but yards are bigger than feet, so there should be fewer of them. He should divide: 15 ÷ 3 = 5 yards.

Learn 2: Capacity and Weight in the Kitchen and Barn

  1. A canning recipe needs 3 quarts of water. How many cups is that?
    Answer: 12 cups
    3 × 2 = 6 pints; 6 × 2 = 12 cups (or 1 quart = 4 cups).
  2. A family's goats give 2 gallons of milk a day. How many pints is that?
    Answer: 16 pints
    2 × 4 = 8 quarts; 8 × 2 = 16 pints.
  3. A bag of chicken feed weighs 50 pounds. How many ounces is that?
    Answer: 800 ounces
    50 × 16 = 800 ounces.
  4. A package of ground turkey weighs 40 ounces. How many pounds is that?
    Answer: 2.5 pounds
    40 ÷ 16 = 2.5 pounds.
  5. A recipe says "8 ounces of honey." Ana pours 1 cup (8 fluid ounces) of honey. Is that the same thing? Explain.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Not necessarily: fluid ounces measure volume, ounces measure weight; Honey is heavier than water, so 1 cup of honey weighs more than 8 ounces (she should weigh it)
    Model answer: No. Fluid ounces measure how much space something takes up, but ounces on a scale measure weight. Honey is heavier than water, so a cup of honey weighs more than 8 ounces. She should weigh it.

Learn 3: The Metric System: Powers of 10

  1. A cross-country race is 2.5 kilometers. How many meters is that?
    Answer: 2500 meters
    2.5 × 1,000 = 2,500 m.
  2. A sunflower is 350 centimeters tall. How many meters is that?
    Answer: 3.5 meters
    350 ÷ 100 = 3.5 m.
  3. A water bottle holds 1.2 liters. How many milliliters is that?
    Answer: 1200 mL
    1.2 × 1,000 = 1,200 mL.
  4. Compare converting 3.2 kilometers to meters with converting 3.2 miles to feet. Which is easier, and why?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Kilometers is easier: multiply by 1,000 (move the decimal) = 3,200 m; Miles needs 5,280, a harder number (16,896 feet); metric uses powers of 10
    Model answer: Kilometers is easier: 3.2 × 1,000 = 3,200 meters, just a decimal move. Miles to feet means 3.2 × 5,280 = 16,896, which takes real multiplication, because customary units don't use powers of 10.

Learn 4: Money: Currency Exchange

  1. At 1 USD = 18 MXN, how many Mexican pesos do you get for $50?
    Answer: 900 pesos
    50 × 18 = 900 pesos.
  2. A souvenir in Jamaica costs 4,650 Jamaican dollars. At 1 USD = 155 JMD, how much is that in U.S. dollars?
    Answer: $30
    4,650 ÷ 155 = $30.
  3. You exchange $300 for euros at 1 USD = 0.90 EUR, but the airport charges a 5% fee first. How many euros do you receive?
    Answer: 256.50 euros
    Fee: 5% of $300 = $15, leaving $285. $285 × 0.90 = 256.50 euros.
  4. In Paris, a sandwich costs 9 euros. At home the same sandwich costs $9.50. At 1 USD = 0.90 EUR, which is more expensive? Show your work.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Converts 9 euros to dollars: 9 ÷ 0.90 = $10; Concludes Paris is more expensive by $0.50
    Model answer: 9 euros ÷ 0.90 = $10. So the Paris sandwich costs $10, which is $0.50 more than the $9.50 one at home.

Hands-On

  1. Record your measurements and conversions for length, capacity and pacing. Show the ratio or operation you used for each.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Three length measurements converted to feet and yards, one to meters; Cups to fill a gallon recorded and used for quarts and pints; Step length and paced distance converted; Correct multiply or divide each time
    Model answer: Door: 80 in ÷ 12 = 6 2/3 ft; ÷ 36 = 2 2/9 yd. Table: 72 in = 6 ft = 2 yd. Bed: 75 in = 6.25 ft. Door in cm: 203 cm ÷ 100 = 2.03 m. Gallon: 16 cups, so a quart is 16 ÷ 4 = 4 cups and a pint is 2 cups. 10 steps = 240 in, so 1 step = 24 in = 2 ft. Yard: 45 steps × 2 ft = 90 ft = 30 yd.
  2. Did your gallon count match the 16 cups in the lesson? If not, give two reasons your count might be off.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Compares the count with 16; Two sensible sources of error (cups not filled exactly, spills, jug not exactly a gallon, foam or bubbles)
    Model answer: I counted 16 1/2 cups. My cups were probably not filled exactly to the line, and some water spilled, so my count was a little off.

Think Deeper

  1. A family needs 150 feet of fencing for a goat pen. Fencing is sold in rolls of 10 yards for $24 per roll. How many rolls should they buy, and what will it cost? Then explain what could go wrong if they forgot to convert units.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Converts 150 feet to 50 yards; 5 rolls; $120; Explains the error (thinking 150 feet means 15 rolls and spending $360, or buying too little)
    Model answer: 150 feet ÷ 3 = 50 yards. Each roll is 10 yards, so they need 50 ÷ 10 = 5 rolls, costing 5 × $24 = $120. If they forgot to convert, they might think they need 150 ÷ 10 = 15 rolls and spend $360, three times too much.
  2. A family is taking $300 to Mexico. The airport exchange gives 16.5 pesos per dollar. A bank at home gives 17.8 pesos per dollar. How many more pesos does the bank give? About how many dollars is that difference worth? Should they care? Explain.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Airport 4,950 pesos; bank 5,340 pesos; difference 390 pesos; 390 ÷ 17.8 ≈ $21.91 (about $22); A reasoned judgment (yes: about 8% more money; compare rates and fees before exchanging)
    Model answer: Airport: 300 × 16.5 = 4,950 pesos. Bank: 300 × 17.8 = 5,340 pesos. The bank gives 390 more pesos, which is about 390 ÷ 17.8 ≈ $21.91. Yes, they should care: that is about 8% more money, enough for a few meals, just for comparing rates before they travel.

Exit Check

  1. How many yards are in 9 feet?
    Answer: 3 yards
    9 ÷ 3 = 3 yards.
  2. How many cups are in 6 quarts?
    Answer: 24 cups
    6 × 4 = 24 cups.
  3. How many grams are in 4.2 kilograms?
    Answer: 4200 grams
    4.2 × 1,000 = 4,200 g.
  4. At 1 USD = 155 JMD, how many Jamaican dollars do you get for $40?
    Answer: 6200 Jamaican dollars
    40 × 155 = 6,200 JMD.
