Unit 5: Expressions and Equations · Week 22 · Math · Teacher Plan

Writing and Evaluating Expressions

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Identify the parts of an algebraic expression: terms, variables, coefficients and constants.
  • Translate words into algebraic expressions and expressions into words.
  • Evaluate expressions by substituting values and using the order of operations.
  • Use properties (distributive, commutative, associative) to write equivalent expressions.
  • Write and use cost formulas for real businesses.

Standards

  • MA.6.AR.1.1: Given a mathematical or real-world context, translate written descriptions into algebraic expressions and translate algebraic expressions into written descriptions.
  • MA.6.AR.1.3: Evaluate algebraic expressions using substitution and order of operations.
  • MA.6.AR.1.4: Apply the properties of operations to generate equivalent algebraic expressions with integer coefficients.
  • Financial literacy: comparing prices and plans; writing cost formulas (Jump$tart Spending and Saving).

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • A cereal box or shoebox, scissors, index cards, a marker, tape
  • Paper and pencil
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • variable: A letter or symbol that stands for an unknown or changing number.
  • expression: A mathematical phrase with numbers, variables and operations but no equal sign.
  • term: A part of an expression separated by + or − signs.
  • coefficient: The number multiplied by a variable, such as 4 in 4x.
  • constant: A term with no variable; its value doesn't change.
  • substitute: To replace a variable with a number.
  • equivalent expressions: Expressions that have the same value for every value of the variable.

Key questions to ask

  • What does the variable stand for here?
  • Which is the rate and which is the fixed fee?
  • Did you use parentheses when you substituted?
  • How could you check whether two expressions are equivalent?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • "7 less than n" means 7 − n.
  • 3(x + 2) = 3x + 2.
  • Variables always stand for one fixed secret number.
  • 4x means 4 + x or a 40-something number.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Highlight coefficients, variables and constants in three colors.
  • Use a words-to-symbols reference card.
  • Allow input-output tables before writing formulas.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Write a cost formula for a business with two variables (for example, a price per hour and a price per mile).
  • Make a table of costs for Plans A and B for 0 to 5 GB and find where they are closest.

Background for the teacher

Expressions in this lesson use integer coefficients, consistent with MA.6.AR.1.4. Equations and inequalities are taught in Weeks 23 and 24; the Think Deeper profit question is solved by testing values, not by solving an inequality. Prices are practice figures.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. Evaluate 3⁴.
    Answer: 81
    3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 81.
  2. Evaluate: 2 + 5 × (6 − 4)²
    Answer: 22
    2² = 4; 5 × 4 = 20; 2 + 20 = 22.
  3. How much simple interest does $400 earn in 3 years at 5%?
    Answer: 60 dollars
    400 × 0.05 × 3 = 60. I = Prt is itself an expression with variables.
  4. Last week you used I = P × r × t. What do P, r and t stand for, and why is it useful to write a formula with letters instead of numbers?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Names what each letter stands for; Letters let one formula work for any amount, rate and time
    Model answer: P is the principal, r is the rate and t is the time in years. Using letters means one formula works for any amount of money, any rate and any number of years.

Learn 1: The Language of Algebra

  1. For the expression 8y − 3 + 2z:
    Answer: 3; 8; −3
    Terms 8y, −3 and 2z. The constant is −3 because subtraction makes it negative.
  2. Write an expression for "10 less than a number n."
    Answer: n − 10
    Start with n, then subtract 10. 10 − n would be "n less than 10."
  3. Write an expression for "twice the sum of a number x and 5."
    Answer: 2(x + 5) or 2x + 10
    The sum goes in parentheses so all of it is doubled. Type the parentheses with no spaces.
  4. Write the expression 3(k − 4) in words, then explain how it is different from 3k − 4.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Correct words for 3(k − 4); 3(k − 4) multiplies all of (k − 4) by 3; 3k − 4 multiplies only k, then subtracts 4
    Model answer: Three times the difference of k and 4. In 3(k − 4), you subtract 4 from k first and then multiply the whole result by 3. In 3k − 4, you multiply only k by 3, then subtract 4.

Learn 2: Evaluating Expressions

  1. Evaluate 6x + 11 when x = 7.
    Answer: 53
    6(7) + 11 = 42 + 11 = 53.
  2. Evaluate 3m² − m when m = 4.
    Answer: 44
    3(16) − 4 = 48 − 4 = 44.
  3. Evaluate (p + q)/2 when p = 13 and q = −5.
    Answer: 4
    (13 + (−5))/2 = 8/2 = 4.
  4. Kai evaluated −x² when x = 3 and got 9. Explain his mistake and give the correct value.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Explains which part the exponent applies to, then gives the correct value; Identifies what Kai squared by mistake
    Model answer: The expression −x² means the opposite of x². So −(3)² = −9. Kai squared −3, which would be (−x)², a different expression.

Learn 3: Equivalent Expressions and Properties

  1. Use the distributive property to rewrite 6(2y − 5) without parentheses.
    Answer: 12y − 30
    6 × 2y = 12y and 6 × 5 = 30.
  2. Combine like terms: 9a + 4 − 2a + 7
    Answer: 7a + 11
    9a − 2a = 7a; 4 + 7 = 11.
  3. Are 2(x + 4) and 2x + 4 equivalent? Test them with x = 3 and explain.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Correct value of each expression for x = 3; Correct conclusion with the correct equivalent expression
    Model answer: With x = 3, 2(3 + 4) = 14 but 2(3) + 4 = 10. They give different values, so they aren't equivalent. The correct equivalent expression is 2x + 8.

Learn 4: Money: Writing Cost Formulas

  1. Use Maya's formula 9n + 4. How much does an order of 7 loaves cost?
    Answer: 67 dollars
    9(7) + 4 = 63 + 4 = 67.
  2. A bike rental costs $8 per hour plus a $5 helmet fee. Write an expression for the cost of h hours.
    Answer: 8h + 5
    Rate × hours + fixed fee.
  3. Plan A costs 30 + 5g dollars a month and Plan B costs 15 + 10g dollars, where g is gigabytes used. Which plan is cheaper for 2 GB? For 5 GB? Explain what the coefficients and constants tell you.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Correct costs and cheaper plan for 2 GB; Correct costs and cheaper plan for 5 GB; Explains coefficients as cost per GB and constants as the fixed fee
    Model answer: For 2 GB: Plan A costs 30 + 5(2) = $40 and Plan B costs 15 + 10(2) = $35, so B is cheaper. For 5 GB: A costs $55 and B costs $65, so A is cheaper. The constants are the fixed monthly fees and the coefficients are the cost per gigabyte. B has a lower fee but a higher rate, so it's better only for light users.

Hands-On

  1. Write two of the secret rules you played with, the inputs and outputs for each, and how you figured out the rule.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Two rules with input-output pairs that fit; A clear strategy for finding the rule
    Model answer: Rule 3x + 2: inputs 1, 2, 5, 10 gave 5, 8, 17, 32. Each time the input went up by 1, the output went up by 3, so the coefficient was 3; then 3(1) = 3 and 5 − 3 = 2, so the constant was 2. Rule 4x − 1: inputs 0, 1, 3 gave −1, 3, 11.
  2. Share your business, your cost formula (explain each part), and your price list for 1 to 6 units.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A formula with a coefficient and constant explained; Six correct prices
    Model answer: Dog walking: 6w + 3, where w is the number of walks: $6 per walk plus a $3 booking fee. Prices: 1 walk $9, 2 walks $15, 3 walks $21, 4 walks $27, 5 walks $33, 6 walks $39.

Think Deeper

  1. A rectangular garden is x feet long and 4 feet wider than it is long. Write an expression for its width and two different equivalent expressions for its perimeter. Then find the perimeter when x = 10, using both expressions.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Correct expression for the width; Two correct equivalent perimeter expressions; Both evaluated correctly for x = 10
    Model answer: The width is x + 4. Perimeter: 2x + 2(x + 4), which simplifies to 2x + 2x + 8 = 4x + 8. When x = 10: 2(10) + 2(14) = 20 + 28 = 48 feet, and 4(10) + 8 = 48 feet. Both give the same answer because the expressions are equivalent.
  2. Maya's costs are $3 in ingredients per loaf plus $10 a week for gas. She charges 9n + 4 per order. Write an expression for her weekly costs if she bakes n loaves, and an expression for her profit if she sells all n loaves in one order. How many loaves must she sell in one order to make a profit of at least $40?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Correct cost expression; Correct, simplified profit expression; Tests values and finds the correct number of loaves
    Model answer: Costs: 3n + 10. Profit: (9n + 4) − (3n + 10) = 9n − 3n + 4 − 10 = 6n − 6. If n = 7, profit is 6(7) − 6 = $36, not enough. If n = 8, profit is 6(8) − 6 = $42. She needs to sell at least 8 loaves.

Exit Check

  1. In the expression 5w + 9, what is the coefficient?
    Answer: 5
    It multiplies the variable w.
  2. Evaluate 4t − 7 when t = 6.
    Answer: 17
    24 − 7 = 17.
  3. Write an expression for "8 more than the product of 3 and a number n."
    Answer: 3n + 8
    The product of 3 and n is 3n; 8 more adds 8.
  4. Why is it useful to write a business's prices with a variable?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: One formula gives the price for any number of items; Quick, consistent pricing and easy comparison
    Model answer: One formula gives the price for any amount, so the business can quote prices quickly and the same way every time.

Answer key: printable page

  1. Identify the terms, coefficients and constant in 7a − 2b + 10.
    Answer: Terms: 7a, −2b, 10; coefficients 7 and −2; constant 10.
  2. Write expressions: (a) 12 less than a number n (b) the quotient of a number and 6 (c) 4 times the sum of a number and 9
    Answer: (a) n − 12 (b) n/6 (c) 4(n + 9)
  3. Evaluate 5x² − 2y when x = 3 and y = −4.
    Answer: 5(9) − 2(−4) = 45 + 8 = 53.
  4. Simplify: 3(2m + 5) − 4m
    Answer: 6m + 15 − 4m = 2m + 15.
  5. A car wash charges $12 per car plus a $5 travel fee. Write a formula and find the cost for 6 cars.
    Answer: 12c + 5; 12(6) + 5 = $77.
  6. Show that 4(x − 2) and 4x − 8 are equivalent by testing x = 5.
    Answer: 4(3) = 12 and 20 − 8 = 12; same value (and the distributive property shows they are always equal).
