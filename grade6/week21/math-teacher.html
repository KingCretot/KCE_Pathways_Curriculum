Unit 5: Expressions and Equations · Week 21 · Math · Teacher Plan

Exponents and Order of Operations

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Write repeated multiplication with exponents and evaluate powers, including squares and cubes.
  • Evaluate powers of fractions, decimals and integers.
  • Apply the order of operations, including parentheses and exponents, to evaluate expressions.
  • Explain and correct common order-of-operations mistakes.
  • Calculate simple interest and compare how savings grow over time.

Standards

  • MA.6.NSO.3.3: Evaluate positive rational numbers and integers with natural number exponents.
  • MA.6.NSO.3.4: Express composite whole numbers as a product of prime factors with natural number exponents.
  • MA.6.AR.1.3: Evaluate algebraic expressions using substitution and order of operations.
  • Financial literacy: saving, interest and growth (Jump$tart Saving).

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • Uncooked rice or dried beans, a muffin tin or egg carton
  • A deck of playing cards (face cards removed)
  • Paper and pencil
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • exponent: A small raised number that tells how many times to use the base as a factor.
  • base: The number being multiplied repeatedly in a power.
  • power: An expression with a base and an exponent, such as 2⁵.
  • squared: Raised to the power 2, like the area of a square.
  • cubed: Raised to the power 3, like the volume of a cube.
  • principal: The amount of money first saved or borrowed.
  • simple interest: Interest paid only on the principal: I = P × r × t.

Key questions to ask

  • What is the base, and how many times is it a factor?
  • What does the exponent touch?
  • Which operation comes next, and are these two done left to right?
  • Is the interest earned only on the principal?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • 2⁵ means 2 × 5.
  • Multiplication always comes before division.
  • −4² equals 16.
  • Squaring always makes a number bigger.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Write out repeated multiplication before evaluating.
  • Underline the next operation at each step.
  • Use a percent-to-decimal reference card.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • How many grains would be on the whole chessboard? (2⁶⁴ − 1.) Research how that compares with world rice production.
  • Compare simple and compound interest on $1,000 at 5% for 20 years.

Background for the teacher

The wheat and chessboard (or rice) legend appears in many versions, often attributed to India and to the invention of chess (chaturanga). 2⁶³ ≈ 9.22 × 10¹⁸; the whole board totals 2⁶⁴ − 1 ≈ 1.8 × 10¹⁹ grains. A 2-pound bag of long-grain rice holds roughly 30,000 to 45,000 grains, depending on the variety. Compound interest appears here only as a preview; it is not assessed.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. (−3) × (−3) = ?
    Answer: 9
    Same signs: positive. Today you'll write it as (−3)².
  2. (−2) × (−2) × (−2) = ?
    Answer: -8
    Three negatives: negative.
  3. Evaluate: −10 + 4 × (−3)
    Answer: -22
    Multiply first: 4 × (−3) = −12; −10 + (−12) = −22.
  4. In Week 20, Leo subtracted before multiplying and got the wrong answer. Why do we need everyone to agree on an order of operations?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Without an agreed order, people would get different answers to the same expression
    Model answer: If everyone did the steps in a different order, the same expression would give different answers, so we agree on one order.

Learn 1: Exponents: Repeated Multiplication

  1. Evaluate 4³.
    Answer: 64
    4 × 4 × 4 = 64.
  2. Write 6 × 6 × 6 × 6 × 6 using an exponent.
    Answer: 6⁵
    Five factors of 6. On a keyboard, type 6^5.
  3. Aria says 5² = 10. Explain her mistake and give the correct value.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: She multiplied base × exponent; 5² means 5 × 5 = 25
    Model answer: Aria multiplied 5 × 2. The exponent means use 5 as a factor two times: 5 × 5 = 25.

Learn 2: Powers of Fractions, Decimals and Negatives

  1. Evaluate (2/3)². Type a fraction.
    Answer: 4/9
    2/3 × 2/3 = 4/9.
  2. Evaluate (1.5)².
    Answer: 2.25
    1.5 × 1.5 = 2.25.
  3. Explain why (−5)² and −5² have different values, and find both.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: (−5)² = (−5)(−5) = 25: the exponent applies to −5; −5² = −(5 × 5) = −25: the exponent applies only to 5
    Model answer: In (−5)², the parentheses make the whole −5 the base: (−5) × (−5) = 25. In −5², only the 5 is squared, then the result is made negative: −25.

Learn 3: Order of Operations

  1. Evaluate: 20 − 12 ÷ 4 × 3
    Answer: 11
    12 ÷ 4 = 3; 3 × 3 = 9; 20 − 9 = 11.
  2. Evaluate: (6 + 4)² ÷ 5 − 2³
    Answer: 12
    10² = 100; 100 ÷ 5 = 20; 2³ = 8; 20 − 8 = 12.
  3. Evaluate: [18 − (3 + 1)²] × (−2)
    Answer: -4
    (4)² = 16; 18 − 16 = 2; 2 × (−2) = −4.
  4. Jayden evaluated 24 ÷ 4 × 2 as 24 ÷ 8 = 3. Explain his mistake and find the correct value.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Multiplication and division are done left to right, so divide first; 24 ÷ 4 = 6; 6 × 2 = 12
    Model answer: Jayden multiplied first because M comes before D in the memory aid, but multiplication and division are done left to right. 24 ÷ 4 = 6, then 6 × 2 = 12.

Learn 4: Money: Simple Interest and Growth

  1. How much simple interest does $800 earn in 3 years at 5% per year?
    Answer: 120 dollars
    800 × 0.05 × 3 = 120.
  2. Maya's $500 earns 4% simple interest. What is her total after 8 years?
    Answer: 660 dollars
    Interest: 500 × 0.04 × 8 = 160; total 500 + 160 = 660.
  3. After 5 years, Maya's $500 grows to $600 with simple interest but about $608.33 with yearly compound interest. Explain why compound interest grows more.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Compound interest is paid on the principal plus earlier interest; So each year's interest is a little bigger, and the gap grows over time
    Model answer: With compound interest, each year's interest is added to the balance, and the next year's interest is figured on that bigger amount. So the interest itself earns interest, and each year's growth is a little larger than the year before.

Hands-On

  1. Record how many grains were in each cup you filled, written as powers of 2. Which power of 2 is closest to 40,000? Explain what this shows about how fast doubling grows.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Correct powers of 2 for each cup; 2¹⁵ = 32,768 (or 2¹⁶ = 65,536) closest to 40,000; Explains rapid growth
    Model answer: Cups: 2⁰ = 1, 2¹ = 2, 2² = 4, 2³ = 8, 2⁴ = 16, 2⁵ = 32, 2⁶ = 64, 2⁷ = 128, 2⁸ = 256. 2¹⁵ = 32,768 is closest to 40,000, so a whole 2-pound bag would run out around cup 16. Doubling grows so fast that just 16 cups use up a whole bag, and 64 squares would need more rice than exists.
  2. Write your two best expressions from the card game, evaluate each step by step, and name the target.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Two expressions using four cards; Correct step-by-step evaluation following the order of operations; Target named
    Model answer: Target 10, cards 2, 3, 4, 6: (3 + 2) × (6 − 4) = 5 × 2 = 10, an exact match for 2 points. Target 18, cards 4, 1, 5, 2: 4² + (5 − 2) − 1 = 16 + 3 − 1 = 18, also exact. I used each card once and did the parentheses first, then the exponent, then left to right.

Think Deeper

  1. Maya has $1,200 to save for 6 years. Bank A pays 3% simple interest. Bank B pays 2.5% simple interest but adds a one-time $50 bonus when she opens the account. Which bank gives her more money after 6 years? Show your work for both.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Bank A: 1,200 × 0.03 × 6 = 216; total 1,416; Bank B: 1,200 × 0.025 × 6 = 180, plus 50 = 230; total 1,430; Bank B gives $14 more; Clear work
    Model answer: Bank A: 1,200 × 0.03 × 6 = $216 interest, so $1,416. Bank B: 1,200 × 0.025 × 6 = $180 interest plus a $50 bonus = $230, so $1,430. Bank B gives Maya $14 more after 6 years.
  2. Insert parentheses to make each statement true, and explain how parentheses change the value: (a) 2 + 3² × 4 = 100 (b) 18 − 6 ÷ 3 + 3 = 7
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: (a) (2 + 3)² × 4 = 100; (b) (18 − 6) ÷ 3 + 3 = 7; Explains that parentheses change which operation happens first
    Model answer: (a) (2 + 3)² × 4 = 5² × 4 = 25 × 4 = 100. Without parentheses it would be 2 + 9 × 4 = 38. (b) (18 − 6) ÷ 3 + 3 = 12 ÷ 3 + 3 = 4 + 3 = 7. Without parentheses it would be 18 − 2 + 3 = 19. Parentheses tell you which operation to do first, which changes the answer.

Exit Check

  1. Evaluate 2⁶.
    Answer: 64
    2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 64.
  2. Evaluate: 5 + 3 × 2²
    Answer: 17
    2² = 4; 3 × 4 = 12; 5 + 12 = 17.
  3. How much simple interest does $300 earn in 2 years at 6%?
    Answer: 36 dollars
    300 × 0.06 × 2 = 36.
  4. List the order of operations in your own words.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Grouping symbols, exponents, multiply and divide left to right, add and subtract left to right
    Model answer: First do anything in parentheses or other grouping symbols, then exponents, then multiply and divide from left to right, then add and subtract from left to right.

Answer key: printable page

  1. Evaluate: (a) 3⁴ (b) 10⁵ (c) (1/3)² (d) (−2)⁵
    Answer: (a) 81 (b) 100,000 (c) 1/9 (d) −32
  2. Write with an exponent: (a) 7 × 7 × 7 (b) 0.5 × 0.5 × 0.5 × 0.5
    Answer: (a) 7³ (b) (0.5)⁴
  3. Evaluate: 4 + 18 ÷ 3² × 2
    Answer: 3² = 9; 18 ÷ 9 = 2; 2 × 2 = 4; 4 + 4 = 8.
  4. Evaluate: (15 − 3 × 2)² − 4 × 5
    Answer: 15 − 6 = 9; 9² = 81; 81 − 20 = 61.
  5. Find the simple interest on $650 at 4% for 3 years, and the total.
    Answer: 650 × 0.04 × 3 = $78; total $728.
  6. Explain the difference between −3² and (−3)².
    Answer: −3² = −9 (only 3 is squared); (−3)² = 9 (−3 is the base).
