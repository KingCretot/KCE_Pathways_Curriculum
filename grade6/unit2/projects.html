Unit 2 Projects and Rubrics

Each project is scored with five traits worth up to 4 points each, for a total of 20. Multiply by 5 for a score out of 100. Mastery is 85 (17 of 20). A project below mastery is returned with feedback and revised once. Read the rubric before you start.

ELA: Informational Article

Task: Research a question you care about and write an informational article of 400 to 700 words that teaches a reader something real. Research question and note cards in Week 9; outline, drafting, revision and publishing in Week 10.

Must include: a title; an introduction with a hook and a central idea (thesis); two to four body sections, each with a heading and a T-E-E-C paragraph; facts from at least two reliable sources, in your own words, with in-text citations; varied transitions; one visual with a caption; a conclusion that restates the central idea and explains why it matters; an MLA works cited list in alphabetical order.

Turn in: the final article in the Week 10 lesson (Hands-On), your note cards (photo), and your reflection.

Science: Cell Model Project

Task: Build a three-dimensional model of a plant cell or an animal cell that teaches how a cell works, then give a 2-minute tour. Planned and built in Week 10.

Must include: at least 8 labeled parts (see the Week 10 list for each cell type); a key explaining each material and each part's job; an analogy card ("A cell is like a ___ because...") with at least five matched parts, not a city; one process shown with arrows or movable pieces (photosynthesis, active transport or osmosis); a limits note; a photo of the model; the tour.

Safety: ask a parent before using hot glue, knives or anything that must be heated. Photograph edible models before eating.

Turn in: photos of the model, key and analogy card; the tour script (Week 10 Hands-On); and a short video of the tour (or a parent's three-line note: how long it ran, what the student explained best, one question asked).

Social Studies: Nile Valley Project

Task: Build an illustrated scale timeline and map of the Nile Valley, then give a 2- to 3-minute Historian's Talk answering: Why must the story of the Nile Valley include both Kemet and Kush? Planned and built in Week 10.

Must include: a timeline from about 5000 BCE to 940 CE drawn to a stated scale, with rows for Kemet, Nubia/Kush and Aksum; at least 12 events from Weeks 6 to 10, each with a date, a one-sentence explanation and a drawing; a map with at least eight labeled places; a talk with a claim, three pieces of evidence and one named source; photos and the talk.

Turn in: photos of the timeline and map; the talk script (Week 10 Hands-On); and a recording of the talk (or a parent's three-line note: how long it ran, the strongest evidence, one question asked).
