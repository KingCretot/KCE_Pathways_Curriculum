Unit 2: Fractions and Ratios · Week 7 · Math · Teacher Plan

Dividing Fractions and Mixed Numbers

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Explain what dividing by a fraction means using a model.
  • Divide fractions and mixed numbers using reciprocals.
  • Predict whether a quotient will be greater or less than the dividend.
  • Find the number of servings in a package and the cost per serving.
  • Solve multi-step problems with recipes, servings and grocery costs.

Standards

  • MA.6.NSO.2.2: Extend previous understanding of multiplication and division to compute products and quotients of positive fractions by positive fractions, including mixed numbers, with procedural fluency.
  • MA.6.NSO.2.3: Solve multi-step real-world problems involving any of the four operations with positive multi-digit decimals or positive fractions, including mixed numbers.
  • MA.K12.MTR.2.1: Demonstrate understanding by representing problems in multiple ways.
  • MA.K12.MTR.6.1: Assess the reasonableness of solutions.
  • Financial literacy: reading labels, servings and cost per serving (Jump$tart Spending).

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • Measuring cups (1, 1/2, 1/3, 1/4) and dry rice or water
  • Three pantry packages with labels and prices
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • reciprocal: The number you multiply by to get 1. The reciprocal of 3/4 is 4/3; the reciprocal of 5 is 1/5.
  • dividend: The number being divided.
  • divisor: The number you divide by.
  • quotient: The answer to a division problem.
  • serving size: The amount of food a nutrition label counts as one serving.
  • cost per serving: The price of one serving: total cost ÷ number of servings.

Key questions to ask

  • How many of these fit in that?
  • Will the quotient be bigger or smaller than the dividend? Why?
  • Which number do you flip, and why?
  • Does the label's serving match real life?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Flipping the dividend instead of the divisor.
  • Believing division always makes numbers smaller.
  • Forgetting to change mixed numbers to improper fractions.
  • Rounding a number of meals up when only full meals count.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Use measuring cups to model every division before computing.
  • Provide a keep-change-flip-think card.
  • Start with whole numbers divided by unit fractions.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Explain why dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal using a number line.
  • Plan a week of family dinners on a set budget using cost per serving.

Background for the teacher

Prices and package details are illustrative and realistic for Florida in 2026; Florida does not charge sales tax on most groceries. The rice bag: 2 lb of long grain rice is roughly 5 to 5 1/2 cups dry; the label rounds servings. The "think" step stresses estimation and checking by multiplication.

Sources

  • CPALMS: MA.6.NSO.2.2 and MA.6.NSO.2.3.
  • U.S. Food and Drug Administration: How to Understand and Use the Nutrition Facts Label.

How the lesson runs

During the live lesson, the student shares their screen and answers the Reconnect questions and the Learn checks live while you teach. The Hands-On activity, Think Deeper and Exit Check are completed after class.

Scoring

The interactive lesson scores itself: full points on the first try, half points on the second try, then the answer and explanation appear. Mastery is 85. Written responses arrive in your results email with the score; score them with the rubric below before the student moves on.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. 3/4 × 8/9 = ? Type in simplest form.
    Answer: 2/3
    Simplify first: (1 × 2)/(1 × 3) = 2/3.
  2. 2 1/2 × 1 1/5 = ?
    Answer: 3
    5/2 × 6/5 = 30/10 = 3.
  3. A recipe uses 3/4 cup of milk. How much milk for 2/3 of the recipe? Type a fraction.
    Answer: 1/2 cup
    2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12 = 1/2 cup.
  4. In 3/4, which number is the denominator?
    Answer: 4
    The denominator, 4, tells how many equal parts make the whole.
  5. Explain why 1 1/2 × 1 1/2 is not 1 1/4, and give the correct product.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Cannot multiply whole parts and fraction parts separately; Correct product 2 1/4
    Model answer: You cannot multiply the whole numbers and the fractions separately, because that skips the cross parts. 3/2 × 3/2 = 9/4 = 2 1/4.

Learn 1: What Dividing by a Fraction Means

  1. How many 1/3-cup scoops of flour are in 2 cups?
    Answer: 6 scoops
    Each cup holds three 1/3-cup scoops, so 2 ÷ 1/3 = 6.
  2. A 4-foot board is cut into pieces 2/3 of a foot long. How many pieces?
    Answer: 6 pieces
    4 ÷ 2/3 = 4 × 3/2 = 12/2 = 6.
  3. Without calculating, will 5 ÷ 1/4 be more or less than 5? Explain using what the division means.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: More; Explains how many quarters fit into 5 (many small pieces)
    Model answer: More, because I am asking how many quarters fit into 5, and there are four in each whole, so 20.

Learn 2: Keep, Change, Flip, Then Think

  1. 3/5 ÷ 9/10 = ? Type in simplest form.
    Answer: 2/3
    3/5 × 10/9 = 30/45 = 2/3.
  2. 7/8 ÷ 1/4 = ? Type a mixed number.
    Answer: 3 1/2
    7/8 × 4/1 = 28/8 = 3 1/2.
  3. 4 1/2 ÷ 1 1/8 = ?
    Answer: 4
    9/2 ÷ 9/8 = 9/2 × 8/9 = 72/18 = 4.
  4. Malik solved 2/3 ÷ 4 by flipping the first fraction: 3/2 × 4 = 6. Explain his mistake, find the correct quotient, and use estimation to show why 6 cannot be right.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: He flipped the wrong number; flip the divisor; Correct: 2/3 × 1/4 = 1/6; Estimate: sharing less than 1 among 4 must give less than 1
    Model answer: He flipped the dividend instead of the divisor. The correct way is 2/3 × 1/4 = 2/12 = 1/6. Splitting 2/3 into 4 equal parts must give a small piece, less than 1, so 6 is impossible.

Learn 3: Servings and Cost per Serving

  1. Amara's family cooks 3/4 cup of dry rice at each dinner. How many full dinners will the 5 1/2-cup bag provide?
    Answer: 7 dinners
    11/2 ÷ 3/4 = 11/2 × 4/3 = 44/6 = 7 1/3, so 7 full dinners (with 1/4 cup left over).
  2. What is the cost of the rice for one of those dinners, to the nearest cent? (3/4 cup is 3 servings.)
    Answer: $0.39
    3 servings × $0.13 = $0.39.
  3. A 12-ounce box of oats costs $3.60. One serving is 1 1/2 ounces. What is the cost per serving?
    Answer: $0.45
    12 ÷ 3/2 = 8 servings. $3.60 ÷ 8 = $0.45.
  4. Store A sells a 16-ounce jar of peanut butter (serving size 2 tablespoons, about 14 servings) for $3.22. Store B sells a 40-ounce jar (about 35 servings) for $7.35. Find the cost per serving for each and decide which is the better buy for a family that eats peanut butter every day.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A: $0.23 per serving; B: $0.21 per serving; Chooses B with a real-life reason, or explains a reason to choose A
    Model answer: Store A: $3.22 ÷ 14 = $0.23 per serving. Store B: $7.35 ÷ 35 = $0.21 per serving. Store B is the better buy for a family that eats it every day, because they will finish the big jar before it spoils.

Learn 4: Scaling Recipes With Division

  1. A smoothie recipe uses 2/3 cup of yogurt. How many smoothies can you make with 4 cups of yogurt?
    Answer: 6 smoothies
    4 ÷ 2/3 = 4 × 3/2 = 6.
  2. A family has 3 3/4 pounds of ground turkey. Each meal uses 1 1/4 pounds. How many meals?
    Answer: 3 meals
    15/4 ÷ 5/4 = 15/4 × 4/5 = 3 meals.
  3. A 7 1/2-cup pot of soup is shared equally among 6 people. How much does each person get? Type a mixed number.
    Answer: 1 1/4 cups
    15/2 ÷ 6 = 15/2 × 1/6 = 15/12 = 1 1/4 cups.

Hands-On

  1. Type a table for your three foods: total amount, serving size, servings (your calculation), price, cost per serving, and cost per family meal. Show the division for one of them.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Three foods with all columns; Correct division with fractions; Cost per serving and per meal correct to the cent
    Model answer: Oats: 42 oz, serving 1 1/2 oz, 42 ÷ 3/2 = 28 servings, $4.79, $0.17 per serving; family uses 6 oz per breakfast = 4 servings = $0.68 per meal. Pasta: 16 oz, serving 2 oz, 8 servings, $1.25, $0.16 per serving, family meal 16 oz = $1.25. Rice: 5 1/2 cups, serving 1/4 cup, 22 servings, $2.86, $0.13 per serving, family meal 3/4 cup = $0.39.
  2. Which food gave your family the most meals for the money? Did the label's servings match how much your family really eats? Explain.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Names the best-value food with numbers; Compares label serving to real use
    Model answer: Rice gave us the most meals for the money at $0.39 per family dinner. The label serving is 1/4 cup, but our family uses 3/4 cup, so a bag lasts about 7 dinners, not 22.

Think Deeper

  1. Write a real-world word problem that is solved by 2 1/2 ÷ 1/4, solve it, and explain what the quotient means in your story. Then write a different problem solved by 2 1/2 × 1/4 and explain how the two situations differ.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Division story asks how many 1/4s fit in 2 1/2 (answer 10); Multiplication story asks for 1/4 of 2 1/2 (answer 5/8); Explains the difference between the two situations
    Model answer: Division: I have 2 1/2 cups of trail mix and put 1/4 cup in each snack bag. How many bags? 2 1/2 ÷ 1/4 = 10 bags. Multiplication: I eat 1/4 of a 2 1/2-cup bag of trail mix. How much did I eat? 1/4 × 2 1/2 = 5/8 cup. Division asks how many groups of 1/4 fit; multiplication asks for a part of the amount.
  2. Amara's family has $12.00 for breakfast for 5 days for 4 people. A 42-ounce canister of oats costs $4.79 and a serving is 1 1/2 ounces. A gallon of milk costs $3.89 and holds 16 one-cup servings. Each person eats one serving of oats and 1/2 cup of milk each day. Is $12.00 enough? Show every step.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Oats: 20 servings needed; canister has 28; buy one canister $4.79; Milk: 10 cups needed; one gallon $3.89; Total $8.68, so yes, with $3.32 left
    Model answer: 4 people × 5 days = 20 servings of oats. One canister has 42 ÷ 3/2 = 28 servings, so one canister ($4.79) is enough. Milk: 20 × 1/2 = 10 cups, and one gallon has 16 cups ($3.89). Total: $4.79 + $3.89 = $8.68. Yes, $12.00 is enough, with $3.32 left.

Exit Check

  1. 5/6 ÷ 5/12 = ?
    Answer: 2
    5/6 × 12/5 = 60/30 = 2.
  2. 3 1/3 ÷ 5/6 = ?
    Answer: 4
    10/3 × 6/5 = 60/15 = 4.
  3. 6 ÷ 3/8 = ?
    Answer: 16
    6 × 8/3 = 48/3 = 16.
  4. A 3/4-pound block of cheese is cut into 6 equal pieces. How much does each piece weigh? Type a fraction.
    Answer: 1/8 pound
    3/4 ÷ 6 = 3/4 × 1/6 = 3/24 = 1/8 pound.
  5. A 15-ounce can of beans costs $1.08 and has about 3 1/2 servings. What is the cost per serving, to the nearest cent?
    Answer: $0.31
    1.08 ÷ 3.5 = 0.3085..., about $0.31 per serving.

Answer key: printable page

  1. 4 ÷ 2/5 = ____ 3/4 ÷ 3/8 = ____
    Answer: 10; 2
  2. 5/9 ÷ 10/27 = ____ 2 2/3 ÷ 1 1/3 = ____
    Answer: 1 1/2; 2
  3. Draw a model to show how many 1/2-cup servings are in 2 1/2 cups.
    Answer: Five 1/2-cup servings (2 1/2 ÷ 1/2 = 5).
  4. A 24-ounce bag of frozen vegetables costs $2.76. A serving is 3/4 cup and the bag has about 8 servings. What is the cost per serving?
    Answer: 2.76 ÷ 8 = $0.345, about $0.35
  5. You have 3 3/4 cups of flour. A batch of muffins uses 1 1/4 cups. How many batches can you make?
    Answer: 15/4 ÷ 5/4 = 3 batches
  6. Explain why 8 ÷ 1/2 is greater than 8.
    Answer: It asks how many halves fit in 8; there are 16 halves, so the quotient is greater.
