Unit 2: Explaining the World (Informational) · Week 7 · ELA · Teacher Plan

Text Structures

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Identify five text structures: sequence, cause and effect, compare and contrast, problem and solution, and description.
  • Use signal words and graphic organizers to identify a structure.
  • Explain how a text's structure helps the author's purpose and central idea.
  • Rewrite information in a different structure.
  • Use the roots sequ, dict, fer and ven.

Standards

  • ELA.6.R.2.1: Explain how individual text sections and/or features convey meaning in texts.
  • ELA.6.R.2.2: Analyze the central idea(s), implied or explicit, and its development throughout a text.
  • ELA.6.C.1.4: Write expository texts to explain and/or analyze information from multiple sources, using a logical organizational structure, relevant elaboration, and varied transitions.
  • ELA.6.V.1.2: Apply knowledge of Greek and Latin roots and affixes to determine meanings of words and phrases in grade-level content.
  • ELA.K12.EE.2.1: Read and comprehend grade-level complex texts proficiently.

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • Five sheets of paper, index cards, tape and markers
  • A recipe, manual or news article from home
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • text structure: The way an author organizes information in a text.
  • sequence: Events or steps in time order.
  • cause and effect: A structure that explains why something happened (cause) and what happened because of it (effect).
  • compare and contrast: A structure that shows how two or more things are alike and different.
  • problem and solution: A structure that explains a problem and one or more ways to solve it.
  • description: A structure that explains a topic by listing its features, characteristics or examples.
  • signal words: Words that hint at a structure, such as because, however, first or as a result.
  • sequ / dict: Latin roots meaning follow (sequence, consequence) and say (dictionary, predict).
  • fer / ven: Latin roots meaning carry (transfer, refer) and come (event, convention).

Key questions to ask

  • What words signal the structure?
  • What shape would you draw to organize these ideas?
  • Why did the author choose this structure for this purpose?
  • Does the text mix structures?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Choosing a structure from one signal word without looking at the whole passage.
  • Confusing sequence with cause and effect because both use time words.
  • Thinking a text can have only one structure.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Provide a signal word bank on a card.
  • Give pre-drawn organizers for each structure.
  • Start with very short passages of three sentences.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Find a news article that mixes three structures and label each part.
  • Write the same 50-word set of facts in all five structures.

Background for the teacher

All four passages are original to KCE. Passage A: Diop published Nations nègres et culture in 1954 and founded the IFAN radiocarbon laboratory in Dakar in 1966; the University of Dakar was renamed in 1987, the year after his death. Passage B reflects Florida DEP and water management district information about the Floridan Aquifer, spring flow and nutrient pollution. Passage C connects to Social Studies Weeks 8 and 9 (Kush and Meroe). Passage D reflects the global shift from kerosene to solar lanterns in off-grid communities.

Sources

  • Florida Department of Environmental Protection: Florida's springs and the Floridan Aquifer.
  • UNESCO General History of Africa, Volume II.
  • World Health Organization: household air pollution from kerosene lamps.

How the lesson runs

During the live lesson, the student shares their screen and answers the Reconnect questions and the Learn checks live while you teach. The Hands-On activity, Think Deeper and Exit Check are completed after class.

Scoring

The interactive lesson scores itself: full points on the first try, half points on the second try, then the answer and explanation appear. Mastery is 85. Written responses arrive in your results email with the score; score them with the rubric below before the student moves on.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. What is the difference between a topic and a central idea? Give an example of each about Florida's springs.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Topic is a word or phrase; central idea is a full sentence about it; Accurate examples
    Model answer: A topic is a phrase, like Florida's springs. A central idea is a sentence about it, like: Florida's springs are threatened by overpumping and pollution.
  2. "About 18 million hectares were restored by 2020" is what kind of supporting detail?
    Answer: statistic
    A fact given as a number is a statistic.
  3. Complete with the root meanings.
    Answer: water / earth
    hydr/aqua mean water; geo/terr mean earth or land.
  4. Explain the difference between a supporting detail and a minor detail.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Supporting detail helps prove the central idea; Minor detail is true but does not support it
    Model answer: A supporting detail helps prove or explain the central idea. A minor detail may be true and interesting but does not help prove the central idea.

Learn 1: Sequence and Cause and Effect

  1. Name the structure of each passage.
    Answer: A: sequence; B: cause and effect
    Passage A uses dates and first-next-then-finally; Passage B uses when, as a result and because of this to explain causes and effects.
  2. List three signal words or phrases in Passage A and explain how they help the reader.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Three signals (In 1954, Next, Then, in 1974, Finally, after); Explains they show time order
    Model answer: In 1954, Next and Finally. They tell the reader the order the events happened, so it is easy to follow Diop's life step by step.
  3. Passage B describes two cause-and-effect chains. Write both as Cause → Effect → Effect.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Overpumping → aquifer drops → springs weaker; Fertilizer soaks in → algae grow → native plants crowded out
    Model answer: 1. Too much pumping → the aquifer level drops → springs flow more weakly. 2. Fertilizer soaks into the ground → algae grow quickly → native plants are crowded out.

Learn 2: Compare and Contrast and Problem and Solution

  1. Passage C compares Kemet and Kush. List one similarity and two differences, and name the signal words that showed you.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Similarity: both on the Nile with kings and pyramids; Two differences: pyramid size and slope; iron versus copper and bronze; Signal words: both, However, In contrast, while
    Model answer: Similarity: both grew along the Nile and built pyramids. Differences: Meroe's pyramids were smaller and steeper, and Kush was known for iron while Kemet used copper and bronze. Signal words: both, However, In contrast and while.
  2. What problem and solution does Passage D describe? What extra benefit of the solution does the author add?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Problem: no reliable electricity at night for homework; Solution: solar lamps; Extra benefit: replace smoky kerosene lamps, protecting health
    Model answer: The problem is that many children have no reliable electricity at night to do homework. The solution is small solar lamps charged by the sun. The extra benefit is that they replace kerosene lamps, whose smoke is harmful to breathe.
  3. Which signal word in Passage C shows a contrast is coming? Type one word or phrase.
    Answer: However (also In contrast, while)
    However, in contrast and while signal differences.
  4. Name the structure of Passage E and give two pieces of evidence from the passage that prove it.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Description; Lists features or characteristics (temperature, plants, clarity) with no causes, steps or problem
    Model answer: Description. It lists features of the spring, like its steady 72 °F water and its eelgrass, cypress trees and clear water, without telling steps, causes or a problem.

Learn 3: Why Structure Matters

  1. You are writing an article to convince your town to protect a local spring. Which structure would you choose for the main body, and why?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Problem and solution (or cause and effect); Explains how the structure fits the purpose
    Model answer: Problem and solution, because I want readers to understand the threat to the spring and then see clear steps the town can take, which makes them more likely to act.
  2. Look back at paragraph 3 of the Week 6 article: "Planting trees in a desert, however, turned out to be harder than it sounded..." What structure does that paragraph use, and how do you know?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Problem and solution (trees died; project changed to a patchwork planned with communities); Cites the problem and the change
    Model answer: It uses problem and solution. The problem is that many young trees died because no one was paid to water or protect them. The solution was to change the plan into a patchwork of farms, forests and grasslands planned with local people.

Learn 4: Roots: Follow, Say, Carry, Come

  1. Using the root, what is a consequence ?
    Answer: something that follows from an action (a result)
    sequ means follow: a consequence follows from a cause.
  2. Complete each sentence with a root word.
    Answer: predict / transfer / fertile
    predict (say before), transfer (carry across), fertile (able to bring forth crops).
  3. Using roots, what does it mean to contradict someone?
    Answer: to say the opposite of what they said
    contra (against) + dict (say).

Hands-On

  1. List your ten cards and the structure you placed each under, with signal words circled (capitalize them). Then name the home text you analyzed and its structure.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Ten cards sorted correctly; Signal words identified; A real home text correctly analyzed
    Model answer: Sequence: "NEXT, he spent years building the evidence." "FINALLY, after his death..." Cause and effect: "AS A RESULT, some springs flow more weakly." "BECAUSE OF THIS, algae can grow." Compare: "HOWEVER, the two kingdoms were different." "IN CONTRAST, the pyramids of Meroe were steeper." Problem and solution: "ONE SOLUTION has been small solar lamps." From home: "FIRST, preheat the oven" (sequence), "Contains vitamins SUCH AS iron and calcium" (description). Home text: our pancake mix box uses sequence, because the directions are numbered steps.
  2. Which card did your family member challenge, and who was right? Explain.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Names the challenged card; Explains the reasoning with signal words
    Model answer: My mom challenged "When people pump too much water, the aquifer drops." She thought it was sequence because of when. I explained it is cause and effect, because the pumping causes the drop.

Think Deeper

  1. Take the facts from Passage C (Kemet and Kush) and rewrite them as a short paragraph with a different structure: either sequence or cause and effect. Use at least three signal words for your new structure and underline them by writing them in capital letters.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Uses the chosen new structure throughout; Accurate facts from the passage; Three or more correct signal words; Clear paragraph
    Model answer: Sequence: FIRST, around 2560 BCE, Kemet built the Great Pyramid, a huge pyramid with gently sloping sides. LATER, after about 500 BCE, iron working grew at Meroe in Kush, while Kemet still relied mostly on copper and bronze. THEN, from about 300 BCE, the people of Kush built Meroe's pyramids, smaller and much steeper than Egypt's.
  2. Why do you think the author of Passage B (Florida's springs) chose cause and effect instead of description? What does that choice help readers understand?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Purpose: explain why springs are in trouble; Cause and effect shows human actions lead to harm; Suggests readers can change the causes
    Model answer: The author wanted readers to understand why the springs are in trouble, not just what they look like. Cause and effect shows that human actions like overpumping and using fertilizer lead to weaker springs and algae, which suggests that changing those actions could help protect them.

Exit Check

  1. Read: Unlike the Nile, the Niger River flows in a great arc through West Africa. Both rivers, however, flood each year. Name the structure and quote the two signal words that prove it.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Compare and contrast; Signal words: unlike, both (or however)
    Model answer: Compare and contrast. The signal words unlike and both show a difference and a similarity.
  2. Read: Heavy rain fell for three days, so the river rose over its banks. As a result, the town closed its roads. Name the structure and explain how you know.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Cause and effect; Signal words so and as a result connect causes to effects
    Model answer: Cause and effect, because so and as a result show that the rain caused the river to rise, which caused the roads to close.
  3. Write one sentence about a problem in your community and one sentence offering a solution.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A real problem; A connected solution with a signal word
    Model answer: Our park's trash cans overflow every weekend. One solution is to add recycling bins and ask families to take their trash home.
  4. What does the root sequ mean?
    Answer: follow
    sequ means follow, as in sequence.

Answer key: printable page

  1. Name the five text structures and two signal words for each.
    Answer: Sequence (first, then); cause and effect (because, as a result); compare and contrast (both, however); problem and solution (problem, solution); description (for example, such as).
  2. Read: "Because the Nile flooded every year, the soil stayed rich. As a result, farmers grew extra grain." What is the structure? How do you know?
    Answer: Cause and effect; signal words because and as a result.
  3. Write two sentences comparing Florida's springs and the Nile flood as water sources, using two compare-and-contrast signal words.
    Answer: For example: Both Florida's springs and the Nile flood supply fresh water. However, the springs flow from underground all year, while the Nile flood came once a year.
  4. Write the steps Cheikh Anta Diop took to build and share his evidence, using four sequence signal words.
    Answer: For example: First, in 1954, he published his book. Next, he built a radiocarbon laboratory in Dakar. Then, in 1974, he presented his evidence at UNESCO. Finally, the University of Dakar was renamed for him.
  5. Write a word with each root and its meaning: sequ ____ dict ____ fer ____ ven ____
    Answer: sequence (follow); predict (say); transfer (carry); event (come)
  6. Which structure would you use to explain how to plant a garden? Why?
    Answer: Sequence, because planting happens in steps in a certain order.
