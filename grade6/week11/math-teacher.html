Unit 3: Rates and Percents · Week 11 · Math · Teacher Plan

Unit Rates

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Write a rate for two quantities with different units.
  • Find and interpret unit rates, including rates with fractions.
  • Use both unit rates of a ratio (miles per hour and hours per mile) to solve problems.
  • Use double number lines and unit rates to predict and compare.
  • Spot shrinkflation and compare cost per use at the store.

Standards

  • MA.6.AR.3.2: Given a real-world context, determine a rate for a ratio of quantities with different units. Calculate and interpret the corresponding unit rate.
  • MA.6.AR.3.5: Solve mathematical and real-world problems involving ratios, rates and unit rates, including comparisons, mixtures, ratios of lengths and conversions within the same measurement system.
  • MA.6.NSO.2.2: Extend previous understanding of multiplication and division to compute products and quotients of positive fractions by positive fractions, including mixed numbers, with procedural fluency.
  • MA.K12.MTR.7.1: Apply mathematics to real-world contexts.
  • Financial literacy: unit pricing, shrinkflation and cost per use (Jump$tart Spending).

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • A timer, a tape measure, a book
  • One package your family buys often, plus an older receipt, photo or website listing showing its earlier size (if available)
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • rate: A ratio that compares two quantities with different units, such as 150 miles in 3 hours.
  • unit rate: A rate for one unit of the second quantity, such as 50 miles per hour.
  • per: For each; the word that signals a rate.
  • double number line: Two number lines lined up so that equivalent amounts sit across from each other.
  • pace: Time per unit of distance, such as minutes per mile.
  • shrinkflation: When a package gets smaller but the price stays the same or goes up, raising the unit price.

Lesson flow

6 minReconnect
Run the Reconnect questions.
5 minHook
Kipchoge's marathon. Have the student guess his minutes per mile before calculating.
12 minRates and unit rates
Build the double number line for 150 miles in 3 hours. Practice heartbeats and pages.
10 minTwo unit rates
Find both unit rates for the marathon and the fraction hiker. Choose the useful one.
10 minComparing
Compare buses and typists. Discuss why a bigger pace means slower.
10 minMoney
Work the shrinkflation and cost-per-wear examples. Check whether a package your family buys has shrunk.
7 minAssign
Start the heart rate measurement together; assign the rest of the activity, Think Deeper and the Exit Check.

Key questions to ask

  • How much for one?
  • Which unit rate answers this question?
  • Does a bigger number mean better here?
  • Did the package change size?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Dividing in the wrong order (hours ÷ miles when miles per hour is asked).
  • Thinking a bigger pace number means faster.
  • Assuming a rate stays constant forever (sprinting versus marathons).
  • Trusting the price tag without checking the unit price.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Use a double number line for every problem.
  • Write "___ per 1 ___" as a frame.
  • Provide a multiplication chart.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Find two real products that have shrunk in your house and compute how much more you pay per unit.
  • Convert Kipchoge's pace to miles per hour.

Background for the teacher

Kipchoge's 2:01:09 at the 2022 Berlin Marathon (September 25, 2022) was the world record until October 2023. 121.15 minutes ÷ 26.2 miles ≈ 4.62 minutes per mile (about 4:37). Usain Bolt's 100 m world record is 9.58 seconds (Berlin, 2009). Sarah the cheetah's 5.95-second 100 m run (Cincinnati Zoo, June 2012) was reported by National Geographic. Shrinkflation examples are invented with realistic numbers.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. Complete the ratio table: 4 bottles cost $6.
    Answer: 1; 3
    Halve 4 : 6 to get 2 : 3; halve again to get 1 : 1.50. Today, the column with 1 has a special name.
  2. Find the missing number: 3 : 5 = 12 : ___
    Answer: 20
    3 × 4 = 12, so 5 × 4 = 20.
  3. 3/4 ÷ 1/2 = ? Type a mixed number.
    Answer: 1 1/2
    3/4 × 2/1 = 6/4 = 1 1/2. You will divide fractions to find unit rates today.
  4. In the Unit 2 Field Day budget, how did you decide whether the prize money was enough?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Found the prize budget (1/6 of $240 = $40) and compared it with the cost (24 × $3 = $72)
    Model answer: I found the prize budget, 1/6 of $240, which is $40, and compared it with 24 medals at $3 each, which is $72. It was not enough.

Learn 1: Rates and Unit Rates

  1. A family drives 186 miles in 3 hours. What is their average speed in miles per hour?
    Answer: 62 miles per hour
    186 ÷ 3 = 62 miles per hour.
  2. A heart beats 18 times in 15 seconds. How many beats is that per minute?
    Answer: 72 beats per minute
    15 seconds is 1/4 of a minute, so multiply by 4: 18 × 4 = 72 beats per minute.
  3. A reader finishes 45 pages in 1 1/2 hours. How many pages per hour is that?
    Answer: 30 pages per hour
    45 ÷ 1 1/2 = 45 ÷ 3/2 = 45 × 2/3 = 30 pages per hour.
  4. A sprinkler uses 120 gallons in 40 minutes. Find the unit rate and explain what it means in a complete sentence.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: 3 gallons per minute; A sentence explaining that each minute the sprinkler uses 3 gallons
    Model answer: 120 ÷ 40 = 3 gallons per minute. That means every minute the sprinkler runs, it uses 3 gallons of water.

Learn 2: Two Unit Rates for Every Rate

  1. A turtle crawls 2/3 of a meter in 1/6 of a minute. What is its speed in meters per minute?
    Answer: 4 meters per minute
    2/3 ÷ 1/6 = 2/3 × 6 = 4 meters per minute.
  2. A cyclist rides 12 miles in 48 minutes. What is her pace in minutes per mile?
    Answer: 4 minutes per mile
    48 ÷ 12 = 4 minutes per mile.
  3. At that pace, how many minutes will she take to ride 7 1/2 miles?
    Answer: 30 minutes
    7 1/2 × 4 = 30 minutes.
  4. Painting a fence: 3 gallons of paint cover 900 square feet. Write both unit rates, and explain which one you would use to find how much paint you need for a 1,500-square-foot fence.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: 300 square feet per gallon; 1/300 gallon per square foot; Use square feet per gallon: 1,500 ÷ 300 = 5 gallons (or gallons per square foot × 1,500)
    Model answer: The rates are 300 square feet per gallon and 1/300 gallon per square foot. I would use 300 square feet per gallon: 1,500 ÷ 300 = 5 gallons of paint.

Learn 3: Comparing with Unit Rates

  1. Ama types 270 words in 6 minutes. Kofi types 320 words in 8 minutes. Who types faster? Type the name.
    Answer: Ama (45 words per minute versus 40)
    Ama: 270 ÷ 6 = 45 words per minute. Kofi: 320 ÷ 8 = 40 words per minute.
  2. Runner A's pace is 9 minutes per mile. Runner B's pace is 11 minutes per mile. Which runner is faster, and why might a student get this wrong?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Runner A; A bigger pace number means more minutes per mile, which is slower; students may think bigger means faster
    Model answer: Runner A is faster, because she needs only 9 minutes for each mile. A student might pick B because 11 is bigger, but a bigger pace means more time per mile, which is slower.
  3. A farm's well pump fills 15 gallons in 2 1/2 minutes. A garden hose fills 12 gallons in 3 minutes. How many more gallons per minute does the pump fill?
    Answer: 2 gallons per minute
    Pump: 15 ÷ 2 1/2 = 15 ÷ 5/2 = 6 gallons per minute. Hose: 12 ÷ 3 = 4. 6 − 4 = 2.

Learn 4: Money: Price per Unit at the Store

  1. A bag of rice shrinks from 30 ounces to 24 ounces, and the price stays $3.60. By how many cents per ounce did the unit price go up?
    Answer: 3 cents
    $3.60 ÷ 30 = $0.12 per ounce; $3.60 ÷ 24 = $0.15 per ounce. The unit price went up 3 cents per ounce.
  2. A pack of 60 vitamins costs $9.00 and you take 2 a day. What is the cost per day?
    Answer: $0.30
    60 vitamins ÷ 2 per day = 30 days. $9.00 ÷ 30 = $0.30 per day.
  3. Why do you think companies shrink packages instead of raising prices? What can a shopper do about it?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Shoppers notice price increases more than smaller packages; Check the unit price on the shelf tag, compare sizes, consider other brands
    Model answer: Shoppers notice a higher price right away, but they may not notice a box that is a little smaller. A smart shopper checks the unit price on the shelf tag every time and compares it with other sizes and brands.

Hands-On

  1. Record your measurements and show the work for each unit rate (beats per minute at rest and after exercise, meters per second walking, pages per hour reading).
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Measurements recorded; Correct calculations for each unit rate with units; Work shown (e.g. 19 beats in 15 s × 4 = 76 bpm)
    Model answer: Resting: 19 beats in 15 seconds × 4 = 76 beats per minute. After exercise: 31 × 4 = 124 beats per minute. Walking: 20 m in 16 seconds = 1.25 meters per second. Reading: 6 pages in 5 minutes = 1.2 pages per minute, × 60 = 72 pages per hour.
  2. Use your walking rate to predict how long it would take you to walk 1 kilometer (1,000 meters). Then use your reading rate to predict how long a 180-page book would take. Are your predictions reasonable? Why might they be off?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Correct predictions from their unit rates; Reasonableness check; Sensible reasons predictions could be off (getting tired, rest breaks, harder pages, measurement error)
    Model answer: At 1.25 meters per second, 1,000 m takes 800 seconds, about 13 minutes. At 72 pages per hour, a 180-page book takes 2.5 hours. These seem reasonable, but I might slow down when tired, and some chapters are harder to read, so real times could be longer.

Think Deeper

  1. A family drives from Arcadia to Tallahassee, about 300 miles. Their car goes 30 miles per gallon, and gas costs $3.20 per gallon. If they average 60 miles per hour, how long will the drive take, and how much will the gas cost? Show each unit rate you use.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Time: 300 ÷ 60 = 5 hours; Gas used: 300 ÷ 30 = 10 gallons; Cost: 10 × $3.20 = $32.00; Unit rates named and used correctly
    Model answer: Time: at 60 miles per hour, 300 ÷ 60 = 5 hours. Gas: at 30 miles per gallon, 300 ÷ 30 = 10 gallons. Cost: at $3.20 per gallon, 10 × $3.20 = $32.00. So the trip takes about 5 hours and costs about $32 in gas.
  2. In 2012, Sarah, a cheetah at the Cincinnati Zoo, ran 100 meters in 5.95 seconds. Usain Bolt's world record is 100 meters in 9.58 seconds. Find each speed in meters per second (round to the nearest tenth), then explain why a cheetah would not win a marathon. (Cheetahs can keep up top speed for only a short burst, usually less than a minute.)
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Cheetah: about 16.8 meters per second; Bolt: about 10.4 meters per second; Cheetahs can sprint only for short distances before tiring; a unit rate assumes a constant speed, which is not true over long distances
    Model answer: Cheetah: 100 ÷ 5.95 ≈ 16.8 meters per second. Bolt: 100 ÷ 9.58 ≈ 10.4 meters per second. But a cheetah can keep up its top speed for only a short burst, usually less than a minute, before it must stop and rest. A unit rate assumes the speed stays the same, which is not true over a long race.

Exit Check

  1. A machine fills 240 bottles in 8 minutes. How many bottles per minute?
    Answer: 30 bottles per minute
    240 ÷ 8 = 30.
  2. A hiker walks 1 1/2 miles in 1/2 hour. What is her speed in miles per hour?
    Answer: 3 miles per hour
    1 1/2 ÷ 1/2 = 3/2 × 2 = 3.
  3. Runner A runs 3 miles in 27 minutes. Runner B runs 4 miles in 34 minutes. What is the faster runner's pace, in minutes per mile?
    Answer: 8.5 minutes per mile
    A: 27 ÷ 3 = 9. B: 34 ÷ 4 = 8.5. Fewer minutes per mile is faster, so B's 8.5 is faster.
  4. A chip bag shrinks from 10 ounces to 8 ounces but still costs $4.00. Find both unit prices and explain what happened.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: $0.40 per ounce before; $0.50 per ounce after; The price per ounce went up even though the price tag stayed the same (shrinkflation)
    Model answer: Before: $4.00 ÷ 10 = $0.40 per ounce. After: $4.00 ÷ 8 = $0.50 per ounce. The unit price went up by $0.10 an ounce even though the price tag did not change.

Answer key: printable page

  1. A car travels 228 miles in 4 hours. What is its speed?
    Answer: 57 miles per hour
  2. A printer prints 56 pages in 1 3/4 minutes. How many pages per minute?
    Answer: 56 ÷ 7/4 = 56 × 4/7 = 32 pages per minute
  3. A swimmer swims 1,200 meters in 30 minutes. Write both unit rates.
    Answer: 40 meters per minute; 1/40 minute per meter
  4. Which is faster: 200 words in 5 minutes or 252 words in 6 minutes?
    Answer: 40 vs 42 words per minute; 252 in 6 is faster
  5. Cereal shrinks from 20 oz to 16 oz at $5.00. Find both unit prices.
    Answer: $0.25 per oz → $0.3125 (about $0.31) per oz
  6. A $45 museum membership for your co-op family is used 9 times. Single tickets cost $7 each. What is the membership's cost per visit, and which is the better deal for 9 visits?
    Answer: $45 ÷ 9 = $5 per visit versus $7; the membership.
