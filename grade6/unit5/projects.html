Grade 6 · Unit 5 · Projects due in Week 25

Unit 5 Projects and Rubrics

Each project is scored with five traits worth up to 4 points each, for a total of 20. Multiply by 5 for a score out of 100. Mastery is 85 (17 of 20). A project below mastery is returned with feedback and revised once. Read the rubric before you start.

ELA: Literary Response Project

Task: (1) Write a literary analysis essay of 500 to 800 words about The Call of the Wild, and (2) create a creative response that shows the same idea in another form. Plan in Week 24 if you like; draft, revise and finish in Week 25.

Choose an essay question (or propose your own to a parent): How does London use a symbol (the club, the fire, the trail or the call) to develop a theme? Is Buck's ending a triumph, a loss or both? How do foils reveal what London believes about survival? How does the Klondike setting drive Buck's change? How should a reader today respond to London's portrayal of the Yeehats?

Essay must include: a title; an introduction with a hook, brief context (title, author, 1903, the Klondike) and a clear thesis; at least three body paragraphs, each with a claim, at least one exact quotation with its chapter, and commentary longer than the quotation; evidence from at least three different chapters; a conclusion that explains why the idea matters; correct quotation punctuation.

Creative response (choose one): a one-page illustrated map of Buck's journey with a quotation at each stop; a poem of at least 16 lines in Buck's voice; a scene of 400 to 600 words from Thornton's or another character's point of view (not a Yeehat point of view unless it follows the Week 24 guidelines: only what the novel says and ordinary human feelings, no invented customs, clothing or speech); or a 2-minute recorded reading of a chosen passage with an introduction explaining why you chose it.

Turn in: the essay, the creative response (or a photo or recording of it), and the record in the Week 25 lesson.

Science: Ecosystem Study Project

Task: Study a real ecosystem near you (a park, pond, beach, spring, wooded lot or backyard) on at least three visits, then present a field guide as a poster or a slideshow of 6 to 10 slides.

Must include: the site's name and a simple map; a data table from three visits (date, time, weather, temperature and counts from a 1-meter quadrat or a 15-minute observation); at least five biotic and three abiotic factors; a food web of at least eight organisms with arrows showing energy flow; one matter cycle (water or carbon) drawn for your site; at least three relationships (predation, competition, mutualism, commensalism or parasitism) you observed or researched for your site; one human impact on the site and a realistic solution; a short list of sources, including any field guide or app used.

Safety: visit only with an adult; keep well back from water where alligators may live; do not touch or feed wildlife; follow park rules about collecting.

Turn in: the poster or slideshow (or photos of it), your data table, and the record in the Week 25 lesson.

Social Studies: Trade Route Project

Task: Design and build a board game about one of Unit 5's trade networks (trans-Saharan or Indian Ocean), with a one-page rule sheet and a "history behind the game" guide, then playtest it with your family.

Must include: a game board with an accurate map showing at least five real places (cities, deserts, rivers, ports); at least ten event cards based on real history (such as sandstorms, monsoon seasons, Mansa Musa's gold, the Portuguese attack on Kilwa); trade goods that match the real network; one rule that depends on geography (oases, the Niger's flood, monsoon direction); a history guide of 300 to 500 words explaining what the game teaches and citing at least one primary source from Unit 5 (al-Bakri, Ibn Battuta or Leo Africanus); a fair, respectful portrayal of African peoples and cultures, with no stereotypes; a list of at least three sources.

Turn in: photos of the game, the rule sheet, the history guide, and the playtest record in the Week 25 lesson.
