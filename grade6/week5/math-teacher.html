Unit 1: Number Power · Week 5 · Math · Teacher Plan

GCF, LCM and Unit 1 Checkpoint

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Find the prime factorization of a whole number with a factor tree.
  • Find the greatest common factor (GCF) and least common multiple (LCM) of two numbers.
  • Decide whether a real-world problem calls for the GCF or the LCM.
  • Plan a savings goal: find how much is left and how many weeks it will take.
  • Show mastery of Unit 1 on the checkpoint (85 or higher).

Standards

  • MA.6.NSO.3.1: Given a mathematical or real-world context, find the greatest common factor and least common multiple of two whole numbers.
  • MA.6.NSO.3.2: Rewrite the sum of two composite whole numbers having a common factor, as a common factor multiplied by the sum of two whole numbers.
  • MA.6.NSO.3.3: Evaluate positive rational numbers and integers with natural number exponents (prime factorization with exponents, preview).
  • MA.6.NSO.2.1: Multiply and divide positive multi-digit numbers with decimals to the thousandths, including using a standard algorithm with procedural fluency (checkpoint).
  • Financial literacy: setting and planning savings goals (Jump$tart Spending and Saving).

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • Counters, beans or coins (36 of one kind and 48 of another) for the bag-packing model
  • Paper for factor trees
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • factor: A whole number that divides another number with no remainder. 4 is a factor of 36.
  • multiple: The product of a number and a whole number. 36, 72 and 108 are multiples of 36.
  • prime number: A whole number greater than 1 with exactly two factors, 1 and itself, such as 2, 3, 5, 7 and 11.
  • composite number: A whole number greater than 1 with more than two factors, such as 4, 9 and 36.
  • prime factorization: A number written as a product of prime numbers: 36 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3.
  • greatest common factor (GCF): The largest factor two or more numbers share.
  • least common multiple (LCM): The smallest multiple two or more numbers share (other than 0).
  • savings goal: A specific amount of money you plan to save by a certain time, for a certain purpose.

Lesson flow

6 minReconnect
Run the Week 4 Reconnect questions.
4 minHook
Have the student physically sort 36 and 48 counters into identical groups. Ask for the greatest number of groups.
10 minPrime factorization
Build factor trees for 36 and 48 two different ways to show the primes are the same.
10 minGCF
Find the GCF by listing factors, then with the Venn diagram. Connect to the counters.
10 minLCM
Hot dogs and buns. Practice deciding GCF or LCM with the ribbon and lighthouse problems.
10 minSavings goal
Work Amara's mixer plan. Stress rounding weeks up. Start the student's own savings plan.
10 minCheckpoint preview
Explain that the checkpoint is completed after class, independently, without a calculator. Mastery is 85; below 85 means a review lesson and a retake.

Key questions to ask

  • Are we splitting into groups or waiting for things to line up?
  • How do you know a number is prime?
  • Why do we round the number of weeks up?
  • What would change your savings plan?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Confusing GCF and LCM, or always multiplying the two numbers to get the LCM.
  • Thinking 1 is prime, or that odd numbers like 51 and 91 are prime.
  • Rounding a partial week down in a savings plan.
  • Stopping a factor tree before every branch is prime.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Provide a hundreds chart with primes shaded.
  • Use counters for GCF problems before moving to numbers.
  • Let the student list multiples in two rows to find the LCM.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Find the GCF and LCM of three numbers: 12, 18 and 30 (GCF 6, LCM 180).
  • Plan a family savings goal that includes sales tax and a deadline.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. 4.56 ÷ 0.12 = ?
    Answer: 38
    Multiply both by 100: 456 ÷ 12 = 38.
  2. 8.75 ÷ 5 = ?
    Answer: 1.75
    Long division with the decimal point straight up: 1.75.
  3. In 8.75 ÷ 5 = 1.75, which number is the divisor?
    Answer: 5
    The divisor is the number you divide by.
  4. A 24-ounce box of cereal costs $5.04. What is the unit price per ounce?
    Answer: $0.21 per ounce
    5.04 ÷ 24 = $0.21 per ounce.
  5. A 15-ounce box of the same cereal costs $3.45. How much cheaper per ounce is the 24-ounce box, in dollars?
    Answer: $0.02 per ounce
    3.45 ÷ 15 = $0.23 per ounce. 0.23 − 0.21 = $0.02 cheaper.
  6. Give one reason a family might buy the 15-ounce box anyway, even though it costs more per ounce.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A real-life reason: spoilage, storage, budget today, trying a new kind
    Model answer: If they have only $4 left in this week's budget, they cannot afford the big box today, even though it is the better unit price.

Learn 1: Prime Factorization

  1. Is 51 prime or composite? Type the word, then give a factor pair to prove it.
    Answer: composite (3 × 17 = 51)
    5 + 1 = 6, which is divisible by 3, so 51 = 3 × 17. Many people guess 51 is prime.
  2. Complete the prime factorization of 60.
    Answer: 60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5
    60 = 6 × 10 = (2 × 3) × (2 × 5) = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5.
  3. What number has the prime factorization 2 × 3 × 3 × 7?
    Answer: 126
    2 × 3 = 6, × 3 = 18, × 7 = 126.
  4. Jalen made a factor tree for 48 starting with 6 × 8. Priya started with 4 × 12. Will they end with the same primes? Explain why.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Yes; Every number has only one prime factorization, no matter how you start
    Model answer: Yes. Both trees end with 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3, because every whole number has exactly one set of prime factors. Only the order of splitting changes.

Learn 2: Greatest Common Factor (GCF)

  1. What is the GCF of 24 and 40?
    Answer: 8
    24 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 and 40 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 5. Shared: 2 × 2 × 2 = 8.
  2. A homeschool co-op has 54 pencils and 72 erasers. They want identical supply kits with nothing left over. What is the greatest number of kits?
    Answer: 18 kits
    GCF of 54 and 72 is 18.
  3. How many pencils and erasers go in each of those kits?
    Answer: 3 / 4
    54 ÷ 18 = 3 pencils; 72 ÷ 18 = 4 erasers.
  4. Use the distributive property and the GCF to rewrite 36 + 48 as 12 × (3 + ___). What goes in the blank?
    Answer: 4
    36 + 48 = 12 × 3 + 12 × 4 = 12 × (3 + 4) = 84.

Learn 3: Least Common Multiple (LCM)

  1. What is the LCM of 6 and 9?
    Answer: 18
    Multiples of 9: 9, 18. 18 is also a multiple of 6.
  2. Kwame waters the garden-center ferns every 4 days and the orchids every 6 days. He watered both today. In how many days will he water both on the same day again?
    Answer: 12 days
    LCM of 4 and 6 is 12.
  3. One bus leaves the station every 15 minutes and another every 20 minutes. Both leave at 8:00 AM. How many minutes until they leave together again?
    Answer: 60 minutes
    LCM of 15 and 20 is 60, so 9:00 AM.
  4. For each situation, decide GCF or LCM and explain how you knew: (a) cutting a 24-inch and a 36-inch ribbon into equal pieces as long as possible; (b) two lighthouses flash every 8 and 12 seconds, and you want to know when they flash together.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: (a) GCF, because it splits into the largest equal pieces (12 inches); (b) LCM, because it finds when events line up (24 seconds)
    Model answer: (a) GCF, because I am splitting both ribbons into the longest equal pieces; the GCF of 24 and 36 is 12 inches. (b) LCM, because I want the first time both flashes happen together; the LCM of 8 and 12 is 24 seconds.

Learn 4: Planning a Savings Goal

  1. How much more money does Amara need?
    Answer: $147.50
    189.50 − 42.00 = $147.50.
  2. After 7 weeks of saving $18.50, how much will Amara have in total?
    Answer: $171.50
    42.00 + 7 × 18.50 = 42.00 + 129.50 = $171.50, still short of $189.50.
  3. Amara wants the mixer in 5 weeks instead. How much must she save each week, to the nearest cent?
    Answer: $29.50
    147.50 ÷ 5 = $29.50 a week.
  4. Kwame earns $15.75 an hour and works 10 hours a week. He wants a $350.00 used bike in 6 weeks and has $65.00 saved. Can he do it if he saves 0.3 of each paycheck (30 cents of every dollar)? Show your work and explain.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Weekly pay $157.50; Saving 0.3 of pay is $47.25 a week; 6 weeks saves $283.50; with $65 = $348.50, $1.50 short; Clear conclusion with a suggestion
    Model answer: His weekly pay is 15.75 × 10 = $157.50. Saving 0.3 of it is 0.3 × 157.50 = $47.25 a week. In 6 weeks that is $283.50, plus his $65.00 makes $348.50. He would be $1.50 short, so he should save a little more one week or wait one more week.

Hands-On

  1. Write your goal, price, current savings, weekly amount and the number of weeks needed. Show all calculations.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: States item, price and date; Correct amount left; Correct weeks, rounded up; Shows calculations
    Model answer: Goal: a $64.99 telescope plus 7% tax = $69.54, by March 1. I have $15.00, so I need $54.54 more. I can save $8.00 a week from chores. 54.54 ÷ 8 = 6.8, so I need 7 weeks.
  2. What could make your plan go faster or slower? Name one risk and one way to stay on track.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A realistic risk; A realistic strategy
    Model answer: If I spend some of my chore money on snacks, the plan will take longer. To stay on track, I will put my savings in the envelope the same day I get paid.

Think Deeper

  1. For 12 and 18, find the GCF and the LCM. Then multiply GCF × LCM and compare it with 12 × 18. Try one more pair of your own. What do you notice, and why might it be true?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: GCF 6 and LCM 36; Notices GCF × LCM = product of the numbers (216); Tests another pair correctly; Gives a reason using shared primes
    Model answer: GCF(12, 18) = 6 and LCM = 36. 6 × 36 = 216 and 12 × 18 = 216, the same. With 8 and 10: GCF 2, LCM 40, 2 × 40 = 80 = 8 × 10. It works because the GCF holds the shared primes once and the LCM holds every prime, so together they use every prime from both numbers exactly as many times as the two numbers do.

Checkpoint: Part A: Whole numbers and long division

  1. 6,384 ÷ 24 = ?
    Answer: 266
    24 × 266 = 6,384.
  2. A school orders 1,250 books packed 32 to a box. How many boxes are needed?
    Answer: 40 boxes
    1,250 ÷ 32 = 39 R2. The 2 extra books need one more box: 40.

Checkpoint: Part B: Adding, subtracting and multiplying decimals

  1. 14.3 − 6.78 = ?
    Answer: 7.52
    14.30 − 6.78 = 7.52.
  2. A receipt shows $3.49, $12.05 and $0.89. What is the total?
    Answer: $16.43
    3.49 + 12.05 + 0.89 = $16.43.
  3. 0.06 × 4.5 = ?
    Answer: 0.27
    6 × 45 = 270, with 3 decimal places: 0.270 = 0.27.
  4. Gas costs $3.289 per gallon. What do 11.5 gallons cost, to the nearest cent?
    Answer: $37.82
    3.289 × 11.5 = 37.8235, which rounds to $37.82.

Checkpoint: Part C: Dividing decimals and unit prices

  1. 9.36 ÷ 0.4 = ?
    Answer: 23.4
    Multiply both by 10: 93.6 ÷ 4 = 23.4.
  2. A 3-pound bag of apples costs $4.47 and a 5-pound bag costs $6.95. What is the unit price of the better buy, per pound?
    Answer: $1.39 per pound
    4.47 ÷ 3 = $1.49 and 6.95 ÷ 5 = $1.39. The 5-pound bag is the better buy at $1.39 per pound.

Checkpoint: Part D: Factors, multiples and money

  1. What is the GCF of 42 and 70?
    Answer: 14
    42 = 2 × 3 × 7, 70 = 2 × 5 × 7. Shared: 2 × 7 = 14.
  2. Juice boxes come in packs of 6 and snack bars in packs of 9. What is the fewest juice boxes you can buy to have the same number of each?
    Answer: 18 juice boxes
    LCM of 6 and 9 is 18.
  3. Maya earns $16.25 an hour and works 8 hours. She saves 0.25 of her pay (25 cents of every dollar). How much does she save?
    Answer: $32.50
    16.25 × 8 = $130.00. 0.25 × 130 = $32.50.
  4. Andre says 4.8 ÷ 0.06 = 8. Find his mistake, give the correct answer, and show how to check it.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Correct answer 80; Explains he did not move both decimal points the same number of places; Checks by multiplying 0.06 × 80 = 4.8
    Model answer: He moved the decimal point two places in 0.06 but only one place in 4.8, so he divided 48 by 6. Both must move two places: 480 ÷ 6 = 80. Check: 0.06 × 80 = 4.80.

Answer key: printable page

  1. Make a factor tree and write the prime factorization of 84.
    Answer: 84 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 7
  2. Find the GCF and LCM of 18 and 24.
    Answer: GCF 6; LCM 72
  3. A teacher has 30 red beads and 45 blue beads. What is the greatest number of identical bracelets she can make with no beads left over? How many of each color go on each bracelet?
    Answer: 15 bracelets; 2 red and 3 blue on each
  4. One light blinks every 6 seconds and another every 10 seconds. They blink together now. When will they blink together next?
    Answer: In 30 seconds (LCM of 6 and 10)
  5. Rewrite 24 + 40 using the GCF and the distributive property.
    Answer: 8 × (3 + 5)
  6. You want a $95.00 tablet case. You have $20.00 and save $12.50 a week. How many weeks until you can buy it? Explain your rounding.
    Answer: 75 ÷ 12.50 = 6 weeks exactly (if it were not exact, round up).
