Unit 1: Number Power · Week 2 · Math · Teacher Plan

Adding and Subtracting Decimals

Lesson length: 60 minutes live on Zoom, plus 35 to 45 minutes for the after-class interactive lesson and store activity

Objectives

  • Read, compare and order decimals to the thousandths using place value.
  • Add and subtract decimals by lining up place values and using zeros as placeholders.
  • Estimate decimal sums and differences to check reasonableness.
  • Check a receipt for accuracy and make correct change using the counting-up method.

Standards

  • MA.6.NSO.2.1: Multiply and divide positive multi-digit numbers with decimals to the thousandths (addition and subtraction fluency this week supports it).
  • Review of MA.5.NSO.2.3 and MA.5.NSO.1.4: add and subtract multi-digit numbers with decimals to the thousandths; compare decimals.
  • MA.K12.MTR.6.1: Assess the reasonableness of solutions.
  • MA.K12.MTR.7.1: Apply mathematics to real-world contexts.
  • Financial literacy: reading receipts, sales tax, making change (Jump$tart Spending and Saving).

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • Coins and bills or play money
  • A real receipt
  • Grid paper for lining up digits
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • decimal point: The dot that separates the whole-number part of a number from the part less than one.
  • tenths: The first place to the right of the decimal point. 0.3 is three tenths.
  • hundredths: The second place to the right of the decimal point. 0.08 is eight hundredths. A cent is one hundredth of a dollar.
  • thousandths: The third place to the right of the decimal point. 0.005 is five thousandths.
  • placeholder zero: A zero written to keep digits in the correct place, such as writing 8.6 as 8.60 when adding to 12.75.
  • subtotal: The total of the items before tax is added.
  • sales tax: Money added to a purchase that goes to the government.
  • change: The money returned when you pay more than the total.

Lesson flow

6 minReconnect
Run the Week 1 Reconnect questions. If long division is still shaky, schedule five minutes of extra practice; it returns in Week 4 with decimals.
5 minHook
Show a real receipt on camera. Ask: how would you know if this is wrong?
10 minPlace value
Build 47.385 on the place-value chart. Use money: tenths are dimes, hundredths are pennies. Compare 0.6 and 0.58 with dimes and pennies.
12 minAdd and subtract
Model lining up decimal points on grid paper. Have the student explain why placeholder zeros do not change a number's value. Work two of each type with estimates first.
15 minReceipts and change
Complete the receipt together. Role-play the counting-up method live: you are the customer and the student is the cashier.
8 minError analysis
Present Devon's mistake. Have the student find and explain it before seeing the answer.
4 minAssign
Assign the KCE Store activity, Think Deeper and the Exit Check.

Key questions to ask

  • Why do we line up decimal points instead of the last digits?
  • Does writing 8.6 as 8.60 change its value? How do you know?
  • What is your estimate, and is your answer close?
  • How does counting up help a cashier avoid mistakes?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Thinking longer decimals are larger (0.45 > 0.5).
  • Right-aligning digits instead of decimal points.
  • Forgetting to regroup across a zero in 20.00 − 7.46.
  • Dropping the decimal point in the answer.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Use grid paper so each digit sits in one square.
  • Model with real coins: $0.50 versus $0.45.
  • Have the student write every number to the same number of decimal places before calculating.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Ask the student to find a receipt at home with tax and work out what percent the tax was (preview of Unit 3).
  • Have the student create a receipt with an error that is hard to spot and trade with you.

Background for the teacher

Florida's state sales tax is 6 percent, and counties may add a surtax; Polk County (Lakeland) adds 1 percent for a total of 7 percent. Most grocery food is tax-exempt in Florida, so the receipt shows taxable baking supplies. The $1.38 tax is exactly 7 percent of $19.74, which makes the challenge question come out evenly.

How the lesson runs

During the live lesson, the student shares their screen and answers the Reconnect questions and the Learn checks live while you teach. The Hands-On activity, Think Deeper and Exit Check are completed after class.

Scoring

The interactive lesson scores itself: full points on the first try, half points on the second try, then the answer and explanation appear. Mastery is 85. Written responses arrive in your Formspree email with the score; score them with the rubric below before the student moves on.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. 4,128 ÷ 16 = ?
    Answer: 258
    41 ÷ 16 = 2 R9, 92 ÷ 16 = 5 R12, 128 ÷ 16 = 8. Check: 16 × 258 = 4,128.
  2. 306 × 27 = ?
    Answer: 8,262
    306 × 7 = 2,142 and 306 × 20 = 6,120. 2,142 + 6,120 = 8,262.
  3. 130 guests are coming to a co-op dinner. Each table seats 8. How many tables are needed?
    Answer: 17 tables
    130 ÷ 8 = 16 R2. Sixteen tables leave 2 guests standing, so round up to 17.
  4. A lawn-care business earned $520 in income and had $346 in expenses. What was its profit?
    Answer: $174
    Profit = income − expenses = 520 − 346 = $174.
  5. Estimate 6,290 ÷ 7 using compatible numbers. What is your estimate?
    Answer: 900
    6,290 is close to 6,300, and 6,300 ÷ 7 = 900.
  6. Vocabulary from Week 1.
    Answer: divisor / quotient / budget
    Divisor, quotient, budget.

Learn 1: Decimal Place Value

  1. In 47.385, what digit is in the hundredths place?
    Answer: 8
    Tenths 3, hundredths 8, thousandths 5.
  2. Which is greater: 0.7 or 0.69 ? Type the number.
    Answer: 0.7
    Write 0.70 and 0.69: 7 tenths beats 6 tenths.
  3. Order from least to greatest .
    Answer: 2.05 → 2.459 → 2.5 → 2.51
    Write each to the thousandths: 2.050, 2.459, 2.500, 2.510.
  4. Write three and forty-two thousandths as a decimal.
    Answer: 3.042
    Forty-two thousandths fills three places: 0.042. So the number is 3.042.

Learn 2: Adding Decimals

  1. 4.37 + 12.8 = ?
    Answer: 17.17
    4.37 + 12.80 = 17.17. Estimate: 4 + 13 = 17.
  2. A receipt's subtotal is $25.08. Two items cost $8.49 and $11.20. The third price is smudged. What is it?
    Answer: $5.39
    8.49 + 11.20 = 19.69. 25.08 − 19.69 = $5.39. Check: 19.69 + 5.39 = 25.08.
  3. Amara buys 2.25 kg of flour, 0.75 kg of sugar and 1.5 kg of bananas. What is the total mass?
    Answer: 4.5 kg
    2.25 + 0.75 = 3.00, and 3.00 + 1.50 = 4.50 kg.
  4. Estimate first, then add: $19.99 + $4.89 + $0.65
    Answer: $25.53
    Estimate 20 + 5 + 1 = 26. Exact: 19.99 + 4.89 = 24.88, plus 0.65 = $25.53.

Learn 3: Subtracting Decimals

  1. Amara has $20.00. She wants items costing $6.79, $8.45 and $3.99, and the tax will be $1.34. How much more money does she need?
    Answer: $0.57
    6.79 + 8.45 + 3.99 = 19.23, and 19.23 + 1.34 = $20.57. 20.57 − 20.00 = $0.57 short.
  2. 8.3 − 2.755 = ?
    Answer: 5.545
    8.300 − 2.755 = 5.545.
  3. Tampa got 6.42 inches of rain in June and 4.9 inches in May. How many more inches fell in June?
    Answer: 1.52 inches
    6.42 − 4.90 = 1.52 inches.
  4. A runner's times were 58.47 seconds and 61.2 seconds. What is the difference?
    Answer: 2.73 seconds
    61.20 − 58.47 = 2.73 seconds.

Learn 4: Check the Receipt, Count the Change

  1. What is the subtotal on Amara's receipt?
    Answer: $19.74
    4.89 + 12.50 + 2.35 = $19.74.
  2. What is the total after tax?
    Answer: $21.12
    19.74 + 1.38 = $21.12.
  3. Amara paid with $25.00. How much change should she get?
    Answer: $3.88
    25.00 − 21.12 = $3.88.
  4. Later, Amara buys pans for $36.47 and pays with a $50 bill. What is her change?
    Answer: $13.53
    50.00 − 36.47 = $13.53. Count up: 3¢ to $36.50, 50¢ to $37, $3 to $40, $10 to $50.

Think Deeper

  1. Devon added 3.5 + 1.25 and got 1.60. What is the correct sum?
    Answer: 4.75
    Lining up the decimal points: 3.50 + 1.25 = 4.75.
  2. Explain Devon's mistake and how an estimate would have warned him.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Identifies that he lined up the last digits instead of the decimal points; Shows the correct alignment 3.50 + 1.25; Uses an estimate (about 3 + 1 = 4) to show 1.60 is impossible
    Model answer: Devon lined up the last digits instead of the decimal points, so he added 3.5 as if it were 0.35. Written correctly, 3.50 + 1.25 = 4.75. An estimate of 3 + 1 = 4 shows 1.60 is impossible, because the answer must be more than 3.5.
  3. Your younger cousin insists 0.45 is bigger than 0.5 because 45 is bigger than 5. Explain why they are wrong in a way a 9-year-old would understand. Use money or a drawing in words.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Uses place value or a money comparison (50 cents versus 45 cents); Rewrites 0.5 as 0.50 to compare; Explanation is clear for a younger child
    Model answer: Think of money. 0.5 is the same as 0.50, which is 50 cents, and 0.45 is 45 cents. Fifty cents is more than forty-five cents, so 0.5 is bigger. The 45 looks bigger only because it has more digits, but the first digit after the point (5 tenths versus 4 tenths) decides.

Exit Check

  1. In 6.209, what digit is in the thousandths place?
    Answer: 9
    Tenths 2, hundredths 0, thousandths 9.
  2. A receipt lists $3.25, $4.80 and $2.15, and prints a subtotal of $11.20. What should the subtotal be?
    Answer: $10.20
    3.25 + 4.80 + 2.15 = $10.20. The store overcharged by $1.00, which is why checking matters.
  3. A cashier owes you $0.68 in coins. What is the fewest number of coins she can use? (quarters, dimes, nickels, pennies)
    Answer: 7 coins
    2 quarters (50¢) + 1 dime (60¢) + 1 nickel (65¢) + 3 pennies (68¢) = 7 coins. Using the largest coin first gives the fewest.
  4. 9.07 − 3.6 = ?
    Answer: 5.47
    9.07 − 3.60 = 5.47.
  5. Order from least to greatest.
    Answer: 0.307 → 0.37 → 0.703 → 0.73
    0.307, 0.370, 0.703, 0.730.
  6. A receipt shows $8.49, $3.75 and $11.20, plus $1.64 tax. What is the total?
    Answer: $25.08
    8.49 + 3.75 + 11.20 = 23.44. 23.44 + 1.64 = $25.08.
  7. You pay for that $25.08 total with $40. What is your change?
    Answer: $14.92
    40.00 − 25.08 = $14.92.
