Unit 1: Stories That Shape Us (Narrative) · Week 2 · ELA · Teacher Plan

Character and Point of View

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Identify first person, third person limited and third person omniscient points of view.
  • Explain how a narrator's point of view shapes what the reader knows and feels.
  • Analyze characters using five methods of characterization: speech, thoughts, effect on others, actions and looks.
  • Use the roots spect, vid/vis, aud and phon to figure out word meanings.
  • Keep pronouns consistent and rewrite a scene from a different point of view.

Standards

  • ELA.6.R.1.3: Explain the influence of multiple narrators and/or shifts in point of view in a literary text.
  • ELA.6.R.1.1: Analyze how the interaction between characters contributes to the development of a plot in a literary text.
  • ELA.6.V.1.2: Apply knowledge of Greek and Latin roots and affixes to determine the meanings of words.
  • ELA.6.C.3.1: Follow the rules of standard English grammar, punctuation, capitalization and spelling (pronoun consistency).
  • ELA.6.C.1.2: Write personal or fictional narratives using narrative techniques, precise words and phrases, and figurative language.

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • Moving Day text (print for annotating if helpful)
  • A phone or camera for the photo walk
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • point of view: The perspective from which a story is told.
  • first person: A point of view in which the narrator is a character in the story and uses I, me and we.
  • third person limited: An outside narrator who uses he, she and they and shares the thoughts of only one character.
  • third person omniscient: An outside narrator who knows and shares the thoughts and feelings of every character.
  • characterization: The ways a writer reveals what a character is like.
  • direct characterization: When the writer states a trait outright: She was brave.
  • indirect characterization: When the writer shows a trait through speech, thoughts, effect on others, actions or looks, and the reader infers it.
  • character trait: A word that describes a character's personality, such as stubborn, kind or determined.
  • spect / vid, vis: Latin roots meaning look and see, as in perspective, inspect, video and visible.
  • aud / phon: Roots meaning hear (Latin aud) and sound (Greek phon), as in audience and telephone.

Lesson flow

6 minReconnect
Run the Week 1 Reconnect questions. The last question sets up today: The Cast Net used an outside narrator.
5 minHook
Ask the student to describe a recent disagreement from their side, then from the other person's side.
12 minRead Moving Day
Read Part 1 aloud together, pause, and predict what the boy is thinking. Then read Part 2. Discuss the gap between Priya's guess and Dominic's reality.
10 minThree points of view
Use the camera analogy. Convert one sentence into all three points of view on the whiteboard.
12 minSTEAL characterization
Fill in a STEAL chart for Dominic together. Push for evidence: which exact words show the trait?
10 minRoots and pronouns
Teach the four roots by building words. Have the student fix two pronoun shifts out loud.
5 minAssign
Assign the photo walk, the Dominic rewrite and the Exit Check.

Key questions to ask

  • Whose thoughts can we hear? Whose can we only guess?
  • What would change if Dominic told the whole story?
  • What exact words led you to that character trait?
  • Why might a writer hide one character's thoughts from the reader?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Identifying point of view from pronouns inside dialogue (a third-person story can include I in quotations).
  • Confusing limited and omniscient when only one character appears in a passage.
  • Naming a feeling (sad) instead of a lasting trait (determined) when asked for a character trait.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Give a STEAL graphic organizer with the five rows filled in for Priya as a model.
  • Highlight pronouns outside quotation marks in one color to identify point of view.
  • Offer a character-trait word bank.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Write a third, omniscient version of paragraph 7 that includes Priya's mom's thoughts.
  • Find a book on the family's shelf told in alternating narrators and explain why the author chose that structure.

Background for the teacher

STEAL (Speech, Thoughts, Effect on others, Actions, Looks) is a widely used middle school framework for indirect characterization. The story connects to the personal narrative project: students choose how to use first person to reveal their own thoughts.

How the lesson runs

During the live lesson, the student shares their screen and answers the Reconnect questions and the Learn checks live while you teach. The Hands-On activity, Think Deeper and Exit Check are completed after class.

Scoring

The interactive lesson scores itself: full points on the first try, half points on the second try, then the answer and explanation appear. Mastery is 85. Written responses arrive in your results email with the score; score them with the rubric below before the student moves on.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. Plot parts in order.
    Answer: rising action / falling action
    Exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, resolution.
  2. In The Cast Net, Mateo thinks, "Maybe I'm just not a fisherman." Is that an internal or external conflict?
    Answer: internal
    The struggle is inside his own mind.
  3. What do the roots in biography mean together?
    Answer: life + write: a written story of someone's life
    Bio means life and graph means write.
  4. What is a rubric ?
    Answer: A scoring guide that describes each level of quality
    A rubric lists the traits of the work and describes each level.
  5. Rewrite as showing, not telling: Mateo was frustrated. Do not use the word frustrated.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Shows the feeling with action, speech or body language; Does not name the feeling
    Model answer: Mateo yanked the dripping net out of the water and kicked the empty bucket across the pier.
  6. In The Cast Net, the story is told by an outside narrator using he and Mateo . Is the narrator a character in the story? Type yes or no.
    Answer: no
    The narrator is outside the story and refers to Mateo as he. Today you will name this point of view.

Learn 1: Read: Moving Day

  1. Part 1 uses I and my . Who is the narrator of Part 1?
    Answer: priya
    Priya tells Part 1 herself.
  2. Why does Priya say "I'm busy" and grab the heaviest box? What is she really feeling?
    Answer: She is nervous or sad about moving and wants to avoid meeting someone new.
    She uses the box as an excuse to avoid saying hi. The heavy box also shows she wants to feel strong.
  3. What does Part 2 reveal about Dominic that Priya could never know from her side of the street?
    Answer: That he was once the new kid himself and knows how lonely it feels, and that he wanted to help but was nervous.
    Only Dominic's thoughts reveal his memory of being the new kid at the co-op.
  4. Priya thinks the boy is "pretending not to watch." Based on Part 2, what is he actually doing?
    Answer: He wants to help but is working up the courage to go over.
    Priya reads his behavior one way; his thoughts show another. That gap is the power of two points of view.

Learn 2: Three Points of View

  1. What point of view is Part 2 written in?
    Answer: third person limited
    An outside narrator uses he and shares only Dominic's thoughts.
  2. "Marcus hoped nobody noticed his shaking hands, while his coach secretly worried the team was not ready." What point of view is this?
    Answer: third person omniscient
    The narrator knows the thoughts of two characters, Marcus and the coach.
  3. Why might a writer choose first person for a story about moving to a new city?
    Answer: So the reader feels the character's emotions and thoughts up close.
    First person puts the reader inside the character's head, which makes emotions vivid.

Learn 3: How Writers Reveal Character

  1. "Priya was stubborn" is direct characterization. Rewrite it as indirect characterization, showing the trait with one STEAL method.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Shows stubbornness through speech, thoughts, effect, actions or looks; Does not use the word stubborn
    Model answer: "I said I'm carrying it myself," Priya snapped, pulling the box away when Mom reached for it.
  2. Name one character trait for Dominic based on paragraph 7.
    Answer: kind, brave or helpful (any trait supported by the text)
    Crossing the street to help a stranger shows kindness and courage.
  3. Which STEAL method reveals Dominic's trait in paragraph 7: speech, thoughts, effect, actions or looks?
    Answer: actions
    He crosses the street and lifts the box: actions.
  4. Priya's last line is "Wait until you see the second box." What does this joke show about how she has changed since paragraph 4?
    Answer: She is relaxing and starting to open up to Dominic; she is willing to talk and joke now.
    In paragraph 4 she refused to talk. Now she jokes with him, a sign she is beginning to feel at home.

Learn 4: Roots and Pronouns

  1. Perspective contains the root spect. Using the root, what is a perspective?
    Answer: A way of looking at or seeing something (a point of view)
    Per (through) + spect (look): the way you look at something.
  2. Complete with a root word.
    Answer: spectator / audible / visible
    Spectator (one who looks), audible (able to be heard), visible (able to be seen).
  3. Fix the pronoun shift by typing the corrected sentence: I carried the box carefully because you never know what might break.
    Answer: I carried the box carefully because I never knew what might break.
    Keep the first-person pronoun I throughout the sentence.

Hands-On

  1. Describe what each of your three camera positions showed and hid. How is a camera position like a narrator's point of view?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Completes every step of the activity; Includes specific details, numbers or observations from the activity; Explains thinking in complete sentences
  2. Which person from your personal narrative did you retell the moment as? What did they notice or feel that you did not?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Completes every step of the activity; Includes specific details, numbers or observations from the activity; Explains thinking in complete sentences

Think Deeper

  1. Rewrite paragraph 4 of Moving Day from Dominic's first-person point of view, starting with "I watched her..." Include at least one thought he has that Priya could never know, and use at least one STEAL method to reveal Priya's character through his eyes.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Consistent first-person point of view as Dominic (I, me); Includes a thought only Dominic could have; Reveals Priya's character through a STEAL method seen by Dominic; Stays true to the events of the story; Correct capitals, punctuation and pronouns
    Model answer: I watched her grab the biggest box in the truck like she had something to prove. Her arms were shaking, but her face looked fierce, like she would rather drop dead than drop that box. I knew that look. Last year I wore it every day at the co-op so nobody would see how much I wanted someone to talk to me.
  2. The writer told this story in two parts with two points of view instead of one. Explain what the reader gains. Use evidence from both parts.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Explains that the reader learns both characters' hidden thoughts; Uses evidence from Part 1 and Part 2; Explains the gap between what Priya thinks and what Dominic feels
    Model answer: With two points of view, the reader knows more than either character. In Part 1, Priya thinks the boy is just staring at her. In Part 2, we learn Dominic was the new kid last year and wanted to help but was too nervous. That gap makes the moment he crosses the street feel brave, and it shows how often people misread each other.

Exit Check

  1. "I could not believe my eyes." Point of view?
    Answer: first person
    The narrator uses I.
  2. "She tiptoed down the hall, wondering if anyone heard." (Only her thoughts are shared in the whole story.) Point of view?
    Answer: third person limited
    Outside narrator, one character's thoughts.
  3. A narrator who shares the thoughts of every character uses which point of view?
    Answer: third person omniscient
    Omniscient means all-knowing.
  4. What does the S in STEAL stand for?
    Answer: speech
    Speech, Thoughts, Effect on others, Actions, Looks.
  5. "Her hands trembled as she opened the letter." Which trait could you infer, and is this direct or indirect characterization? Answer like this: trait, direct or indirect.
    Answer: nervous, indirect
    Trembling hands show nervousness without stating it: indirect characterization.
  6. Using the root aud, what is an auditorium ?
    Answer: A large room where an audience listens to a performance or speech
    Aud means hear: a place built for hearing performances.

Answer key: printable page

  1. Name the point of view of Part 1 and Part 2, and give one clue for each.
    Answer: Part 1: first person (I, my). Part 2: third person limited (he; only Dominic's thoughts).
  2. Fill in one piece of STEAL evidence about Dominic: Speech ______ Thoughts ______ Actions ______
    Answer: Speech: "Your books weigh a ton." Thoughts: remembering being the new kid / his mouth went dry. Actions: crosses the street and lifts the box.
  3. Write one character trait for Priya and one quotation that supports it.
    Answer: Determined: "I carried it all the way up the driveway without stopping." (Other supported traits accepted.)
  4. Rewrite in third person limited: I wanted to help, but my mouth had gone dry.
    Answer: He wanted to help, but his mouth had gone dry.
  5. Write the meaning of each root and one word that uses it: spect ____ vis ____ aud ____ phon ____
    Answer: spect = look (inspect); vis = see (vision); aud = hear (audio); phon = sound (telephone)
  6. Fix the pronoun shift: We love the beach because you can swim all day.
    Answer: We love the beach because we can swim all day.
  7. How would the story change if Priya told Part 2 as well? What would the reader lose?
    Answer: The reader would never learn that Dominic was once the new kid and wanted to help; we would only see Priya's assumption that he was staring.
