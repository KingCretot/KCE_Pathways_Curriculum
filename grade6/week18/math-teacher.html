Unit 4: Integers and the Coordinate Plane · Week 18 · Math · Teacher Plan

Adding and Subtracting Integers

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Model integer addition with counters (zero pairs) and on a number line.
  • Add integers with the same sign and with different signs fluently, and explain the rules.
  • Subtract integers by adding the opposite, and explain why it works.
  • Find the change between two integers (temperature, elevation, balance) by subtracting.
  • Track a loan and a credit card balance below zero, and explain how debt and interest work.

Standards

  • MA.6.NSO.4.1: Apply and extend previous understandings of operations with whole numbers to add and subtract integers with procedural fluency.
  • MA.6.NSO.1.2: Given a mathematical or real-world context, represent quantities that have opposite direction using rational numbers. Compare them on a number line and explain the meaning of zero within its context.
  • MA.K12.MTR.2.1: Demonstrate understanding by representing problems in multiple ways.
  • Financial literacy: borrowing, debt, credit and interest (Jump$tart Managing Credit).

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • Playing cards or numbered index cards
  • Two colors of counters (about 20 each)
  • Tape or chalk number line from −20 to 20
  • Paper and pencil
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • zero pair: A positive and a negative of the same size, such as 1 and −1, that add to zero.
  • additive inverse: A number's opposite; a number plus its additive inverse equals 0.
  • sum: The result of adding.
  • difference: The result of subtracting.
  • debt: Money you owe; it can be shown as a negative balance.
  • credit: Trust that lets you borrow now and pay later.
  • interest: Extra money charged for borrowing, added to a debt over time.

Key questions to ask

  • Are there more positives or more negatives?
  • What is the opposite of the number being subtracted?
  • Which way does this move on the number line?
  • Is the change a rise or a drop?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Two negatives always make a positive (true for multiplying, not adding).
  • Subtracting always makes a number smaller.
  • −9 + 4 = −13 or 13 (adding absolute values with different signs).
  • Change means start minus final.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Keep counters available for every problem.
  • Use a vertical number line (thermometer) for temperature problems.
  • Color-code positive and negative numbers.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Create a magic square where every row, column and diagonal of integers sums to −3.
  • Find the record low and high temperatures for your county and compute the range.

Background for the teacher

The Loma, Montana, record (January 14-15, 1972: −54°F to 49°F, a 103-degree rise in 24 hours) is listed by NOAA's National Centers for Environmental Information. Kilimanjaro is commonly given as 5,895 m (19,341 ft); Lake Assal's surface is about 155 m (509 ft) below sea level. Maya's loan and the card figures are practice data; typical U.S. credit card interest rates have been above 20 percent a year since 2023 (Federal Reserve G.19). The rules here are taught as summaries of counter and number-line models, not memorized first.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. Which quadrant is (−3, −8) in? Type the Roman numeral.
    Answer: III
    Both coordinates are negative.
  2. How many units apart are (−4, 6) and (5, 6)?
    Answer: 9 units
    |−4| + |5| = 9.
  3. Reflect (2, −9) across the x-axis. Type the ordered pair.
    Answer: (2, 9)
    Across the x-axis, y changes sign.
  4. Last week, to find the distance from −4 to 5 you added 4 + 5. Explain in your own words why you added instead of subtracted.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: The numbers are on opposite sides of zero, so you travel 4 to reach zero, then 5 more
    Model answer: −4 and 5 are on opposite sides of zero. You go 4 units to reach zero, then 5 more, so you add 4 + 5 = 9.

Learn 1: Adding Integers with Counters

  1. −7 + (−5) = ?
    Answer: -12
    Same signs: 7 + 5 = 12, keep the negative sign.
  2. −11 + 6 = ?
    Answer: -5
    Different signs: 11 − 6 = 5; there are more negatives.
  3. 14 + (−9) = ?
    Answer: 5
    14 − 9 = 5; there are more positives.
  4. Explain, using zero pairs, why 8 + (−8) = 0. Then give a real-life example.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Each positive pairs with a negative; all 8 pairs make 0; A real example (earn $8, spend $8; climb 8 feet, descend 8 feet)
    Model answer: 8 yellow and 8 red counters make 8 zero pairs, and each pair is worth 0, so the sum is 0. If I earn $8 and then spend $8, I'm back where I started.

Learn 2: Adding on a Number Line

  1. A scuba diver is at −30 feet and rises 12 feet. What is the new depth? (Write −30 + 12.)
    Answer: -18 feet
    Start at −30 and move 12 toward zero.
  2. 6 + (−4) + 9 + (−8) + (−5) = ?
    Answer: -2
    Positives 15; negatives −17; 15 + (−17) = −2.
  3. Lena says −8 + 5 and 5 + (−8) have different answers because one starts negative. Is she right? Show both on a number line in words.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Both equal −3 (addition is commutative); Describes both moves: start at −8 move 5 right; start at 5 move 8 left
    Model answer: She's not right. Start at −8 and move 5 right: −3. Start at 5 and move 8 left: −3. Addition works in any order.

Learn 3: Subtracting: Add the Opposite

  1. 8 − 15 = ?
    Answer: -7
    8 + (−15) = −7.
  2. −3 − (−10) = ?
    Answer: 7
    −3 + 10 = 7.
  3. The highest point in Africa, Mount Kilimanjaro in Tanzania, is about 19,341 feet above sea level. The shore of Lake Assal in Djibouti, Africa's lowest point, is about 509 feet below sea level. What is the difference in elevation?
    Answer: 19850 feet
    19,341 − (−509) = 19,341 + 509 = 19,850 feet.
  4. Explain with a money story why 10 − (−4) = 14.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Subtracting a negative = removing a debt or loss; So the total goes up by 4
    Model answer: I have $10 but also an IOU saying I owe my brother $4. If he tears up the IOU, a −4 is taken away, and I'm worth $14 now. Taking away a negative makes the total bigger.

Learn 4: Money: Debt and Credit, Going Below Zero

  1. After Saturday 2, Maya's balance is −$30. What is her balance after two more $15 payments?
    Answer: 0 dollars
    −30 + 15 + 15 = 0: the loan is paid off.
  2. A credit card balance is −$120. The owner pays $50, then buys a $35 bike helmet. What is the new balance?
    Answer: -105 dollars
    −120 + 50 + (−35) = −105.
  3. In January a card balance was −$240. In February it was −$95. What was the change in the balance? (February − January)
    Answer: 145 dollars
    −95 − (−240) = −95 + 240 = 145: the debt shrank by $145.
  4. Two friends each owe $200 on credit cards that charge interest. One pays it off this month; the other pays only $10 a month. Explain, using what you know about negative balances, why the second friend could end up paying much more than $200.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Interest adds to the negative balance each month the debt remains; Small payments barely reduce it, so interest keeps being added for a long time
    Model answer: Every month the debt stays, interest is added, making the balance more negative. Paying only $10 barely brings it toward zero, so interest keeps getting added for many months, and the total paid ends up much more than $200.

Hands-On

  1. Write three addition and two subtraction problems from your card game, with answers. For one subtraction, show how you rewrote it as adding the opposite.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Five problems with correct answers; One subtraction rewritten correctly as adding the opposite
    Model answer: Black 7 + red 9: 7 + (−9) = −2. Red 4 + red 6: −10. Black 8 + red 3: 5. Red 5 − black 2: −5 − 2 = −5 + (−2) = −7. Black 3 − red 8: 3 − (−8) = 3 + 8 = 11.
  2. What was the credit card's final balance? Then share your own borrowing-and-repaying list and its final balance.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Card balance: −4; Own list of six steps with a correct final balance; Says whether the debt is paid off
    Model answer: Credit card: −12 − 6 + 10 − 8 − 3 + 15 = −4, so $4 is still owed. My list: borrow $18 for a skateboard (−18), pay +5, +5, borrow $4 for wheels (−4), pay +6, +6: final balance 0, paid off.

Think Deeper

  1. At 6 a.m. the temperature in Fairbanks, Alaska, was −23°F. By 2 p.m. it was −9°F, and by midnight it was −31°F. Find the change from 6 a.m. to 2 p.m. and from 2 p.m. to midnight, and the total change. Explain how you know whether each change is a rise or a drop.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: 6 a.m. to 2 p.m.: −9 − (−23) = 14 (rise); 2 p.m. to midnight: −31 − (−9) = −22 (drop); Total: −31 − (−23) = −8 (or 14 + (−22) = −8); The sign of the change shows rise (+) or drop (−)
    Model answer: 6 a.m. to 2 p.m.: −9 − (−23) = −9 + 23 = 14, a rise of 14 degrees. 2 p.m. to midnight: −31 − (−9) = −31 + 9 = −22, a drop of 22 degrees. Total: 14 + (−22) = −8, a drop of 8 degrees. A positive change means the temperature rose; a negative change means it dropped.
  2. Kai says, "Subtracting always makes a number smaller." Is he right? Give an example that supports him and one that proves him wrong, and explain.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Supporting example: subtracting a positive (10 − 3 = 7); Counterexample: subtracting a negative (5 − (−2) = 7); Explains that subtracting a negative is adding a positive
    Model answer: He's only sometimes right. 10 − 3 = 7 gets smaller. But 5 − (−2) = 5 + 2 = 7 gets bigger. Subtracting a negative is the same as adding a positive, so the result increases.

Exit Check

  1. −15 + 8 = ?
    Answer: -7
    15 − 8 = 7; more negatives.
  2. 4 − 11 = ?
    Answer: -7
    4 + (−11) = −7.
  3. −6 − (−6) = ?
    Answer: 0
    −6 + 6 = 0, a zero pair.
  4. Rewrite 7 − (−3) as an addition problem and solve it. Why does that work?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: 7 + 3 = 10; Subtracting a number equals adding its opposite
    Model answer: 7 + 3 = 10. Subtracting a number is the same as adding its opposite, and the opposite of −3 is 3.

Answer key: printable page

  1. Find each sum: (a) −8 + (−9) (b) −13 + 5 (c) 21 + (−14)
    Answer: (a) −17 (b) −8 (c) 7
  2. Find each difference: (a) 6 − 14 (b) −7 − 9 (c) −12 − (−20)
    Answer: (a) −8 (b) −16 (c) 8
  3. A hiker starts at 125 feet below sea level in Death Valley and climbs to 410 feet above sea level. How many feet did she climb?
    Answer: 410 − (−125) = 535 feet.
  4. A temperature falls from 5°F to −12°F. What is the change?
    Answer: −12 − 5 = −17 degrees (a drop of 17).
  5. A loan balance is −$85. The borrower pays $30, then borrows $12 more, then pays $40. What is the balance?
    Answer: −85 + 30 − 12 + 40 = −27; $27 is still owed.
  6. Explain why subtracting a negative number gives a greater result.
    Answer: Subtracting a negative is adding its opposite (a positive), like canceling a debt.
