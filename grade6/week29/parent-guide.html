Grade 6 · Week 29 · Parent Guide

Week 29: Mean, Median and Mode, Speaking and Presenting, Natural Selection and the Columbian Exchange

Math summarizes data with the mean, median, mode, range and box plots, and uses averages to compare real prices. ELA turns the research report into a presentation with clear visuals, spoken citations and a Q&A. Science explains how natural selection changes populations over many generations, using Florida beach mice, anoles and panthers. Social Studies meets the Taíno, reads Columbus's own journal critically, and traces the catastrophe and the Columbian Exchange that followed 1492.

How scoring works: each lesson scores itself. Your child gets two tries per question: full points on the first try, half points on the second, then the answer and explanation appear. A score of 85 or higher means the lesson is mastered. Written answers go to Jonathan, who scores them with a rubric.

Time this week: About 60 minutes of live lessons per subject, plus a hands-on activity, Think Deeper questions and an Exit Check for each. Building and rehearsing the ELA presentation takes about 1 to 2 extra hours.

Gather this week: Grocery flyers, store websites or a store visit with you, paper and pencil, graph paper, a ruler, an optional calculator, and a family member (Math); your child's research report draft, a computer or tablet with slide software (or poster board and markers), 5 to 8 index cards, a timer, an optional phone or tablet to record, and at least one family member as an audience (ELA); a sheet of newspaper for the habitat, two sheets of plain white paper and two extra sheets of newspaper to cut into dots, scissors or a hole punch, tweezers or a clothespin, a timer, paper and pencil, graph paper, a ruler, and a family member (Science); access to your kitchen pantry and refrigerator, paper and pencil, a world map or atlas, colored pencils or two colors of sticky dots, and a family member (Social Studies).

Heads up: In Social Studies, your child reads Columbus's own words about the Taíno and learns about enslavement, forced labor, disease and the deaths of most Taíno people; it's taught honestly and at a sixth-grade level, and the lesson also stresses that Taíno descendants and culture live on today. The lesson mentions the transatlantic slave trade, which continues in later study. Science discusses antibiotics: use the moment to reinforce taking medicine only as prescribed. Visit stores only together for Math's price hunt. Help with scissors, and keep paper dots and hole-punch pieces away from young children and pets (choking hazard). In ELA, help your child use only images they took, drew or have permission to use.

ELA: Speaking and Presenting

Building on the Week 20 speech, your child adapts the research report into a 4- to 6-minute presentation for a specific audience, designs clear slides or posters with source lines, explains numbers aloud with oral citations, rehearses with a timer and practices answering questions honestly.

Ask at dinner: "Who is your audience, and what are the three things they should remember?"

Try it together: Be the audience: ask two questions, including one that's hard to answer, and give one specific strength and one specific change.

Signs it's clicking: They say "I didn't find that in my research, but I'd look in..." instead of guessing. Signs they need help: They read slides word for word. Suggest key words on note cards and more of the talk spoken aloud.

Math: Mean, Median, Mode and Range

Your child finds the mean, median, mode, range and interquartile range, builds box plots, sees how an outlier pulls the mean but not the median, chooses the best measure for a situation, and collects real prices for one product to find the best buy.

Ask at dinner: "Is there an outlier here, and which average gives a fairer picture?"

Try it together: Go on the price hunt together and compare your child's median price with what you usually pay.

Signs it's clicking: They explain why an ad might report a mean home price instead of a median. Signs they need help: They find the middle of an unordered list. Have them write the values in order first.

Life Science: Natural Selection Over Time

Your child learns natural selection as V-I-S-T (variation, inheritance, selection, time), corrects the myth that animals change because they need to, sees natural selection measured in finches, Florida anoles and bacteria, looks at fossil and anatomy evidence, and connects lack of variation to extinction through the dusky seaside sparrow and Florida panther.

Ask at dinner: "Did one animal change, or did the population change over generations?"

Try it together: Scatter the paper dots for your child and watch which kind survives each generation.

Signs it's clicking: They explain why giraffes didn't get long necks by stretching. Signs they need help: They say an animal adapted because it wanted to. Ask, "Which ones survived and had more babies, and why?"

World and American History: 1492, the Taíno and the Columbian Exchange

Your child learns about Taíno society in the Caribbean, reads short passages from Columbus's 1492 journal and asks what they reveal about his goals, studies the encomienda system, epidemics and Taíno resistance led by Hatuey and Enriquillo, and maps the plants, animals, people and diseases that crossed the Atlantic in the Columbian Exchange.

Ask at dinner: "What did Columbus notice about the Taíno, and what did he want from them?"

Try it together: Do the kitchen audit together and find which foods in your home came from the Americas and which from Afro-Eurasia.

Signs it's clicking: They explain why Columbus's journal is valuable evidence even though it's one-sided. Signs they need help: They think the Taíno disappeared completely. Point to the lesson on Taíno descendants in Puerto Rico, Cuba and the Dominican Republic today.
