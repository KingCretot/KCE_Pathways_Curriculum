Unit 6: Geometry and Data · Week 29 · Math · Teacher Plan

Mean, Median, Mode and Range

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Find the mean, median and mode of a data set and explain what each tells you.
  • Find the range and interquartile range to describe spread.
  • Create and interpret a box plot using the five-number summary.
  • Explain how an outlier affects the mean and median, and choose the best measure of center.
  • Compare prices and data sets using measures of center and spread.

Standards

  • MA.6.DP.1.2: Given a numerical data set within a real-world context, find and interpret mean, median, mode and range.
  • MA.6.DP.1.3: Given a box plot within a real-world context, determine the minimum, the lower quartile, the median, the upper quartile and maximum. Use this summary of the data to describe the spread and distribution of the data.
  • MA.6.DP.1.4: Given a histogram or line plot within a real-world context, qualitatively describe and interpret the spread and distribution of the data, including any symmetry, skewness, gaps, clusters, outliers and the range.
  • MA.6.DP.1.6: Given a real-world scenario, determine and describe how changes in data values impact measures of center and variation.
  • Financial literacy: comparing prices to make spending decisions (Jump$tart Spending and Saving).

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • Grocery flyers or store websites, paper and pencil, graph paper and a ruler, optional calculator
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • mean: The sum of the values divided by the number of values; the average.
  • median: The middle value when the data are in order (or the mean of the two middle values).
  • mode: The value that appears most often (a data set may have no mode or more than one).
  • range: The difference between the greatest and least values.
  • outlier: A value much greater or much less than the rest of the data.
  • interquartile range (IQR): The range of the middle half of the data: the third quartile minus the first quartile.
  • box plot: A display showing the minimum, first quartile, median, third quartile and maximum.

Key questions to ask

  • Is there an outlier, and how does it affect the mean?
  • Which measure best describes a typical value here?
  • How spread out are the data?
  • What does the box plot tell you at a glance?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • The median is the middle of the unordered list.
  • The mean is always the best average.
  • Range tells you everything about spread.
  • A data set must have exactly one mode.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Have students write data in order on sticky notes and physically find the middle.
  • Provide a five-number summary template.
  • Use small data sets with whole numbers first.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Compute the mean absolute deviation for the bakery prices.
  • Compare box plots of prices at two different stores and decide which is more consistent.

Background for the teacher

Quartile methods vary; this lesson uses the halves method that excludes the median for odd counts. With that method the bakery quartiles are $7 and $10. Verify MA.6.DP.1.2 to 1.6 wording against CPALMS. Prices are practice figures except in the hands-on.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. Write one statistical question about your homeschool or family.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A question that expects many different answers from a group
    Model answer: How many minutes do the kids in my co-op read each day?
  2. What display groups data into equal intervals, with bars that touch?
    Answer: a histogram
    For many different values.
  3. Last week you described where data "cluster." Why might it be useful to describe that center with a single number?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Explains that one number summarizes and makes comparison easy
    Model answer: One number makes it easy to summarize the data and compare it with another group, like saying a typical customer buys 3 loaves.

Learn 1: Mean, Median and Mode

  1. Find the mean of these quiz scores: 82, 90, 76, 88, 94.
    Answer: 86
    430 ÷ 5 = 86.
  2. Find the median of these hours of sleep: 9, 7, 8, 10, 8, 9.
    Answer: 8.5 hours
    In order: 7, 8, 8, 9, 9, 10; middle two are 8 and 9; (8 + 9) ÷ 2 = 8.5.
  3. Find the mode of these shoe sizes: 6, 7, 7, 8, 7, 9, 6.
    Answer: 7
    7 appears three times.

Learn 2: Spread: Range and Interquartile Range

  1. Find the range of these daily high temperatures: 88, 91, 85, 94, 90.
    Answer: 9 degrees
    94 − 85 = 9.
  2. Two homeschool families each read a median of 30 minutes a day. Family A's times range from 25 to 35 minutes; Family B's range from 0 to 90. Describe how their reading habits differ.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Uses spread to compare; Describes consistency versus variability
    Model answer: Both read about 30 minutes on a typical day, but Family A is very consistent, always 25 to 35 minutes, while Family B varies wildly, from not reading at all to an hour and a half.

Learn 3: Box Plots

  1. Find the five-number summary of: 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 12.
    Answer: 2; 4; 7; 9; 12
    Median 7; lower half 2, 4, 5 (Q1 4); upper half 8, 9, 12 (Q3 9).
  2. A box plot of game scores has a median of 15, Q1 of 10, Q3 of 18 and whiskers from 4 to 30. What fraction of scores were between 10 and 18, and what does the long right whisker suggest?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: About half the scores; Explains that a few scores were much higher
    Model answer: About half of the scores were between 10 and 18. The long right whisker means a few games had much higher scores than usual, up to 30.

Learn 4: Outliers and Choosing a Measure; Money: Comparing Prices

  1. Five friends earn weekly allowances of $10, $12, $10, $15 and $53. Find the mean.
    Answer: 20 dollars
    100 ÷ 5 = 20.
  2. Find the median of the same allowances.
    Answer: 12 dollars
    In order: 10, 10, 12, 15, 53.
  3. For the allowances above, which measure gives a fairer picture of a "typical" allowance, the mean or the median? Explain.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Chooses a measure; Justifies it using the outlier
    Model answer: The median, $12, is fairer. The $53 allowance is an outlier that pulls the mean up to $20, which is more than four of the five friends actually get.
  4. Using the bakery data, recommend a price for Maya's sourdough. Use at least two measures (center or spread) in your reasoning.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A specific price; Uses at least two measures correctly; Considers the outlier or business factors
    Model answer: I'd charge $8. That's the median, so half the bakeries charge less and half charge more, and it's inside the middle half of prices ($7 to $10). The mean of $8.77 is pulled up by the $14 shop, so it overstates what most customers expect to pay.

Hands-On

  1. List your prices and show your mean, median, mode, range and IQR. Was there an outlier?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: At least 9 prices; Correct calculations; Identifies whether there is an outlier
    Model answer: Gallon of milk: $3.19, $3.29, $3.49, $3.49, $3.59, $3.79, $3.99, $4.29, $6.49 (organic). Mean: 35.61 ÷ 9 ≈ $3.96. Median: $3.59. Mode: $3.49. Range: $3.30. Q1: $3.39, Q3: $4.14, IQR: $0.75. The $6.49 organic milk is an outlier.
  2. Which measure best describes a typical price for your product, and where should your family buy it? Explain with your box plot.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Chooses a measure with a reason; A recommendation based on the data; Refers to the box plot
    Model answer: The median, $3.59, is best because the $6.49 organic milk pulls the mean up. My box plot shows most prices between $3.39 and $4.14. I'd recommend the $3.19 store, which is below Q1, as long as it's convenient.

Think Deeper

  1. A real estate ad says, "The average home price in our neighborhood is $650,000!" but most homes sell for around $300,000. Explain how this can be true and why the ad might use the mean instead of the median.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Explains how a few very expensive homes raise the mean; Explains the seller's motive; Connects to judging sources (Week 27 ELA)
    Model answer: A few mansions worth millions can pull the mean far above what most homes cost, while the median would stay near $300,000. A seller might report the mean to make the neighborhood sound more valuable. Like we learned in ELA, always ask who made a claim and why.
  2. Create a data set of 7 numbers with a mean of 10, a median of 8 and a mode of 6. Show that it works and explain your strategy.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A correct data set meeting all three conditions; Shows the checks; Explains the strategy
    Model answer: 6, 6, 6, 8, 12, 16, 16. Sum is 70, so the mean is 70 ÷ 7 = 10; the middle value is 8; 6 appears three times, the most. I started with the median in the middle and three 6s, then chose large numbers to raise the total to 70.

Exit Check

  1. Find the mean of 4, 7, 9, 10, 15.
    Answer: 9
    45 ÷ 5 = 9.
  2. Find the median of 3, 8, 1, 6, 9, 4.
    Answer: 5
    In order: 1, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9; (4 + 6) ÷ 2 = 5.
  3. Find the range of 12, 5, 19, 8, 14.
    Answer: 14
    19 − 5 = 14.
  4. Which measure of center is least affected by an outlier, and why?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Names the median; Explains why
    Model answer: The median, because it depends only on the middle value, not on how extreme the largest or smallest values are.

Answer key: printable page

  1. Find the mean, median, mode and range: 5, 8, 8, 10, 12, 15.
    Answer: Mean 58 ÷ 6 ≈ 9.67; median 9; mode 8; range 10.
  2. Find the five-number summary and IQR: 3, 6, 7, 9, 11, 14, 20.
    Answer: 3, 6, 9, 14, 20; IQR 8.
  3. Test scores: 88, 92, 85, 90, 40. Find the mean and median. Which better describes a typical score?
    Answer: Mean 79; median 88; median, because 40 is an outlier.
  4. Which measure would you use for the most popular shirt size in a class? Why?
    Answer: The mode, because it shows the most common choice.
  5. Eight stores sell the same cereal for $3.99, $4.29, $3.79, $4.49, $3.99, $5.99, $4.19, $3.89. Find the median price.
    Answer: In order: 3.79, 3.89, 3.99, 3.99, 4.19, 4.29, 4.49, 5.99; median (3.99 + 4.19) ÷ 2 = $4.09.
