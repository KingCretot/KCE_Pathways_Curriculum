Unit 6: Geometry and Data · Week 27 · Math · Teacher Plan

Nets, Surface Area and Volume

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Represent a right rectangular prism with a net and identify matching faces.
  • Find the surface area of a rectangular prism using a net.
  • Find the volume of a rectangular prism with whole-number and fraction or decimal edges, using V = l × w × h.
  • Explain the difference between surface area and volume and choose the right one for a problem.
  • Compare packaging and shipping costs using surface area, volume and price.

Standards

  • MA.6.GR.2.3: Solve mathematical and real-world problems involving the volume of right rectangular prisms with positive rational number edge lengths using a visual model and a formula.
  • MA.6.GR.2.4: Given a mathematical or real-world context, find the surface area of right rectangular prisms and right rectangular pyramids using the figure's net.
  • Financial literacy: comparing costs to make business decisions (Jump$tart Spending and Saving).

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • An empty cereal box, scissors, ruler, graph paper or cardstock, tape, optional cubes, a small product to package
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • rectangular prism: A solid with six rectangular faces, like a box.
  • face: A flat surface of a solid figure.
  • edge: A line segment where two faces meet.
  • net: A flat pattern that folds up to make a solid figure.
  • surface area: The total area of all the faces of a solid, in square units.
  • volume: The amount of space a solid takes up, in cubic units.

Key questions to ask

  • Are you covering the outside or filling the inside?
  • Which faces match?
  • What units does your answer need?
  • Is the cheapest box the best box?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Surface area and volume are the same thing.
  • Counting only three faces for surface area.
  • Using square units for volume.
  • A bigger box is always better.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Let students handle and unfold real boxes.
  • Color-code the three pairs of faces.
  • Provide a surface area table with three rows.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Find the dimensions of the box with the least surface area that holds 1,000 cubic inches.
  • Research how a real carrier calculates dimensional weight and apply it to Maya's boxes.

Background for the teacher

MA.6.GR.2.4 asks for surface area of right rectangular prisms and pyramids using nets (pyramids are practiced in the Surface Area section); MA.6.GR.2.3 includes fractional and decimal edges. Box prices and shipping charges are practice figures.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. Find the area of a 12-inch by 8-inch rectangle.
    Answer: 96 square inches
    12 × 8 = 96.
  2. Find the area of a triangle with base 10 cm and height 7 cm.
    Answer: 35 square cm
    ½ × 10 × 7 = 35.
  3. Flooring costs $3.00 per square foot. What does it cost for a 9-foot by 10-foot room?
    Answer: 270 dollars
    90 × 3 = 270.
  4. Last week you found the area of an L-shaped room by splitting it into rectangles. How might that same idea help you find the total area of all the sides of a box?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Connects splitting a figure into parts with adding the areas of a box's faces
    Model answer: A box is made of six rectangles, so I can find the area of each one and add them, just like adding the two rectangles of the L-shaped room.

Learn 1: Faces, Edges and Nets

  1. How many faces does a rectangular prism have?
    Answer: 6
    Three matching pairs.
  2. Someone drew a "net" for a 12 × 8 × 6 box with two 12 × 8 rectangles, two 12 × 6 rectangles and two 12 × 12 rectangles. Explain why it can't fold into the box and fix it.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Identifies the wrong pair; Names the correct dimensions of that pair
    Model answer: The left and right faces must match the box's width and height, so they should be 8 × 6, not 12 × 12. Two 12 × 12 squares would stick out and wouldn't fold to close the box.

Learn 2: Surface Area

  1. Find the surface area of a box 10 cm long, 5 cm wide and 4 cm tall.
    Answer: 220 square cm
    2(50) + 2(40) + 2(20) = 100 + 80 + 40 = 220.
  2. A cube has edges of 3 inches. What is its surface area?
    Answer: 54 square inches
    6 faces × 9 = 54.
  3. A gift box is a pyramid with a 6 in × 6 in square base and four triangular faces, each with a base of 6 in and a height of 5 in. Find its surface area using its net.
    Answer: 96 square inches
    Base 36 + 4 triangles × (½ × 6 × 5 = 15) = 36 + 60 = 96.
  4. A box has a volume of 480 cubic inches and a base 10 in by 8 in. How tall is it?
    Answer: 6 inches
    10 × 8 × h = 480, so h = 6.
  5. You're wrapping a 14 × 10 × 3 inch board game. Find the surface area, then explain why you'd cut a little more wrapping paper than that.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Correct surface area; A sensible reason for extra paper
    Model answer: 2(14 × 10) + 2(14 × 3) + 2(10 × 3) = 280 + 84 + 60 = 424 in². I'd cut a bit more because the paper needs to overlap at the seams and fold over the ends.

Learn 3: Volume

  1. Find the volume of the 12 × 8 × 6 inch shipping box.
    Answer: 576 cubic inches
    12 × 8 × 6 = 576.
  2. A planter box is 2.5 feet long, 1.5 feet wide and 1 foot deep. How many cubic feet of soil fill it?
    Answer: 3.75 cubic feet
    2.5 × 1.5 × 1 = 3.75.
  3. For each job, say whether you need surface area or volume, and why: (a) painting a toy chest, (b) filling a fish tank, (c) buying cardboard for boxes, (d) buying mulch for a garden bed.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Four correct choices; A reason for each based on covering versus filling
    Model answer: (a) Surface area, because paint covers the outside. (b) Volume, because water fills the inside. (c) Surface area, because cardboard covers the faces. (d) Volume, because mulch fills the bed's space.

Learn 4: Money: Packaging and Shipping Costs

  1. How much does Maya save per order using Box A instead of Box B (box plus shipping)?
    Answer: 4.2 dollars
    (1.60 + 18.20) − (1.10 + 14.50) = 19.80 − 15.60 = 4.20.
  2. If Maya ships 25 orders a month, how much does she save in a month with Box A?
    Answer: 105 dollars
    25 × 4.20 = 105.
  3. Box C is 9 × 9 × 5 in and costs $1.05 with $14.10 shipping. Can it hold Maya's 400 in³ of loaves? Should she switch? Show your work and consider more than the price.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Correct volume of Box C; A decision based on volume and cost; Considers another factor such as the loaves' shape or padding
    Model answer: Box C's volume is 9 × 9 × 5 = 405 in³, just over 400, so it barely fits, with no room for padding. It saves $0.45 an order over Box A, but I'd stay with Box A because loaves squashed in a tight box would disappoint customers.

Hands-On

  1. Report the cereal box's dimensions, your predicted and measured surface area, and its volume. How close was your prediction, and what caused any difference?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Dimensions and calculations shown; Compares prediction with measurement; Explains a likely cause of any difference
    Model answer: The box was 7.5 × 2.5 × 11 in. I predicted surface area 2(7.5 × 2.5) + 2(7.5 × 11) + 2(2.5 × 11) = 37.5 + 165 + 55 = 257.5 in². The unfolded cardboard was a little more because of the glued flaps. Volume: 7.5 × 2.5 × 11 = 206.25 in³.
  2. Describe the product you packaged, your box's dimensions, its surface area and volume, and how much empty space was left around the product.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Product and box dimensions; Correct surface area and volume; Explains the empty space and whether it's a good design
    Model answer: I packaged a mug that measures 4 × 3.5 × 4.5 in (including the handle). My box is 5 × 4.5 × 5.5 in: surface area 2(22.5) + 2(27.5) + 2(24.75) = 149.5 in², volume 123.75 in³. The mug's bounding box is 63 in³, so about 60 in³ is left for padding, which is good for something breakable.

Think Deeper

  1. Two boxes both hold 64 cubic inches: a 4 × 4 × 4 cube and a 16 × 2 × 2 box. Find the surface area of each. Which uses less cardboard, and what does that suggest about designing packages?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Correct surface area for each box; Identifies which uses less material; Draws a design conclusion
    Model answer: The cube's surface area is 6 × 16 = 96 in². The long box's is 2(32) + 2(32) + 2(4) = 136 in². The cube uses less cardboard for the same volume, so boxes closer to cube-shaped waste less material, which saves money and trees.
  2. If you double every edge of a box, what happens to its surface area and its volume? Test with a 2 × 3 × 4 box and explain.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Correct calculations for both boxes; States how many times larger the surface area and the volume become; An explanation
    Model answer: The 2 × 3 × 4 box has surface area 52 and volume 24. The 4 × 6 × 8 box has surface area 208 (4 times) and volume 192 (8 times). Area uses two dimensions, so it multiplies by 2 × 2; volume uses three, so it multiplies by 2 × 2 × 2.

Exit Check

  1. Find the volume of a box 6 cm by 5 cm by 3 cm.
    Answer: 90 cubic cm
    6 × 5 × 3 = 90.
  2. Find the surface area of the same box.
    Answer: 126 square cm
    2(30) + 2(18) + 2(15) = 60 + 36 + 30 = 126.
  3. Which measure tells how much wrapping paper covers a box?
    Answer: surface area
    It measures the outside, in square units.
  4. Why is volume measured in cubic units but surface area in square units?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Connects square units to flat faces and cubic units to filling space
    Model answer: Surface area covers flat faces, measured with squares; volume fills space, measured with cubes.

Answer key: printable page

  1. Find the surface area and volume of a box 9 in by 4 in by 5 in.
    Answer: SA = 2(36) + 2(45) + 2(20) = 202 in²; V = 180 in³.
  2. Find the volume of a box 3½ ft by 2 ft by 1½ ft.
    Answer: 3.5 × 2 × 1.5 = 10.5 ft³.
  3. A cube has 5-cm edges. Find its surface area and volume.
    Answer: SA = 150 cm²; V = 125 cm³.
  4. Surface area or volume? (a) gift wrap (b) sand in a sandbox (c) labels on a box
    Answer: (a) surface area (b) volume (c) surface area
  5. Box A costs $1.20 + $12.80 shipping; Box B costs $0.95 + $13.40. Which is cheaper per order, and by how much?
    Answer: A: $14.00; B: $14.35; A is cheaper by $0.35.
