Unit 3: Ancient Asia · Week 13 · World and American History · Teacher Plan

Ancient China: Dynasties and Ideas

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Locate the Yellow and Yangtze rivers and explain how geography shaped and protected early China.
  • Describe the Shang dynasty's oracle bones and the beginnings of Chinese writing.
  • Explain the Mandate of Heaven and the dynastic cycle.
  • Compare Confucianism, Daoism and Legalism, using primary sources.
  • Evaluate the achievements and costs of the Qin and Han dynasties.

Standards

  • SS.6.W.4.6: Describe the concept of the Mandate of Heaven and its connection to the Zhou and later dynasties.
  • SS.6.W.4.7: Explain the basic teachings of Laozi, Confucius, and Han Fei Zi.
  • SS.6.W.4.8: Describe the contributions of classical and post classical China.
  • SS.6.W.4.9: Identify key figures from classical and post classical China.
  • SS.6.W.2.4: Compare the economic, political, social, and religious institutions of ancient river civilizations.
  • SS.6.G.2.1: Explain how major physical characteristics, natural resources, climate, and absolute and relative locations have influenced settlement, interactions, and the economies of ancient civilizations of the world.
  • SS.6.W.1.3: Interpret primary and secondary sources.

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • A world map or globe
  • A soft paintbrush, water or thinned washable paint, newspaper or paper bags
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • dynasty: A series of rulers from the same family.
  • oracle bone: A bone or turtle shell carved with questions to the ancestors, then cracked with heat to read an answer.
  • Mandate of Heaven: The Chinese belief that Heaven gives a just ruler the right to rule and takes it away from an unjust one.
  • dynastic cycle: The pattern of dynasties rising, flourishing, declining and being replaced.
  • Confucianism: Teachings of Confucius about good behavior, respect, family duty and wise government.
  • Daoism: Teachings, linked to Laozi, about living in harmony with nature and the Dao (the Way).
  • Legalism: Teachings, best known from Han Feizi, that strict laws and harsh punishments are needed to keep order.
  • civil service: Government jobs filled by trained officials; in Han China, often chosen for learning and merit.

Key questions to ask

  • How did geography shape China?
  • What made a ruler legitimate?
  • Which philosophy would you choose, and why?
  • Was Qin's unity worth its cost?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • China was always one unified country.
  • The Great Wall was built all at once by one emperor (most of today's wall is from the later Ming dynasty).
  • Confucianism is the same as a religion with gods (it is mainly a philosophy of ethics and government).
  • Chinese characters are all pictures (most combine meaning and sound parts).

Support (if the student is struggling)

Background for the teacher

Shang dynasty about 1600 to 1046 BCE; oracle bones rediscovered around 1899 and excavated at Anyang (Yinxu) from 1928; about 4,000 distinct characters attested, of which about a third are deciphered. The Zhou conquest about 1046 BCE; the Mandate of Heaven appears in early Zhou texts. Warring States period 475 to 221 BCE (Eastern Zhou from 770 BCE). Confucius 551 to 479 BCE; the Analects quotations are from James Legge's 1861 translation (public domain): Analects 15.24 and 2.15. Qin unification 221 BCE; the Terracotta Army was found by farmers in March 1974; estimates of about 8,000 figures. The name China is often traced to Qin, though this is debated. Han 206 BCE to 220 CE; Cai Lun's papermaking report is dated 105 CE, but earlier paper fragments have been found; Zhang Heng's seismoscope 132 CE. Han Feizi died 233 BCE; Legalism's 'two handles' of government are reward and punishment. Benchmarks SS.6.W.4.6 to 4.9 checked word for word on CPALMS.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. Name two features that show Indus cities were carefully planned.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Grid streets; standard bricks; covered drains; wells; Great Bath
    Model answer: Grid streets and covered brick drains.
  2. Why can't historians read the Indus script yet?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Very short inscriptions; no bilingual text; unknown language
    Model answer: The inscriptions are very short, and no text gives the same words in a known language.
  3. Complete the pattern of Indus weights.
    Answer: 16
    Each weight doubles.
  4. So far you have studied three river civilizations. Name the river or rivers of each one.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Kemet: Nile; Mesopotamia: Tigris and Euphrates; Indus Valley: Indus
    Model answer: Kemet grew on the Nile, Mesopotamia on the Tigris and Euphrates, and the Indus Valley on the Indus River.

Learn 1: Rivers, Mountains and Deserts

  1. Why was the Yellow River called both a gift and "China's Sorrow"?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Gift: fertile loess soil for farming; Sorrow: destructive floods that killed people and destroyed fields
    Model answer: Its fertile yellow soil made the farmland rich, but its floods were so destructive that they killed many people and wiped out fields, so it brought both life and sorrow.
  2. How did China's natural barriers both help and limit it?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Helped: protection from invasion; independent culture; Limited: less contact and trade with other civilizations; northern plains still open to steppe peoples
    Model answer: Mountains, deserts and the ocean protected China and let its culture develop independently, but they also limited contact and trade with other civilizations, and the northern plains were still open to raiders.
  3. What is the name of the fine yellow soil carried by the Yellow River?
    Answer: loess
    Loess, wind-blown silt that colors the river.

Learn 2: The Shang and the Zhou: Writing and the Mandate of Heaven

  1. Explain the Mandate of Heaven in your own words. How could it be used both to support a ruler and to justify overthrowing one?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Heaven gives a just ruler the right to rule; Supports a good ruler; a disaster or bad rule could be taken as proof the Mandate was lost, justifying rebellion
    Model answer: The Mandate of Heaven means Heaven gives the right to rule only to a just ruler. A good king could point to peace and good harvests as proof he had it. But floods, famine or cruelty could be taken as signs he had lost it, so rebels could say they had the right to overthrow him.
  2. Put the stages of the dynastic cycle in order, starting with a new dynasty.
    Answer: A new dynasty claims the Mandate of Heaven → Peace and prosperity → Decline: corruption and disasters → Rebellion → A new dynasty claims the Mandate of Heaven again
    The cycle repeats.
  3. Is there any idea in your own country's government that is similar to the Mandate of Heaven, the idea that leaders must govern well or lose their right to lead? Explain.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A reasonable comparison (elections, impeachment, the Declaration of Independence's idea that governments get power from the people); Notes a difference (Heaven versus the people)
    Model answer: In the United States, leaders can be voted out or impeached if they govern badly, and the Declaration of Independence says governments get their power from the people. The difference is that the Mandate came from Heaven, while American leaders get power from voters.

Learn 3: Three Ways to Bring Order: Confucius, Laozi and the Legalists

  1. Explain Confucius's saying, "What you do not want done to yourself, do not do to others," and give an example from daily life. Does it remind you of a rule from another culture or religion?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Meaning: treat others as you would want to be treated (avoid harming others); A daily-life example; A connection (the Golden Rule in many traditions)
    Model answer: It means you should not treat people in a way you would not want to be treated. If I would not want someone to laugh at my mistake, I should not laugh at theirs. It reminds me of the Golden Rule found in many religions and cultures.
  2. "Learning without thought is labor lost; thought without learning is perilous." What do you think Confucius meant? Use an example from school.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Memorizing without thinking is wasted effort; Having opinions without knowledge is dangerous; A school example
    Model answer: He meant that memorizing facts without thinking about them is wasted effort, but forming opinions without learning facts is dangerous. For example, memorizing the steps for dividing fractions without understanding them doesn't help me solve new problems, but guessing an answer without learning the method leads to mistakes.
  3. Match each belief to its school of thought: Confucianism, Daoism or Legalism.
    Answer: Legalism; Daoism; Confucianism
    Legalism: laws and punishment. Daoism: harmony with nature. Confucianism: relationships, respect and example.

Learn 4: Qin and Han: Unity at a Cost

  1. Qin Shi Huang standardized writing, money, weights and cart axles. Choose two and explain how each helped unite the empire.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Writing: officials across regions could read the same documents; Money, weights or axles: easier trade, fair exchange, carts fit the same road ruts
    Model answer: A single writing system meant officials everywhere could read the same laws and orders. Standard coins and weights made trade fair and simple across the empire, because a coin or a pound meant the same thing everywhere.
  2. Was the Qin dynasty's unity worth its cost? Give one achievement and one cost, then state your opinion with a reason.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Achievement (unification, standardization, Great Wall, roads); Cost (forced labor, harsh punishments, book burning, short dynasty); A reasoned opinion
    Model answer: Qin united China and standardized writing and money, which lasted for centuries. But it forced hundreds of thousands of people to labor and burned books it disagreed with. I think the unity was important, but it did not have to be so cruel; the Han showed that unity could last with fairer rule.
  3. Which dynasty made Confucianism the official philosophy and began choosing officials for their learning?
    Answer: the Han dynasty
    The Han (206 BCE to 220 CE).

Hands-On

  1. Which character was hardest to write with a brush, and why? How did the brush change the way you wrote compared with a pencil?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Names a character and a real difficulty; Compares brush and pencil (thick and thin strokes, pressure, slower, more careful)
    Model answer: 木 was hardest because the two slanting strokes kept turning into blobs. The brush makes lines thick or thin depending on how hard I press, so I had to slow down and be much more careful than with a pencil.
  2. Describe or draw your invented character, its meaning, and how it became simpler. Why do you think writing systems simplify pictures over time?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Describes an invented character and its simplification; Reasonable explanation (faster to write, easier to learn, fits the writing tool)
    Model answer: I drew a cup with steam lines for "coffee," then simplified it to a square with two short lines. Writing systems simplify pictures because simple shapes are faster to write and fit the tools, like brushes or carving.

Think Deeper

  1. Imagine you are an adviser to a new emperor after years of war. Write a speech recommending Confucianism, Daoism or Legalism as the way to rule. Explain why your choice will bring peace and respond to one argument from another school.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Clear recommendation; Accurate description of the chosen school's ideas; Reasons it would bring peace; Responds to a rival school's argument; Persuasive organization
    Model answer: Your Majesty, after so many years of war, I recommend you rule by the teachings of Confucius. If you lead by good example, treat your people fairly and choose officials for their learning and honesty, your people will respect you instead of fearing you. Families that honor their parents will raise children who respect the law. A Legalist adviser would say that people are selfish and only harsh punishment keeps order. But the Qin used harsh laws, and their dynasty fell within years of the First Emperor's death. Respect lasts longer than fear. Rule with virtue, and you will keep the Mandate of Heaven.
  2. Compare ancient China with one other river civilization you have studied (Kemet, Mesopotamia or the Indus Valley). Give one similarity and two differences.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Accurate similarity (rivers, early writing, bronze, kings, irrigation); Two accurate differences (writing still readable vs. Indus; Mandate of Heaven vs. divine pharaoh; natural barriers; philosophy); Specific evidence
    Model answer: Like Mesopotamia, China grew along rivers that flooded dangerously, and both developed writing early. But Chinese writing has continued in a direct line to today, while cuneiform stopped being used about 2,000 years ago. Also, China's rulers claimed the Mandate of Heaven, which could be lost if they ruled badly, while Mesopotamian kings relied on written law codes like Hammurabi's.

Exit Check

  1. What are the two great rivers of ancient China? (Use either their English or Chinese names.)
    Answer: the Yellow River and the Yangtze
    The Yellow River (Huang He) and the Yangtze (Chang Jiang).
  2. What is the Mandate of Heaven?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: The belief that Heaven gives a just ruler the right to rule and takes it away from an unjust one
    Model answer: The belief that Heaven gives the right to rule to a just ruler and takes it away from a cruel or unjust one.
  3. Which thinker taught respect for parents, good relationships and leading by example?
    Answer: Confucius
    Confucius (Kongzi).
  4. Name one achievement and one cost of Qin Shi Huang's rule.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Achievement: unification, standardization, Great Wall, roads; Cost: forced labor, harsh laws, book burning
    Model answer: He united China and standardized writing and money, but he forced hundreds of thousands of people to labor on his projects.

Answer key: printable page

  1. Why was the Yellow River called "China's Sorrow"?

  2. What were oracle bones used for, and why are they important?
