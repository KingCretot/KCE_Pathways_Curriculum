Unit 1: How We Know the Past · Week 1 · World and American History · Teacher Plan

Historians, Sources and Evidence

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Tell the difference between primary and secondary sources and explain the strengths of each.
  • Explain how historians learn about the past from artifacts, archaeology and oral tradition, not only from written records.
  • Read a timeline using BCE and CE, and calculate the time between events.
  • Identify point of view and bias, and explain why historians compare more than one source.
  • Collect and analyze sources from your own family's history.

Standards

  • SS.6.W.1.3: Interpret primary and secondary sources.
  • SS.6.W.1.4: Describe the methods of historical inquiry and how history relates to the other social sciences.
  • SS.6.W.1.1: Use timelines to identify chronological order of historically significant persons, events and time periods within the ancient world.
  • SS.6.W.1.6: Describe how history transmits culture and heritage and provides models of human character.
  • C3 Framework D2.His.4.6-8 and D2.His.10.6-8: Analyze multiple factors that influenced perspectives; detect possible limitations in the historical record based on evidence.

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • Two or three items from your own family to model the activity on camera
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • primary source: A source created by someone who was there at the time: a letter, diary, photograph, artifact or eyewitness account.
  • secondary source: A source created later by someone studying the past: a textbook, encyclopedia article or documentary.
  • artifact: An object made or used by people in the past, such as a tool, pot or coin.
  • archaeology: The study of the human past by digging up and analyzing artifacts and sites.
  • oral tradition: History, stories and knowledge passed down by speaking from one generation to the next.
  • griot: A West African historian, storyteller and musician who preserves a community's history through oral tradition.
  • BCE / CE: Before the Common Era and Common Era. BCE years count down toward year 1; CE years count up from it.
  • point of view: The position or perspective from which a person sees and describes events.
  • bias: A one-sided view that favors some people or ideas over others.
  • corroborate: To confirm a piece of evidence by finding another source that agrees with it.

Key questions to ask

  • Who made this source, and when?
  • What can this source tell us, and what can it not tell us?
  • Whose voice is missing from this account?
  • How could you check whether this is true?
  • Is an older number always an older date? Why not?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Thinking bigger BCE numbers are more recent.
  • Thinking the 1900s are the 19th century.
  • Believing primary sources are always true; they carry the creator's point of view.
  • Believing societies without writing had no history or were less advanced.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Draw a number line with 1 BCE and 1 CE side by side (there is no year 0) to model BCE and CE, like negative and positive numbers (connects to Math Unit 4).
  • Give a sorting card set: primary on one pile, secondary on the other.
  • Provide interview questions on a printed card.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Ask the student to explain why there is no year 0 between 1 BCE and 1 CE and how that changes the exact count across the line by one year.
  • Have the student find a modern news story told differently by two outlets and identify the word choices that reveal point of view.

Background for the teacher

Nabta Playa, about 100 kilometers west of Abu Simbel in southern Egypt, was used by cattle-herding peoples during the wetter Holocene period, when the Sahara was greener. Its calendar circle is dated to about 4800 BCE (roughly 7,000 years ago) and appears aligned with the summer solstice sunrise, which came near the start of the rainy season. Stonehenge's first phase dates to about 3000 BCE. Some details at Nabta Playa, such as whether the earliest cattle were domesticated and exactly how the stones align with the sky, are still debated by archaeologists.

The Epic of Sundiata tells of Sundiata Keita, who founded the Mali Empire around 1235 CE; it has been preserved by Mande griots (jeli) for centuries. The Calusa of Southwest Florida built shell-mound towns such as Mound Key, repelled Juan Ponce de León's ships in 1513, and wounded him fatally in 1521.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. How many continents are there?
    Answer: 7
    In the United States we count seven: Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Australia (Oceania), Europe, North America and South America. Students in many other countries learn six or five, for example by counting the Americas as one continent. Global thinkers know the count depends on the model.
  2. Which continent is Egypt in?
    Answer: Africa
    Egypt is in northeastern Africa. This matters a great deal in Unit 2.
  3. What is the largest ocean on Earth?
    Answer: Pacific Ocean
    The Pacific Ocean is the largest and deepest ocean.
  4. A map's ___ explains what the symbols and colors on the map mean. What is it called?
    Answer: key (legend)
    The key, or legend, tells you what each symbol means.
  5. Fill in the time words.
    Answer: 10 / 100 / 1000
    Decade 10, century 100, millennium 1,000.
  6. On a number line, which is farther from zero: −30 or −10? Type the number.
    Answer: -30
    −30 is 30 units from zero; −10 is only 10. BCE dates work the same way: 30 BCE is farther back in time than 10 BCE.
  7. A letter written by a Civil War soldier in 1863 is what kind of source?
    Answer: primary
    It was written at the time by someone who lived through the event.

Learn 1: Primary and Secondary Sources

  1. A diary written by a girl during a hurricane in 1926. Primary or secondary?
    Answer: primary
    She wrote it at the time she lived through the event.
  2. Your history textbook. Primary or secondary?
    Answer: secondary
    It was written later by people studying the past.
  3. A stone tool made 10,000 years ago is a primary source even though it has no words. Name two things it can tell a historian and one thing it cannot.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Two reasonable things the tool reveals (skills, food, materials, trade); One clear limit (names, beliefs, language, exact purpose)
    Model answer: It can show what stone people used and what jobs they did, such as cutting meat or scraping hides. It cannot tell us the toolmaker's name, language or beliefs.
  4. Name one advantage a secondary source has over a single primary source.
    Answer: It brings together many sources and explains the big picture.
    Secondary sources combine and compare many primary sources, so they can explain more than one eyewitness can.

Learn 2: Evidence Beyond Writing

  1. What do we call a West African keeper of oral history?
    Answer: griot
    Griots preserve history through speech, song and music.
  2. What kind of evidence is the stone circle at Nabta Playa?
    Answer: Archaeological evidence (a primary source)
    It is a physical site studied by archaeologists.
  3. Which is older: Nabta Playa or Stonehenge?
    Answer: Nabta Playa
    Nabta Playa is about 7,000 years old; Stonehenge is about 5,000 years old.
  4. Why would a historian studying the Mali Empire need oral tradition and not only written records?
    Answer: Because much of Mali's history was kept by griots through speaking, not writing.
    Leaving out oral tradition would leave out much of West Africa's own record of its past.

Learn 3: Reading Time: BCE and CE

  1. Which happened first: 3000 BCE or 2000 BCE?
    Answer: 3000 BCE
    BCE years count down, so 3000 BCE came 1,000 years before 2000 BCE.
  2. How many years passed from 2500 BCE to 1500 BCE?
    Answer: 1,000
    Both are BCE, so subtract: 2500 − 1500 = 1,000 years.
  3. About how many years passed from 300 BCE to 200 CE?
    Answer: 500 years
    One BCE and one CE, so add: 300 + 200 = 500 years. (Because there is no year 0, the exact count is 499; either answer is accepted.)
  4. Mali was founded around 1235 CE. How many years before 2026 is that?
    Answer: 791 years
    Both CE: 2026 − 1235 = 791 years.
  5. In what century was the year 1776?
    Answer: 18th century
    The 1700s are the 18th century, because centuries are named one number ahead.

Learn 4: Point of View and Bias

  1. Which word in Source A shows the Spanish writer's point of view?
    Answer: discovered
    "Discovered" treats the land as unknown, ignoring the people who already lived there.
  2. Name one Indigenous nation that lived in Florida before 1513.
    Answer: Calusa (also Timucua, Tequesta, Apalachee and others)
    Many nations lived in Florida for thousands of years before Spanish ships arrived; you will study them in Unit 6. The Seminole formed later, in the 1700s, from Creek and other peoples who moved into Florida.
  3. What does it mean to corroborate a source?
    Answer: To confirm it by checking it against another source that agrees
    Corroborating means confirming evidence with another independent source.
  4. Rewrite Source A's first sentence so it is fair to both points of view.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Replaces discovered with a fair verb such as arrived or reached; Acknowledges the people already living there; Keeps the facts (1513, Ponce de León, La Florida)

Hands-On

  1. Describe your three sources: what each is, when and by whom it was made, and whether it is primary or secondary.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Completes every step of the activity; Includes specific details, numbers or observations from the activity; Explains thinking in complete sentences
  2. Who did you interview? Write one exact quote, and explain how you could corroborate one fact they told you.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Completes every step of the activity; Includes specific details, numbers or observations from the activity; Explains thinking in complete sentences

Think Deeper

  1. A classmate says, "Oral history is less reliable than written history, because people forget things." Do you agree or disagree? Use the griots, your own interview, and the idea of bias in written sources to support your answer.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Takes a clear position; Uses the griot example accurately; Uses evidence from the student's own interview; Recognizes that written sources can also be biased or wrong; Explains how corroboration helps with any kind of source
    Model answer: I disagree. Griots trained for years to memorize Sundiata's epic exactly, and it has survived for nearly 800 years. My grandmother remembered details about her school that no written record I found includes. Written sources can also be biased, like the textbook sentence that said Ponce de León discovered Florida. Historians should corroborate both kinds of sources.
  2. Think of an event in your own life that two people would describe differently, such as an argument or a game. How would each person's point of view change the story?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Describes a real event; Shows how two points of view differ; Connects to how historians read sources

Exit Check

  1. A photograph taken at a 1963 civil rights march. Primary or secondary?
    Answer: primary
    It was created at the event.
  2. A 2025 website summarizing the history of the Calusa. Primary or secondary?
    Answer: secondary
    It was created long afterward by someone studying the past.
  3. How many years passed from 1800 BCE to 1200 BCE?
    Answer: 600 years
    Both BCE: 1800 − 1200 = 600 years.
  4. About how many years passed from 100 BCE to 400 CE?
    Answer: 500 years
    One BCE, one CE: 100 + 400 = 500 years.
  5. In what century was the year 1492?
    Answer: 15th century
    The 1400s are the 15th century.
  6. What is the study of the past through digging up artifacts called?
    Answer: archaeology
    Archaeologists study physical remains of the past.
  7. Why should a historian compare more than one source about the same event?
    Answer: Because each source has a point of view or bias, and comparing them gets closer to the truth.
    Comparing sources reveals bias and confirms facts.

Answer key: printable page

  1. Label each P (primary) or S (secondary). ___ A Roman coin ___ A documentary about Rome ___ A soldier's letter from 1918 ___ An encyclopedia article ___ A recorded interview with your grandfather
    Answer: P, S, P, S, P
  2. Explain why a stone tool with no writing on it can still be a primary source.
    Answer: It was made and used by people at the time, so it gives direct evidence about their lives.
  3. How many years passed from 3100 BCE to 2600 BCE? Show your work.
    Answer: 3100 − 2600 = 500 years
  4. About how many years passed from 750 BCE to 250 CE? Show your work.
    Answer: 750 + 250 = about 1,000 years
  5. What century is each year in? 1350 ______ 1865 ______ 2026 ______
    Answer: 14th, 19th, 21st
  6. What is a griot, and why are griots important to the history of West Africa?
    Answer: A griot is a West African oral historian and musician who preserves history, such as the Epic of Sundiata, through speaking and song.
  7. A source says a European explorer "discovered" a river in Africa. Explain the bias in this word and rewrite the sentence fairly.
    Answer: People living along the river already knew it; for example: The explorer reached a river that local peoples had lived beside for centuries.
