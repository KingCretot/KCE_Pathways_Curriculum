Unit 6: Geometry and Data · Week 26 · Math · Teacher Plan

Area of Triangles, Quadrilaterals and Composite Figures

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Explain how the formulas for parallelograms and triangles come from the rectangle.
  • Find the area of right and non-right triangles using A = ½bh.
  • Find the area of parallelograms, trapezoids and other quadrilaterals by decomposing them.
  • Find the area of composite figures by breaking them into rectangles and triangles.
  • Use area to estimate the cost of flooring, paint or sod.

Standards

  • MA.6.GR.2.1: Derive a formula for the area of a right triangle using a rectangle. Apply a formula to find the area of a triangle.
  • MA.6.GR.2.2: Solve mathematical and real-world problems involving the area of quadrilaterals and composite figures by decomposing them into triangles or rectangles.
  • MA.6.AR.1.3: Evaluate algebraic expressions using substitution and order of operations.
  • Financial literacy: estimating project costs (Jump$tart Spending and Saving).

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • A tape measure, graph paper, ruler, colored pencils, scissors and paper
  • A store website or flyer for prices; optional calculator
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • area: The amount of surface a flat figure covers, measured in square units.
  • base: A side of a figure chosen as the bottom for measuring.
  • height: The perpendicular distance from the base to the opposite side or vertex.
  • perpendicular: Meeting at a right angle (90°).
  • composite figure: A figure made of two or more simpler shapes.
  • decompose: To break a figure into simpler parts.

Key questions to ask

  • Which length is the height?
  • How could you split this shape?
  • Do you add or subtract here?
  • Why do installers buy extra?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Using the slanted side as the height.
  • Forgetting the ½ for triangles.
  • Writing area in plain units instead of square units.
  • Double-counting an overlapping piece in a composite figure.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Let students trace and cut shapes to see the formulas.
  • Provide grid paper for counting squares.
  • Color each piece of a composite figure.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Find the area of a regular hexagon by splitting it into triangles.
  • Price a full room with flooring, two coats of paint on four walls and a budget limit.

Background for the teacher

Florida's MA.6.GR.2.1 and 2.2 cover triangles, quadrilaterals and composite figures; the trapezoid formula is shown as a shortcut but every problem can be solved by decomposing. Prices are practice figures except in the hands-on, which uses real local prices.

Sources

  • Jump$tart Coalition, National Standards for Personal Financial Education (2021).

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. Evaluate b × h when b = 12 and h = 7.5.
    Answer: 90
    12 × 7.5 = 90.
  2. Evaluate ½ × b × h when b = 10 and h = 9.
    Answer: 45
    ½ × 90 = 45.
  3. A rectangle has area 84 square feet and a length of 12 feet. Solve 12w = 84 for the width.
    Answer: 7 feet
    Divide by 12.
  4. Last unit you planned a budget. If paint costs $32 a gallon and one gallon covers 350 square feet, what would you need to know about a room to plan the paint budget?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Names what must be measured; Explains how it connects to gallons and cost
    Model answer: I'd need the area of the walls, the length times the height of each wall minus doors and windows, so I could figure out how many gallons to buy and multiply by $32.

Learn 1: From Rectangles to Parallelograms

  1. A parallelogram has a base of 15 cm, a height of 8 cm and a slanted side of 10 cm. What is its area?
    Answer: 120 square cm
    15 × 8 = 120. The slanted side is not used.
  2. Ravi found the area of that parallelogram as 15 × 10 = 150 cm². Explain his mistake.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Identifies that he used the slanted side; Explains that the height must be perpendicular to the base
    Model answer: Ravi used the slanted side, 10 cm, instead of the height. The height has to be perpendicular to the base, which is 8 cm, so the area is 15 × 8 = 120 cm².

Learn 2: Triangles: Half a Parallelogram

  1. A triangular sail has a base of 6 feet and a height of 11 feet. What is its area?
    Answer: 33 square feet
    ½ × 6 × 11 = 33.
  2. A right triangle has legs of 9 m and 14 m. What is its area?
    Answer: 63 square meters
    ½ × 9 × 14 = 63.
  3. Explain, using a drawing in words, why the triangle formula has ½ in it.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Describes two identical triangles forming a parallelogram or rectangle; Concludes that one triangle is half the area
    Model answer: If you copy a triangle, flip it and fit it next to the original, the two make a parallelogram with the same base and height. Each triangle is half of that parallelogram, so its area is half of b × h.

Learn 3: Trapezoids and Composite Figures

  1. A trapezoid has parallel bases of 10 inches and 16 inches and a height of 5 inches. What is its area?
    Answer: 65 square inches
    ½ × (10 + 16) × 5 = 65.
  2. A yard is a 30-foot by 20-foot rectangle with a 6-foot by 4-foot shed on it. How many square feet of grass are there?
    Answer: 576 square feet
    600 − 24 = 576 (subtract the cut-out).
  3. Describe two different ways to split Jada's L-shaped room into rectangles. Do both ways give the same total? Explain why.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Two different valid decompositions; Explains that the total area stays the same however you split it
    Model answer: I could cut it with a horizontal line into a 14 × 7 rectangle and an 8 × 2.5 rectangle, or with a vertical line into an 8 × 9.5 rectangle and a 6 × 7 rectangle. Both give 118 square feet, because the floor's area doesn't change; only the way I break it up changes.

Learn 4: Money: Pricing a Room Makeover

  1. Carpet costs $2.75 per square foot. How much would it cost to carpet a 12-foot by 15-foot room (no extra)?
    Answer: 495 dollars
    180 × 2.75 = 495.
  2. A wall is 14 feet long and 8 feet high, with a 3-foot by 4-foot window. How many square feet will be painted?
    Answer: 100 square feet
    112 − 12 = 100.
  3. Sod costs $0.55 per square foot. A homeschool garden plot is a right triangle with legs of 18 feet and 12 feet. Find the cost, then explain whether you'd buy a little extra and why.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Correct area and cost; A reasoned decision about extra material
    Model answer: Area = ½ × 18 × 12 = 108 ft², so 108 × $0.55 = $59.40. I'd buy about 10 percent extra, around 119 ft² for about $65, because sod pieces have to be cut to fit the triangle's corners.

Hands-On

  1. Show your floor plan measurements, how you decomposed the shape, the total area, and your flooring cost with 10 percent extra.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Measurements and decomposition shown; Correct total area; Correct cost including 10 percent extra
    Model answer: Our living room is an L shape: a 16 × 12 ft rectangle plus a 5 × 4 ft nook. Area: 192 + 20 = 212 ft². With 10 percent extra, 233.2 ft², about 234 ft². Luxury vinyl at $2.99 per ft²: 234 × 2.99 = $699.66.
  2. Show your wall area (with windows or doors subtracted), how many gallons you'd need and the cost. What would you tell your family about the makeover budget?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Correct wall area with subtraction; Gallons and cost; A practical recommendation
    Model answer: One wall is 16 × 8 = 128 ft², minus a 3 × 7 door = 21 ft², so 107 ft². One gallon covers 350 ft², so one gallon is enough even for two coats (214 ft²), at $34. I'd tell my family the paint is cheap, but the floor is the big expense, so we might start with paint.

Think Deeper

  1. Draw (in words) a rectangle, a parallelogram and a triangle that all have an area of 24 square units. Give their dimensions and explain how you made sure they matched.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Three correct figures with dimensions; Explains the reasoning, including the ½ for the triangle
    Model answer: Rectangle: 6 by 4. Parallelogram: base 8, height 3. Triangle: base 8, height 6. For the rectangle and parallelogram I chose factors of 24. For the triangle I needed b × h = 48, because half of 48 is 24.
  2. If you double the base of a triangle and also double its height, what happens to its area? Test with an example and explain why.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Correct conclusion about the new area; A tested example; An explanation
    Model answer: The area becomes four times as large. A triangle with base 4 and height 6 has area 12; with base 8 and height 12 its area is 48, which is 4 × 12. Doubling both multiplies b × h by 2 × 2 = 4, and the ½ stays the same.

Exit Check

  1. Find the area of a triangle with base 16 cm and height 9 cm.
    Answer: 72 square cm
    ½ × 16 × 9 = 72.
  2. Find the area of a parallelogram with base 11 m and height 6 m.
    Answer: 66 square meters
    11 × 6 = 66.
  3. Tile costs $4.50 per square foot. How much does it cost to tile a 6-foot by 8-foot bathroom floor?
    Answer: 216 dollars
    48 × 4.50 = 216.
  4. What must be true about the height you use in an area formula?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: States the relationship between the height and the base
    Model answer: It must be perpendicular to the base, meeting it at a right angle.

Answer key: printable page

  1. Find the area: (a) triangle, base 12 ft, height 7 ft (b) parallelogram, base 9 cm, height 4.5 cm
    Answer: (a) 42 ft² (b) 40.5 cm²
  2. Find the area of a trapezoid with bases 8 m and 12 m and height 6 m.
    Answer: ½ × 20 × 6 = 60 m².
  3. An L-shaped patio is a 20 × 10 ft rectangle plus a 6 × 5 ft rectangle. Find its area.
    Answer: 200 + 30 = 230 ft².
  4. A 25 × 18 ft lawn has a 6 × 6 ft pool deck. Find the grass area.
    Answer: 450 − 36 = 414 ft².
  5. Flooring costs $3.10 per ft². Find the cost for a 10 × 12 ft room with 10 percent extra.
    Answer: 120 × 1.1 = 132 ft²; 132 × 3.10 = $409.20.
  6. Explain why the area of a triangle is half of base times height.
    Answer: Two identical triangles make a parallelogram with the same base and height.
