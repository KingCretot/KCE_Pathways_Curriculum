Unit 6: The Americas and the Meeting of Worlds · Week 26 · World and American History · Teacher Plan

The Olmec and the Maya

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Locate Mesoamerica and explain how its geography supported farming and cities.
  • Describe Olmec achievements and why historians call their culture a foundation for later Mesoamerica.
  • Explain how Maya city-states were governed and how they used writing, astronomy and mathematics.
  • Read Maya bar-and-dot numbers and explain the importance of zero.
  • Evaluate causes of the Classic Maya decline and recognize that Maya people and cultures continue today.

Standards

  • SS.6.G.2.1: Explain how major physical characteristics, natural resources, climate, and absolute and relative locations have influenced settlement, interactions, and the economies of ancient civilizations of the world.
  • SS.6.W.1.3: Interpret primary and secondary sources.
  • SS.6.W.1.5: Describe the roles of historians and recognize varying historical interpretations (historiography).
  • SS.6.W.1.6: Describe how history transmits culture and heritage and provides models of human character.

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • Air-dry clay, salt dough or a new craft foam sheet; a pencil or craft stick
  • Dried beans, craft or pretzel sticks, small shells or paper circles
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • Mesoamerica: The region of southern Mexico and Central America where the Olmec, Maya, Mexica and other civilizations developed.
  • maize: Corn, first domesticated in Mexico thousands of years ago; the most important food in Mesoamerica.
  • city-state: An independent city that rules itself and the land around it.
  • glyph: A symbol that stands for a word, sound or idea in a writing system.
  • codex: A folded book; Maya codices were painted on bark paper (plural: codices).
  • stela: A tall carved stone monument, often recording a ruler's deeds and dates (plural: stelae).

Common mistakes to watch for

  • The Maya mysteriously vanished.
  • The Olmec and Maya lived at the same time in the same place.
  • Maya writing was only pictures.

Background for the teacher

Dates: Olmec about 1200 to 400 BCE (San Lorenzo about 1200 to 900, La Venta about 900 to 400); 17 colossal heads are known, the largest about 3 m tall; basalt from the Tuxtla Mountains. Maya Classic period about 250 to 900 CE; Pakal ruled Palenque 615 to 683 and his tomb was found by Alberto Ruz Lhuillier in 1952. Landa's auto-da-fé at Maní was in July 1562; four codices survive (Dresden, Madrid, Paris and the Maya Codex of Mexico). Nojpetén (Tayasal) fell in 1697. Estimates of Mayan-language speakers today range from about 6 to 7 million in roughly 30 languages. The "mother culture" debate is ongoing among archaeologists. Ancient Mesoamerica is outside Florida's Grade 6 NGSSS world history range (which ends with Rome); the standards above cover the skills taught.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. What seasonal winds carried ships across the Indian Ocean?
    Answer: the monsoon winds
    They reverse about every six months.
  2. Which people's ancestors built Great Zimbabwe?
    Answer: the Shona
    Colonial myths denied it; archaeology proved it.
  3. Which city on the Niger became a world center of learning?
    Answer: Timbuktu
    Books sold for more than any other goods.
  4. The civilizations of Unit 5 traded with distant lands. Mesoamerica had no contact with Africa, Europe or Asia before 1492. Predict one way developing in isolation might make its civilizations different.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A reasonable prediction about crops, animals, technology or ideas developing independently
    Model answer: They would develop their own crops, writing and calendars without borrowing from other continents, and they would have no horses or iron from the Old World.

Learn 1: Mesoamerica and the Olmec

  1. What are the "three sisters" crops of Mesoamerica? Name all three.
    Answer: maize, beans and squash
    They grow well together and feed people well.
  2. The basalt for the colossal heads came from mountains about 50 miles away, and the heads weigh up to 40 tons. What does moving and carving them tell historians about Olmec society?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Explains what the effort shows about organization and leadership; Explains what it shows about skill, labor or resources
    Model answer: Moving 40-ton stones 50 miles without wheels or draft animals needed hundreds of workers, careful planning and leaders powerful enough to organize and feed them. It shows the Olmec had skilled stone carvers, a strong government and enough food surplus to free people from farming.
  3. Some historians call the Olmec a "mother culture"; others say Mesoamerican cultures were "sister cultures" that grew up together. What evidence would help decide between these views?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Explains the difference between the two views; Names a kind of evidence that would help (dates of artifacts, where symbols appear first, trade goods)
    Model answer: If Olmec symbols and styles appear earlier than anywhere else and then spread outward, that supports "mother culture." If similar symbols appear in several regions at the same time, with trade going both ways, that supports "sister cultures." Careful dating of artifacts would help decide.

Learn 2: Maya City-States

  1. Pakal became ruler of Palenque in 615 CE and ruled for 68 years. In what year did his rule end?
    Answer: 683
    615 + 68 = 683.
  2. Compare Maya city-states with the Greek city-states from Week 17. Give one similarity and one difference.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: One accurate similarity; One accurate difference
    Model answer: Like the Greeks, the Maya had independent city-states that shared a culture and religion but often fought each other. Unlike Athens, which developed democracy, Maya city-states were ruled by holy lords who claimed a connection to the gods.

Learn 3: Writing, Mathematics and the Calendar

  1. Using bars (5) and dots (1), what number is shown by 2 bars and 3 dots?
    Answer: 13
    5 + 5 + 3 = 13.
  2. How would you write 17 in Maya numbers? Tell how many bars and how many dots.
    Answer: 3 bars and 2 dots
    5 + 5 + 5 + 2 = 17.
  3. Why was having a symbol for zero such an important invention for Maya mathematicians and astronomers?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Explains that zero lets place value work (an empty place); Connects to counting large numbers of days or tracking astronomy
    Model answer: Zero lets a place-value system show an empty place, so the Maya could write very large numbers clearly, like the number of days since the Long Count began. That made it possible to track the movements of the sun, moon and Venus over centuries.
  4. Diego de Landa ordered Maya books burned in 1562, and only four codices survive. What was lost, and how have historians and Maya communities worked to recover Maya history?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Explains what kind of knowledge was in the books; Names ways history has been recovered (reading stelae and buildings, archaeology, Maya languages and oral tradition)
    Model answer: The books held Maya history, astronomy, religion and calendars, so centuries of knowledge were destroyed. Scholars recovered much of it by deciphering the inscriptions on stelae and buildings, working with Maya speakers, studying archaeology and listening to stories and traditions Maya communities kept alive.

Learn 4: Decline and Continuity

  1. Explain how two causes of the Classic Maya decline could have worked together to make each other worse.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Names two causes; Explains how one made the other worse
    Model answer: Drought reduced harvests just as populations were at their largest, so there wasn't enough food. Hungry city-states then fought more wars over farmland and water, which destroyed fields and drove people away, making the food shortage even worse.
  2. Why is it inaccurate to say the Maya "mysteriously vanished"? Use evidence from the lesson.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: States that Maya people and cultures continue; Uses at least two pieces of evidence
    Model answer: Only some southern cities were abandoned; northern cities like Chichén Itzá kept going, Maya kingdoms resisted the Spanish until 1697, and more than six million Maya people speak Mayan languages today. The cities changed, but the people never vanished.

Hands-On

  1. Describe your stela: your glyph design, your birth year in Maya numbers (show the places) and your achievement. What was hardest about writing a large number in base 20?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Describes the glyph and achievement; Shows the birth year correctly in base-20 places; Reflects on what was hard
    Model answer: My name glyph is a sun with a book, because I love reading outside. For 2014, I used three places: 5 × 400 = 2,000 on top (one bar), 0 × 20 in the middle (a shell), and 14 ones on the bottom (two bars and four dots). The hardest part was remembering that the third place counts 400s, not 100s.
  2. Show one addition problem you did with Maya numbers, including a regroup (five dots to a bar, or twenty in one place to one dot above).
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A correct addition problem; Shows a regroup correctly
    Model answer: I added 14 (two bars, four dots) and 9 (one bar, four dots). That made three bars and eight dots; five dots became one more bar, so four bars and three dots, or 23. Four bars is 20, so it regrouped to one dot in the twenties place and three dots in the ones place: 20 + 3 = 23.

Think Deeper

  1. Which Olmec or Maya achievement do you think was most impressive: the colossal heads, writing, the zero, the calendars or the cities? Make a claim and support it with at least two facts, then explain what it shows about the people who created it.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A clear claim; At least two accurate supporting facts; Explains what the achievement shows about the civilization
    Model answer: The Maya zero is most impressive. The Maya were using a zero symbol by the 300s CE, centuries before it reached Europe, and it let them build calendars that counted days back to 3114 BCE. It shows that Maya scholars were brilliant mathematicians and astronomers who developed powerful ideas entirely on their own.
  2. Compare the burning of Maya books in 1562 with the attacks on Timbuktu's manuscripts in 2012 (Week 23). What do these events show about the power of written knowledge, and why do people try to destroy it?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Accurate details of both events; A comparison; An explanation of why people target written knowledge
    Model answer: In 1562 Landa burned Maya books he called works of the devil, and in 2012 armed groups occupied Timbuktu and later burned thousands of manuscripts. In both cases, outsiders who wanted to control people attacked their written history. Books hold a people's memory, beliefs and identity, so destroying them is a way of trying to erase a culture, which is why communities risk so much to protect them.

Exit Check

  1. Which civilization carved the colossal stone heads?
    Answer: the Olmec
    About 1200 to 400 BCE on Mexico's Gulf Coast.
  2. In Maya numbers, what is 3 bars and 4 dots?
    Answer: 19
    15 + 4 = 19.
  3. What is the K'iche' Maya book of creation and history called?
    Answer: the Popol Vuh
    Written down in the 1500s.
  4. In one or two sentences, explain why the Maya were not one empire.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Explains the city-state system with independent rulers
    Model answer: The Maya lived in many independent city-states, each with its own holy lord, that traded, intermarried and fought but were never united under one ruler.

Answer key: printable page

  1. Where and when did the Olmec live, and name two of their achievements.
    Answer: Gulf Coast of Mexico, about 1200 to 400 BCE; colossal heads, the rubber ballgame, jade carving, trade networks.
  2. How were Maya city-states governed?
    Answer: Each was independent, ruled by a holy lord (k'uhul ajaw).
  3. Write these in Maya numbers (bars and dots): 8, 15, 19.
    Answer: 8: 1 bar, 3 dots; 15: 3 bars; 19: 3 bars, 4 dots.
  4. Why was zero important to the Maya?
    Answer: It made place value work, so they could write large numbers and build accurate calendars.
  5. Name two causes of the Classic Maya decline.
    Answer: Drought, overpopulation, warfare, loss of faith in rulers.
  6. Name two Maya peoples who live today.
    Answer: K'iche', Yucatec, Q'eqchi', Mam, Kaqchikel.
