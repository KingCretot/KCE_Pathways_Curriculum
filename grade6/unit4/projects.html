Grade 6 · Unit 4 · Projects due in Week 20

Unit 4 Projects and Rubrics

Each project is scored with five traits worth up to 4 points each, for a total of 20. Multiply by 5 for a score out of 100. Mastery is 85 (17 of 20). A project below mastery is returned with feedback and revised once. Read the rubric before you start.

ELA: Argument Project (Essay and Speech)

Task: (1) Write a researched argumentative essay of 500 to 800 words, and (2) deliver it as a 2- to 3-minute speech to an audience and record it. Start planning in Week 19 if you like; plan, research, write, rehearse and record in Week 20.

Choose a question (or propose your own to a parent): Should our county add protected bike lanes on main roads? Should every Florida child learn to swim before age 7? Should homeschool students take part in community service each year? Should students still learn cursive? Should kids ages 11 to 13 have their own smartphones? Should our town build a splash pad or community garden?

Must include: a title; an introduction with a hook, background and a clear, arguable thesis; at least two body paragraphs, each with a reason, evidence and explanation; a counterclaim paragraph with a fair counterclaim, a concession and a rebuttal; a conclusion with a call to action; at least three credible sources, cited in the essay and listed in a works-cited list; no fallacies. Speech: note cards (not a script), eye contact, clear voice and pauses.

Turn in: the essay and works-cited list, the speech recording, and the reflection in the Week 20 lesson.

Science: Body Systems Project

Task: Build a life-size body map showing at least five organ systems, and write a 300- to 500-word "Day in the Life" explanation of how your body systems work together during one real activity you do (a swim, a dance class, a soccer game, a hike).

Must include: your traced body outline; organs from at least five systems (skeletal, muscular, circulatory, respiratory, digestive, nervous) drawn about life size in the right places and labeled with their jobs; red and blue paths for oxygen-rich and oxygen-poor blood; at least three interaction arrows with explanations; the written explanation using at least eight Unit 4 vocabulary words and the idea of homeostasis; one piece of data you collected this unit (heart rate, breathing rate or reaction time) used in your explanation; a short list of sources.

Turn in: a photo of the body map, the written explanation, and the record in the Week 20 lesson.

Social Studies: Mediterranean Project

Task: Create a museum exhibit of four panels (poster board, a folded display, or four slides) titled "One Sea, Many Peoples," comparing Persia, Greece, Rome and Carthage, and give a 3- to 4-minute tour to your family as the museum guide.

Must include: one panel per civilization with a hand-drawn map, its government, one major achievement, one war or conflict and who was left out of power; a fifth "connections" section showing at least three ideas or goods that traveled between civilizations (one must involve Africa); one short quotation from a primary source studied in Unit 4 (Herodotus, Ezra, Pericles as recorded by Thucydides, or Livy) with a sentence on who wrote it, when and why; a timeline with at least eight dates; a list of at least three sources.

Turn in: photos of the exhibit, a recording of the tour, and the record in the Week 20 lesson.
