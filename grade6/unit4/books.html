Grade 6 · Unit 4 · Free to read or borrow

Unit 4 Book List

Every book here can be read free. The first group is in the public domain and free to read online. The second group is free to borrow with a public library card: look for it in your library's catalog or its free e-book apps, or borrow a digital copy through Open Library when one is available. Availability varies by library.

Free to read online (public domain)

BookSubjectWhy read it
Common Sense
Thomas Paine (1776)		ELAThe pamphlet from Week 18. Short and readable in sections; try the part answering objections to independence. Older spellings throughout.
My Bondage and My Freedom
Frederick Douglass (1855)		ELADouglass's second autobiography; the appendix prints his 1852 Fourth of July speech from Week 17. Preview first: describes the violence of slavery.
Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass
Frederick Douglass (1845)		ELAHis first autobiography, including how he learned to read. Preview first: describes the violence of slavery.
The History of Herodotus, Volume 2
Herodotus, translated by G. C. Macaulay (1890)		Social StudiesBooks 5 to 9 cover the Royal Road and the Persian Wars (Weeks 16 and 17). Best read in short passages with a parent.
History of the Peloponnesian War
Thucydides, translated by Richard Crawley (1874)		Social StudiesBook 2 has Pericles' Funeral Oration (Week 17).
The History of Rome, Books 1 to 8
Livy, translated by D. Spillan (1853)		Social StudiesBook 3 tells the story of Cincinnatus (Week 18); Book 1 has Rome's legendary founding.
The History of Rome, Books 9 to 26
Livy, translated by D. Spillan and Cyrus Edmonds (1849)		Social StudiesBook 21 follows Hannibal over the Alps (Week 19).
