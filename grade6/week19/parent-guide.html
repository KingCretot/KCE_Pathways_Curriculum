Grade 6 · Week 19 · Parent Guide

Week 19: Multiplying and Dividing Integers, the Argumentative Essay, Digestion and the Nervous System, and Carthage

Math finishes integer operations and tracks a home bakery's profit and loss. ELA plans, drafts and revises a full argumentative essay on whether students should still learn cursive. Science follows a meal through the digestive system and tests reaction time. Social Studies tells the story of Carthage, Hannibal and Roman Africa.

How scoring works: each lesson scores itself. Your child gets two tries per question: full points on the first try, half points on the second, then the answer and explanation appear. A score of 85 or higher means the lesson is mastered. Written answers go to Jonathan, who scores them with a rubric.

Time this week: About 60 minutes of live lessons per subject, plus a hands-on activity, Think Deeper questions and an Exit Check for each. ELA's essay will need extra writing time.

Gather this week: A deck of playing cards with face cards removed, a family member, paper and pencil, and optionally a real recipe and grocery receipt or online prices (Math); a printed copy of the model essay (or a handwritten copy), scissors, highlighters or colored pencils in 4 colors, about 20 sticky notes, a wall, door or large poster board, and a family member (ELA); a gallon zipper bag, 4 plain crackers or a slice of bread, 1/2 cup water, 2 tablespoons vinegar, an old, clean pair of tights or a knee-high stocking, scissors (used by an adult), a bowl, a 30-centimeter (12-inch) ruler, a family member, and paper and pencil (Science); paper, pencil and a fine marker, a printed or hand-drawn Phoenician alphabet chart (from a library book or museum website, with a parent), optionally a piece of chalk, a cup of white vinegar and a cup of water, and a calculator for checking (Social Studies).

Heads up: Science's digestion model uses food and vinegar: no one should eat or drink any of it, keep it away from pets, throw it away afterward and wash hands. An adult cuts the tights. Social Studies' optional chalk test uses vinegar; pour the liquids down the sink and don't drink them. The ELA essay is a multi-day piece of writing; it's normal for it to need a second draft, and your feedback on the outline helps a lot.

ELA: Writing an Argumentative Essay

Your child studies a model essay, writes an introduction with a hook and thesis, uses a source packet honestly with citations, writes a counterclaim paragraph and a strong conclusion, and revises a full essay on whether students should still learn cursive.

Ask at dinner: "Does your source really say that, or does it say something smaller?"

Try it together: Listen to your child read their sticky-note outline aloud and tell them which reason is strongest.

Signs it's clicking: They cite sources and admit a study's limits instead of overstating it. Signs they need help: They think revising means fixing spelling. Ask them to improve one idea or piece of evidence first.

Math: Multiplying and Dividing Integers

Your child multiplies and divides integers, discovers why a negative times a negative is positive through patterns, finds average change, and calculates a home bakery's weekly profit and loss and average weekly profit.

Ask at dinner: "Are the signs the same or different? So is the answer positive or negative?"

Try it together: Play Integer Product War with a deck of cards.

Signs it's clicking: They explain the pattern 2 × (−4) = −8, 1 × (−4) = −4, 0 × (−4) = 0, −1 × (−4) = 4. Signs they need help: They say −6 + (−4) = 10 because "two negatives make a positive." That rule is for multiplying and dividing, not adding.

Life Science: Digestive and Nervous Systems

Your child traces food through the digestive system, compares mechanical and chemical digestion, learns how villi absorb nutrients, studies neurons, the brain and reflexes, measures reaction time with a ruler, and considers the ethics of a famous 1820s digestion experiment.

Ask at dinner: "What happens to your sandwich after you swallow it?"

Try it together: Do the ruler-drop test together and compare reaction times.

Signs it's clicking: They explain why a reflex is faster than a decision. Signs they need help: They think most digestion happens in the stomach. Most chemical digestion and absorption happen in the small intestine.

World and American History: Carthage and Roman Africa

Your child learns about Carthage, the North African trading city founded by Phoenicians, the alphabet, the Punic Wars and Hannibal's march over the Alps, reads Livy's account critically, and studies Roman Africa, the empire's breadbasket and home of the emperor Septimius Severus, the playwright Terence and the thinker Augustine.

Ask at dinner: "Why should we be careful when only the winners' side of a story survives?"

Try it together: Trade secret messages written in the Phoenician alphabet.

Signs it's clicking: They give evidence that Africa was central to the Roman world. Signs they need help: They think Hannibal lost in Italy. He won there for years; Rome defeated him at Zama in Africa.
