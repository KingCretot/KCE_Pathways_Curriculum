Unit 4: Integers and the Coordinate Plane · Week 19 · Math · Teacher Plan

Multiplying and Dividing Integers

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Model multiplication of integers as repeated addition and with patterns.
  • Explain and apply the sign rules for multiplying and dividing integers.
  • Divide integers and interpret the quotient in context (average change, equal shares).
  • Evaluate expressions with integers using the order of operations you already know.
  • Calculate profit and loss for a small business, including average weekly profit.

Standards

  • MA.6.NSO.4.2: Apply and extend previous understandings of operations with whole numbers to multiply and divide integers with procedural fluency.
  • MA.6.NSO.4.1: Apply and extend previous understandings of operations with whole numbers to add and subtract integers with procedural fluency.
  • MA.K12.MTR.5.1: Use patterns and structure to help understand and connect mathematical concepts.
  • Financial literacy: revenue, expenses, profit and loss (Jump$tart Earning Income).

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • A deck of playing cards (face cards removed)
  • Paper and pencil
  • Optional: a recipe and grocery prices
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • product: The result of multiplying.
  • quotient: The result of dividing.
  • factor: A number being multiplied.
  • average change: The total change divided by the number of time periods.
  • revenue: All the money a business takes in from sales.
  • profit: Revenue minus expenses, when the result is positive.
  • loss: Revenue minus expenses, when the result is negative.

Key questions to ask

  • How many negative factors are there?
  • What does this quotient mean in the story?
  • Is this rule for adding or for multiplying?
  • Did the business make or lose money, and why?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Two negatives always make a positive, even when adding.
  • Negative × negative is negative because "negatives make it smaller."
  • Order of operations doesn't apply to integers.
  • Average must be positive.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Keep the sign-rule card visible.
  • Use repeated addition on a number line for positive × negative.
  • Let students build the pattern table themselves.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Find three integers whose product is −24 and whose sum is −3.
  • Investigate: is (−2) to the power 10 positive or negative? What about (−2) to the power 11?

Background for the teacher

MA.6.NSO.4.2 completes the integer operations benchmarks in Grade 6 B.E.S.T. The pattern argument and the "opposite of" argument are both standard justifications for the sign rules; avoid teaching the rule as an unexplained slogan. Order of operations with whole numbers was taught earlier in the year; exponents are formally studied in Week 21. All business figures are practice data.

Sources

  • Florida Department of Education, B.E.S.T. Standards for Mathematics (2020).
  • Jump$tart Coalition, National Standards for Personal Financial Education (2021).

How the lesson runs

During the live lesson, the student shares their screen and answers the Reconnect questions and the Learn checks live while you teach. The Hands-On activity, Think Deeper and Exit Check are completed after class.

Scoring

The interactive lesson scores itself: full points on the first try, half points on the second try, then the answer and explanation appear. Mastery is 85. Written responses arrive in your results email with the score; score them with the rubric below before the student moves on.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. −8 + (−8) + (−8) = ?
    Answer: -24
    Three groups of −8: −24. Today you'll write this as 3 × (−8).
  2. 6 − (−9) = ?
    Answer: 15
    6 + 9 = 15.
  3. A credit card balance is −$70. The owner pays $45. What is the new balance?
    Answer: -25 dollars
    −70 + 45 = −25.
  4. Explain why subtracting a negative number makes the result greater.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Subtracting a number is adding its opposite; the opposite of a negative is positive (or: taking away a debt leaves you better off)
    Model answer: Subtracting a number is the same as adding its opposite, and the opposite of a negative number is positive, so the result goes up.

Learn 1: Multiplying: Repeated Groups

  1. 6 × (−7) = ?
    Answer: -42
    Six groups of −7.
  2. A diver descends 9 feet per minute for 4 minutes. Write the change in depth as an integer.
    Answer: -36 feet
    4 × (−9) = −36 feet.
  3. Without multiplying, decide whether (−1) × (−1) × (−1) × (−1) × (−1) is positive or negative, and explain how you know.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Negative; Five negative factors is an odd number
    Model answer: Negative. There are five negative factors, an odd number, so the product is negative. (It equals −1.)

Learn 2: Why Negative × Negative Is Positive

  1. (−8) × (−5) = ?
    Answer: 40
    Negative × negative = positive.
  2. (−2) × 3 × (−5) = ?
    Answer: 30
    Two negatives: positive. 2 × 3 × 5 = 30.
  3. Use the pattern table or a story to explain to a younger student why (−2) × (−6) = 12.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Uses a correct pattern (products increase by 6 as the first factor decreases) or a valid story (reversing a loss, the opposite of a negative); Reaches 12 with clear reasoning
    Model answer: 2 × (−6) = −12, 1 × (−6) = −6, 0 × (−6) = 0. Each time the first number goes down by 1, the answer goes up by 6. Keep going: −1 × (−6) = 6, −2 × (−6) = 12.

Learn 3: Dividing Integers

  1. −56 ÷ (−8) = ?
    Answer: 7
    Same signs: positive.
  2. A stock's value changed by −$45 over 5 days. What was the average change per day?
    Answer: -9 dollars
    −45 ÷ 5 = −9 dollars per day.
  3. Evaluate: 10 + (−12) ÷ 4 × (−2)
    Answer: 16
    −12 ÷ 4 = −3; −3 × (−2) = 6; 10 + 6 = 16.
  4. Explain why −15 ÷ 0 has no answer. Use the connection between multiplication and division.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Division undoes multiplication: −15 ÷ 0 = ? means 0 × ? = −15; No number times 0 gives −15
    Model answer: −15 ÷ 0 would have to be a number that, times 0, gives −15. But any number times 0 is 0, so no number works. Division by zero is undefined.

Learn 4: Money: Profit and Loss

  1. What is Maya's total for the six weeks?
    Answer: 160 dollars
    48 − 14 + 52 + 38 − 24 + 60 = 160.
  2. What is her average weekly profit? Round to the nearest cent if needed.
    Answer: 26.67 dollars
    160 ÷ 6 ≈ 26.67 dollars per week.
  3. Maya sells loaves at $9. Each loaf costs her $3 in ingredients. One week, she bakes 20 loaves but sells only 14, and the rest go stale. What is her profit or loss? Use a negative number for a loss.
    Answer: 66 dollars
    Revenue: 14 × $9 = $126. Expenses: 20 × $3 = $60. 126 − 60 = $66 profit.
  4. In Week 5, flour prices jumped and Maya lost money. Suggest two business decisions she could make, and use integers to show how one of them would change her result.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Two reasonable decisions (raise prices, bake fewer loaves, buy flour in bulk, add a higher-priced product); Shows with numbers how one decision changes profit or loss
    Model answer: She could raise her price to $10 or buy flour in bulk at a lower price. If she sells 14 loaves at $10 instead of $9, that's 14 × $1 = $14 more revenue, which would turn her −$24 week into −$10, much closer to breaking even.

Hands-On

  1. Write five Product War rounds and two quotient rounds with the cards and answers. Did you notice a pattern about when products were positive?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Five products and two quotients, with correct signs and values; States the pattern: same signs positive, different signs negative
    Model answer: Black 7 × red 6 = −42. Red 4 × red 9 = 36. Black 8 × black 3 = 24. Red 10 × black 2 = −20. Red 5 × red 5 = 25. Quotients: red 8 ÷ black 2 = −4; red 9 ÷ red 3 = 3. Pattern: two cards of the same color gave a positive product; different colors gave a negative one.
  2. Share your business, your cost and price per item, your 6-week ledger with reasons for losses, and your total and average weekly result.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Business, cost and price; Six weekly integers including two losses with reasons; Correct total and average
    Model answer: Dog treats: cost $0.50 a bag, price $3. Week 1: +$24. Week 2: −$10 (bought cookie cutters and bags). Week 3: +$18. Week 4: −$4 (rainy market, few sales). Week 5: +$30. Week 6: +$20. Total: +$78. Average: 78 ÷ 6 = $13 a week.

Think Deeper

  1. Over 5 nights in Tallahassee, the low temperature changed from the night before by −4, +2, −7, −3 and +2 degrees. Find the total change and the average change per night, and describe what the average means.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Total: −10 degrees; Average: −10 ÷ 5 = −2 degrees per night; Interprets: on average the low dropped 2 degrees each night
    Model answer: Total change: −4 + 2 − 7 − 3 + 2 = −10 degrees. Average: −10 ÷ 5 = −2 degrees per night. It means that, overall, the nights got colder by about 2 degrees each night, even though some nights warmed up.
  2. Jordan says, "Two negatives make a positive, so −6 + (−4) = 10." Explain his mistake, give the correct answer, and give an example where "two negatives make a positive" really is true.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: The rule applies to multiplying and dividing, not adding; Correct answer: −10; A correct example (−6 × (−4) = 24, or −24 ÷ (−6) = 4)
    Model answer: Jordan mixed up the rules. "Two negatives make a positive" is true for multiplying and dividing, not adding. Adding two negatives is like owing $6 and then owing $4 more: −6 + (−4) = −10. But −6 × (−4) = 24, where the rule does work.

Exit Check

  1. (−9) × 7 = ?
    Answer: -63
    Different signs: negative.
  2. (−72) ÷ (−9) = ?
    Answer: 8
    Same signs: positive.
  3. A business had results of +$30, −$12 and −$6 over three weeks. What was its average weekly profit?
    Answer: 4 dollars
    (30 − 12 − 6) ÷ 3 = 12 ÷ 3 = 4.
  4. How do you decide the sign of the answer when you multiply or divide two integers?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Same signs: positive; different signs: negative
    Model answer: If the signs are the same, the answer is positive. If they're different, it's negative.

Answer key: printable page

  1. Find each product: (a) 8 × (−6) (b) (−7) × (−7) (c) (−1) × 4 × (−3) × (−2)
    Answer: (a) −48 (b) 49 (c) −24
  2. Find each quotient: (a) −81 ÷ 9 (b) −36 ÷ (−4) (c) 50 ÷ (−10)
    Answer: (a) −9 (b) 9 (c) −5
  3. A submarine descends 25 feet per minute for 8 minutes. What is its change in depth?
    Answer: 8 × (−25) = −200 feet.
  4. The temperature dropped 21 degrees over 7 hours. What was the average change per hour?
    Answer: −21 ÷ 7 = −3 degrees per hour.
  5. Evaluate: −20 ÷ 5 + 3 × (−4)
    Answer: −4 + (−12) = −16.
  6. A lemonade stand has weekly results of +$15, −$6, +$21, −$3 and +$13. Find the total and the average weekly profit.
    Answer: Total $40; average $8 per week.
