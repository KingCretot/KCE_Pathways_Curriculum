Unit 1: Stories That Shape Us (Narrative) · Week 3 · ELA · Teacher Plan

Theme in Story and Poem

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Tell the difference between a topic and a theme, and write a theme as a complete statement.
  • Analyze how an extended metaphor develops theme in Langston Hughes's "Mother to Son."
  • Explain why a writer might use dialect, and respect it as an authentic voice.
  • Compare themes across a poem and a story.
  • Use the roots struct, port, rupt and ject, and write a theme paragraph supported by evidence.

Standards

  • ELA.6.R.1.2: Analyze the development of stated or implied theme(s) throughout a literary text.
  • ELA.6.R.3.1: Explain how figurative language contributes to tone and meaning in text(s).
  • ELA.K12.EE.1.1: Cite evidence to explain and justify reasoning.
  • ELA.6.V.1.2: Apply knowledge of Greek and Latin roots and affixes to determine the meanings of words.
  • ELA.6.C.3.1: Follow the rules of standard English grammar, punctuation, capitalization and spelling.
  • ELA.K12.EE.2.1: Read and comprehend grade-level complex texts proficiently.

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • The poem printed for annotating
  • Index cards and a staircase (or cards on the floor)
  • Optional: a recording of Langston Hughes reading his work (the Library of Congress and Academy of American Poets host recordings)
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • theme: The message about life or human nature that a text develops. It is written as a complete sentence.
  • topic: What a text is about, in one or two words, such as courage or family.
  • metaphor: A comparison that says one thing is another, without like or as: Life is a staircase.
  • extended metaphor: A metaphor that continues through many lines or a whole text.
  • imagery: Words that create a picture in the reader's mind through the senses.
  • dialect: A form of a language spoken by a particular group or region, with its own words, grammar and pronunciation.
  • speaker: The voice that is talking in a poem. The speaker is not always the poet.
  • Harlem Renaissance: A flowering of Black art, music and literature centered in Harlem, New York, in the 1920s and 1930s.
  • struct / port: Latin roots meaning build (structure, construct) and carry (transport, portable).
  • rupt / ject: Latin roots meaning break (erupt, interrupt) and throw (project, reject).

Lesson flow

6 minReconnect
Run the Reconnect questions. Use Mateo's change to preview how character change points to theme.
5 minHook
Discuss The Tortoise and the Hare. Contrast a stated moral with an implied theme.
8 minTopic versus theme
Sort the examples. The student writes a theme for Moving Day and you test it: does it name a character? Is it an order?
14 minRead the poem
Student reads aloud, then you read aloud. Annotate every image of hardship in one color and every action word (climbin', reachin', turnin') in another.
10 minExtended metaphor
Use the two-staircase visual. Ask what a crystal stair would mean in a real life and who in 1922 might have had one.
9 minDialect and voice
Discuss why Hughes chose dialect. Practice code-switching by rewriting one line in Standard English, while stressing that dialect is not an error.
8 minRoots and assign
Teach the four roots with prefixes. Assign the staircase activity, the theme paragraph and the Exit Check.

Key questions to ask

  • What does the mother do on the stairs, and what does that show?
  • Why does she repeat the crystal stair line at the end?
  • Who in 1922 might have had a crystal stair, and who would not?
  • Why does it matter that a mother is giving this advice to her son?
  • Is your theme a sentence about life, or a summary of the poem?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Writing a one-word topic or a command (Never give up) as a theme.
  • Treating dialect as incorrect English rather than a rule-governed variety.
  • Assuming the speaker is the poet himself.
  • Summarizing the poem instead of explaining how images develop the theme.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Provide the theme sentence frame: In this text, the author shows that people who... can/will...
  • Give a two-column chart: image from the poem | what it might stand for.
  • Read the poem aloud together line by line.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Read Hughes's "The Negro Speaks of Rivers" (1921, public domain) and compare how both poems connect one person's life to a longer history.
  • Ask the student to write their own short extended-metaphor poem comparing life to a journey (a road, a river, a game).

Background for the teacher

"Mother to Son" was first published in The Crisis, the NAACP's magazine edited by W. E. B. Du Bois, in December 1922, and was collected in Hughes's first book, The Weary Blues (1926). Works published in the United States in 1930 or earlier are in the public domain as of 2026, so the full text can appear in the lesson. Hughes was born in Joplin, Missouri, in 1901 and died in New York in 1967. The poem's vernacular reflects Southern African American speech; linguists recognize African American English as a rule-governed dialect.

How the lesson runs

During the live lesson, the student shares their screen and answers the Reconnect questions and the Learn checks live while you teach. The Hands-On activity, Think Deeper and Exit Check are completed after class.

Scoring

The interactive lesson scores itself: full points on the first try, half points on the second try, then the answer and explanation appear. Mastery is 85. Written responses arrive in your results email with the score; score them with the rubric below before the student moves on.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. In Moving Day, Part 1 is told with I and my. What point of view is that?
    Answer: first person
    The narrator, Priya, is a character using I.
  2. Name the five STEAL methods of characterization.
    Answer: speech, thoughts, effect on others, actions, looks
    Speech, Thoughts, Effect on others, Actions, Looks.
  3. How does Mateo change from the beginning to the end of The Cast Net?
    Answer: He goes from doubting himself and wanting to quit to feeling confident and ready to keep practicing.
    A character's change often points straight to the theme.
  4. Using the root aud, what does audible mean?
    Answer: able to be heard
    Aud means hear; -ible means able to be.
  5. Fix the pronoun shift: I practiced every day because you have to work hard to improve.
    Answer: I practiced every day because I had to work hard to improve.
    Keep the same point of view: I throughout.
  6. On a rubric, which trait checks spelling, capitals and punctuation?
    Answer: conventions
    Conventions. Today's paragraph is scored with the same rubric.

Learn 1: Topic Versus Theme

  1. "Kindness" is a topic. Is "Kindness" alone a theme? Type yes or no.
    Answer: no
    A single word is a topic. A theme is a full sentence about life.
  2. Is this a strong theme statement? Dominic helps Priya carry boxes. Explain, and rewrite it as a real theme.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Not a theme: it is plot summary naming characters; Rewrites as a universal statement
    Model answer: No. It just tells what happens and names characters. A real theme would be: Small acts of kindness can help someone feel at home in a new place.
  3. Write a theme statement for Moving Day. Make it a complete, universal sentence that does not name Priya or Dominic.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Complete sentence; Universal (no character names); Supported by the story (kindness, empathy, starting over)
    Model answer: People who remember how it felt to be left out are often the ones who reach out to someone new.

Learn 2: Read: Mother to Son

  1. Who is the speaker of the poem, and who is she talking to?
    Answer: A mother speaking to her son
    The title and the first line tell us: a mother speaking to her son.
  2. The speaker repeats one line at the beginning and the end. What does she say life has not been for her?
    Answer: a crystal stair
    "Life for me ain't been no crystal stair" opens and closes the poem.
  3. Name two different problems on her staircase.
    Answer: tacks, splinters, boards torn up, no carpet, places with no light
    Hughes lists tacks, splinters, boards torn up, bare places with no carpet, and climbing in the dark.
  4. What three things does she tell her son not to do?
    Answer: Don't turn back, don't sit down on the steps, don't fall
    Her three warnings are all about not stopping.

Learn 3: The Extended Metaphor

  1. In the metaphor, what do the landings she reaches probably stand for?
    Answer: Goals or resting points: times of progress or relief in life
    A landing is a flat place to rest after climbing; in life it is a point of progress.
  2. What might "going in the dark where there ain't been no light" stand for in someone's life?
    Answer: Times without hope or help, when you cannot see what comes next
    Darkness often stands for fear, hopelessness or the unknown.
  3. Write a theme statement for "Mother to Son" as a complete, universal sentence.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Complete, universal sentence; About perseverance through hardship; Does not just say never give up
    Model answer: Even when life is hard and unfair, a person who keeps moving forward can reach a better place and inspire others to do the same.

Learn 4: Dialect and Voice

  1. Rewrite the line in Standard English: Life for me ain't been no crystal stair.
    Answer: Life for me has not been a crystal stair.
    The double negative ain't been no becomes has not been a in Standard English.
  2. Why did Hughes choose to write the poem in dialect instead of Standard English? Explain what the choice adds.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Authentic voice of a real mother; Presents it as a deliberate artistic choice; Honors real speech as rule-governed and valuable
    Model answer: Hughes made a deliberate choice to let the mother speak in her own voice, the way real people in his community spoke. It makes her sound real and strong, and it shows that her way of speaking is valuable and worth putting in a poem.

Learn 5: Roots: Building and Breaking

  1. Using the parts, what does interrupt mean?
    Answer: to break into the middle of (a conversation or activity)
    inter (between) + rupt (break).
  2. Complete each sentence with a root word.
    Answer: erupt / transport / construct / reject
    erupt (break out), transport (carry across), construct (build together), reject (throw back).

Hands-On

  1. List each image and the real-life hardship you connected it to. Which connection felt strongest and why? What could each landing mean?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Lists all five images; Each image connected to a reasonable real-life hardship; Explains the strongest connection with a reason
    Model answer: Tacks could be hurtful words, splinters could be small daily problems, boards torn up could be losing a job, no carpet could be having no comfort or money, and the dark could be not knowing what comes next. The halfway landing could be a moment of rest or a small success, and the top landing could be reaching a goal. The dark felt strongest to me because not knowing what is ahead is scary, but she climbed anyway.
  2. Who heard you recite the poem? What did reading it aloud help you notice that silent reading did not?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Completes every step of the activity; Includes specific details, numbers or observations from the activity; Explains thinking in complete sentences

Think Deeper

  1. Write a paragraph that answers: What is the theme of "Mother to Son," and how does Hughes develop it? Begin with a theme statement. Support it with at least two exact quotations from the poem, and explain how each one develops the theme.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Ideas: a clear, universal theme statement; Evidence: two or more exact quotations, each explained; Organization: topic sentence, transitions, closing sentence; Conventions: Standard English, correct quotation marks
    Model answer: In "Mother to Son," Langston Hughes shows that even when life is hard and unfair, a person who keeps climbing can reach a better place. The mother admits her life has had "tacks," "splinters" and "boards torn up," images that stand for the pain and unfairness she has faced. Still, she says, "I'se been a-climbin' on," which shows she never stopped moving forward. Because she has survived, she can warn her son, "don't you turn back." Hughes uses the staircase to show that perseverance is a gift one generation can pass to the next.
  2. Compare the theme of "Mother to Son" with the theme of The Cast Net. How are the messages alike, and how are they different?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: States a theme for each text; Explains a similarity (perseverance); Explains a difference (lifelong hardship and unfairness versus learning a skill; advice from an elder in both); Uses evidence from both texts
    Model answer: Both texts are about perseverance, and in both an elder teaches a young person not to quit: Abuelo says "Again," and the mother says "don't you turn back." The difference is the size of the struggle. Mateo struggles to learn one skill on one morning, while the mother describes a whole life of hardship and unfairness. The Cast Net shows that patience leads to success, while "Mother to Son" shows that you must keep going even when success is not guaranteed.

Exit Check

  1. Is "courage" a topic or a theme? Explain how you know.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Topic; A single word, not a complete statement about life
    Model answer: It is a topic, because it is only one word. A theme would be a complete sentence about life, like: Courage means acting even when you are afraid.
  2. Turn the topic honesty into a universal theme statement.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Complete sentence about life; Universal, not a command
    Model answer: Telling the truth can be hard at first, but it builds trust that lasts.
  3. A metaphor that continues through a whole poem is called an ___ metaphor.
    Answer: extended
    An extended metaphor carries one comparison through the text.
  4. In "Mother to Son," what is life compared to?
    Answer: a staircase
    The whole poem compares life to climbing a staircase.
  5. What does the root port mean?
    Answer: carry
    Port means carry: transport, portable.
  6. Using roots, what is a projector ?
    Answer: A machine that throws an image forward onto a screen
    pro (forward) + ject (throw): it throws light and pictures forward.
  7. Which 1920s movement in Harlem, New York, was Langston Hughes a leader of?
    Answer: the Harlem Renaissance
    The Harlem Renaissance celebrated Black art, music and literature.

Answer key: printable page

  1. What is the difference between a topic and a theme? Give one example of each.
    Answer: A topic is one or two words (perseverance); a theme is a complete statement about life (People who keep going through hard times can reach a better place).
  2. Explain the extended metaphor in "Mother to Son." What is compared to what?
    Answer: Life is compared to a staircase; the hard parts of the stair (tacks, splinters, darkness) stand for hardships, and climbing stands for perseverance.
  3. Choose one image (tacks, splinters, boards torn up or going in the dark). What might it stand for in a real person's life?
    Answer: Answers vary; must connect the image to a reasonable hardship (e.g., darkness = times without hope).
  4. Why does the mother tell her son not to turn back or sit down?
    Answer: She wants him to keep going through hardship as she has; quitting would mean never reaching a better place.
  5. Rewrite in Standard English: Don't you set down on the steps 'cause you finds it's kinder hard.
    Answer: Do not sit down on the steps because you find it is kind of hard.
  6. Write the meaning of each root and a word that uses it: struct ____ port ____ rupt ____ ject ____
    Answer: build (structure); carry (portable); break (erupt); throw (project)
  7. Write a theme statement that fits BOTH The Cast Net and "Mother to Son."
    Answer: For example: People who keep going after failure or hardship can succeed and pass their strength on to others.
