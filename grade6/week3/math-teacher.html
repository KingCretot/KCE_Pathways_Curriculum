Unit 1: Number Power · Week 3 · Math · Teacher Plan

Multiplying Decimals

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Model decimal multiplication on a hundredths grid and explain why 0.3 × 0.4 = 0.12.
  • Multiply multi-digit decimals with the standard algorithm and place the decimal point correctly.
  • Predict whether a product will be greater or less than a factor.
  • Multiply by powers of ten (10, 100, 1,000, 0.1, 0.01) by reasoning about place value.
  • Calculate earnings from an hourly wage and round money to the nearest cent.

Standards

  • MA.6.NSO.2.1: Multiply and divide positive multi-digit numbers with decimals to the thousandths, including using a standard algorithm with procedural fluency.
  • MA.5.NSO.2.4 and MA.5.NSO.2.5 (review): explore multiplying decimals with models; multiply by powers of ten.
  • MA.K12.MTR.2.1: Demonstrate understanding by representing problems in multiple ways (grid models and the algorithm).
  • MA.K12.MTR.6.1: Assess the reasonableness of solutions.
  • Financial literacy: earning income, hourly wages and gross pay; unit pricing (Jump$tart Earning Income).

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • Hundredths grid paper or a printed 10 by 10 grid
  • Colored pencils
  • A kitchen scale and three pieces of produce
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • factor: A number being multiplied. In 2.4 × 1.35, both numbers are factors.
  • product: The answer to a multiplication problem.
  • decimal places: The number of digits to the right of the decimal point. 1.35 has two decimal places.
  • power of ten: A number such as 10, 100, 1,000 or 0.1 made by multiplying or dividing 1 by 10 again and again.
  • hourly wage: The amount of money a worker earns for each hour of work.
  • gross pay: Total earnings before anything, such as taxes, is taken out.
  • unit price: The cost for one unit, such as one pound or one ounce.

Lesson flow

6 minReconnect
Run the Week 2 Reconnect questions.
5 minHook
Read Kwame's paycheck situation. Ask the student to estimate his Saturday pay before calculating (about 16 × 6 = 96).
12 minGrid model
Shade 0.3 × 0.4 on a hundredths grid together. Then the student shades 0.6 × 0.7. Discuss why the product is smaller than both factors.
12 minAlgorithm
Model 2.4 × 1.35. Stress estimating to check the decimal point. The student works 6.25 × 3.2 and explains each step.
10 minHourly wage
Calculate Kwame's pay for both days. Practice rounding money to the nearest cent.
10 minPowers of ten
Use a place-value chart to show digits moving. Connect to converting meters to centimeters, which returns in Science Week 3.
5 minAssign
Assign the Price by the Pound activity, Think Deeper and the Exit Check.

Key questions to ask

  • Before you calculate, will the product be bigger or smaller than this factor? How do you know?
  • Why don't we line up the decimal points when we multiply?
  • How does your estimate tell you where the decimal point goes?
  • Why do we round money to two decimal places?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Lining up decimal points as in addition.
  • Counting decimal places in only one factor.
  • Believing multiplication always makes numbers bigger.
  • Dropping placeholder zeros needed in products like 0.08.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Use hundredths grids for every tenths × tenths problem.
  • Have the student write the estimate first in a box beside each problem.
  • Provide a place-value chart for powers of ten.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Kwame's employer withholds about 7.65 percent for Social Security and Medicare. Estimate his take-home pay for 10.75 hours (preview of percents in Unit 3).
  • Find the cost of 0.375 pounds of a $14.80-per-pound spice and round to the nearest cent ($5.55).

Background for the teacher

The federal minimum wage has been $7.25 an hour since 2009. Florida's minimum wage reached $15.00 an hour on September 30, 2026, and is adjusted for inflation after that, so every wage in this lesson is $15.00 or more. Kwame's $15.75 is a realistic first-job wage. The paycheck puzzle shows why employers round once, at the end.

How the lesson runs

During the live lesson, the student shares their screen and answers the Reconnect questions and the Learn checks live while you teach. The Hands-On activity, Think Deeper and Exit Check are completed after class.

Scoring

The interactive lesson scores itself: full points on the first try, half points on the second try, then the answer and explanation appear. Mastery is 85. Written responses arrive in your Formspree email with the score; score them with the rubric below before the student moves on.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. 14.6 + 3.85 = ?
    Answer: 18.45
    14.60 + 3.85 = 18.45.
  2. 20 − 6.37 = ?
    Answer: 13.63
    20.00 − 6.37 = 13.63.
  3. Order from least to greatest.
    Answer: 0.054 → 0.405 → 0.45 → 0.5
    Write each to thousandths: 0.054, 0.405, 0.450, 0.500.
  4. In 3.142, which digit is in the thousandths place?
    Answer: 2
    Tenths 1, hundredths 4, thousandths 2.
  5. A receipt shows $7.89 and $12.40 plus $1.22 tax. You pay with $30. What is your change?
    Answer: $8.49
    7.89 + 12.40 + 1.22 = $21.51. 30.00 − 21.51 = $8.49.
  6. From Week 1: 1,764 ÷ 42 = ?
    Answer: 42
    42 × 42 = 1,764. Long division keeps coming back, including next week with decimals.
  7. Which is greater, 0.6 or 0.58? Type the greater number.
    Answer: 0.6
    0.6 = 0.60, and 60 hundredths is more than 58 hundredths (6 tenths is more than 5 tenths).

Learn 1: Seeing Decimal Multiplication

  1. Use the grid idea: 0.6 × 0.7 = ?
    Answer: 0.42
    6 tenths × 7 tenths = 42 hundredths = 0.42.
  2. 0.2 × 0.5 = ?
    Answer: 0.1
    2 tenths × 5 tenths = 10 hundredths = 0.10 = 0.1.
  3. Without calculating, will 48 × 0.75 be more or less than 48? Explain using what multiplying by a number less than 1 means.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Says less; Explains that 0.75 is less than 1, so the product is only part of 48
    Model answer: Less, because multiplying by 0.75 means taking three fourths of 48, which is only part of it (36).
  4. Will 48 × 1.5 be greater than or less than 48?
    Answer: greater
    1.5 is greater than 1, so the product is more than 48 (it is 72).

Learn 2: The Standard Algorithm

  1. 3.6 × 2.5 = ?
    Answer: 9
    36 × 25 = 900, with 2 decimal places: 9.00 = 9.
  2. 0.08 × 4.5 = ?
    Answer: 0.36
    8 × 45 = 360, with 3 decimal places: 0.360 = 0.36.
  3. 12.5 × 0.04 = ?
    Answer: 0.5
    125 × 4 = 500, with 3 decimal places: 0.500 = 0.5.
  4. 6.25 × 3.2 = ?
    Answer: 20
    625 × 32 = 20,000, with 3 decimal places: 20.000 = 20.
  5. 1.8 × 2.05 = ?
    Answer: 3.69
    18 × 205 = 3,690, with 3 decimal places: 3.690 = 3.69.

Learn 3: Earning an Hourly Wage

  1. How many hours did Kwame work in total?
    Answer: 10.75 hours
    6.5 + 4.25 = 10.75 hours.
  2. What was his pay for Sunday (4.25 hours)? Round to the nearest cent.
    Answer: $66.94
    15.75 × 4.25 = 66.9375, which rounds to $66.94.
  3. Kwame adds his rounded daily amounts. What total does he get?
    Answer: $169.32
    $102.38 + $66.94 = $169.32.
  4. Kwame's paycheck says $169.31, one cent less than his total. The company multiplied his wage by his total hours: 15.75 × 10.75. Calculate it, then explain who is right and why the two answers differ.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Calculates 15.75 × 10.75 = 169.3125, which rounds to $169.31; Explains that rounding each day separately added extra fractions of a cent; Concludes the paycheck is correct (or that both methods are reasonable but the company rounds once)
    Model answer: 15.75 × 10.75 = 169.3125, which rounds to $169.31. When I rounded each day first, Saturday went up 0.5 cent and Sunday went up 0.25 cent, and those small bits added up to an extra cent. The company rounded only once at the end, so the paycheck is correct.
  5. Kwame fills the family car with 12.6 gallons of gas at $3.459 per gallon. What does it cost, to the nearest cent?
    Answer: $43.58
    3.459 × 12.6 = 43.5834, which rounds to $43.58. Gas prices go to the thousandths of a dollar.
  6. Ground turkey costs $4.80 per pound. A package weighs 2.375 pounds. What does it cost?
    Answer: $11.40
    4.80 × 2.375 = 11.4000, so $11.40. Deli and meat scales often weigh to the thousandths of a pound.
  7. A store sells cheese at $9.85 per pound. A piece weighs 3.25 pounds. What does it cost, rounded to the nearest cent?
    Answer: $32.01
    9.85 × 3.25 = 32.0125, which rounds to $32.01.

Learn 4: Multiplying by Powers of Ten

  1. A hardware store sells screws for $0.035 each. What do 1,000 screws cost?
    Answer: $35
    0.035 × 1,000: each digit moves three places left, so $35.00.
  2. A coupon takes 0.1 of the bill off a $34.50 order. How much money does the coupon save?
    Answer: $3.45
    34.50 × 0.1: each digit moves one place right, so $3.45.
  3. A shelf is 1.85 meters long. How many centimeters is that? (1 m = 100 cm)
    Answer: 185 cm
    1.85 × 100 = 185 cm.
  4. A garden center sells mulch for $3.99 a bag. What do 100 bags cost?
    Answer: $399
    3.99 × 100 = $399.

Hands-On

    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Completes every step of the activity; Includes specific details, numbers or observations from the activity; Explains thinking in complete sentences

Think Deeper

  1. Maya says 0.4 × 0.2 = 0.8. What is the correct product?
    Answer: 0.08
    4 × 2 = 8, with 2 decimal places: 0.08.
  2. Explain Maya's mistake, and use a hundredths grid or an estimate to prove your answer.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Names the mistake: she counted only one decimal place, or added instead of multiplying; Explains that tenths × tenths = hundredths; Uses a grid, estimate or reasoning (0.4 of 0.2 must be less than 0.2)
    Model answer: Maya only gave her answer one decimal place, but tenths times tenths makes hundredths, so the product needs two places: 0.08. Also, 0.4 × 0.2 means four tenths of 0.2, which must be less than 0.2, so 0.8 is impossible.
  3. Your friend says, "Multiplying always makes a number bigger." Write a response that explains when this is true and when it is false, with an example of each and a real-life situation.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Explains that factors greater than 1 increase the product; Explains that factors between 0 and 1 decrease it; Gives a correct numeric example of each; Connects to a real situation such as discounts or half portions
    Model answer: It is only true when you multiply by a number greater than 1, like 20 × 1.5 = 30. When you multiply by a number between 0 and 1, you take only part, so the product shrinks: 20 × 0.5 = 10. In real life, if a $20 shirt is on sale for 0.75 of its price, you pay $15, which is less.

Exit Check

  1. 4.7 × 0.6 = ?
    Answer: 2.82
    47 × 6 = 282, with 2 decimal places: 2.82.
  2. 15.25 × 2.4 = ?
    Answer: 36.6
    1,525 × 24 = 36,600, with 3 decimal places: 36.600 = 36.6.
  3. 0.35 × 0.2 = ?
    Answer: 0.07
    35 × 2 = 70, with 3 decimal places: 0.070 = 0.07.
  4. 2.08 × 1,000 = ?
    Answer: 2,080
    Each digit moves three places left: 2,080.
  5. A worker earns $15.50 an hour and works 12.4 hours. What is her gross pay?
    Answer: $192.20
    15.50 × 12.4 = $192.20.
  6. Grapes cost $2.49 per pound. Ade buys 2.5 pounds. What does he pay, rounded to the nearest cent?
    Answer: $6.23
    2.49 × 2.5 = 6.225, which rounds to $6.23.
  7. A $2.125-per-pound bag of onions weighs 3.2 pounds. What does it cost?
    Answer: $6.80
    2.125 × 3.2 = 6.8000, so $6.80.

    Answer: 8.84

    Answer: 0.364
  4. 14.5 × 0.06
    Answer: 0.87

    Answer: 930; 420; 5.6

    Answer: 15.20 × 15.5 = $235.60
