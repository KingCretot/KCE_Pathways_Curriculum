Grade 6 · Unit 3 · Free to read or borrow

Unit 3 Book List

Every book here can be read free. The first group is in the public domain and free to read online. The second group is free to borrow with a public library card: look for it in your library's catalog or its free e-book apps, or borrow a digital copy through Open Library when one is available. Availability varies by library.

Free to read online (public domain)

BookSubjectWhy read it
Harlem Shadows
Claude McKay (1922)		ELAMcKay's collection, including "The Tired Worker" (Week 13). Hughes's The Weary Blues, with the poems from Weeks 13 and 14, is on the Unit 1 list. Preview first: a few poems deal with racial violence.
The Book of American Negro Poetry
edited by James Weldon Johnson (1922)		ELAThe anthology that holds McKay's "The Tired Worker," Fauset's "Oriflamme" and Georgia Douglas Johnson's "The Heart of a Woman." Johnson's preface is a strong read for parents. Preview first: a few poems deal with violence.
The Complete Poems of Paul Laurence Dunbar
Paul Laurence Dunbar (1913)		ELAHome of "Sympathy" (Week 11) and "We Wear the Mask" (the Week 15 checkpoint). Many poems are in dialect, which Dunbar wrote alongside standard English. Also on the Unit 1 list.
A Child's Garden of Verses
Robert Louis Stevenson (1885)		ELA"From a Railway Carriage" (Week 12) and dozens of short poems with strong rhythm, good for recitation practice.
Leaves of Grass
Walt Whitman (1891–92 edition)		ELA (parents and strong readers)Whitman's life work, including "I Hear America Singing" (Week 14). Long; dip in rather than read straight through.
The Children of the Night
Edwin Arlington Robinson (1897)		ELARobinson's early poems, including the villanelle "The House on the Hill" (Week 14) and the famous "Richard Cory."
The Chinese Classics: Confucian Analects
translated by James Legge (1861)		Social StudiesThe sayings of Confucius from Week 13, in the translation the lesson quotes. Short passages make good discussion starters.
The Periplus of the Erythraean Sea
translated by Wilfred H. Schoff (1912)		Social Studies (parents and strong readers)The first-century merchant's guide from Week 14, with Adulis, Aksum, India and the monsoon trade. Free on Wikisource.

Free to borrow with a library card

BookSubjectWhy read it
Langston Hughes (Poetry for Young People)
edited by David Roessel and Arnold Rampersad		ELAAn illustrated selection of Hughes's poems with short notes, a natural next step after Week 13.
Love to Langston
Tony Medina		ELAA biography of Langston Hughes told in poems, for readers who want to know the person behind the poetry.
Harlem Stomp! A Cultural History of the Harlem Renaissance
Laban Carrick Hill		ELA and Social StudiesA richly illustrated history of the writers, musicians and artists of the Harlem Renaissance.
Microbes: Discover an Unseen World
Christine Burillo-Kirch		ScienceExplanations and 25 hands-on projects about bacteria, archaea, protists and fungi, matching Weeks 14 and 15.
Tiny Creatures: The World of Microbes
Nicola Davies		ScienceAn illustrated introduction to microorganisms and what they do, matching Week 14. A short picture book, good to share with younger siblings.
The Silk Road: Explore the World's Most Famous Trade Route
Kathy Ceceri		Social StudiesHands-on projects and history of the Silk Roads, good support for the Week 15 travel journal.
Search your library: ancient Mesopotamia, the Indus Valley and ancient China
Many authors		Social StudiesChildren's nonfiction on these topics varies by library; look for books that use museum photographs and recent archaeology.

Parents: preview any book first. Links open a search on Project Gutenberg or Open Library, so you can choose the edition.
