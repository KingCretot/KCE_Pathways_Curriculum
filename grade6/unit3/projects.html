Grade 6 · Unit 3 · Projects due in Week 15

Unit 3 Projects and Rubrics

Each project is scored with five traits worth up to 4 points each, for a total of 20. Multiply by 5 for a score out of 100. Mastery is 85 (17 of 20). A project below mastery is returned with feedback and revised once. Read the rubric before you start.

ELA: Poetry Analysis Project

Task: Choose one poem from Unit 3 (Weeks 11 to 14), or two poems in conversation, and (1) write an analysis of 400 to 600 words explaining how the poet creates meaning, and (2) recite the poem from memory and record it. Planning in Week 14 if you like; writing, memorizing and recording in Week 15.

Must include: a title; an introduction that names the poem and poet and ends with a thesis naming at least two techniques; three body paragraphs in T-E-E-C form, each with a short, accurate quotation (line breaks shown with a slash); analysis of the poem's form and at least two devices, explaining their effect; a conclusion; an MLA citation of the poem. Recitation: the whole poem, or at least 14 lines of a longer one, from memory, with clear voice and pauses at punctuation.

Turn in: the analysis in the Week 15 lesson (Hands-On), the recording, and your reflection.

Science: Field Guide Project

Task: Create a field guide to at least 8 living things near your home: at least 5 you observed in person, at least 1 fungus, and 1 microorganism page. Fieldwork and drafting in Week 15.

Must include: a cover and a table of contents; one page per organism with a labeled drawing or your own photo, the common name, the scientific name written correctly, classification as far as you can find it (domain to genus), at least two observable identifying traits, where and when you saw it, one fact about how it lives, and whether it is native to Florida or invasive; a microorganism page (for example, the mold from your bread test or Karenia brevis); a tested dichotomous key to all of your organisms; a sources page.

Safety: observe with an adult; look but don't touch unknown plants and animals; no collecting in state parks; wash hands after fieldwork.

Turn in: the field guide (paper, photos of the pages, or a slide file), your Week 15 field notes and your key-test notes.

Social Studies: Ancient Asia Project (Silk Roads Travel Journal)

Task: Invent a traveler around 100 CE (a Sogdian merchant's apprentice, a Han envoy, a Buddhist monk or an Aksumite sailor) and write a historically accurate travel journal of a journey on the land Silk Roads, the Indian Ocean sea roads, or both. Planned and written in Week 15.

Must include: at least five dated journal entries of 100 to 200 words; a hand-drawn route map with at least six real, labeled stops and the deserts, mountains, rivers and seas crossed; real goods, foods, animals, money, dangers and beliefs of the time; at least two civilizations from Unit 3 visited or discussed (for example, the ruins of Babylon or a conversation about Confucius); at least one primary source quoted or described (such as the Periplus or the Analects); no anachronisms; a Historian's Note that separates real facts from invented details, with at least three sources.

Turn in: the journal and map (or photos), and the Historian's Note.
