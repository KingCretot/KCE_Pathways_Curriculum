Unit 5: Empires and Global Trade · Week 23 · World and American History · Teacher Plan

Songhai, Timbuktu and Centers of Learning

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Explain how the Songhai Empire rose under Sunni Ali and Askia Muhammad.
  • Describe how Askia Muhammad organized and governed the empire.
  • Explain what made Timbuktu a world center of learning, using a primary source.
  • Analyze the causes and effects of Songhai's fall at the Battle of Tondibi in 1591.
  • Evaluate why the Timbuktu manuscripts matter today and how people have protected them.

Standards

  • SS.6.G.2.1: Explain how major physical characteristics, natural resources, climate, and absolute and relative locations have influenced settlement, interactions, and the economies of ancient civilizations of the world.
  • SS.6.E.3.4: Describe the relationship among civilizations that engage in trade, including the benefits and drawbacks of voluntary trade.
  • SS.6.W.1.3: Interpret primary and secondary sources.
  • SS.6.W.1.5: Describe the roles of historians and recognize varying historical interpretations (historiography).
  • SS.6.W.1.6: Describe how history transmits culture and heritage and provides models of human character.

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • Paper, a paper bag, cold tea (optional), pencil, pen, ruler, scissors, yarn and blunt needle or stapler
  • 20 index cards, a shoebox, a timer
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • Songhai: A West African people of the middle Niger, and the empire they built, with its capital at Gao (about 1460s to 1591).
  • Askia: The title used by the dynasty of Songhai rulers founded by Askia Muhammad in 1493.
  • scholar: A person who has studied deeply and teaches or writes about a subject.
  • manuscript: A book or document written by hand.
  • province: A region of an empire governed by an official appointed by the ruler.
  • arquebus: An early gun, heavy and slow to load, used in the 1500s.

Key questions to ask

  • Who wrote this source, and what did they want readers to believe?
  • What made learning valuable in Timbuktu?
  • What caused Songhai to fall, and what survived?
  • How do we know what we know about West Africa?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Timbuktu was a mythical, faraway place (it was a real, connected trading and learning city).
  • Africa had no written history.
  • Timbuktu had one university building (learning happened in many mosques and scholars' homes).
  • Songhai fell because its people were less advanced (Morocco had new gun technology and Songhai faced internal disputes).

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Provide a ruler timeline card: Sunni Ali, Askia Muhammad, Tondibi, Ahmad Baba.
  • Read the 1600 quotation aloud together, word by word.
  • Offer sentence starters for source analysis: "This source shows...", "I should be careful because..."

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Research how Timbuktu's manuscripts are being preserved and digitized today, and present one manuscript topic (astronomy, medicine or law).
  • Compare Songhai's provinces with Persia's satrapies (Week 16) in a short paragraph.

Background for the teacher

Dates: Sunni Ali about 1464 to 1492 (Timbuktu taken 1468; the Djenné siege's seven-year length is traditional); Askia Muhammad 1493 to 1528 (hajj 1496 to 1497; deposed by his son); Battle of Tondibi 1591 (Moroccan force often given as about 4,000, with firearms, under Judar Pasha; the stampede detail comes from the Timbuktu chronicles). Ahmad Baba (1556 to 1627) was deported in 1593 or 1594, returned in 1608, and wrote Mi'raj al-Su'ud in 1615. The 1600 quotation is from John Pory's translation of Leo Africanus, A Geographical Historie of Africa (Hakluyt Society edition, 1896), checked against the scanned text; spelling kept. The 2012 to 2013 evacuation figures (about 350,000 to 377,000 manuscripts moved; about 4,000 burned) vary by source. Population figures for Timbuktu are estimates. Medieval West Africa is outside Florida's Grade 6 NGSSS world history range; the standards above cover the skills taught.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. What title did Mali's emperors hold?
    Answer: mansa
    Mansa Musa is the most famous.
  2. Which two goods were traded for each other across the Sahara and made Mali rich?
    Answer: gold and salt
    Gold came from the south; salt came from Saharan mines.
  3. Which river was Mali's highway for trade?
    Answer: the Niger River
    Songhai's cities sit on the same river.
  4. Mali was huge and far-flung. Based on what you know about empires (Persia, Rome), predict one reason a big empire like Mali might weaken after a strong ruler dies.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A reasonable cause; Connects to an earlier empire or to Mali
    Model answer: After a strong ruler dies, relatives may fight over the throne, and distant towns may stop paying tribute, as happened when Rome grew too big to defend.

Learn 1: The Rise of Songhai

  1. What was the capital city of the Songhai Empire?
    Answer: Gao
    It sits on the Niger, downstream from Timbuktu.
  2. Timbuktu's scholars wrote about Sunni Ali as a cruel tyrant, while Songhai oral tradition praises him. Why might the two sources disagree, and what would a historian do with them?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A reason for the disagreement; A historian's approach
    Model answer: The scholars had opposed Sunni Ali and suffered under him, so they had reason to describe him badly, while the oral tradition was kept by people who honored Songhai's rulers. A historian would compare both, look for facts they agree on, like his conquests, and keep each source's point of view in mind.

Learn 2: Askia Muhammad Organizes an Empire

  1. Who founded the Askia dynasty in 1493?
    Answer: Askia Muhammad (Muhammad Ture)
    He ruled until 1528.
  2. Explain how standard weights and measures would help both buyers and sellers in Songhai's markets. Connect your answer to the unit prices you study in math this week.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Explains how standard measures help both buyers and sellers; Connects to this week's unit-price math
    Model answer: If every market used the same measures, a buyer knew a measure of salt was the same everywhere, and a seller couldn't cheat with a smaller one. It's like unit prices in math: you can only compare the price per unit if everyone means the same unit.
  3. Askia Muhammad created a full-time army. Give one benefit and one risk of this choice for the empire.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A benefit; A risk
    Model answer: A benefit is that soldiers were always trained and ready, and farmers could stay home to grow food. A risk is the cost of paying them all year, and a strong army can help an ambitious general seize the throne, just as Askia Muhammad himself had done.

Learn 3: Timbuktu: A City of Scholars

  1. What is the name for an African language written in Arabic script?
    Answer: Ajami
    It shows that West Africans wrote in their own languages as well as Arabic.
  2. Reread Leo Africanus's description. What two pieces of evidence does it give that Timbuktu valued learning? What is one reason a historian should be careful with this source?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Two pieces of evidence taken from the quotation; One reason for caution about who wrote it, when, or how it reached English
    Model answer: First, the king paid many learned men to live and work there. Second, handwritten books sold for more money than any other goods. A historian should be careful because Leo was a visitor writing from memory years later, and his book, written in Italian, reached English through a Latin translation.
  3. Compare learning in Timbuktu with your own homeschool. Give one similarity and one difference.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A similarity; A difference
    Model answer: Like my homeschool, students in Timbuktu learned from a teacher they or their families chose and moved from basics to harder subjects. Unlike me, they had to copy every book by hand and sometimes traveled hundreds of miles to study with one scholar.

Learn 4: The Fall of Songhai and the Fate of the Books

  1. Songhai fell in 1591. Askia Muhammad took power in 1493. How many years passed between them?
    Answer: 98 years
    1591 − 1493 = 98.
  2. Explain two causes of Songhai's defeat at Tondibi and one effect on Timbuktu's scholars.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Two causes; An effect
    Model answer: Morocco wanted Songhai's gold and sent soldiers with arquebuses and cannons, which Songhai's spears and bows could not match, and the noise panicked Songhai's cattle. Afterward, the Moroccans arrested Timbuktu's scholars and took Ahmad Baba to Marrakesh, where he lost his library.
  3. Ahmad Baba argued in 1615 that skin color was no reason for slavery and that free Muslims could not lawfully be enslaved. Why was this an important argument in his time, and how does it connect to what you are learning about Africa's history?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Explains what was happening with slavery in his time; Connects his argument to African scholarship
    Model answer: In his time, the trade in enslaved Africans was growing across the Sahara and the Atlantic, and some people claimed dark skin was a reason to enslave. Ahmad Baba, a Black African scholar, used law and reason to reject that idea, which shows that Africans were writing serious arguments against injustice centuries ago.

Hands-On

  1. What did you copy into your manuscript, and what notes did you add in the margins? How long did it take, and what does that tell you about why manuscripts were valuable?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Describes the text and the margin notes; Time it took; Connects effort and rarity to value
    Model answer: I copied my grandmother's cornbread recipe and our equation-solving method from math, and in the margins I wrote tips like "use buttermilk" and "check by substituting." It took me about 40 minutes for eight small pages. Copying a whole law book by hand could take months, so each copy was rare and valuable.
  2. List the 8 manuscripts you saved and explain the rule you used to decide. What was hardest to leave behind?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Lists 8 choices; A clear rule; Reflects on a hard choice
    Model answer: I saved the only copy of the town's history, the medicine book, the scholar's law book, the astronomy tables, a family's letters, a book in Ajami, a map and the oldest Qur'an. My rule was to save items with only one copy first. Leaving the family letters was hardest, because they were one of a kind too, but the town history mattered to more people.

Think Deeper

  1. Some people once claimed that Africa south of the Sahara had no written history. Use at least three pieces of evidence from Weeks 22 and 23 to prove this claim false.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: At least three pieces of evidence; Clear argument structure
    Model answer: The claim is false. First, Timbuktu's families kept hundreds of thousands of handwritten manuscripts on law, medicine, astronomy and history, many still surviving. Second, West Africans wrote their own languages in Arabic script, called Ajami. Third, scholars like Ahmad Baba wrote books, including a 1615 argument against slavery based on skin color. Visitors like Leo Africanus also described a city where books sold for more than any other goods.
  2. Compare how Timbuktu's families protected manuscripts after 1591 with how librarians saved them in 2012 and 2013. What do the two rescues have in common, and what can communities today learn about protecting knowledge?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Describes both; A commonality; A lesson for today
    Model answer: After 1591, families hid manuscripts in chests, walls and underground, and in 2012 librarians packed them in rice sacks and trunks and moved them in secret to Bamako. In both cases ordinary people took risks and used secrecy to save knowledge. Communities today can learn to make copies, digitize important records and treat libraries as something everyone must protect.

Exit Check

  1. Which Songhai ruler divided the empire into provinces and standardized weights and measures?
    Answer: Askia Muhammad
    He ruled from 1493 to 1528.
  2. In what year did Morocco defeat Songhai at the Battle of Tondibi?
    Answer: 1591
    Moroccan guns overwhelmed Songhai's army.
  3. Which Timbuktu scholar was exiled to Marrakesh and later argued that skin color was no reason for slavery?
    Answer: Ahmad Baba
    The institute in Timbuktu bears his name.
  4. In one or two sentences, explain what made Timbuktu a center of learning.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: At least two features from the lesson that made Timbuktu a center of learning
    Model answer: Rulers paid scholars, students came from far away to learn in its mosques and homes, and families built libraries of handwritten books that sold for more than gold.

Answer key: printable page

  1. How did Sunni Ali build the Songhai Empire?
    Answer: He used war canoes and cavalry to conquer cities like Timbuktu (1468) and Djenné.
  2. Name three ways Askia Muhammad organized the empire.
    Answer: Provinces with governors; a full-time army; standard weights and measures; support for scholars and judges.
  3. Gloss these 1600 spellings: iudges, diuers, merchandize. What does the passage show about Timbuktu?
    Answer: judges, various, merchandise; learned men were paid by the king and books sold for more than any other goods.
  4. What subjects did students study in Timbuktu?
    Answer: Qur'an, Arabic, law, grammar, logic, rhetoric, history, astronomy, mathematics, medicine.
  5. Explain the causes and effects of the Battle of Tondibi.
    Answer: Morocco wanted gold and had guns; Songhai lost in 1591, the empire broke apart and scholars were exiled.
  6. Why is Ahmad Baba remembered?
    Answer: A great Timbuktu scholar, exiled to Marrakesh, who wrote that skin color was no reason for slavery.
