Grade 6 · Unit 6 · Free to read or borrow

Unit 6 Book List

Every book here can be read free. The first group is in the public domain and free to read online. The second group is free to borrow with a public library card: look for it in your library's catalog or its free e-book apps, or borrow a digital copy through Open Library when one is available. Availability varies by library.

Free to read online (public domain)

BookSubjectWhy read it
The Journal of Christopher Columbus
translated by Clements R. Markham (1893)		Social StudiesThe source of the Week 29 and Week 30 excerpts, preserved in Las Casas's abstract. Read short passages with a parent and ask what Columbus wanted his royal readers to believe.
The Memoirs of the Conquistador Bernal Diaz del Castillo
translated by John Ingram Lockhart (1844)		Social StudiesThe eyewitness account quoted in Week 27, describing Tenochtitlan. Old-fashioned English and scenes of war; best for parents to preview and read aloud in short sections.
Experiments in Plant Hybridization
Gregor Mendel, translated by William Bateson (1901)		ScienceMendel's pea-plant paper behind Weeks 26 and 27. Technical; read the opening pages together and look at his counts of tall and short plants.
On the Origin of Species
Charles Darwin (1859)		ScienceThe book that explained natural selection (Week 29). Written for adults; read the short introduction or the chapter on variation together.

Free to borrow with a library card

BookSubjectWhy read it
Morning Girl
Michael Dorris		Social StudiesA short novel told by a Taíno sister and brother in the Bahamas in 1492, ending with an excerpt from Columbus's journal (Week 29).
Encounter
Jane Yolen, illustrated by David Shannon		Social StudiesA picture book telling Columbus's arrival from a Taíno boy's point of view; good for comparing with the journal (Week 29).
Fort Mose
Glennette Tilley Turner		Social StudiesThe story of the first legally sanctioned free Black town in what is now the United States, near St. Augustine (Week 30 preview).
Gregor Mendel: The Friar Who Grew Peas
Cheryl Bardoe, illustrated by Jos. A. Smith		ScienceAn illustrated biography of Mendel and his pea experiments (Weeks 26 and 27).
One Beetle Too Many: The Extraordinary Adventures of Charles Darwin
Kathryn Lasky, illustrated by Matthew Trueman		ScienceDarwin's life and voyage on the Beagle for young readers (Week 29).
The Beak of the Finch
Jonathan Weiner		ScienceThe Pulitzer Prize-winning account of the Grants' finch studies (Week 29). Adult nonfiction for parents to read and summarize.

Parents: preview any book first. Links open a search on Project Gutenberg, the Internet Archive or Open Library, so you can choose the edition.
