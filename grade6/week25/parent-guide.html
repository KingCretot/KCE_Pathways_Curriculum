Week 25: Unit 5 Review, Checkpoints and Projects

This is the last week of Unit 5. In every subject your child reviews the unit, finishes a project (a literary response to The Call of the Wild, a field study of a local ecosystem and a trade route board game) and takes the Unit 5 checkpoint. Math reviews the whole unit by planning a co-op spring fair on a budget.

How scoring works: each lesson scores itself. Your child gets two tries per question: full points on the first try, half points on the second, then the answer and explanation appear. A score of 85 or higher means the lesson is mastered. Written answers go to Jonathan, who scores them with a rubric.

Checkpoint scoring: checkpoint questions give one try and do not show answers, so the checkpoint fairly measures what your child can do alone. Jonathan adds the points for written answers and shares the checkpoint score. Mastery is 85; below 85 means a review lesson, then a retake. Projects below 85 are returned with feedback and revised once.

Time this week: About 60 minutes of live lessons per subject, plus project time (about 3 hours per project across the week, including at least three short field visits for science) and one checkpoint per subject.

Gather this week: 4 sheets of paper labeled Station 1 to 4, index cards and a marker, a timer, a family member, paper and pencil, and optionally painter's tape (Math); 8 to 10 sheets of paper or sticky notes, markers, painter's tape, The Call of the Wild, your child's project draft or outline, and a family member as a gallery visitor (ELA); a shoebox or box lid, index cards, scissors and a marker, yarn or string in 3 colors, tape, your child's field notes, and a family member (Science); your child's trade route game board or pieces, index cards and a marker, a die, small household items or paper squares as goods and coins, and a family member (Social Studies).

Heads up: Checkpoints are taken after the lesson, independently, with one try per question and no answers shown until you review them together. Mastery is 85; below 85 means a review lesson and a retake. Your child may use the novel, but not notes, for Parts B and C of the ELA checkpoint. Field visits for the science project should be supervised: stay well back from water where alligators may live, don't touch or feed wildlife, and follow park rules. Keep tape off walkways, and keep small game pieces, the die and yarn away from young children (choking hazard) and pets.

ELA: Unit 5 Checkpoint and Literary Response Project

Your child reviews setting, conflict, character change, symbols, theme and craft across The Call of the Wild, writes a literary analysis essay with a creative response, and analyzes a new passage from Chapter VI on the checkpoint.

Ask at dinner: "Is that a claim about how the book works, or a summary of what happens?"

Try it together: Be the visitor on your child's evidence gallery walk and ask "So what?" at each quotation.

Signs it's clicking: Their commentary explains how a quotation proves a point, not just what it says. Signs they need help: They retell the plot. Ask, "Why did London write it that way?"

Math: Unit 5 Review and Checkpoint

Your child reviews exponents, the order of operations and simple interest, writes and simplifies expressions and cost formulas, solves one-step equations and works with inequalities, then plans a $300 co-op fair budget.

Ask at dinner: "Are you evaluating, simplifying, solving or comparing, and how can you tell?"

Try it together: Run the four-station relay together and write one card with a deliberate mistake for your child to find.

Signs it's clicking: They explain when an equation fits and when an inequality fits. Signs they need help: They forget to check solutions. Ask them to substitute their answer back in.

Life Science: Unit 5 Checkpoint and Ecosystem Study Project

Your child connects biotic and abiotic factors, energy flow, matter cycles and interactions, completes a field study of a real local ecosystem and builds a shoebox model to test how one change ripples through it.

Ask at dinner: "If this one piece disappeared, what else would change?"

Try it together: Join one field visit and help count organisms in the quadrat.

Signs it's clicking: They explain the difference between what happens to energy and what happens to carbon in one meal. Signs they need help: Their field notes say "some birds." Ask for counts, times and the weather.

World and American History: Unit 5 Checkpoint and Trade Route Project

Your child connects Ghana, Mali, Songhai, the Swahili city-states and Great Zimbabwe, compares the trans-Saharan and Indian Ocean trade networks, reviews how historians use written sources and archaeology, and builds and playtests a trade route board game.

Ask at dinner: "What moved along these routes besides goods?"

Try it together: Playtest your child's board game and tell them which rule confused you.

Signs it's clicking: They explain how archaeology disproved the Great Zimbabwe myth. Signs they need help: They mix up the order of the empires. Remind them: Ghana, then Mali, then Songhai.
