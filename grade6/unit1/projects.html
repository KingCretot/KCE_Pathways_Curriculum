Grade 6 · Unit 1 · Projects due in Week 5

Unit 1 Projects and Rubrics

Each project is scored with five traits worth up to 4 points each, for a total of 20. Multiply by 5 for a score out of 100. Mastery is 85 (17 of 20). A project below mastery is returned with feedback and revised once. Students should read the rubric before they start, as they learned in ELA Week 1.

ELA: Personal Narrative

Task: Write a true story about a moment that changed you, 400 to 800 words, told in first person. Started in Week 1 (story mountain and opening), drafted in Week 4, revised and published in Week 5.

Must include: a title; an opening that drops the reader into the scene; at least three lines of correctly punctuated dialogue; sensory details and strong verbs; a slowed-down climax; transitions; an ending that reflects on how you changed; the Week 5 reflection.

Turn in: the final story in the Week 5 lesson (Think Deeper), the reflection, and the Author's Chair feedback.

Science: Investigation Project

Task: Plan and carry out your own fair test at home, then report it like a scientist. Started with testable questions in Week 4; planned and run in Week 5.

Must include: a testable question; a hypothesis (If... then... because...); independent, dependent and three controlled variables; a numbered, repeatable procedure and materials list; a data table with units and three trials; averages; the correct graph with TAILS; a CER conclusion; two limits and a next question; a photo of the setup.

Safety: get a parent's permission for anything with heat, sharp tools, chemicals or the outdoors. Never taste materials.

Turn in: the Week 5 Hands-On records, plus the graph (photo or drawing) and the setup photo emailed to your teacher or uploaded where your teacher directs.

Social Studies: Museum Exhibit Project

Task: Become a curator. Build a home museum exhibit that answers one big question about Unit 1, then give a 3-minute tour. Planned and built in Week 5.

Must include: a big question; an introduction panel (3 to 5 sentences); at least five objects (real items, replicas, drawings or printed images) from at least three weeks of Unit 1; a label for each object with title, place and date, material, story and a visitor question; a map or timeline; photos of the exhibit and each label; the tour and visitor feedback.

Ideas for objects: Week 1 family interview or timeline; Week 2 true-size map; Week 3 dig box artifacts or a replica Lomekwi tool; Week 4 hand-ground flour or a clay Çatalhöyük house.

Turn in: the Week 5 Hands-On records; photos of the exhibit and each label, emailed to your teacher or uploaded where your teacher directs; and a short video of the tour (or a parent's three-line tour note: how long it ran, what the student explained best, one visitor question).
