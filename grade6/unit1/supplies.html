Grade 6 · Unit 1 · Weeks 1 to 5

Unit 1 Supply List

Almost everything comes from a normal home kitchen, pantry or junk drawer. Gather each week's items before Monday. Items marked optional can be skipped.

Every week

  • A computer or tablet with a camera, microphone and the video-call app used for live lessons
  • A notebook or binder for each subject, pencils, an eraser and colored pencils
  • Index cards, graph paper and a ruler with centimeters and millimeters
  • A printer (or a local print shop) for printable pages and project labels

Week by week

WeekSubjectActivity and supplies
Week 1MathThe Real Receipt Budget Lab
  • A recent grocery receipt or a store flyer (a printed price list is in the teacher plan if you have neither)
  • Sticky notes or small slips of paper
  • Pencil and scrap paper (no calculator until the very end)
  • Whiteboard or paper for working problems live on Zoom
  • A grocery receipt or store flyer for the hands-on lab
  • Sticky notes

  • An identical empty container if you have one
  • A kitchen scale (optional) and a ruler

Week 1World and American HistoryFamily History Detective
  • Three sources from home: a photograph, a document (letter, card, certificate, recipe) and an object (anything older than you)
  • A family member or family friend, older than 40 if possible, to interview
  • Paper or a phone to record notes (ask permission before recording)
  • Two or three items from your own family to model the activity on camera
Week 2MathOpen the KCE Store
  • Real coins and bills, or play money (cut paper bills work)
  • 5 household items with price tags you write, using cents (for example $3.79)
  • A family member to be the customer
  • Paper for receipts

  • A real receipt
  • Grid paper for lining up digits
Week 2ELAPoint of View Photo Walk
  • A phone or camera (or paper and pencil to sketch)
  • One ordinary object or scene at home: the kitchen table, a pet, the front yard
  • Moving Day text (print for annotating if helpful)
  • A phone or camera for the photo walk
Week 2Life ScienceThe Fair Paper Towel Test
  • Three kinds of paper: two brands of paper towel and a paper napkin (or toilet paper)
  • A kitchen scale that shows grams
  • A bowl of water
  • A timer and scissors
  • A cookie sheet or tray to keep things tidy
  • Paper towels, napkins, three identical cups, a measuring cup in milliliters, a timer
Week 2World and American HistoryThe Orange Peel Projection
  • An orange (or clementine)
  • A fine-tip permanent marker
  • A paper plate
  • A ruler
  • A globe if you have one
  • An orange and a permanent marker

Week 3MathPrice by the Pound
  • A kitchen scale that shows pounds (or ounces), or produce bags labeled with weights
  • 3 fruits or vegetables at home (or a store flyer showing prices per pound)
  • Paper and pencil; a calculator only for checking at the end
  • Hundredths grid paper or a printed 10 by 10 grid
  • Colored pencils
  • A kitchen scale and three pieces of produce
Week 3ELAWalk the Staircase
  • A staircase at home (or a row of 6 index cards laid on the floor like steps)
  • 6 index cards or sticky notes
  • A marker
  • The poem printed for annotating
  • Index cards and a staircase (or cards on the floor)
  • Optional: a recording of Langston Hughes reading his work (the Library of Congress and Academy of American Poets host recordings)
Week 3Life ScienceKitchen Metric Lab
  • A ruler with centimeters
  • A kitchen scale that shows grams
  • A measuring cup with milliliter marks
  • A thermometer if you have one (a meat or candy thermometer works with adult help)
  • Five household objects, such as a spoon, a book, an apple, a pencil and a cup of water
  • Ruler with millimeters, kitchen scale in grams, measuring cup in milliliters
  • A clear glass and water to show a meniscus on camera
  • Thermometer if available
Week 3World and American HistoryDig Box Archaeology
  • A clear plastic container or shoebox
  • Three or four materials for layers: slightly damp sand or soil (so layers hold their shape), plus rice, oatmeal or birdseed
  • Four small objects to bury as artifacts (a coin, a button, a pebble, a toy figure)
  • A spoon, a paintbrush, a tray and a ruler
  • A family member to build the dig box without showing you
  • A world map or globe
  • Dig box materials (prepared by a family member before the after-class activity)
Week 4MathPantry Unit Price Detective
  • Four packaged foods from your pantry or fridge with the price (from a receipt, the store app or website, or a store flyer), plus internet or a flyer to find a second size of one item
  • Paper and pencil; a calculator only for checking
  • Four grocery packages or flyer pages with prices and package sizes
Week 4ELAAct Out Your Climax in Slow Motion
  • A family member or friend to help
  • Your Week 1 story mountain cards
  • A phone to record (optional)
  • The student's story mountain cards and opening paragraph from Week 1
  • Space to act out the climax
Week 4Life ScienceSun vs. Shade Temperature Investigation
  • Two thermometers that read the same (check them side by side first), or one thermometer moved between spots
  • A sunny spot and a shady spot
  • A timer or clock
  • Graph paper
  • Two colored pencils
  • A thermometer
  • Graph paper and two colored pencils
  • A globe or map to discuss models
  • A current or past NHC forecast graphic on screen (optional)
Week 4World and American HistoryGrind It Like the First Farmers
  • A flat stone or a sturdy cutting board, plus a smooth fist-sized stone (washed) or a mortar and pestle
  • 1/4 cup of dry whole grain (wheat berries, uncooked rice, popcorn kernels or dried corn, or oats)
  • A timer
  • A tablespoon measuring spoon, a fine-mesh strainer or sieve and a small bowl
  • A clean towel underneath to catch spills
  • A world map or globe
  • A flat stone or cutting board, a smooth stone or mortar and pestle, and 1/4 cup of dry grain
  • A timer, a tablespoon, a fine strainer and a towel
Week 5MathMake Your Own Savings Goal Plan
  • A real item you want (from a store website or flyer, with its price)
  • Paper or a notebook for a savings chart
  • Colored pencils
  • A jar or envelope labeled with your goal (optional)
  • Counters, beans or coins (36 of one kind and 48 of another) for the bag-packing model

Week 5ELAAuthor's Chair

  • The student's Week 4 draft (printed if possible)

  • The Unit 1 Projects page with the narrative rubric
Week 5Life ScienceRun Your Investigation
  • The materials your procedure needs (gather them before you start)
  • A ruler, scale, thermometer, measuring cup or timer, depending on your test
  • Graph paper and colored pencils

  • The Unit 1 Projects page with the investigation rubric
Week 5World and American HistoryBuild Your Museum Exhibit

  • Five or more objects: real items, replicas you make (clay, cardboard, paper), drawings or printed images
  • Index cards or card stock for labels
  • Markers or a printer
