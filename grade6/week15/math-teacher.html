Unit 3: Rates and Percents · Week 15 · Math · Teacher Plan

Unit 3 Review and Checkpoint

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Choose the right strategy for rate, percent, markup and conversion problems.
  • Find and compare unit rates.
  • Solve percent problems: a percent of a number, the whole from a part, sales tax, tips, discounts and markups.
  • Convert units within a measurement system and convert between currencies.
  • Plan a multi-step budget that combines all of these skills.

Standards

  • MA.6.AR.3.4: Apply ratio relationships to solve mathematical and real-world problems involving percentages using the relationship between two quantities.
  • MA.6.AR.3.5: Solve mathematical and real-world problems involving ratios, rates and unit rates, including comparisons, mixtures, ratios of lengths and conversions within the same measurement system.
  • MA.K12.MTR.5.1: Use patterns and structure to help understand and connect mathematical concepts.
  • MA.K12.MTR.7.1: Apply mathematics to real-world contexts.
  • Financial literacy: budgeting, comparing shipping costs, sales tax and exchange rates (Jump$tart Spending).

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • 5 or 6 household items, a scale or package weights, a ruler
  • A store flyer or website with prices
  • Paper, pencil, a calculator for checking only
  • The Unit 3 checkpoint (in the lesson)

Vocabulary

  • unit rate: A rate for one unit, such as $0.20 per ounce.
  • percent: A rate per 100.
  • discount and markup: An amount taken off a price, and an amount added to a cost.
  • conversion factor: A ratio of equal amounts in different units.
  • flat rate: One price no matter the weight or amount, such as a flat-rate shipping box.

Key questions to ask

  • A rate per one, a percent of what, cost or price, or which units?
  • Is the answer reasonable?
  • Which shipping choice is cheaper for this weight?
  • Did you round money correctly?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Subtracting percents of different wholes (discount minus tax).
  • Taking markup as a percent of the selling price.
  • Multiplying when converting to a larger unit.
  • Rounding down when you must buy whole rolls or boxes.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Keep the toolkit chart visible.
  • Allow ratio tables for every percent and conversion problem.
  • Break the care package into one step per line.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Find a real shipping rate to Jamaica online with a parent and redo the comparison.
  • Write two checkpoint-style problems of your own, with answer keys.

Background for the teacher

Shipping prices, the $60 budget and the 155 JMD exchange rate are illustrative examples, not real carrier rates or current market rates. Checkpoint total: 52 points (Parts A to D). Rounding: Florida computes sales tax with a bracket system that rounds a half cent up, so $2.025 becomes $2.03.

Sources

  • Florida Department of Revenue, Sales and Use Tax (bracket system).
  • NIST Handbook 44, Appendix C.
  • Jump$tart Coalition, National Standards for Personal Financial Education.

How the lesson runs

During the live lesson, the student shares their screen and answers the Reconnect questions and the Learn checks live while you teach. The Hands-On activity, Think Deeper and Exit Check are completed after class.

Scoring

The interactive lesson scores itself: full points on the first try, half points on the second try, then the answer and explanation appear. Mastery is 85. Written responses arrive in your results email with the score; score them with the rubric below before the student moves on.

Checkpoint scoring

Checkpoint questions are scored separately from the lesson. The student gets one try per question and the answers are not shown, so the checkpoint measures independent mastery and a retake stays fair. Your results email shows the auto-scored checkpoint points, a list of each item marked correct or MISSED, and the points available for the written items. Score the written items with their rubrics, then: checkpoint score = (auto points + rubric points) / total possible × 100. Mastery is 85. Below 85: teach a targeted review lesson on the parts missed (A to D), then the student retakes the checkpoint. Record both attempts in the Weekly Score Log.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. Week 11: a 32-ounce jar of peanut butter costs $5.12. What is the unit price in cents per ounce? (Type the number of cents.)
    Answer: 16 cents per ounce
    $5.12 ÷ 32 = $0.16 = 16 cents per ounce.
  2. Week 12: 21 is 35% of what number?
    Answer: 60
    35% → 21, so 5% → 3, so 100% → 60.
  3. Week 13: a $70 tent is 30% off. What is the sale price?
    Answer: $49
    Pay 70%: 0.7 × $70 = $49.
  4. Week 14: how many ounces are in 4 pounds?
    Answer: 64 ounces
    4 × 16 = 64 ounces.

Learn 1: Review: Unit Rates and Comparing Deals

  1. A 12-pack of pencils costs $3.00 and a 30-pack costs $6.00. What is the unit price of the 30-pack in cents per pencil? (Type the number of cents.)
    Answer: 20 cents per pencil
    $6.00 ÷ 30 = $0.20; the 12-pack is $0.25 each.
  2. A mail carrier drives 84 miles in 3 1/2 hours. What is the average speed in miles per hour?
    Answer: 24 miles per hour
    84 ÷ 3.5 = 24.
  3. The 30-pack of pencils is the better unit price. Give one reason a family might still buy the 12-pack.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A sensible reason: they only need a few, limited budget today, storage, the extra would go to waste
    Model answer: If they only need 10 pencils, the 12-pack costs less money overall, and the extra 18 pencils might just get lost.

Learn 2: Review: Percents, Discounts, Tax, Tips and Markups

  1. A $36 box of chapter books is 25% off. Then 7.5% sales tax is added to the sale price. What is the total? Round to the nearest cent.
    Answer: $29.03
    Sale: 0.75 × $36 = $27. Tax: $27 × 0.075 = $2.025, about $2.03. Total $29.03.
  2. A bookstore buys a dictionary for $16 and sells it for $28. What is the markup percent?
    Answer: 75 %
    Markup $12; $12 ÷ $16 = 0.75 = 75%.
  3. After a 20% discount, a backpack costs $32. What was the original price?
    Answer: $40
    $32 is 80%: 80% → 32, 10% → 4, 100% → 40.
  4. The co-op parents' lunch bill is $86 before tax. They leave an 18% tip on that amount. How much is the tip?
    Answer: $15.48
    0.18 × $86 = $15.48.

Learn 3: Review: Converting Units and Currency

  1. The care package box is 18 inches long. How many feet is that? Write a decimal.
    Answer: 1.5 feet
    18 ÷ 12 = 1.5 feet.
  2. A package weighs 5 pounds 6 ounces. How many ounces is that in all?
    Answer: 86 ounces
    5 × 16 = 80; 80 + 6 = 86 ounces.
  3. A storybook costs $12 in Florida. At 1 USD = 155 JMD, how many Jamaican dollars is that?
    Answer: 1860 Jamaican dollars
    12 × 155 = 1,860 JMD.
  4. Tomás converts 2,400 grams to kilograms and gets 2,400,000. Explain his mistake and give the correct answer.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Kilograms are larger, so he should divide by 1,000, not multiply; Correct answer: 2.4 kg
    Model answer: A kilogram is bigger than a gram, so there should be fewer kilograms. He should divide by 1,000: 2,400 g = 2.4 kg.

Learn 4: Money: Mid-Year Money Review

  1. Using the example prices ($9.50 plus $3.25 per pound), what does it cost to ship a 10-pound package by weight?
    Answer: $42
    $9.50 + 10 × $3.25 = $9.50 + $32.50 = $42.
  2. At what weight in pounds does shipping by weight cost the same as the $44 flat-rate box? Round to the nearest tenth of a pound.
    Answer: 10.6 pounds
    $44 − $9.50 = $34.50; $34.50 ÷ $3.25 ≈ 10.6 pounds.
  3. A family's package weighs 12 pounds. Which shipping choice should they pick, and how much do they save? Show your work.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: By weight: $9.50 + 12 × $3.25 = $48.50; Flat rate $44 is cheaper by $4.50
    Model answer: By weight: $9.50 + 12 × $3.25 = $9.50 + $39 = $48.50. The flat-rate box is $44, so they should use it and save $4.50.

Hands-On

  1. Show your item list with prices, sale prices, subtotal, tax and total. Did you stay within $60?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Items with prices; sale prices correct; Correct subtotal, tax and total; Compared with the $60 budget
    Model answer: Book $12, notebook set $8 (25% off: $6), colored pencils $9, word-search book $7, card game $10 (20% off: $8), stickers $4. Subtotal: $46. Tax: $46 × 0.075 = $3.45. Total: $49.45, under $60.
  2. Show the total weight in ounces, pounds and ounces, and pounds as a decimal; your box length in feet; both shipping prices; your choice; and two items in Jamaican dollars.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Weight converted correctly; Box length converted; Both shipping options calculated; correct choice; Two currency conversions correct
    Model answer: Weights: 14 + 10 + 9 + 8 + 6 + 1 = 48 oz = 3 lb 0 oz = 3 pounds. Box: 14 in ÷ 12 ≈ 1.17 ft. By weight: $9.50 + 3 × $3.25 = $19.25; flat rate $44; I choose by weight and save $24.75. Book $12 × 155 = 1,860 JMD; card game $8 × 155 = 1,240 JMD.

Think Deeper

  1. The co-op has $500 for 8 care packages. Each package's items cost $46 before tax (7.5%), and each ships for $22. Can they afford all 8? If not, how many can they send, and how much money is left? Show every step.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Items with tax: $46 × 1.075 = $49.45; Per package with shipping: $49.45 + $22 = $71.45; 8 packages = $571.60, over $500; They can send 6 ($428.70) with $71.30 left (or explains 7 = $500.15, just over)
    Model answer: Items with tax: $46 × 1.075 = $49.45. With shipping: $49.45 + $22 = $71.45 per package. Eight packages cost 8 × $71.45 = $571.60, which is over $500. Seven would cost $500.15, just 15 cents over, so they can send 6 for $428.70 and have $71.30 left, unless they find a small discount to afford the seventh.
  2. Rafael says: "A $50 jacket is 20% off, and then 7.5% tax is added. That's like 12.5% off, so I pay $43.75." Is he right? Calculate the real total and explain his mistake.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Real total: $50 × 0.8 = $40; $40 × 1.075 = $43.00; Explains that the tax percent is of the sale price, so you cannot just subtract percents; his $43.75 is wrong
    Model answer: No. The sale price is 80% of $50 = $40. Tax is 7.5% of $40 = $3, so the total is $43.00, not $43.75. The 20% and 7.5% are percents of different amounts ($50 and $40), so he cannot just subtract them.

Checkpoint: Part A: Unit rates

  1. A farm stand sells 5 pounds of oranges for $6.25. What is the price per pound?
    Answer: $1.25
    $6.25 ÷ 5 = $1.25.
  2. A cyclist rides 27 miles in 1 1/2 hours. What is her speed in miles per hour?
    Answer: 18 miles per hour
    27 ÷ 1.5 = 18.
  3. Which is the better buy: A) 20 ounces of rice for $2.40 or B) 32 ounces for $3.52? Type A or B.
    Answer: B ($0.11 per ounce vs. $0.12)
    A: $2.40 ÷ 20 = $0.12 per ounce. B: $3.52 ÷ 32 = $0.11 per ounce.

Checkpoint: Part B: Percents

  1. Complete the conversions.
    Answer: 35; 37.5
    0.35 × 100 = 35%; 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375 = 37.5%.
  2. What is 45% of 80?
    Answer: 36
    0.45 × 80 = 36 (or 10% = 8, 40% = 32, 5% = 4).
  3. 27 is 60% of what number?
    Answer: 45
    60% → 27, 20% → 9, 100% → 45.
  4. Nadia answered 42 out of 48 questions correctly. What percent is that?
    Answer: 87.5 %
    42 ÷ 48 = 0.875 = 87.5%.

Checkpoint: Part C: Discounts, tax, tips and markups

  1. A $64 pair of hiking boots is 35% off. What is the sale price?
    Answer: $41.60
    Pay 65%: 0.65 × $64 = $41.60.
  2. Find the total cost of a $48 item with 7.5% sales tax.
    Answer: $51.60
    Tax $3.60; total $51.60.
  3. A shop pays $24 for a lamp and uses a 60% markup. What is the selling price?
    Answer: $38.40
    60% of $24 = $14.40; $24 + $14.40 = $38.40.
  4. A sale price of $63 is 30% off the original. What was the original price?
    Answer: $90
    $63 is 70%: 10% → $9, 100% → $90.
  5. A family's restaurant bill is $54 before tax. Explain how to find a 15% tip using mental math, and give the tip.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric (3 points toward the checkpoint).
    Rubric: 10% = $5.40, 5% = half of that = $2.70; Tip = $8.10
    Model answer: 10% of $54 is $5.40. Half of that is 5%, $2.70. Add them: 15% is $8.10.

Checkpoint: Part D: Converting units and currency

  1. How many inches are in 3 1/2 feet?
    Answer: 42 inches
    3.5 × 12 = 42.
  2. How many cups are in 3 gallons?
    Answer: 48 cups
    3 × 16 = 48 cups.
  3. Convert 875 centimeters to meters.
    Answer: 8.75 meters
    875 ÷ 100 = 8.75 m.
  4. At 1 USD = 18 Mexican pesos, how many U.S. dollars is 630 pesos?
    Answer: $35
    630 ÷ 18 = $35.
  5. A fence needs 240 feet of wire. Wire is sold in 25-yard rolls for $30 each. How many rolls are needed and what is the cost? Explain each step.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric (4 points toward the checkpoint).
    Rubric: 240 ft ÷ 3 = 80 yards; 80 ÷ 25 = 3.2, so 4 rolls (you cannot buy part of a roll); 4 × $30 = $120
    Model answer: 240 feet ÷ 3 = 80 yards. Each roll is 25 yards, and 80 ÷ 25 = 3.2, so they need 4 whole rolls, because 3 rolls would be too short. Cost: 4 × $30 = $120.
