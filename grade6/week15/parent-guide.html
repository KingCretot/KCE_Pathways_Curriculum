Week 15: Mid-Year Review, Unit 3 Checkpoints and Projects

This is the last week of Unit 3 and the middle of the school year. In every subject your child reviews the unit, works on a project (a poetry analysis and recitation, a field guide, and a Silk Roads travel journal) and takes the Unit 3 checkpoint. Math plans a care package for a pen pal and reviews every Unit 3 skill.

How scoring works: each lesson scores itself. Your child gets two tries per question: full points on the first try, half points on the second, then the answer and explanation appear. A score of 85 or higher means the lesson is mastered. Written answers go to Jonathan, who scores them with a rubric.

Checkpoint scoring: checkpoint questions give one try and do not show answers, so the checkpoint fairly measures what your child can do alone. Jonathan adds the points for written answers and shares the checkpoint score. Mastery is 85; below 85 means a review lesson, then a retake. Projects below 85 are returned with feedback and revised once.

Time this week: About 60 minutes of live lessons per subject, plus project time (about 2 to 3 hours per project across the week) and one checkpoint per subject.

Gather this week: A large sheet of paper or an opened paper bag, colored pencils or markers, a world map, atlas or globe, index cards, paper and pencil or a computer, and optionally string and tape (Social Studies); a notebook or clipboard, pencils, colored pencils, a ruler with centimeters, a phone or camera, water, sunscreen and closed-toe shoes, and optionally a magnifying glass, binoculars and a Florida field guide from the library (Science); 5 or 6 household items to "pack," a kitchen or bathroom scale (or package weights), a ruler or tape measure, a store flyer or website with prices, paper and pencil, and a calculator for checking only (Math); the Unit 3 poems, index cards, paper and pencil or a computer, and a phone or tablet to record a recitation (ELA).

Heads up: Checkpoints are taken after class, independently, one try per question, with no answers shown. Science fieldwork goes outdoors: please go along, and help your child look without touching unknown plants and animals (Florida has venomous snakes, fire ants and poison ivy); collecting is not allowed in state parks. ELA needs a quiet few minutes to record a recitation, and your child will want an audience. If last week's bread test is still running, keep the bags sealed and throw them away unopened when observations end.

ELA: Mid-Year Review and Poetry Analysis Project

Your child reviews figurative language, sound devices and the poetic forms of Unit 3 (haiku, sonnet, free verse, villanelle), learns to write a poetry analysis with a thesis and quotations, and practices reciting from memory. The project is a 400- to 600-word analysis of one Unit 3 poem and a recorded recitation. The checkpoint includes an unseen poem, Paul Laurence Dunbar's "We Wear the Mask."

Ask at dinner: "How does the poet make you feel that, not just what does the poem say?"

Try it together: Be the audience for the final recitation and tell your child one thing that worked well.

Signs it's clicking: They explain how a technique creates meaning instead of retelling the poem. Signs they need help: They pause at the end of every line. Ask them to read to the punctuation instead.

Math: Unit 3 Review and Checkpoint

Your child reviews unit rates, percents, discounts, sales tax, tips, markups, unit conversions and currency exchange, compares shipping by weight with a flat rate, and plans a care package for a pen pal within a budget. Then they take the Unit 3 checkpoint.

Ask at dinner: "Is this a rate per one, a percent of something, a cost compared with a price, or a change of units?"

Try it together: Weigh the care package items together and have your child convert ounces to pounds.

Signs it's clicking: They pick the right tool before calculating and check whether the answer is reasonable. Signs they need help: They subtract percents of different amounts (discount minus tax). Ask them to work it in steps.

Life Science: Mid-Year Review and Field Guide Project

Your child connects the characteristics of life, classification, dichotomous keys and microorganisms to the cells studied in Unit 2, learns about naturalist Mark Catesby and community science, and builds a field guide to living things near home with scientific names, classification, observations and a dichotomous key. Then they take the Unit 3 checkpoint.

Ask at dinner: "How could someone else use your key to name this plant?"

Try it together: Take the fieldwork walk together and test your child's dichotomous key.

Signs it's clicking: They write scientific names correctly and use observable traits. Signs they need help: They call fungi plants. Ask whether a mushroom has chloroplasts.

World and American History: Mid-Year Review and Ancient Asia Project

Your child compares Kemet, Mesopotamia, the Indus Valley and ancient China, reviews their writing systems, and writes a historically accurate Silk Roads travel journal around 100 CE with a hand-drawn route map and a Historian's Note separating fact from fiction. Then they take the Unit 3 checkpoint.

Ask at dinner: "Is that detail true to 100 CE, and how do you know?"

Try it together: Read one journal entry and see if you can spot anything that seems out of its time.

Signs it's clicking: They support every journal detail with a source and avoid anachronisms. Signs they need help: They mix up fact and fiction. Ask them to point to the source for each detail.
