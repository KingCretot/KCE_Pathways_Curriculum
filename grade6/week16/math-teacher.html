Unit 4: Integers and the Coordinate Plane · Week 16 · Math · Teacher Plan

Integers, Opposites and Absolute Value

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Use positive and negative integers to describe quantities with opposite directions and explain what zero means in each situation.
  • Plot, order and compare integers on horizontal and vertical number lines.
  • Find the opposite of an integer and recognize that opposites are the same distance from zero.
  • Interpret absolute value as distance from zero and compare absolute values in real situations.
  • Track a bank balance with deposits and withdrawals, and explain what a negative balance means.

Standards

  • MA.6.NSO.1.1: Extend previous understanding of numbers to define rational numbers. Plot, order and compare rational numbers.
  • MA.6.NSO.1.2: Given a mathematical or real-world context, represent quantities that have opposite direction using rational numbers. Compare them on a number line and explain the meaning of zero within its context.
  • MA.6.NSO.1.3: Given a mathematical or real-world context, interpret the absolute value of a number as the distance from zero on a number line. Find the absolute value of rational numbers.
  • MA.6.NSO.1.4: Solve mathematical and real-world problems involving absolute value, including the comparison of absolute value.
  • Financial literacy: bank balances, deposits, withdrawals and overdraft fees (Jump$tart Saving and Spending).

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • Painter's tape or sidewalk chalk, a marker
  • Paper and pencil
  • Optional: a thermometer
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • integer: A whole number or its opposite: ..., −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...
  • negative number: A number less than zero, written with a minus sign, such as −8.
  • opposites: Two numbers the same distance from zero on opposite sides, such as 6 and −6.
  • absolute value: A number's distance from zero, written |−6| = 6. Distance is never negative.
  • deposit: Money put into a bank account; it increases the balance.
  • withdrawal: Money taken out of a bank account; it decreases the balance.
  • balance: The amount of money in an account. A negative balance means you owe the bank money.

Key questions to ask

  • What does zero mean here?
  • Which direction is positive?
  • Which number is farther from zero?
  • Is a bigger absolute value always a bigger number?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • −15 is greater than −5 because 15 > 5.
  • Absolute value "makes a number positive" without meaning distance.
  • Zero degrees means no temperature.
  • A negative bank balance means the account is simply empty.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Use a vertical thermometer drawing for comparisons.
  • Color negative numbers red and positive numbers blue.
  • Keep the human number line taped up all week.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Research the lowest and highest points on each continent and order them.
  • Find your bank's or a local credit union's real overdraft fee and compare it with $35.

Background for the teacher

Britton Hill (345 feet) is the lowest state high point in the United States. Death Valley's Badwater Basin is about 282 feet below sea level. Overdraft fees vary; many large U.S. banks have historically charged about $35, though several have reduced or eliminated them since 2022. Grade 6 B.E.S.T. limits absolute-value work to integers and does not include operations with negative numbers; the bank walk uses movement on a number line only, previewing Weeks 18 and 19.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. A $40 jacket is 15% off. What is the sale price?
    Answer: $34
    Pay 85%: 0.85 × $40 = $34.
  2. How many inches are in 5 feet?
    Answer: 60 inches
    5 × 12 = 60.
  3. A savings account pays 2% of the balance once a year. How much does $300 earn in a year?
    Answer: $6
    0.02 × $300 = $6.
  4. Name one way you checked whether an answer was reasonable in Unit 3.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A real strategy: compare with a benchmark (10%, 50%), ask whether the number should be bigger or smaller, estimate first
    Model answer: I compared my answer with 10% or 50% to see whether it was in the right range, and I asked whether a converted number should get bigger or smaller.

Learn 1: What Zero Means

  1. A submarine is 120 feet below the surface of the ocean. Write its elevation as an integer.
    Answer: -120 feet
    Below sea level is negative: −120.
  2. A quarterback is sacked for a loss of 9 yards. Write the change as an integer.
    Answer: -9 yards
    A loss is negative: −9.
  3. Write each as an integer.
    Answer: 75; −18; 200
    Deposits and heights above are positive; below zero is negative.
  4. On the Fahrenheit scale, 0°F does not mean "no heat at all." Explain why zero is just a starting point on a temperature scale.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Zero is a chosen reference point on the scale; Temperatures can go below 0°F (there is still some heat), so zero does not mean nothing
    Model answer: Zero on the Fahrenheit scale is just where people decided to start counting. Temperatures can go below 0°F, so there is still heat there; zero is a reference point, not "nothing."

Learn 2: Ordering and Comparing Integers

  1. Type less or greater in each blank.
    Answer: less; greater; less
    −9 is left of −3; 0 is right of −5; −1 is left of 1.
  2. Order these morning temperatures from coldest to warmest.
    Answer: −8°F (Minneapolis) → −3°F (Chicago) → 0°F (Denver) → 14°F (Boston) → 41°F (Tallahassee)
    Coldest means least: −8 < −3 < 0 < 14 < 41.
  3. Malia says −15°F is warmer than −5°F because 15 is bigger than 5. Explain her mistake using a thermometer or number line.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: −15 is farther below zero, so it is colder (less); On a thermometer, −15 is lower than −5
    Model answer: On a thermometer, −15 is lower than −5 because it is farther below zero. Lower means colder, so −15°F is colder, even though 15 is a bigger number than 5.

Learn 3: Opposites and Absolute Value

  1. What is |−23|?
    Answer: 23
    −23 is 23 units from zero.
  2. What is the opposite of −14?
    Answer: 14
    14 is the same distance from zero on the other side.
  3. A fish swims at −45 feet. A pelican flies at 38 feet. Which is farther from sea level? Type fish or pelican.
    Answer: the fish (|−45| = 45 > 38)
    Compare absolute values: 45 > 38.
  4. Ravi says, "The absolute value of a number is always positive." Is he exactly right? Explain, using the number 0.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Almost: absolute value is never negative; |0| = 0, which is neither positive nor negative
    Model answer: Not exactly. Absolute value is never negative, but |0| = 0, and zero is neither positive nor negative. So it is more accurate to say absolute value is always positive or zero.

Learn 4: Money: Bank Balances, Deposits and Withdrawals

  1. Starting with the −$15 balance, the account owner deposits $40 from babysitting. What is the new balance?
    Answer: $25
    Owing $15 and adding $40 leaves $25.
  2. A different account has $12. The owner buys a $30 game with a debit card. What is the balance now? Write a negative number if needed.
    Answer: −$18
    $12 − $30 = −$18: the owner is $18 below zero.
  3. Which balance shows the bigger debt: −$42 or −$27? Type the balance.
    Answer: −$42
    |−42| = 42 > 27, so it is the bigger debt, even though −42 is the smaller number.
  4. A bank charges a $35 fee each time an account goes below zero. Why is it smart to track your balance before every purchase? Use the concert ticket example.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Going below zero triggers a fee, which makes the debt bigger; In the example, −$15 would become −$50 with a $35 fee; tracking first could have prevented it
    Model answer: In the example, the ticket took the balance to −$15. With a $35 fee, the owner would owe $50, more than three times as much. Checking the balance before buying the ticket would have shown there wasn't enough money and saved the fee.

Hands-On

  1. In the bank balance walk, you started at 5 and moved −8, +6, −9, +4. Where did you end?
    Answer: -2
    5 − 8 = −3; −3 + 6 = 3; 3 − 9 = −6; −6 + 4 = −2.
    Rubric: Completes every step of the activity; Includes specific details, numbers or observations from the activity; Explains thinking in complete sentences
  2. Describe what you noticed about opposites when you counted steps to zero, and what you noticed when comparing two negative numbers. Use at least one example from your walk.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Opposites take the same number of steps to zero (same absolute value); The person on the right (closer to zero, for negatives) has the greater number; A specific example
    Model answer: When my mom stood on −7 and I stood on 7, we each took 7 steps to zero, so opposites have the same absolute value. When I stood on −2 and she stood on −6, I was on the right, so −2 was greater, even though 6 is bigger than 2.

Think Deeper

  1. Two friends compare accounts. Jordan's balance is −$60. Kai's balance is $45. Kai says, "Jordan has more money, because 60 is bigger." Explain who is better off and how much money Jordan would need to deposit to have the same balance as Kai.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Kai is better off: $45 > −$60 (Jordan owes money); Jordan needs $60 to reach zero plus $45 more: $105; Uses absolute value or a number line correctly
    Model answer: Kai is better off, because $45 is money he has, while −$60 means Jordan owes $60. Jordan would need $60 just to get back to zero, plus $45 more to match Kai, so he would need to deposit $105.
  2. Invent a real-world situation (not money or temperature) where both positive and negative numbers make sense. Explain what zero means, give one positive and one negative example, and compare their absolute values.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A new, sensible situation; Clear meaning of zero; Correct positive and negative examples; Correct comparison of absolute values
    Model answer: Golf scores compare strokes to "par." Zero means you finished exactly at par. A score of +3 means 3 strokes over par, and −2 means 2 strokes under par, which is better. |+3| = 3 is greater than |−2| = 2, so the +3 score is farther from par.

Exit Check

  1. What is |−37|?
    Answer: 37
    Distance from zero.
  2. Type less or greater.
    Answer: greater
    −12 is closer to zero, to the right of −20.
  3. A balance is $20. The owner spends $35. What is the new balance?
    Answer: −$15
    20 − 35 = −15.
  4. In a bank account, what does a balance of zero mean, and what does a negative balance mean?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Zero: no money in the account and nothing owed; Negative: the owner owes the bank money
    Model answer: Zero means the account is empty and nothing is owed. A negative balance means the owner has spent more than they had and owes the bank money.

Answer key: printable page

  1. Write an integer for each: a $60 deposit; 25 feet below sea level; a gain of 8 yards; 4 degrees below zero.
    Answer: 60; −25; 8; −4.
  2. Order from least to greatest: 3, −11, 0, −4, 7, −1.
    Answer: −11, −4, −1, 0, 3, 7.
  3. Find |−19|, |6| and the opposite of −30.
    Answer: 19; 6; 30.
  4. A diver is at −52 feet and a bird is at 47 feet. Which is farther from sea level, and by how many feet?
    Answer: The diver, by 5 feet (52 vs. 47).
  5. A balance of $18 has transactions of −$25, +$10 and −$7. Find the balance after each.
    Answer: −$7; $3; −$4.
  6. Explain why −2 is greater than −9.
    Answer: −2 is closer to zero, to the right of −9 on the number line.
