Unit 4: The Human Body · Week 17 · Life Science · Teacher Plan

Skeletal and Muscular Systems

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Describe five functions of the skeletal system, including making blood cells and storing minerals.
  • Identify the parts of a long bone and explain how bone's structure fits its jobs.
  • Compare types of joints and explain the roles of cartilage, ligaments and tendons.
  • Explain how skeletal muscles work in opposing pairs and how bones act as levers.
  • Explain how the skeletal, muscular and nervous systems interact, and how exercise and nutrition keep them healthy.

Standards

  • SC.6.L.14.5: Identify and investigate the general functions of the major systems of the human body (digestive, respiratory, circulatory, reproductive, excretory, immune, nervous, and musculoskeletal) and describe ways these systems interact with each other to maintain homeostasis.
  • SC.6.L.14.1: Describe and identify patterns in the hierarchical organization of organisms from atoms to molecules to cells to tissues to organs to organ systems to organisms.
  • SC.6.N.3.4: Identify the role of models in the context of the sixth grade science benchmarks.
  • Health connection: exercise, calcium and vitamin D for bone health (NIAMS guidance).

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • 2 cardboard strips, a brass paper fastener, 2 strings (balloons optional), tape, hole punch
  • Marker, paper and pencil
  • Optional: a cleaned chicken drumstick bone and white vinegar
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • marrow: Soft tissue inside bones; red marrow makes blood cells, yellow marrow stores fat.
  • joint: A place where two or more bones meet.
  • cartilage: Smooth, flexible connective tissue that cushions the ends of bones at joints.
  • ligament: A tough band of connective tissue that connects bone to bone.
  • tendon: A strong cord of connective tissue that connects muscle to bone.
  • voluntary muscle: Muscle you control on purpose, such as skeletal muscle.
  • lever: A rigid bar that turns around a fixed point (fulcrum); bones act as levers moved by muscles.

Key questions to ask

  • Which of the five jobs is this bone doing?
  • Which joint type allows this movement?
  • Which muscle is contracting, and which is relaxing?
  • Which other systems are involved?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Bones are dead.
  • Muscles can push.
  • Tendons and ligaments are the same thing.
  • Only old people need to think about bone health.

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Label your own body with sticky notes (shoulder, elbow, knee, Achilles tendon).
  • Provide a joint picture card for each type.
  • Pre-assemble the cardboard joint.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Research how bones heal and how doctors use casts, pins or plates.
  • Compare first-, second- and third-class levers in the body (neck, ankle, elbow).

Background for the teacher

NASA reports bone mineral density losses of about 1 to 1.5 percent per month in weight-bearing bones during long-duration spaceflight, countered by about 2 hours of daily exercise (including the ARED resistance device). Bone counts: about 270 to 300 at birth, 206 in a typical adult. The National Institutes of Health (NIAMS) notes that up to 90 percent of peak bone mass is acquired by about age 18 in girls and 20 in boys. Simple fractures typically heal in about 6 to 8 weeks in children and teens. The vinegar test: acetic acid dissolves calcium phosphate (hydroxyapatite) over several days, leaving collagen; use a cooked, cleaned bone and wash hands.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. Bone is one kind of which tissue type: epithelial, connective, muscle or nervous?
    Answer: connective
    Bone cells sit in a hard matrix.
  2. Unspecialized cells in bone marrow that can become blood cells are called ___ cells.
    Answer: stem
    Week 16: bone marrow stem cells make about 2 million red blood cells a second.
  3. Of the three kinds of muscle tissue, which kind moves your bones?
    Answer: skeletal
    Smooth muscle squeezes organs; cardiac muscle pumps the heart.
  4. Last week you learned about emergent properties. Name one ability your arm has that a single muscle cell does not have.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A whole-arm ability (lifting, throwing, writing, waving) that needs bones, muscles and nerves together
    Model answer: My arm can throw a ball. One muscle cell can only shorten a tiny bit; throwing needs bones, many muscles and nerves working together.

Learn 1: What Bones Do

  1. Name the bone function.
    Answer: protection; making blood cells; storage
    Support, protection, movement, blood cells and storage.
  2. A newborn has about 300 bones and an adult has 206. About how many bones fuse together (or are counted as one) as a person grows?
    Answer: 94 bones
    300 − 206 = 94. Skull bones and the bones at the base of the spine are examples.
  3. Why is it useful for the ends of long bones to be spongy instead of solid all the way through? Connect structure to function.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Spongy (honeycomb) structure is strong but light; Lighter bones need less muscle effort to move; spaces hold red marrow
    Model answer: The honeycomb structure is strong but light, so the bone can hold weight without being so heavy that moving it wastes energy. The spaces also hold red marrow, which makes blood cells.

Learn 2: Joints, Cartilage, Ligaments and Tendons

  1. Which type of joint is your shoulder?
    Answer: ball-and-socket
    It moves in nearly every direction.
  2. Connective tissue jobs.
    Answer: tendon; ligament
    Tip: tendons tie muscle to bone; ligaments link bone to bone.
  3. Your knee is a hinge joint, not a ball-and-socket joint. Why is that a good design for walking and running? What would go wrong if it could twist in every direction?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A hinge moves in one direction, giving stability for weight-bearing and pushing forward; Twisting in every direction would make the leg wobbly and easier to injure
    Model answer: A hinge lets the knee bend and straighten in one direction, which is exactly what walking and running need, and it stays stable while carrying the body's weight. If the knee could twist every way, the leg would be wobbly and ligaments would be injured much more easily.

Learn 3: Muscles Pull in Pairs

  1. When you straighten your arm, which muscle contracts: biceps or triceps?
    Answer: the triceps
    The biceps relaxes.
  2. Is skeletal muscle voluntary or involuntary?
    Answer: voluntary
    You control it on purpose.
  3. Explain why a single muscle could not both bend and straighten your elbow.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: A muscle can only contract (pull), not push; So a second muscle on the other side is needed to pull the bone back
    Model answer: A muscle can only pull by shortening; it can't push. One muscle can bend the elbow, but a second muscle on the other side has to pull to straighten it again.

Learn 4: Keeping Bones and Muscles Healthy

  1. Which mineral do bones store the most of, and need from foods like milk and collard greens?
    Answer: calcium
    Phosphorus is also stored.
  2. Use what you learned to explain why astronauts lose bone in space and why running on a treadmill (with straps pulling them down) helps.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Without weight on the bones, bones are not signaled to rebuild, so osteoclasts break down more than osteoblasts build; Weight-bearing exercise puts load on bones, signaling them to rebuild and stay strong
    Model answer: In space, bones carry almost no weight, so they aren't signaled to rebuild, and more old bone is broken down than new bone is built. Running on a treadmill with straps pulling them down puts weight on their bones again, which signals the bone cells to keep building.

Hands-On

  1. Make a table or list of the six joints you tested, the movements each allowed and its joint type.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Six joints listed; Movements described; Joint types correct (shoulder ball-and-socket, elbow hinge, neck pivot, knee hinge, fingers hinge; for the wrist, a reasonable description such as bending, tilting and small gliding movements)
    Model answer: Shoulder: circles, up, down, forward, back: ball-and-socket. Elbow: bends and straightens: hinge. Neck (top): turns side to side: pivot. Wrist: bends, tilts side to side and makes small slides: the small wrist bones glide against each other. Knee: bends and straightens: hinge. Fingers: bend and straighten: hinge.
  2. Explain how your model arm shows that muscles work in pairs. In what ways is your model unlike a real arm?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Describes which muscle shortens to bend and which shortens to straighten; Notes at least one limit of the model (no nerves or brain signal, balloons don't contract on their own, real muscles attach with tendons, cardboard is not living)
    Model answer: When I bent the model, the front string (biceps) got shorter and the back one (triceps) got longer; to straighten, the back string had to pull. My model is unlike a real arm because the strings don't contract by themselves, there are no nerves sending signals, and real muscles attach with tendons to living bone.

Think Deeper

  1. In the vinegar bone test, vinegar dissolves the calcium minerals in bone, but leaves behind a protein called collagen. Predict how the bone will change, and explain what this shows about why bones need both minerals and collagen.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Prediction: the bone becomes rubbery and bendable; Minerals give hardness and strength against crushing; Collagen gives flexibility so bones don't shatter; Both together make bone strong and slightly flexible
    Model answer: The bone will become rubbery and bend instead of snapping. That shows calcium minerals make bone hard and able to hold weight, while collagen makes it slightly flexible. A bone with only minerals would be like chalk and shatter easily; with only collagen it would be floppy. Together they make bone hard but not brittle.
  2. Describe what happens in your body when you jump to catch a ball, naming at least three body systems and how they work together.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Nervous system: eyes see the ball, brain sends signals to muscles; Muscular system: leg muscles contract to jump, arm muscles to reach; Skeletal system: bones act as levers, joints allow movement, cartilage absorbs landing; Could include circulatory or respiratory supplying oxygen
    Model answer: My eyes see the ball, and my nervous system sends signals from my brain to my muscles. My muscular system contracts my quadriceps and calf muscles to push off, and my arm muscles reach up. My skeletal system provides levers: my leg bones push against the ground, and joints like my knees and shoulders let me move. When I land, cartilage cushions my knees. My circulatory and respiratory systems keep oxygen flowing to my working muscles.

Exit Check

  1. What connects muscle to bone?
    Answer: a tendon
    Ligaments connect bone to bone.
  2. What part of a bone makes blood cells?
    Answer: red marrow
    Yellow marrow stores fat.
  3. What type of joint lets you turn your head side to side?
    Answer: a pivot joint
    At the top of the neck.
  4. Why do skeletal muscles work in pairs?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Muscles can only pull, not push, so a second muscle pulls the bone back
    Model answer: A muscle can only pull, so one muscle bends a joint and its partner pulls it straight again.

Answer key: printable page

  1. List the five functions of the skeleton with an example of each.
    Answer: Support (spine), protection (skull), movement (leg bones as levers), making blood cells (red marrow), storing minerals (calcium).
  2. Name four joint types and one place in the body where each is found.
    Answer: Ball-and-socket (shoulder), hinge (knee), pivot (neck), gliding (wrist); fixed (skull).
  3. Compare tendons and ligaments.
    Answer: Tendons connect muscle to bone; ligaments connect bone to bone.
  4. Explain how the biceps and triceps bend and straighten your arm.
    Answer: Biceps contracts to bend while triceps relaxes; triceps contracts to straighten while biceps relaxes.
  5. Give two ways to build strong bones during your teen years.
    Answer: Weight-bearing exercise; calcium and vitamin D in the diet (also protective gear).
  6. Explain how the nervous, muscular and skeletal systems work together to kick a ball.
    Answer: Brain signals muscles through nerves; muscles contract and pull on bones; bones act as levers at joints.
