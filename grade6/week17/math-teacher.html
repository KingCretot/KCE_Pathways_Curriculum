Unit 4: Integers and the Coordinate Plane · Week 17 · Math · Teacher Plan

The Four-Quadrant Coordinate Plane

Lesson length: 60 minutes live, plus the after-class interactive lesson

Objectives

  • Name the parts of the coordinate plane: the x-axis, y-axis, origin and four quadrants.
  • Plot and name ordered pairs, including negative numbers, fractions and decimals, in all four quadrants and on the axes.
  • Identify the quadrant of a point from the signs of its coordinates.
  • Recognize that points whose coordinates differ only in sign are reflections across an axis, and name the axis.
  • Find horizontal and vertical distances between points to plan a business delivery route.

Standards

  • MA.6.GR.1.1: Extend previous understanding of the coordinate plane to plot rational number ordered pairs in all four quadrants and on both axes. Identify the x- or y-axis as the line of reflection when two ordered pairs have an opposite x- or y-coordinate.
  • MA.6.GR.1.2: Find distances between ordered pairs, limited to the same x-coordinate or the same y-coordinate, represented on the coordinate plane.
  • MA.6.NSO.1.3: Given a mathematical or real-world context, interpret the absolute value of a number as the distance from zero on a number line. Find the absolute value of rational numbers.
  • Financial literacy: estimating business costs (fuel) and pricing decisions (Jump$tart Earning Income).

Materials

  • The interactive lesson
  • Painter's tape or sidewalk chalk, about 33 sticky notes or index cards, a marker
  • Graph paper, a ruler, colored pencils
  • Optional: a neighborhood map
  • Printable practice page

Vocabulary

  • coordinate plane: A flat grid formed by a horizontal number line (the x-axis) and a vertical number line (the y-axis).
  • origin: The point (0, 0), where the axes cross.
  • ordered pair: Two numbers (x, y) that locate a point: x tells left or right, y tells up or down.
  • quadrant: One of the four regions the axes divide the plane into, numbered I to IV counterclockwise from the upper right.
  • reflection: A mirror image of a point across a line, such as an axis.
  • x-coordinate: The first number in an ordered pair: the horizontal position.
  • y-coordinate: The second number in an ordered pair: the vertical position.

Key questions to ask

  • Which number tells left or right?
  • How do the signs tell you the quadrant?
  • Which coordinate changed sign, and which way did the point flip?
  • Same side or opposite sides: add or subtract?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Swapping x and y.
  • Numbering quadrants clockwise.
  • Thinking points on an axis belong to a quadrant.
  • Subtracting coordinates directly when they have opposite signs (5 − 3 instead of 5 + 3).

Support (if the student is struggling)

  • Use the phrase "walk before you climb" (x, then y).
  • Color the four quadrants on the floor grid.
  • Allow counting squares before using absolute value.

Challenge (if the student is ready for more)

  • Find all points that are exactly 5 street blocks from the origin on whole-number coordinates.
  • Reflect a triangle across both axes and describe the final position.

Background for the teacher

MA.6.GR.1.2 limits distances to points sharing an x- or y-coordinate; the Think Deeper question uses horizontal plus vertical street distance (taxicab distance), which is a sum of two such distances and stays within Grade 6. The $0.25-a-mile driving cost is a practice figure close to real per-mile costs for gas and wear.

Answer key: interactive lesson

Reconnect

  1. What is |−9|?
    Answer: 9
    Absolute value is distance from zero.
  2. Which is greater, −4 or −11? Type the number.
    Answer: −4
    −4 is to the right of −11 on a number line.
  3. A bank statement shows a balance of −$15. Does the owner have money in the account or owe the bank? Type have or owe.
    Answer: owe
    A negative balance means the account is overdrawn by $15.
  4. Last week's elevation chart was a vertical number line. Which direction was positive on it, and which was negative?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Up (above sea level) is positive; down (below sea level) is negative
    Model answer: Up, above sea level, was positive, and down, below sea level, was negative.

Learn 1: Axes, Origin and Quadrants

  1. Use the grid. Name the quadrant of each point with a Roman numeral (I, II, III or IV).
    Answer: II; III; IV
    Signs: (−, +) is II, (−, −) is III, (+, −) is IV.
  2. Without plotting, which quadrant is (−7.5, −0.5) in? Type the Roman numeral.
    Answer: III
    Both coordinates are negative.
  3. Where is the point (0, −4)? Type the name of the axis it lies on.
    Answer: on the y-axis
    x = 0 means no movement left or right, so the point is on the y-axis.
  4. Ravi plotted (−3, 6) by going up 3 and left 6. Explain his mistake and where he should have gone.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: He swapped the order: x comes first (left/right), y second (up/down); Correct move: 3 left, then 6 up, ending in Quadrant II
    Model answer: He mixed up the order. The first number, −3, is x, so he should go 3 left; the second number, 6, is y, so then 6 up. The point is in Quadrant II.

Learn 2: Reflections Across the Axes

  1. Reflect (−6, 1) across the x-axis. Type the new ordered pair.
    Answer: (−6, −1)
    Across the x-axis, the y-coordinate changes sign.
  2. The points (4, −7) and (−4, −7) are reflections across which axis? Type x or y.
    Answer: the y-axis
    The x-coordinates are opposites, so the flip is left-right, across the y-axis.
  3. Amara says (3, 8) and (−3, −8) are reflections across one axis. Is she right? Explain, and describe how to get from one point to the other.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Not a reflection across a single axis: both coordinates changed sign; Reflect across one axis, then the other (ending in the opposite quadrant)
    Model answer: She is not right. Both signs changed, so it isn't a single reflection. You would reflect (3, 8) across the y-axis to (−3, 8), then across the x-axis to (−3, −8). The point ends up in the opposite quadrant, III instead of I.

Learn 3: Distance on the Grid

  1. How many units apart are (−6, −2) and (−6, 5)?
    Answer: 7 units
    Opposite sides of the x-axis: |−2| + |5| = 7.
  2. How many units apart are (−8, 4) and (−3, 4)?
    Answer: 5 units
    Same side of the y-axis: |−8| − |−3| = 8 − 3 = 5.
  3. Explain how to decide whether to add or subtract absolute values when finding the distance between (−4, 9) and (−4, −2). Then find the distance.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: The y-coordinates have opposite signs (opposite sides of the x-axis), so add; Distance: 9 + 2 = 11 units
    Model answer: The y-coordinates, 9 and −2, are on opposite sides of the x-axis, so I add their absolute values: 9 + 2 = 11 units.

Learn 4: Money: Mapping a Delivery Route

  1. How many miles is it from the library (−5, 3) to Ms. Ortiz (−5, −4)?
    Answer: 7 miles
    |3| + |−4| = 7 miles.
  2. How many miles is it from Ms. Ortiz (−5, −4) to Mr. Bell (6, −4)?
    Answer: 11 miles
    |−5| + |6| = 11 miles.
  3. Each mile costs about $0.25 in gas. What is the gas cost for those two legs together?
    Answer: 4.5 dollars
    7 + 11 = 18 miles; 18 × $0.25 = $4.50.
  4. Maya sells Ms. Ortiz 2 loaves and Mr. Bell 3 loaves at $9 each. After the $4.50 for gas, how much does she earn from these two customers? Should she offer delivery to a customer 20 miles away who buys just 1 loaf? Show your reasoning.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Earnings: 5 × $9 = $45; $45 − $4.50 = $40.50; Far customer: 20 miles there (and possibly back) at $0.25 = $5 to $10, against $9 of sales; A reasoned decision (charge a delivery fee, require a minimum order, or combine with other stops)
    Model answer: 5 loaves × $9 = $45, and $45 − $4.50 = $40.50. A customer 20 miles away costs $5 in gas one way and $10 round trip, while 1 loaf brings in $9, and that's before flour and her time. She should only deliver that far if the customer orders more loaves, pays a delivery fee, or lives near another stop.

Hands-On

  1. List four ordered pairs you walked, the quadrant (or axis) of each, and one reflection you made with its starting and ending coordinates.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Four ordered pairs with correct quadrants or axes; One reflection with correct before and after coordinates and the axis named
    Model answer: (−3, 5): Quadrant II. (6, −2): Quadrant IV. (0, −7): on the y-axis. (−4.5, −4.5): Quadrant III. Reflection: I stood on (6, −2), flipped across the x-axis and walked to (6, 2).
  2. Describe your neighborhood map: list your five places with coordinates, your three-stop route, and the total number of blocks. Show how you found each leg's distance.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Five places with coordinates; A three-stop route using horizontal and vertical legs; Each leg's distance computed correctly with absolute value (add or subtract); A correct total
    Model answer: Home (0, 0), park (3, 2), grandma's house (−4, 2), church (−4, −3), grocery store (5, −3). Route: home to park: 3 right + 2 up = 5 blocks. Park to grandma's: |3| + |−4| = 7 blocks. Grandma's to church: |2| + |−3| = 5 blocks. Total: 17 blocks.

Think Deeper

  1. Three corners of a rectangle are at (−4, 3), (5, 3) and (5, −2). Find the fourth corner, the length of each side, and the perimeter. Explain how you know.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Fourth corner (−4, −2); Side lengths 9 and 5, found with absolute value; Perimeter 28 units; Clear reasoning (matching coordinates of opposite sides)
    Model answer: The fourth corner is (−4, −2): it lines up with (−4, 3) vertically and with (5, −2) horizontally. The top side, from (−4, 3) to (5, 3), is |−4| + |5| = 9 units. The right side, from (5, 3) to (5, −2), is |3| + |−2| = 5 units. Perimeter = 9 + 5 + 9 + 5 = 28 units.
  2. Maya wants to rent a stall at a second market. Option A is at (−6, −6); option B is at (3, 6). Her current customers are at (−5, 3), (−5, −4) and (6, −4). Which location is closer to more of her customers if she drives only along the grid roads? Show the mile distances and give a business reason for your choice.
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: Correct street distances (horizontal + vertical) from each option to each customer; Comparison of totals or closeness; A business reason (gas cost, time, new customers)
    Model answer: Road distance = horizontal miles + vertical miles. Option A (−6, −6): to (−5, 3) is 1 + 9 = 10; to (−5, −4) is 1 + 2 = 3; to (6, −4) is 12 + 2 = 14; total 27 miles. Option B (3, 6): to (−5, 3) is 8 + 3 = 11; to (−5, −4) is 8 + 10 = 18; to (6, −4) is 3 + 10 = 13; total 42 miles. Option A is closer to two of the three customers and has the smaller total (27 vs. 42 miles), a difference of 15 miles, so delivering from there saves gas and time.

Exit Check

  1. Which quadrant has points with a negative x-coordinate and a positive y-coordinate? Type the Roman numeral.
    Answer: Quadrant II
    (−, +).
  2. Reflect (5, −3) across the y-axis. Type the new ordered pair.
    Answer: (−5, −3)
    Across the y-axis, x changes sign.
  3. How many units apart are (−7, 2) and (3, 2)?
    Answer: 10 units
    |−7| + |3| = 10.
  4. What is the origin, and why do both of its coordinates equal 0?
    Answer: Written response: score with the rubric.
    Rubric: The point where the axes cross; You move 0 left/right and 0 up/down from it
    Model answer: The origin is where the x-axis and y-axis cross. Both coordinates are 0 because it is the starting point: zero steps across and zero steps up or down.

Answer key: printable page

  1. Name the quadrant or axis for each point: (−2, 7), (4, −1), (0, 3), (−5.5, −2).
    Answer: II; IV; y-axis; III.
  2. Reflect (−3, 8) across the x-axis and across the y-axis.
    Answer: Across x: (−3, −8). Across y: (3, 8).
  3. Find the distance between (−9, −1) and (−9, 6).
    Answer: |−1| + |6| = 7 units.
  4. Find the distance between (2, −4) and (10, −4).
    Answer: 10 − 2 = 8 units.
  5. A rectangle has corners (−3, 4), (6, 4), (6, −3) and (−3, −3). Find its perimeter.
    Answer: Sides 9 and 7; perimeter 32 units.
  6. A delivery goes from (−4, 5) to (−4, −2) to (3, −2). How many miles is that? At $0.25 a mile, what is the gas cost?
    Answer: 7 + 7 = 14 miles; $3.50.
